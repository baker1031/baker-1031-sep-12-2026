# 721 Exchanges and the Step-Transaction Doctrine


[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read**

I have been thinking about the difference between a sequence and a prearranged result. The 1031-then-721 DST bridge can be useful, but its tax treatment depends on the steps being more than names on the way to the same destination.

First-order thinking sees a DST followed by a REIT through a [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html). Second-order thinking asks whether the DST was a real investment with its own risk and return, whether time and uncertainty separated the steps, and whether the 721 exit was an option rather than a commitment. The step-transaction doctrine is the IRS and judicial principle that can collapse steps into one transaction when they are really an integrated plan.

## What the step-transaction doctrine is

The step-transaction doctrine is a judicial and IRS principle that can treat formally separate steps as a single integrated transaction for tax purposes when they are components of one prearranged plan. Instead of respecting each step's individual treatment, the doctrine collapses the steps and taxes the overall result as if intermediate steps lacked independent existence. It applies substance over form.

Courts and the IRS use several formulations to ask whether steps should be collapsed: the **end result test**, **interdependence test**, and **binding commitment test**. Each asks, in a different way, whether steps are sufficiently connected to be one transaction. The doctrine is meant to stop taxpayers from obtaining through a sequence of steps a tax result they could not obtain directly.

## The risk to the 1031-then-721 strategy

The [1031-then-721 DST bridge](can-you-1031-then-721-dst-bridge-strategy.html) has two steps: a 1031 into a DST and a later 721 into a REIT. If those steps are seen as one integrated, prearranged plan, the IRS could potentially collapse them and challenge the intended deferral.

The concern is concrete. If the steps were collapsed and the substance were treated as a direct contribution of the original property to the REIT, the 1031 into the DST could be disregarded. A direct contribution to a REIT may not qualify for a 1031 because an investor cannot 1031 directly into a REIT. Collapsing could otherwise challenge the deferral's validity.

> If the 1031 into the DST and the 721 into the REIT are viewed as a single prearranged plan, the step-transaction doctrine could collapse them and potentially challenge the deferral the strategy relies on.

## Why the concern arises

An investor generally cannot 1031 directly into a REIT. The DST bridge reaches the REIT indirectly, which can look like an attempt to work around that direct-1031-into-REIT prohibition if the steps are too integrated.

The risk increases when the DST is a mere conduit: a brief, predetermined stop used solely to reach the REIT, without independent significance. In that framing, the IRS might argue the substance is a non-qualifying direct move into the REIT and that the DST was not a genuine investment.

The response is not a label. It is genuine and independent DST investment, time between the steps, and a 721 exit that is not predetermined. Those facts support separate transactions rather than one prearranged plan.

## Mitigating the risk

Three features support independent significance and mitigate step-transaction risk:

1. **A genuine DST investment.** The DST should be a real, substantive investment. The investor should genuinely invest, bear the DST's risks, and receive its returns; it should not be a conduit.
2. **Time between the steps.** More time between the 1031 and 721, and a meaningful holding period in the DST, can support the conclusion that the transactions are independent. There is no fixed required period; the question is genuine separateness rather than a bright-line duration.
3. **A non-predetermined 721 exit.** The exit should be an option or possibility, not a guaranteed or binding commitment made at the 1031. A non-guaranteed exit supports independence.

These factors weaken the case for collapsing the steps. The analysis is technical and should be addressed through professional structuring and tax and legal guidance.

## The role of the non-guaranteed 721 exit

The non-guaranteed exit is particularly important. If a 721 exit were guaranteed or predetermined at the time of the 1031, the sequence could look like a single prearranged plan and increase risk under the doctrine. An option rather than a guarantee supports the independence of the steps.

That structure also reflects business reality: the REIT decides whether and when to acquire the DST. Exit timing and certainty therefore are not always guaranteed. For investors, this can look like a limitation. It is also partly a tax-structuring feature because uncertainty about the exit can support the step-transaction analysis.

## Key Takeaways

- The step-transaction doctrine can collapse integrated steps into one transaction if they are really one prearranged plan.
- In a 1031-then-721 DST bridge, collapsing the steps could challenge tax deferral.
- A genuine DST investment, time between the steps, and a non-predetermined 721 exit—an option rather than a guarantee—help mitigate risk.
- The non-guaranteed 721 exit partly supports the steps' independence. This is highly technical and requires professional structuring.

## Structuring for independent significance

The practical goal is to structure the bridge so the steps are respected as separate transactions. That means a DST investment with its own merits, risks, and returns; meaningful time in the DST before any 721 exit; and an exit that remains a non-predetermined possibility.

DST sponsors and professionals who design reputable DST-to-REIT offerings take the doctrine into account. Well-designed structures incorporate a genuine DST investment and a non-guaranteed exit. Investors should use sound offerings, treat the DST as a genuine investment rather than a token pass-through, and follow tax and legal guidance while avoiding [common mistakes that derail an UPREIT exchange](common-mistakes-in-a-721-upreit-exchange.html).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the step-transaction doctrine?

It is a judicial and IRS principle that can treat formally separate steps as a single integrated transaction when they are really components of one prearranged plan. The doctrine can disregard each step's treatment and tax the overall result based on substance over form. The end result, interdependence, and binding commitment tests help determine whether steps should be collapsed. It is relevant to a DST bridge because that strategy has multiple steps.

### How does the step-transaction doctrine affect the DST bridge strategy?

It creates potential risk. If the 1031 into a DST and later 721 into a REIT are viewed as one prearranged plan, the IRS could collapse them and challenge deferral. For example, it could see the substance as a direct non-qualifying move into the REIT and disregard the 1031. The steps need independent significance.

### Why is the DST bridge at risk from this doctrine?

An investor cannot generally 1031 directly into a REIT, so a DST bridge reaches the REIT indirectly. If the DST is a mere conduit in a prearranged plan, the IRS might view the substance as a direct non-qualifying REIT move. The DST must be a genuine, independent investment rather than a pass-through.

### How do I mitigate the step-transaction risk?

Use a genuine DST investment with real merits, risks, and returns; allow meaningful time in the DST; and structure a 721 exit as a non-predetermined option or possibility rather than a guaranteed binding commitment. These features support independent significance and should be handled with professional guidance.

### Why isn't the 721 exit guaranteed?

A guaranteed exit at the time of the 1031 can make the steps look like one prearranged plan. A non-predetermined option supports independence. It also reflects business reality because the REIT decides whether and when to acquire the DST. The uncertainty serves business and tax-structuring purposes and is partly a deliberate feature, not merely a limitation.

### Does the DST need to be a genuine investment?

Yes. The investor should genuinely invest in the DST, bear its risks, and receive its returns. A real, substantive DST investment has independent significance. A token or brief predetermined stop on the way to a REIT does not support the analysis.

### How much time should pass between the steps?

More time generally supports independence, but there is no fixed required period. A meaningful DST holding period before a 721 exit can show duration and substance. Tax professionals and offering structure address timing in the context of the complete facts; the goal is genuine separateness, not a bright-line rule.

### Is the step-transaction doctrine commonly applied to DST bridges?

It is a risk that well-structured DST bridges address in advance with genuine investment, a non-guaranteed exit, and appropriate timing. Reputable structures seek to support independent significance. The doctrine is a real consideration, but sound offerings and professional guidance aim to manage it rather than leave it unaddressed.

### What should I do to avoid step-transaction problems?

Use well-structured bridge offerings, genuinely invest in and hold the DST, and follow CPA and attorney advice. Do not treat the DST as a token step in a prearranged plan. This is technical, so use reputable structures and professional guidance.

### Does the step-transaction doctrine apply to a direct 721 exchange?

The concern is mainly for multi-step arrangements such as a 1031-then-721 DST bridge because multiple steps could be collapsed. A direct 721 contribution to a REIT is one step, so the doctrine is not a concern in the same way. It is primarily an issue where the independence of multiple bridge steps matters.

### What happens if the IRS successfully applies the doctrine?

The IRS could challenge the intended tax treatment, disregard the 1031 into the DST, treat the substance as a non-qualifying transaction, and recognize rather than defer gain. Well-structured transactions seek to meet independence requirements and avoid that outcome. The potential loss of deferral is why sound structuring and professional guidance matter.

### Should the step-transaction doctrine deter me from the DST bridge?

Not by itself. It is a technical consideration addressed by well-designed offerings through genuine DST investment, non-guaranteed exit, and appropriate timing. It is a reason to use sound structures and professional guidance, not a fundamental barrier to a properly structured bridge.

## Glossary

**Step-Transaction Doctrine**  
A principle that collapses integrated steps into one transaction.

**Substance Over Form**  
Taxing transaction substance rather than merely its form.

**Integrated Plan**  
Steps that are really one transaction and may be collapsed.

**End Result Test**  
A step-transaction test focused on intended end result.

**Interdependence Test**  
A test focused on the steps' interdependence.

**Binding Commitment Test**  
A test focused on whether steps were committed in advance.

**Independent Significance**  
Steps having their own substance and avoiding collapse.

**Conduit**  
A pass-through step lacking independent significance.

**Genuine DST Investment**  
A real, substantive DST investment that supports independence.

**Non-Predetermined Exit**  
A 721 exit that is an option rather than a guarantee and supports independence.

**Holding Period**  
Time in the DST supporting separateness.

**1031-then-721 Strategy**  
The multi-step DST bridge subject to doctrine analysis.

**DST Bridge**  
Reaching a REIT through a DST structured for independence.

**Prearranged Plan**  
Integrated steps that the doctrine could collapse.

**Collapsing**  
Treating separate steps as one transaction.

**Tax Structuring**  
Designing steps to support their independence.
