# DST Investment Minimums by Sponsor & Offering


[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**Delaware Statutory Trusts · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026**

I start with the practical question behind many DST allocations: how much must actually be committed? There is no universal DST minimum. It varies by sponsor, offering, and whether capital is 1031 proceeds or new cash.

First-order thinking turns an article's $100,000 or $25,000 example into a live term. Second-order thinking checks the current PPM, subscription documents, availability, debt-replacement math, fees, and suitability. Published figures are guidance; the current offering controls.

This guide covers 1031 and cash minimums, sponsor variation, multiple-DST allocations, diversification, and live-term confirmation. DST interests are securities offered through Aurora Securities after suitability review; Baker 1031 does not provide tax or legal advice. [Our DST guide](dst-guide.html) supplies broader context.

## Typical 1031 vs. Cash Minimums

1031 minimums are often around $100,000 and can range roughly from $25,000 to $100,000. Cash, non-1031 minimums can be lower, sometimes around $25,000. The same offering may have a higher exchange threshold and lower cash threshold because exchange proceeds are usually committed in larger increments and carry 1031 requirements.

These were general patterns in offerings reviewed as of 2026, not promises. The key question is whether capital is exchange proceeds from a property sale or new cash. A cash investor has no exchange deadlines or debt-replacement requirement; an exchanger does.

## How Minimums Vary by Sponsor

Every sponsor and offering sets its own threshold according to property, structure, target investor base, equity raise, and desired investor-pool size. One multifamily DST can require a $100,000 1031 investment, another net-lease offering $50,000, and a cash offering $25,000. Larger institutional offerings may set higher minimums; smaller or retail-oriented offerings may set lower ones. [Minimums vary by sponsor](sponsors.html), and there is no market-wide number.

DST beneficial interests are finite. A minimum helps sponsor allocation of a limited equity raise. The specific current offering, not a past deal or general article, supplies the only reliable threshold.

## Planning Multiple-DST Allocations

Low minimums let one exchange be divided among multiple trusts. With $500,000 of proceeds and $100,000 minimums, an investor could in principle [allocate across five DSTs](how-many-dsts-should-you-own.html), or make fewer larger positions. A portfolio can spread exposure among multifamily, net-lease, industrial, medical office, sponsors, and markets.

Do more than divide proceeds by a minimum. A 1031 exchange normally requires [replacing debt as well as equity](1031-exchange-debt-replacement-mortgage-boot.html). Trust-level non-recourse debt can help meet the mortgage replacement requirement, so each position's equity and debt both belong in allocation math.

## Key Takeaways

- 1031 minimums are often around $100,000, sometimes $25,000 to $100,000; cash minimums are sometimes around $25,000.
- Minimums differ by sponsor and offering, with no industry-wide standard.
- Low thresholds can split one exchange across several DSTs while accounting for debt replacement.
- Diversification spreads concentration risk but does not eliminate illiquidity, fees, or the absence of guarantees.

## Minimums, Diversification, and Current Terms

Direct replacement real estate can force one large bet in one building and market. Fractional DST interests can spread capital across assets, sponsors, geographies, and sectors. That cushions a tenant loss, soft market, lease-up problem, or sponsor issue in one position, but it does not remove risk.

Every number can change. A DST with a $100,000 minimum last quarter may have closed, reopened, repriced, or been replaced. Confirm the 1031 or cash minimum, availability, and current terms in the PPM and [private placement memorandum](how-to-review-a-ppm.html). A trust can close once its finite equity is fully subscribed.

The minimum is not total economic cost. Review [upfront costs and ongoing fees](dst-fees-and-load-explained.html), which reduce net return, alongside investment size. [Meeting an offering's dollar minimum](dst-minimum-investment-and-accreditation-requirements.html) does not itself make a DST suitable. Accreditation and a [suitability review](dst-suitability-and-reg-bi.html) consider financial situation, goals, liquidity needs, and risk tolerance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the typical minimum investment for a DST?

Often about $100,000 for a 1031 investment, sometimes $25,000 to $100,000, and sometimes about $25,000 for cash. Each sponsor and offering differs, so current live documents are authoritative.

### Why is the 1031 minimum higher than the cash minimum?

Exchangers often have large property-sale proceeds and sponsors structure exchange allocations in larger increments. Cash has no QI, deadline, or debt-replacement features, so a sponsor may accept a smaller commitment. The real estate can be the same; capital source changes the term.

### Do all DSTs have the same minimum investment?

No. Property, structure, investor base, equity raise, sponsor, and investor-pool design vary. Examples may include $100,000, $50,000, or $25,000, but there is no universal floor.

### Can I split my 1031 exchange across multiple DSTs?

Yes. Low thresholds can create a diversified replacement portfolio by property type, sponsor, and market. Each position must meet minimums and total equity and debt must satisfy 1031 planning.

### How do low DST minimums help diversification?

They replace one concentrated property with several fractional interests. Diversification can reduce reliance on one asset, manager, market, or sector, but cannot remove risk, fees, illiquidity, or variable distributions.

### What is the lowest amount I can invest in a DST?

The live offering decides. Cash minimums can sometimes be around $25,000; 1031 investments are often higher. Find the current cash or exchange threshold in current documents rather than relying on general figures.

### Do DST minimums change over time?

Yes. Offerings close, reopen, or change their terms. Equity-raise size, asset type, markets, and investor base can change a threshold. Verify the present term before committing.

### How do I confirm a DST's current minimum?

Review the live PPM and subscription documents, confirm whether capital is cash or exchange proceeds, and confirm availability with the broker-dealer. A fully subscribed DST may no longer be open.

### Does the minimum include debt in a DST?

Generally, the minimum is equity commitment, not debt. Trust-level non-recourse debt is layered on top. Exchangers must plan both equity and debt because mortgage replacement can matter for complete deferral.

### Can I invest with new cash instead of 1031 proceeds?

Yes. A new-cash investor buys passive real estate directly, often at a lower minimum, without 1031 deadlines or debt replacement. The investment does not defer a property-sale gain.

### How many DSTs should I split my exchange across?

There is no fixed number. It depends on total proceeds, live minimums, diversification objective, risk tolerance, complexity, and full equity/debt replacement. A handful of well-chosen positions may balance diversification and manageability.

### Are DST minimums negotiable?

Generally not. Sponsors set uniform terms for finite beneficial interests. If one minimum is unsuitable, look for a different offering rather than assume a lower threshold can be negotiated.

### What if I have more proceeds than the minimum requires?

Invest more in one trust or divide funds among multiple DSTs, while meeting each threshold and 1031 debt/equity requirements. Unreinvested proceeds can be taxable boot.

### Do I need to be accredited to meet a DST minimum?

Yes. Minimum satisfaction is separate from accredited status and suitability. Regulation D, often Rule 506(c), generally requires qualification based on income above $200,000 individually or $300,000 jointly in the past two years, net worth above $1 million excluding a primary residence, or other criteria. A broker-dealer confirms suitability.

## Glossary

**Investment Minimum** — Smallest investment a DST offering accepts.  
**1031 Minimum** — Minimum for exchange proceeds, often around $100,000.  
**Cash Minimum** — New-cash minimum, sometimes around $25,000.  
**Delaware Statutory Trust (DST)** — A trust holding real estate in which investors own fractional interests.  
**Beneficial Interest** — Fractional ownership stake in a DST.  
**1031 Exchange** — A like-kind exchange deferring capital-gains tax on investment property.  
**Exchange Proceeds** — Property-sale money reinvested through 1031.  
**Replacement Property** — New like-kind property acquired in an exchange.  
**Debt Replacement** — Replacement of sold-property debt for deferral.  
**Non-Recourse Debt** — Trust-level debt without investor personal liability.  
**Diversification** — Spreading capital to reduce concentration risk.  
**Boot** — Unreinvested proceeds that can be taxable.  
**Private Placement Memorandum (PPM)** — Offering document containing terms and minimum.  
**Subscription** — Formal process of committing to a DST.  
**Accredited Investor** — Investor meeting Regulation D income/net-worth standards.
**Suitability Review** — Assessing whether a DST fits the investor before subscription.
