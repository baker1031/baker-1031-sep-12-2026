# How to Write a 1031 Identification Letter

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

1031 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read

I keep seeing the same exchange risk arrive disguised as a paperwork task: the identification letter. It is the written record that fixes the replacement-property choices within the 45-day window. Get it wrong, deliver it late, or describe a property vaguely, and a deal that otherwise looked workable can lose its path to qualification.

The first-order view is that the letter is a form to finish before a deadline. The second-order view is that it sets the outer boundary of the capital you can deploy. You can acquire only replacement property you properly identify. This is why the document needs as much care as the property search itself.

## Why the identification letter matters

Within 45 days after the sale of relinquished property, replacement property must be identified in writing. A property omitted from a proper identification generally cannot become a qualifying replacement. A missing, late, or defective notice can therefore prevent completion of the exchange.

There are [exactly 45 calendar days to deliver a proper written identification](45-day-identification-period.html). After day 45, the list is locked, subject only to changes made before that deadline. There are no extensions built into the weekend or holiday calendar. The investment question and the timing question are inseparable here.

The letter preserves the options needed to complete the exchange only if it is rule-compliant, unambiguous, signed, dated, and timely delivered. That is a high standard for a document that can appear routine.

## The identification rules

One identification rule must be satisfied. The common **three-property rule** permits identification of up to three properties, regardless of value, and acquisition of any or all of them. It is often used for one primary target and up to two backups, sometimes including a DST.

The **200% rule** permits any number of properties when their combined value does not exceed 200% of the relinquished property's value. It can fit a list of several DSTs or multiple smaller properties, so long as the total stays within twice the relinquished value.

The **95% rule** is the rare fallback. If the list exceeds both the three-property and 200% limits, qualification can still be possible only by acquiring at least 95% of the value of everything identified. That acquisition requirement makes it an impractical rule for most exchanges.

Most investors use the three-property rule: up to three properties of any value, with a primary and perhaps one or two backups. The point is not to identify the most names. It is to identify a list that remains usable if the preferred deal changes.

## What the letter must contain

A proper letter must unambiguously describe each property. It must be written rather than oral or informal, and it must be signed by the taxpayer, or exchanger. It must also be dated and delivered within the 45-day period.

The usual recipient is the [qualified intermediary](what-is-a-qualified-intermediary.html). The rules can permit delivery to another party to the exchange who is not a disqualified person, but the QI is the normal recipient. In practical terms, the letter needs clear descriptions, a written form, the taxpayer's signature and date, timely receipt, and delivery to the right party.

Most qualified intermediaries provide an identification form. That is useful, but it does not move responsibility for the substance: the property descriptions still must be clear, the rules still must be met, and delivery still must occur on time.

## Unambiguous property description

The description has to identify the specific property without doubt. “A property in Dallas” is not enough. A street address, a legal description, or another equally specific description normally does the work.

For ordinary real property, an address such as “123 Main Street, Dallas, Texas” will often suffice, and a legal description can add certainty. Raw land may need the legal description because it lacks a clear street address.

For a DST interest, identify the specific DST and the percentage interest being acquired. For property to be constructed in an improvement exchange, describe the property and the planned improvements. The form of the description changes with the asset, but the standard does not: it must be unambiguous.

## Delivering and dating the letter

The QI, or other proper recipient, must receive the identification by [midnight of the 45th day after the relinquished property's sale](1031-exchange-deadline-calculator-45-180.html). Dating a letter is not enough when delivery occurs on day 46. The relevant fact is timely receipt.

Use a delivery method that leaves evidence: a dated and signed submission the QI acknowledges, or a documented transmission. The QI's process normally records the receipt date, and that record matters if timing is questioned later.

The count begins the day after the sale, runs in calendar days, and does not pause for weekends or holidays. Calculate it early and deliver before the last day if possible.

## Key takeaways

- Only properly identified property can serve as replacement property; a late or defective notice can fail the exchange.
- The three-property rule permits up to three properties of any value; the 200% rule permits more within 200% of relinquished value; the 95% rule is a demanding, rarely used fallback.
- The letter needs unambiguous descriptions, a written form, taxpayer signature, date, and timely delivery to the QI.
- A written revocation and replacement identification can be made before day 45. After day 45, the list is fixed.

## Revoking and amending identifications

Before the deadline, an exchanger may revoke a prior identification and submit a new written identification. That can remove a property, add one, or substitute one, provided the new notice is unambiguous, signed, and delivered to the QI by day 45.

The revocation itself must be written and delivered to the same proper party by the deadline. It gives the exchanger room to act on diligence: if an identified property becomes unsuitable, it can be withdrawn and replaced while the window remains open.

After day 45, there is no revocation, amendment, addition, or substitution. The final list is the list from which the exchanger may acquire. Treat the first 45 days as the only period in which this menu can be improved.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a 1031 identification letter?

It is the formal written document identifying replacement property within the 45-day period. It defines the potential replacements because only properly identified property may be acquired as a qualifying replacement. It needs unambiguous descriptions, a written form, the exchanger's signature and date, and timely delivery—usually to the QI. The QI typically supplies the form. A late or defective notice can fail the exchange.

### How many properties can I identify?

The three-property rule allows up to three properties of any value, with any or all acquired. The 200% rule allows more than three if their combined value does not exceed 200% of relinquished value. The rare 95% rule permits an excess only when at least 95% of identified value is acquired. A common three-property list is a primary plus up to two backups, often including a DST.

### What is the three-property rule?

It is the common rule allowing up to three properties regardless of value, with any or all acquired. Its flexibility supports a primary target plus one or two backups, including a fast-closing DST, without a value cap.

### What is the 200% rule?

It permits any number of properties when their combined value stays within 200% of relinquished value. It can be useful for several DSTs or multiple smaller properties, provided the total remains within twice the relinquished value.

### How must I describe the property?

Describe it unambiguously: normally by street address, legal description, or another clear and specific identifier. An address often works for real property, raw land usually needs a legal description, and a DST needs the specific DST and percentage interest. “A property in Dallas” does not qualify.

### Who do I deliver the identification to?

Usually the QI. The rules can permit another non-disqualified party to the exchange, but the QI is the standard and safest recipient. The QI must receive it by midnight of day 45 and normally documents receipt.

### When is the identification due?

It is due by midnight of the 45th calendar day after the sale. The count starts the day after the sale, has no weekend or holiday extension, and a notice received on day 46 is invalid.

### Can I change my identification after I submit it?

Yes, before day 45: revoke and amend in writing, using the same unambiguous, signed, QI-delivered process. After day 45, the list is locked and there can be no addition, change, or substitution.

### What happens if my identification is defective?

An ambiguous description, excess over the applicable rule, missing signature, late delivery, or delivery to the wrong party can make an identification invalid. The affected property cannot then be acquired as a qualifying replacement, and an entirely invalid list can fail the exchange.

### Should I identify backup properties?

Yes. A primary can fall through. Within the applicable rule, one or two backups create an alternative route to completion. A fast-closing DST is often used as a certain backup, so the exchange can still complete if the first choice fails.

### Can I identify a DST as a replacement?

Yes. Identify the particular DST and the percentage interest unambiguously. DSTs can be primary replacements or backups under the three-property or 200% rules. Their speed and certainty are why many investors use them alongside a direct-property target.

### Does the QI prepare the identification for me?

The QI normally provides a form, guidance, and receipt documentation. The exchanger and advisers still choose the properties, write sufficiently clear descriptions, follow the rules, and ensure the submission is timely.

### What counts as the date my 45-day clock starts?

The clock generally starts on the day after the closing, or transfer, of relinquished property; the sale day is not day one. It runs 45 calendar days to midnight. If several relinquished properties are sold in one exchange, the clock generally runs from the first transfer. Confirm the exact date with the QI and CPA.

### Can I identify a property still under construction?

Yes, in an improvement or build-to-suit exchange. The notice needs to describe the property and planned improvements sufficiently clearly. The improvements generally must be completed and received within the 180-day period to count toward exchange value. These exchanges are more complex and often use an exchange accommodation titleholder, so work closely with the QI and advisers.

## Glossary

**Identification Letter:** The written document identifying replacement property within 45 days.

**45-Day Identification Period:** The window from the relinquished sale to identify replacements.

**Three-Property Rule:** The common rule permitting up to three properties of any value.

**200% Rule:** A rule permitting more than three properties within 200% of relinquished value.

**95% Rule:** A rarely used fallback requiring acquisition of 95% of identified value.

**Unambiguous Description:** A clear, specific property description, such as an address or legal description.

**Legal Description:** A precise property description used when an address is insufficient.

**Qualified Intermediary (QI):** The standard recipient of the identification.

**Disqualified Person:** A party who cannot receive the identification, such as an agent or relative.

**Revocation:** Withdrawing an identification in writing before day 45.

**Amendment:** Changing identification in writing before the deadline.

**Backup Property:** An additional identified property that protects against a primary's failure.

**DST Identification:** Identifying the specific DST and percentage interest.

**Delivery:** Submitting identification to the QI within the window, preferably with documentation.

**Calendar Days:** The way the 45 days are counted, without weekend or holiday extensions.

**Improvement Exchange:** An exchange requiring the identification to describe planned improvements.
