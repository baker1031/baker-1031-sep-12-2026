# Net-Lease (NNN) Properties as 1031 Replacement

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**1031 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 15 min read**

I have been thinking about how quickly “passive income” can become shorthand for “safe income.” In a 1031 exchange, a single-tenant net-lease property can replace years of landlord work with a long contract and a clean rent stream. That is a real appeal. It is also a concentrated bet on one tenant, one lease, and one building.

First-order thinking sees a familiar tenant and a 10–20-year lease. Second-order thinking asks about the remaining term, the guarantor, the price paid for the rent, the building's next tenant, and whether the exchange needs control or diversification. This guide covers the mechanics, the appeal, the risks, and direct NNN ownership versus a net-lease DST.

## What Is a Net-Lease Property?

In a triple-net (NNN) lease, the tenant pays rent plus property taxes, insurance, and maintenance. The landlord collects rent while the tenant carries the operating costs, which leaves the owner with limited day-to-day responsibility.

There are single-net and double-net leases, true triple-net leases, and absolute-net leases. In an absolute-net lease, the tenant handles virtually all property costs, including the structure and roof. True NNN and absolute-net arrangements are therefore the most hands-off for the owner.

Common tenants include pharmacies, dollar stores, quick-service and casual restaurants, bank branches, auto-parts stores, convenience stores, and medical or urgent-care facilities. Many are national or regional brands on long leases, but brand recognition alone is not credit analysis.

## Why NNN Appeals to 1031 Exchangers

For an owner moving through a [1031 exchange](1031-exchange-guide.html), NNN can exchange active management for contract-defined income. There are no apartment tenants to chase, no toilets to fix, and fewer operating expenses to manage. This is why it is a frequent choice for retiring landlords who want to retain real-estate exposure and income without the work.

Long lease terms—often **10–20 years**—plus renewal options and rent escalations can make future income more visible. The trade-off is clear: NNN often favors predictability over the higher-effort, potentially higher-upside profile of active property management.

## Credit Tenants and Lease Terms

Income quality depends on the tenant and the lease. A financially strong, investment-grade credit tenant on a long lease with rent escalations is generally the strongest NNN setup. It can provide reliable, growing income, but it still requires diligence.

Weaker non-credit tenants and shorter remaining terms add risk. A smaller operator may have greater default exposure; a short remaining term brings re-leasing or vacancy risk sooner. The guarantor matters too. A corporate guarantee is generally stronger than a guarantee from an individual franchisee.

Review the tenant's finances, the remaining—not just original—term, escalation schedule, renewal options, guarantor, and the tenant's commitment to the site. The steadiness of a rent check can be real, but it can also hide weak terms.

## Single-Tenant Concentration Risk

The convenience of one tenant is also the central risk. One property and one lease means all income depends on one company. If it defaults, enters bankruptcy, restructures, or leaves at the end of the lease, income can fall to zero overnight.

Even a well-known brand can close a location. A vacant single-tenant building earns no rent while it is re-leased, a process that can take months or years and require capital for tenant improvements and leasing costs. The practical mitigants are a stronger credit tenant, a long remaining term, a solid corporate guarantor, a site that can be re-leased, diversification among tenants, or a diversified DST.

## Key Takeaways

- NNN leases move taxes, insurance, and maintenance to the tenant, creating passive income for the owner.
- Tenant credit, the remaining lease term, rent escalations, and the guarantor drive the quality of that income.
- Single-tenant concentration is the key risk; a diversified DST can spread exposure across many tenants and properties.

## NNN Sectors and Tenant Types

Net-lease real estate spans several sectors, each with a different risk profile. [Necessity retail](1031-exchange-for-retail-nnn-owners.html)—pharmacies, dollar stores, convenience stores, and auto parts—can be resilient because customers need the goods regardless of the economy or e-commerce. Quick-service restaurants with corporate guarantees are common, though the quality varies by brand and guarantor.

Medical and urgent-care properties may benefit from demographic demand. Industrial and logistics net leases have benefited from e-commerce demand. Banks and financial branches may be stable today but face longer-term branch-consolidation trends. Sector affects both tenant durability and the building's re-leasing prospects. Necessity and e-commerce-resistant uses generally carry less risk than discretionary or structurally challenged ones.

## Rent Escalations and Income Growth

Unlike a fixed bond, an NNN lease may contain scheduled rent increases. They can be fixed percentages or tied to CPI, and they can offer some inflation protection without new landlord effort. Some leases raise rent annually, some every five years, and some only at renewal.

The schedule matters. Regular, meaningful increases support better long-term income than a flat lease. In practice, terms vary: a lease might provide annual **1.5–2%** increases, periodic increases every five years, a CPI link, or no increase until renewal. Read the actual schedule rather than assuming the label “NNN” solves inflation risk.

## Sale-Leaseback Properties

Many NNN properties reach the market through a sale-leaseback. The company sells its real estate and simultaneously leases it back on a long-term NNN lease. It frees capital while the company keeps operating from the building, and it creates a lease investment for the buyer.

The tenant's decision to sell and keep operating can be a positive sign of site commitment, especially with a corporate guarantee and long escalating lease. It does not replace tenant-credit and lease analysis. A sale-leaseback with a weaker operator carries the same single-tenant risk as any other NNN deal.

## NNN Direct vs. NNN DST

Direct NNN ownership provides control and the whole asset's potential upside. It also concentrates exposure in one tenant and requires the investor to find and close a particular property within the [45/180-day exchange deadlines](how-to-find-1031-replacement-property-fast.html), then qualify for any financing personally.

A net-lease DST can hold multiple NNN properties across tenants and sectors. It is fully passive and can close in days rather than weeks. Its leverage is pre-arranged and non-recourse, so an exchanger can replace debt without separately qualifying for a loan. The costs are fees and no direct control. For an investor concerned about a single tenant or the **45**-day identification period and **180**-day deadline, a diversified DST may fit better. Others use a direct NNN property as an anchor and a DST sleeve for diversification.

## Diligencing an NNN Property

The tenant and lease drive the income, so review financial strength, credit rating, remaining term, rent and escalation schedule, renewal options, guarantor, and the tenant's performance and commitment at the location. Then examine the real estate: location, condition, re-leasing prospects, and rent relative to market. Above-market rent may be hard to replicate after a vacancy.

For a DST, extend the review to the sponsor, tenant and property mix, leverage, projected distributions, fees, and hold. Projected distributions are estimates, not guarantees. An independent advisor can use the same discipline for a direct property and a DST, but the investor still has to decide which concentration and control trade-off is acceptable.

## NNN vs. Multifamily and Other Sectors

Multifamily spreads income across many tenants and can benefit from durable housing demand and inflation-linked rent growth. It has less single-tenant concentration, but it needs more management and has less fixed contractual income than a long NNN lease.

Industrial and logistics can have long leases to creditworthy tenants and e-commerce-linked demand. Medical office can have demographic demand and sticky tenants. Each sector balances tenant diversification, lease duration, growth, and resilience differently. NNN's distinct feature is hands-off, contract-defined income from one tenant: usually more predictable than multifamily, yet more concentrated. Investors who want both diversification and income can blend sectors in a replacement portfolio.

## The NNN Income Profile

NNN income can be bond-like in predictability while still being real estate with appreciation potential. A long lease to a credit tenant with escalations may deliver steady, slightly growing income with limited operating volatility. That profile often appeals to retirees and other owners who value income stability more than active growth.

The yield is typically moderate when the tenant is strong and the term is long; escalations can add modest growth and inflation protection. The price of that apparent stability is dependence on one tenant. It is not a fixed-income instrument, and the tenant must continue to perform.

## Common NNN Mistakes

The most common error is to underestimate concentration because the tenant's name is familiar. Strong brands can close locations and fail. Other mistakes include ignoring a short remaining term, overlooking the guarantor, paying a price supported by above-market rent, and choosing a challenged sector or weak location.

The remedy is discipline: review tenant, lease, guarantor, sector, and real estate together. For some exchangers, a diversified NNN DST spreads the risk that direct ownership concentrates. An experienced advisor can help surface the issues, but no process turns an offering into a guarantee.

## Is NNN Right for Your Exchange?

NNN can be a fit if you want passive, predictable income, limited management, and visibility from a long lease—and if you accept single-tenant concentration directly or diversify it through a DST. It can make sense for retiring landlords and income-focused investors.

It may fit less well if you want active value-add upside, cannot tolerate a one-tenant exposure, or require maximum diversification. The direct-versus-DST choice has real consequences for control, financing, timing, and risk. A suitable option should fit your exchange amount, timetable, and risk tolerance, not just the appeal of a familiar tenant.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a net-lease (NNN) property?

It is a property where the tenant pays rent plus property taxes, insurance, and maintenance—the three nets. The owner receives passive income with few operating responsibilities. Pharmacies, dollar stores, quick-service restaurants, medical facilities, and similar tenants often operate on long leases.

### Why do 1031 exchangers like NNN properties?

They can provide passive, predictable income with long lease terms and little management. That can be useful for owners trading active landlording for hands-off income. Income visibility from a long lease to a credit tenant is a key appeal.

### What is the main risk of a single NNN property?

Single-tenant concentration. If the tenant defaults, goes bankrupt, or leaves at lease end, income can drop to zero. Re-leasing can take months and capital. Diversifying across tenants or using a net-lease DST can reduce, but not eliminate, that exposure.

### Should I buy NNN directly or through a DST?

Direct ownership offers control and whole-asset upside, but leaves you with one tenant, a closing you must complete inside the exchange deadlines, and financing you must qualify for yourself. A DST can diversify across tenants and properties, close quickly, remain passive, and use pre-arranged non-recourse leverage. The trade-offs are fees and no direct control.

### What is a credit tenant?

It is a financially strong, often investment-grade tenant. A long lease to a strong corporate tenant is often considered the preferred NNN profile. A weaker or non-credit tenant adds default and vacancy risk.

### How long are NNN leases?

They are often **10–20 years** with renewal options. The remaining lease term is more important than the original term: a long remaining term provides visibility, while a short one moves re-leasing risk closer.

### What are rent escalations in a net lease?

They are scheduled increases in rent, fixed or CPI-linked, which can grow income and offer inflation protection. The form and timing vary, so inspect the lease schedule rather than assuming it is strong.

### What sectors are NNN properties in?

They include necessity retail, quick-service restaurants, medical and urgent care, industrial and logistics, and bank branches. Necessity and e-commerce-resistant uses often have lower risk than discretionary or structurally challenged sectors, though the particular tenant and location still matter.

### What is a sale-leaseback property?

It is a property a company sells and then leases back under a long-term NNN lease. The company frees capital and continues to operate there. Its commitment to the site can be positive, but the credit and lease terms still require review.

### Can a net-lease DST diversify my risk?

Yes. A net-lease DST may hold multiple NNN properties across tenants and sectors, reducing the one-tenant exposure of a direct NNN property. Its passive, fast-closing form can also help an exchanger facing the 45-day identification window. Risk is not eliminated.

### What should I diligence in an NNN property?

Review the tenant's finances and credit, remaining term, rents and escalations, renewal options, guarantor, real-estate location and condition, re-leasing prospects, and rent versus market. In a DST, also review the sponsor, portfolio, leverage, fees, projected distributions, and hold.

### What happens if my NNN tenant leaves?

The property has no rent until it is re-leased. That can require months or years, tenant-improvement spending, and leasing costs. This is why credit, term, location, and diversification are central.

### Is NNN income guaranteed?

No. A strong credit tenant on a long lease can support dependable income, but companies can close locations or fail. One tenant's performance supports the whole property's income.

### Is a corporate guarantee better than a franchisee guarantee?

Generally, yes. A corporate guarantee places a larger parent company behind the lease, whereas a franchisee guarantee may rely on a single operator with fewer resources. Assess the actual guarantor.

### Is NNN right for my 1031 exchange?

It can fit an investor seeking passive, predictable income and limited management who accepts direct concentration or uses a DST for diversification. It is less suited to an investor seeking active upside or maximum diversification.

### How does NNN compare to multifamily for an exchange?

NNN provides hands-off, contract-defined income from one tenant: predictable but concentrated. Multifamily spreads income across many tenants, benefits from housing demand and rent growth, and needs more management. A DST can provide diversified multifamily exposure.

### What yield do NNN properties offer?

They typically offer a moderate yield when a strong credit tenant and long lease reduce risk. Escalations add modest growth. A weak tenant or short term may offer more yield as compensation for greater risk.

### Is NNN income like a bond?

It can be bond-like in its predictable, slightly growing cash flow from a long credit lease. It remains real estate with appreciation potential and with income dependent on one tenant continuing to perform.

### What is an absolute-net lease?

It is a lease where the tenant handles virtually all property costs, including the structure and roof. It is even more hands-off for the owner than a standard triple-net lease.

### How do I reduce single-tenant risk in NNN?

Diversify across properties and tenants, prefer strong investment-grade credit tenants, long leases, corporate guarantees, resilient sectors, and locations that can be re-leased. A diversified net-lease DST can spread several such exposures in one investment.

### Does the property's location matter in NNN?

Yes. Even when the tenant pays operating costs, location determines re-leasing prospects and value if the tenant leaves. A strong site is easier to re-lease than a weak one.

### Can I exchange into NNN to retire from active management?

Yes. A retiring landlord can exchange into hands-off net-lease income from a credit tenant, or a diversified net-lease DST, and retain real-estate income while leaving active management behind. The concentration risk still needs attention.

### What lease length should I look for in NNN?

A long remaining term—often **10** or more years—can provide income visibility and reduce near-term re-leasing exposure. Also review renewal options. Do not rely solely on the original lease length.

### Are quick-service restaurant NNN properties a good investment?

They can be, particularly with a strong national brand and corporate guarantee on a long lease. A lease backed only by an individual franchisee can be less secure. Evaluate the particular tenant, guarantor, and lease.

### How does a net-lease DST differ from buying NNN directly?

A DST can hold multiple NNN properties across tenants and sectors, offer passive and fast closing, and use non-recourse leverage. Direct NNN offers control, a single-tenant focus, and self-arranged financing. Many exchangers value the DST's diversification.

### What rent escalations are typical in NNN leases?

Some increase rent annually by a fixed percentage such as **1.5–2%**, others every five years, others with CPI, and others only at renewal. More regular and meaningful escalations support better long-term income growth.

### Is NNN a good fit for a retiree?

It can provide passive, predictable income with little management. The main caution is single-tenant concentration, which some retirees address with a diversified net-lease DST rather than one direct property.

### Can I blend direct NNN with a net-lease DST?

Yes. An investor may buy one or two direct NNN properties and add a net-lease DST for diversification. The combination can retain some control while reducing the concentration of an all-direct approach.

### What makes a strong net-lease investment overall?

Look for a strong investment-grade or corporate tenant, a long remaining term, regular escalations, a corporate guarantee, a resilient sector, and a re-leasable location—at a price that is not inflated by above-market rent. If several of those are weak, the income is riskier than it appears.

## Glossary

- **Triple-Net (NNN) Lease:** Tenant pays rent plus property taxes, insurance, and maintenance.
- **Absolute-Net Lease:** Tenant handles virtually all costs, including the structure and roof.
- **Credit Tenant:** A tenant with strong, often investment-grade, creditworthiness.
- **Single-Tenant Concentration:** Dependence on one tenant for all of a property's income.
- **Rent Escalation:** A scheduled lease increase that grows income over time.
- **Guarantor:** The corporate or franchisee party that guarantees lease obligations; corporate is generally stronger.
- **Sale-Leaseback:** A company sells property and leases it back on a long-term NNN lease.
- **Net-Lease DST:** A DST holding multiple net-lease properties for diversified passive income.
- **Re-Leasing Risk:** The risk and cost of finding a new tenant after a vacancy.
- **Renewal Option:** A tenant's right to extend a lease, affecting income visibility.
- **Necessity Retail:** Retail selling needed goods, such as pharmacies or dollar stores, that tends to be more resilient and e-commerce-resistant.
- **Investment-Grade:** A credit rating signaling strong financial strength.
