# DST Loan & Leverage: Matching Debt in a 1031

[Home](baker1031.html) · [Back to All Insights](insights.html)

Delaware Statutory Trusts · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read

I keep seeing 1031 exchange planning focus first on cash proceeds, while the mortgage payoff is left for later. That order creates trouble. To fully defer, the replacement side generally has to account for both the equity and the debt from the relinquished property.

Many Delaware Statutory Trusts have pre-arranged, non-recourse trust-level financing. The investor takes a pro-rata share of that existing debt with a beneficial interest, without applying for, signing, or personally guaranteeing a new loan. That can help replace relinquished debt within a 1031 exchange. DST interests are securities offered to accredited investors after suitability review; projected distributions are never guaranteed, and Baker 1031 does not provide tax or legal advice.

Matching debt is one step in [our 2026 DST guide](dst-guide.html).

## The 1031 debt-replacement rule

For full capital-gains deferral, an investor generally reinvests net proceeds and acquires replacement property with equal or greater value. Debt is part of that comparison. If the relinquished property had a mortgage, the replacement side needs equal or greater debt, or the investor needs additional cash to make up the shortfall. Equity and debt both need to be replaced.

The shortfall is often called [boot](what-is-boot-1031-exchange-how-to-avoid.html). Replacing all cash equity but reducing debt can create taxable mortgage boot. The investor may need comparable replacement financing or fresh cash. Finding appropriate real estate with the needed leverage and qualifying for new financing inside the exchange window is often difficult.

> Reinvesting all cash is only half the exchange. Unreplaced debt can become taxable mortgage boot.

## How DST leverage works

In a leveraged DST, the sponsor acquires property using investor equity and a trust-level mortgage. Each beneficial owner receives a pro-rata share of both equity and debt. If the DST is financed at 50% loan-to-value, the investor’s interest carries approximately that relationship between allocated debt and equity.

That allocated share can count toward debt replacement. Rather than originating a new loan for a particular property, the investor selects a DST with leverage that aligns with the amount of debt needing replacement. The pre-arranged debt may satisfy the equal-or-greater-debt test and help avoid mortgage boot. Debt replacement is accomplished by allocation rather than new loan origination.

First-order thinking treats a high-LTV DST as the simple way to defer tax. Second-order thinking asks whether that LTV matches the investor’s relinquished debt, risk tolerance, debt-service exposure, property quality, refinance risk, and full exchange plan. The matching exercise is also a leverage-risk decision.

## Non-recourse debt and no personal qualifying

DST debt is generally non-recourse to individual investors. The loan is arranged by the sponsor at the trust level and secured by DST property. In a default, the lender’s remedy is the property rather than the investor’s other assets. The beneficial owner is not personally liable for the loan.

The investor is entering existing financing rather than originating debt. There is no personal credit check, income verification, loan application, or personal guarantee. This can be useful for older investors, retirees, or people with complex finances who might not qualify for a new mortgage, and it removes personal recourse exposure. The sponsor completes qualifying and underwriting at the trust level.

> A leveraged DST gives the investor a pro-rata debt share without a personal loan application, guarantee, credit check, or recourse to other assets.

## Loan-to-value by offering

Loan-to-value, or LTV, is debt as a percentage of property value. A 50% LTV DST has debt equal to half the property value; a 60% LTV DST has more debt; an all-cash DST has 0% LTV. An owner with roughly 50% leverage on relinquished property may seek a comparable LTV to align both debt and equity.

Offerings range from debt-free to moderately or more highly leveraged. An exchanger may use one DST or combine multiple DSTs to match the required debt more closely. A common blend is a leveraged DST for debt replacement and an all-cash DST for the remaining equity. Higher LTV replaces more debt and may amplify returns, but it also amplifies losses and refinancing risk.

## Key Takeaways

- Full 1031 deferral generally requires replacing relinquished debt with equal-or-greater debt or added cash; a shortfall is taxable mortgage boot.
- A leveraged DST places a pre-arranged trust-level mortgage behind the property and gives each investor a pro-rata debt share.
- DST debt is non-recourse and avoids personal credit checks, income verification, applications, guarantees, and liability.
- LTV varies by offering. Match it, or blend several DSTs, to replace debt while weighing the additional risk from leverage.

## All-cash vs. leveraged DSTs

An all-cash, debt-free DST owns property free and clear. Without debt, it has no debt service, refinancing risk, or lender foreclosure risk, and income is not exposed to leverage. Its limitation is direct: it replaces no debt. If the relinquished property carried debt, using only an all-cash DST generally produces mortgage boot unless additional cash fills the gap.

A leveraged DST replaces debt through its trust-level loan and can satisfy the equal-or-greater-debt test. Leverage can improve returns when property performs well, but debt service must be paid in all conditions, losses can be amplified, and a maturing loan can create refinancing risk. No debt to replace may point toward all-cash; a mortgage to replace requires leverage or a blend. Confirm the exact math with a tax advisor.

## Matching your debt-replacement need

Start with the closing statement: determine with a tax advisor how much debt was retired on the relinquished property and how much equity is being reinvested. To seek full deferral, target a debt allocation at least equal to the relinquished debt, or add cash to cover any shortfall.

One investor may find a single leveraged DST with appropriate LTV. Another may combine a leveraged DST with an all-cash DST, using the first for debt and the second for remaining equity. This can refine the overall leverage ratio and diversify across properties and sponsors.

The selections must still satisfy 1031 identification rules. Within the 45-day window, an exchanger can use the 3-property rule, identify any number under the 200% rule, or use the more demanding 95% rule. Closing must occur within 180 days. Coordinate with a [qualified intermediary](what-is-a-qualified-intermediary.html) and tax advisor because the debt calculation and deadlines are unforgiving.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the 1031 debt-replacement rule?

To fully defer gain, the replacement property’s value and debt generally need to be equal to or greater than what was relinquished. A mortgage on the sold property must be replaced with equal-or-greater debt or an additional cash contribution. Replacing equity alone is insufficient; the debt shortfall can be taxable mortgage boot. Pre-financed DSTs can help solve the financing component, but tax advisors should confirm individual results.

### What is mortgage boot?

Mortgage boot is taxable value created when replacement debt is less than relinquished debt. The IRS generally treats debt relief as an economic benefit. For example, if a property with a $500,000 mortgage is exchanged into property with only a $300,000 loan, the $200,000 difference is generally taxable mortgage boot absent offsetting cash. Equal-or-greater debt or fresh cash can prevent it.

### How does leverage work in a DST?

The sponsor finances DST property with investor equity and a trust-level loan. Each beneficial owner has a pro-rata share. At 50% LTV, the share has roughly that relationship of allocated debt to equity. The investor can select an offering whose LTV aligns with debt-replacement needs, allowing allocated debt to satisfy the equal-or-greater requirement without originating a new loan in the 45-day and 180-day windows.

### What does non-recourse mean for DST debt?

It means the lender looks to DST property, not the investor’s other assets, if the loan defaults. The loan is arranged by the sponsor and secured by the trust property. Investors are not personally liable, unlike a recourse borrower. Non-recourse financing, together with no personal qualification, makes trust-level debt less burdensome than obtaining a new personal mortgage.

### Do I have to qualify for the loan in a DST?

No. The sponsor arranged the financing at the trust level. An investor buys into existing debt and does not undergo a personal credit check, income verification, loan application, or provide a guarantee. The pro-rata debt allocation can still be used for the 1031 debt-replacement requirement. This can help investors who might find individual mortgage qualification difficult.

### What is loan-to-value (LTV) in a DST?

LTV is debt divided by property value. A 50% LTV DST has debt equal to half its value; 60% LTV carries more debt; a debt-free DST is 0%. LTV determines how much debt an investment replaces. It is also a risk signal: higher leverage can increase return potential, losses, debt-service pressure, and refinancing risk. Review every offering’s LTV.

### What is an all-cash or debt-free DST?

It is a DST with no trust-level mortgage. It has no debt service, refinancing risk, or lender foreclosure exposure and is generally lower-risk. It cannot replace relinquished debt, so it can suit investors with no debt to replace or be combined with a leveraged DST to fine-tune the exchange mix. Used alone after a debt-financed property sale, it can create mortgage boot unless offset with cash.

### Should I choose an all-cash or a leveraged DST?

The first question is debt replacement. Little or no relinquished debt may favor a lower-risk all-cash DST. A mortgage typically requires a leveraged DST or mix that produces adequate allocated debt. Leverage can amplify returns when property performs well, but it also amplifies losses, leaves debt service due, and adds refinancing risk. Match both the exchange requirement and risk tolerance with tax-advisor confirmation.

### Can I combine multiple DSTs to match my debt?

Yes. Offerings have different LTVs, from debt-free to more leveraged. Combining a leveraged DST with an all-cash DST can replace both debt and equity without unnecessarily increasing overall leverage, and can diversify exposure by property, sponsor, and sector. The identified DSTs still must fit the 3-property rule, 200% rule, or 95% rule within 45 days. Coordinate with the QI.

### Does DST leverage increase my risk?

Yes. Debt can increase returns when a property performs well, but it magnifies losses if performance weakens. Debt service still comes first when cash flow is stressed and can pressure or eliminate distributions. A maturing loan can require refinancing at then-current interest rates or an untimely sale. All-cash DSTs avoid these risks but provide no debt replacement. Leverage must fit both the 1031 goal and risk tolerance.

### What are the 1031 identification rules when using multiple DSTs?

The 3-property rule permits identification of up to three properties without regard to total value. The 200% rule permits any number if their combined value does not exceed 200% of relinquished-property value. The 95% rule permits any number at any total value only when at least 95% of identified value is acquired. Select the appropriate rule during the 45-day identification window and close within 180 days.

### Are DST distributions affected by leverage?

Yes. Interest and sometimes principal payments are paid from property income before investor distributions. Leverage can enhance cash-on-cash results when property cash flow is strong, but falling income from vacancy, expenses, or a weak market can make the fixed debt service squeeze or eliminate distributions. An all-cash DST has no leverage claim on cash flow but does not replace debt. All distributions are projections, never guaranteed.

### Why are DSTs especially helpful for debt replacement?

They provide pre-arranged non-recourse trust-level financing. The investor simply takes a pro-rata share: no credit check, income verification, application, guarantee, or personal liability. An LTV-matched DST, or a blend, can satisfy the equal-or-greater-debt test without finding a new leveraged property and originating a mortgage inside 45 and 180 days. That reduces a deadline-driven financing task to choosing suitable leverage.

### How do I figure out how much debt I need to replace?

Start with the mortgage balance retired at sale and the reinvested equity. For full deferral, replacement debt generally equals or exceeds that retired debt, unless additional cash fills a gap. A CPA and qualified intermediary should confirm the closing-statement figures and tax treatment. With the required number in hand, choose a DST LTV that creates a matching debt allocation or blend leveraged and all-cash DSTs. Baker 1031 does not provide tax advice.

## Glossary

**Debt-Replacement Rule:** The 1031 requirement to replace the relinquished property's debt.

**Mortgage Boot:** Taxable amount from replacing less debt than you gave up.

**Boot:** Non-like-kind value received in an exchange, generally taxable.

**Leveraged DST:** A DST with trust-level debt that replaces a mortgage.

**All-Cash (Debt-Free) DST:** A DST with no loan; lower-risk but replaces no debt.

**Loan-to-Value (LTV):** A DST's debt as a percentage of property value.

**Non-Recourse Loan:** Debt for which investors aren't personally liable.

**Personal Qualifying:** Credit and income underwriting DST investors avoid.

**Pro-Rata Debt Share:** An investor's proportional share of the DST's debt.

**Refinancing Risk:** The risk a maturing loan must be refinanced or sold.

**3-Property Rule:** Identify up to three properties regardless of value.

**200% Rule:** Identify any number if total value is within 200%.

**95% Rule:** Identify any number if you acquire 95% of the value.

**45-Day Window:** The deadline to identify replacement property.

**180-Day Deadline:** The deadline to close on replacement property.

**Qualified Intermediary (QI):** The party that holds exchange proceeds and facilitates the swap.
