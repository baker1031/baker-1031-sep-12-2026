# The Agent & Broker's 2026 Guide to 1031 Exchanges

**Filed under:** For Agents & Brokers  
**By:** Baker 1031 Research  
**Updated:** June 2026 · **16 min read**

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

I keep seeing investment-property listings lose momentum the moment an owner starts adding up the tax bill. The concern is rational. It can also be the point at which a capable agent becomes indispensable—or loses both sides of the deal to someone who knows how to frame the choice.

A 1031 exchange can let an investor sell qualifying property and reinvest the proceeds in like-kind real estate while deferring capital gains tax and depreciation recapture. It is one of the most useful tools an agent can put in front of an investor client, because it can turn a tax objection into a live sale and replacement purchase.

First-order thinking says the job is to get the listing signed and let the buyer-side search take care of itself. Second-order thinking recognizes that the exchange calendar, the replacement-property search, and the professional handoffs determine whether the client keeps the deferral, whether the agent earns the second commission, and whether the relationship becomes a source of repeat and referral business.

The agent's work is not tax advice. It is recognizing the fit, raising the issue early, managing the property-search calendar, and bringing the right professionals into the room.

## Key Takeaways for Agents & Brokers

- A [1031 exchange](1031-exchange-guide.html) lets an investor client defer capital gains tax and depreciation recapture by rolling sale proceeds into like-kind real estate. It can turn a tax objection into an active transaction.
- A 1031 is a real-estate transaction inside your license. You can list the relinquished property and represent the client on a replacement purchase, so one client can create two commissions.
- The dates matter: **45 days** to identify replacements in writing and **180 days** to close, both measured from the relinquished closing.
- Your role is to spot the fit, raise it early, and connect the qualified intermediary and CPA. Do not give tax advice or guarantee a tax result.
- If the clock threatens the exchange, a [DST](agent-guide-dst.html) can be a backstop. It is a security, however, and an agent with only a real-estate license may educate and refer, not sell or advise on it.
- A 1031-fluent practice can build a durable niche with investors, landlords, and the CPAs and attorneys who refer them.

## How a 1031 Works: the Agent's Version

In a [1031 exchange](1031-exchange-guide.html), a client sells investment property and reinvests the proceeds in like-kind replacement real estate. The process can defer the capital gains tax and depreciation recapture that would otherwise be due at closing. The tax does not disappear. It carries in the basis of the replacement property until the investor sells without another exchange, or the property is passed to heirs.

Three mechanics should be familiar before you raise the subject.

First, a **qualified intermediary (QI)** must hold the proceeds between the two closings. Money goes from sale escrow to the QI, then from the QI to the replacement purchase. The client never touches it. If the seller receives the cash, even briefly, that is constructive receipt and the exchange fails.

Second, the clock begins at the relinquished closing. The client has **45 days** to identify replacement property in writing and **180 days** to close, or the due date of that year's tax return if it comes first.

Third, full deferral generally requires the client to acquire property of equal or greater value, reinvest all equity, and replace debt paid off in the sale. Cash or value the client takes out, or debt the client does not replace, is **boot** and can be taxable.

The QI and CPA own the tax math. You own the calendar and the property search.

There is one more boundary. The real estate must be held for investment or business use. A primary residence does not qualify, and neither does inventory held by a flipper or dealer for resale. Like-kind treatment for real property is broad: an apartment building, raw land, net-lease retail box, industrial warehouse, and fractional [DST](dst-guide.html) interest can stand in for one another. That range gives clients options, but eligibility is still a question for their CPA and attorney.

## The Deadlines You Have to Manage

The exchange lives or dies on two dates. A missed 45-day identification window has no cure. The [45-day trap](the-45-day-trap.html) catches more exchanges than any other failure because the buy-side search started too late.

Treat the relinquished closing as day zero. Work backward into the replacement search before the property is under contract, not after the seller is already looking at a ticking clock.

| Milestone | Deadline | What the agent does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relinquished closing | Day **0** | Confirm that proceeds wire to the QI, not the client. The clock begins here. |
| Identification period | Day **45** | Have replacement options identified in writing to the QI. There are no extensions or exceptions. |
| Closing period | Day **180** | Close on identified property, or by the tax-return due date if earlier. Keep escrow moving. |

Both windows run from the relinquished closing and overlap.

Formal identification rules are enforced by the QI. The common [three-property rule](1031-exchange-identification-rules.html) allows a client to name up to **three** replacement candidates of any value. Your practical task is to have real, available, appropriately priced properties on that list by day 45, with enough total value to absorb the equity.

If inventory is thin and nothing fits, start the backup conversation early. For fuller mechanics, use the [1031 timeline guide](1031-exchange-timeline.html).

## Why Every Agent Should Understand 1031s

The direct business case is clear. A seller with a long-held rental can face capital gains and depreciation recapture that run a third or more of the gain. That prospective bill can keep an owner from listing. The [capital-gains and depreciation-recapture guide](capital-gains-tax-on-real-estate.html) gives the client a starting point for the question, while the CPA and QI determine the actual result.

A 1031 can also create the second half of the transaction. The client needs a replacement within **180 days**, and the agent who has earned the client's trust may represent the purchase as well as the sale. One conversation can produce a listing-side commission and a buy-side commission inside **six** months.

The longer payoff is repeat and referral business. Investors transact again. CPAs, attorneys, and QIs notice the agents who keep a transaction on schedule and stay within their role. If you do not raise the exchange when an investor mentions tax, another agent may do it and take both sides.

## Which Clients Are Good 1031 Candidates?

Not every seller is a fit. The strongest candidates generally hold property for investment, have meaningful gain or depreciation to defer, and have a reason to keep capital in real estate rather than cash out.

| Client profile | 1031 fit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long-time landlord with a large gain | Strong | Significant deferred tax to protect; may be ready to trade up, simplify, or reduce management. |
| Investor trading up or consolidating | Strong | More units or one larger asset makes equal-or-greater replacement value a natural fit. |
| Owner relocating capital to a new market | Strong | Like-kind real estate can allow an exit from one metro and a tax-deferred purchase in another. |
| Heir or estate-planning owner | Good | May exchange now and later pass property at a stepped-up basis; involve the attorney. |
| Seller of a primary residence | None | A primary home is not held for investment; Section 121, not 1031, is the relevant framework. |
| Flipper or dealer holding for resale | None | Inventory held for resale is not investment property and does not qualify. |

These profiles are illustrative only. Whether a specific client qualifies is a question for that client's CPA and attorney.

For a suitable client, the value is retaining full equity instead of handing part of the gain to the IRS at the sale. The client may trade up into a larger building, combine several small properties, split one property into several, relocate to another market, or move from a management-heavy fourplex to a more passive form of ownership. Some investors exchange across decades and pass the final property to heirs, whose stepped-up basis can erase the deferred tax entirely. Your job is to help the client see the sale as a possible strategic upgrade, not simply as a taxable exit.

## How to Talk to Your Client About It

Raise the subject early, ideally at the listing appointment when an investor hesitates over taxes. You are not giving tax advice; you are opening a door and identifying the professionals who can answer the tax questions.

The substance of a useful opening is straightforward:

> “Before you decide the tax makes selling not worth it, you should know about a 1031 exchange. It can let you sell and reinvest without paying the capital gains tax now. I'm not your tax advisor, but I work with a qualified intermediary and can connect you with a CPA so you can see if it fits your situation.”

Then establish the property-search role:

> “If you do exchange, I can help you find and close the replacement property inside the timeline, so you're not scrambling at day **40**.”

Three habits keep the conversation clean. Surface the 1031 before the client talks themselves out of selling. Route every tax question to the CPA and QI. State plainly that you handle the real estate on both ends, so the client knows to ask you about the replacement purchase.

Avoid two traps. Do not quote a tax number or promise a result, even a small one. And do not oversell certainty. Say “it can defer” and “your CPA can confirm,” not “you won't pay tax.” Educate, frame, and refer.

## When the 45-Day Clock Threatens the Deal

The difficult situation is familiar: the client has sold, the QI holds the money, and day **45** is approaching with no identification that actually works. Inventory may be thin, an offer may have failed, or the only remaining properties may be overpriced. If no replacement is identified, the exchange fails and the gain becomes taxable.

That is why a [Delaware Statutory Trust](agent-guide-dst.html) may be discussed as a backstop before pressure becomes acute. A DST is a fractional interest in institutionally managed real estate. It often has a minimum investment of roughly **$100,000**, no financing contingency, and can close in days instead of weeks. A client may [exchange into a DST](1031-exchange-into-dst.html) to absorb remaining equity, fill a value gap, or provide a replacement when a direct property does not materialize.

Read the boundary carefully: **a DST is a security, not real estate.** An agent operating only under a real-estate license cannot sell it, advise on a specific DST, or receive transaction-based compensation on it. The agent can recognize the timetable problem, explain that a DST backstop exists, and refer the client to a securities-licensed firm and the client's CPA.

DSTs are speculative, illiquid, accredited-investor-only securities sold through a private placement memorandum. They can lose principal. The agent is the early-warning system and connector, not the seller.

## How to Grow Your Business With 1031s

Knowledge only becomes useful when it becomes a process.

1. **Represent both ends by default.** Ask every 1031 seller to let you search for the replacement. It creates the second commission and the client often values continuity.
2. **Build a QI and CPA bench.** A reliable QI and **two or three** capable tax professionals let you make a quick, appropriate handoff. Referrals can run both ways.
3. **Add a securities-licensed DST contact.** If the clock threatens an exchange, you should know who to call that day.
4. **Own the niche publicly.** Market yourself to investors and landlords as a 1031-fluent agent. It separates you from agents focused only on primary residences.
5. **Mine your database.** Long-time landlords with substantial embedded gains are natural people to reconnect with.
6. **Create simple content.** A short “thinking of selling a rental?” piece can communicate what you do and attract inbound listings from people who otherwise would not call.

Each exchange handled well can increase the chance of the next referral—from the client, the CPA, or the QI who saw you keep the process moving. That is how a single tool can become a durable pipeline.

## Staying in Your Lane

A 1031 itself is a real-estate transaction, so a real-estate license covers listing the relinquished property and representing the replacement purchase. The limits concern tax advice and securities.

Do not analyze whether a client qualifies, calculate deferred gain, opine on basis or boot, or guarantee a tax outcome. Send those questions to the CPA and QI. The [CPA guide to 1031 exchanges](cpa-guide-1031-exchange.html) explains the tax professional's side of the handoff.

The securities limit matters when a client cannot find a direct replacement or wants less active management. A [DST](dst-guide.html) and the broader private-placement universe are securities. You cannot sell them, advise on them, or earn securities compensation using only a real-estate license. Bring the three professionals in early: the CPA for tax, the QI for structure and money, and a securities-licensed advisor if a DST becomes relevant. Saying where your lane ends protects both the relationship and your license.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a special license to help with a 1031 exchange?

No. A 1031 is a real-estate transaction, so a real-estate license covers the relinquished-property listing and representation on the replacement purchase. You should not give tax advice or guarantee a result; refer the client to a CPA and QI.

### How does a 1031 exchange earn an agent a second commission?

A 1031 client must buy replacement real estate within **180 days**. If you represent the client on that purchase as well as the sale, one exchange can create a listing commission and a buy-side commission, usually inside six months.

### When should an agent bring up a 1031 exchange with a client?

Bring it up early, ideally at the listing appointment when an investor hesitates over the tax bill. Raising it before the client decides not to sell can save the deal and establish your role on both sides.

### Can a real-estate agent give tax advice about a 1031 exchange?

No. Explain that an exchange exists and can defer capital gains tax, then refer the client to the CPA and QI for analysis, deadlines, and reporting. Do not quote a tax number or guarantee the outcome.

### What are the 45-day and 180-day deadlines in a 1031 exchange?

Both run from the relinquished closing. The client has **45 days** to identify replacement property in writing to the QI and **180 days** to close, or the earlier tax-return due date. A missed 45-day identification period cannot be cured.

### What if a 1031 client cannot find a replacement property in time?

A DST can provide a passive, fast-closing replacement with no financing contingency. It is a security, so the agent may describe the option and refer the client to a securities-licensed firm and CPA, but may not sell or recommend a specific DST.

### Why can't an agent sell or recommend a specific DST?

A DST interest is a security, not real estate. A real-estate license does not authorize the sale of securities, transaction-based securities compensation, or recommendations about a specific DST. Education and referral are permitted roles; the recommendation and sale belong with a securities-licensed representative.

## Glossary

- **1031 Exchange:** A like-kind exchange under Section 1031 that lets an investor defer capital gains tax and depreciation recapture by reinvesting sale proceeds in replacement real estate held for investment or business use.
- **Qualified Intermediary (QI):** The independent party that holds sale proceeds between closings so the client avoids constructive receipt. If the client touches the money, the exchange fails.
- **Relinquished Property:** The investment property sold to start the exchange. Its closing is day zero for both deadlines.
- **Replacement Property:** The like-kind real estate bought to complete the exchange and the agent's buy-side opportunity.
- **45-Day Identification Period:** The window from closing to name replacement property in writing to the QI. It cannot be extended.
- **180-Day Closing Period:** The window to close on replacement property, or the earlier tax-return due date, counted from the relinquished closing.
- **Boot:** Cash or value the client takes out, or debt the client fails to replace. Boot can be taxable inside an otherwise valid exchange.
- **Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** A fractional interest in institutionally managed real estate that can serve as a 1031 replacement property. It is a security, sold only to accredited investors through a private placement memorandum, and an agent cannot sell or advise on it.
