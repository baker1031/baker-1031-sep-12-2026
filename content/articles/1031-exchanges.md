# 1031 Exchanges

I keep seeing exchange conversations start with the tax bill and end before the calendar has entered the room. In a 1031, the clock and the replacement asset are part of the economics.

First-order thinking treats a 1031 exchange as a way to postpone gain. Second-order thinking tests the deadline, replacement-property quality, debt and value matching, boot, and whether tax deferral serves the owner's wider plan.

<div class="article-intro"><p style="margin:0;">Defer capital gains tax by exchanging investment property into like-kind real estate — including DSTs — within the IRS 45-day identification and 180-day closing deadlines.</p></div>

<div class="legacy">
<div class="csec"><span class="kicker">Overview</span><hr class="rule"/><p class="lead">A 1031 exchange — named for Section 1031 of the Internal Revenue Code — lets an investor sell appreciated real estate and reinvest the proceeds into like-kind property without recognizing capital gains tax at the time of sale.</p><p>Section 1031 has been part of the tax code since 1921. It allows the deferral of capital gains (and depreciation recapture) when an investor sells real property held for investment or business use and reinvests the proceeds into other 'like-kind' real property. Done correctly and repeatedly, an investor can defer tax across a lifetime of exchanges and potentially receive a stepped-up basis at death.</p><p>The mechanics are unforgiving on timing. From the day the relinquished property closes, the investor has 45 calendar days to formally identify replacement property and 180 calendar days to close on it. Proceeds must be held by a qualified intermediary — never touched by the investor — and the replacement must carry equal or greater value and debt to fully defer the gain. DSTs have become a popular replacement option precisely because they can be identified and closed quickly within these windows.</p></div>
<div class="csec"><span class="kicker">How it works</span><hr class="rule"/><div class="csteps"><div class="cstep"><div class="no">01</div><h3>Sell and escrow with a QI</h3><p>Before closing the sale, engage a qualified intermediary (QI) to receive the proceeds. If you take possession of the cash, the exchange fails.</p></div><div class="cstep"><div class="no">02</div><h3>Identify within 45 days</h3><p>Formally identify replacement property in writing — commonly under the 3-property rule or the 200% rule. Many investors identify a DST as a backup.</p></div><div class="cstep"><div class="no">03</div><h3>Close within 180 days</h3><p>Acquire the identified replacement property within 180 days of the original sale (or your tax-filing deadline, if earlier).</p></div><div class="cstep"><div class="no">04</div><h3>Match value and debt</h3><p>To defer 100% of the gain, reinvest all equity and replace all debt with equal-or-greater value; any shortfall ('boot') is taxable.</p></div></div></div>
<div class="csec"><span class="kicker">Timeline</span><hr class="rule"/><div class="chartcard"><div class="ct"><h3>The 1031 exchange clock</h3><span class="mk">Calendar days from the sale of the relinquished property</span></div><div style="padding:26px 24px"><div class="ctimeline"><div class="tp"><div class="d">Day 0</div><h4>Relinquished property closes</h4><p>Sale proceeds go to your qualified intermediary — not to you. The clock starts.</p></div><div class="tp"><div class="d">Day 45</div><h4>Identification deadline</h4><p>Identify replacement property in writing under the 3-property or 200% rule. Many investors identify a DST as a backup.</p></div><div class="tp"><div class="d">Day 180</div><h4>Closing deadline</h4><p>Acquire the replacement property. Match value and debt to defer 100% of the gain.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="csec"><span class="kicker">Benefits</span><hr class="rule"/><div class="cgrid2"><div class="citem"><h3>Tax deferral</h3><p>Defer federal and state capital gains plus depreciation recapture, keeping more capital compounding in real estate.</p></div><div class="citem"><h3>Portfolio repositioning</h3><p>Move from active to passive, from one sector to another, or from one geography to another — without a taxable event.</p></div><div class="citem"><h3>Estate planning</h3><p>Heirs may receive a step-up in basis, potentially eliminating the deferred gain — a reason 'swap til you drop' is a common strategy.</p></div><div class="citem"><h3>Leverage of proceeds</h3><p>Reinvesting pre-tax proceeds means more equity at work than an after-tax sale would allow.</p></div></div></div>
<div class="csec"><span class="kicker">Considerations &amp; risks</span><hr class="rule"/><div class="cgrid2"><div class="citem risk"><h3>Strict deadlines</h3><p>The 45- and 180-day clocks are calendar days with essentially no extensions; missing them disqualifies the exchange.</p></div><div class="citem risk"><h3>Qualified intermediary required</h3><p>You cannot touch the proceeds. Choosing a reputable, bonded QI is critical to a valid exchange.</p></div><div class="citem risk"><h3>Boot is taxable</h3><p>Cash taken out or debt not replaced is 'boot' and is taxed; full deferral requires matching value and debt.</p></div><div class="citem risk"><h3>Like-kind, but real estate only</h3><p>Since 2018, 1031 applies only to real property; personal property no longer qualifies.</p></div></div></div>
<div class="csec"><span class="kicker">Compare</span><hr class="rule"/><h2>The three 1031 identification rules</h2><div class="ctable-wrap"><table class="ctable"><caption>The three 1031 identification rules</caption>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Rule</th><th scope="col">What it allows</th><th scope="col">Best for</th></tr></thead>
<tbody><tr><th scope="row">3-property rule</th><td>Identify up to 3 properties of any value; close on one or more</td><td>Most exchangers; allows a DST backup</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">200% rule</th><td>Identify any number of properties so long as total value ≤ 200% of the sale</td><td>Diversifying across several DSTs</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">95% rule</th><td>Identify any number; must close on 95% of total identified value</td><td>Rare; large multi-asset programs</td></tr></tbody>
</table></div><p class="ctnote">These three are the whole set. The 3-property and 200% rules cover almost every exchange; the 95% rule is a fallback for large multi-asset programs. Consult your CPA and QI.</p></div>
