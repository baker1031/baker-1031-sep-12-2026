# Opportunity Zone Funds

I keep seeing the ten-year tax benefit take over the Opportunity Zone conversation. The holding period only matters if the fund can carry its own weight for a decade.

First-order thinking sees a 10-year tax-free appreciation incentive. Second-order thinking weighs the original gain, 180-day reinvestment window, project execution, illiquidity, concentration, and current statutory requirements.

<div class="article-intro"><p style="margin:0;">Reinvest capital gains into a Qualified Opportunity Fund to defer tax now and — after a 10-year hold — eliminate tax on the fund's appreciation entirely.</p></div>

<div class="legacy">
<div class="csec"><span class="kicker">Overview</span><hr class="rule"/><p class="lead">Opportunity Zones, created by the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, let investors roll capital gains from any source into a Qualified Opportunity Fund (QOF) — deferring tax on the original gain and, after a 10-year hold, owing no tax on the fund's own appreciation.</p><p>Unlike a 1031 exchange, an Opportunity Zone investment doesn't require like-kind real estate — the gain can come from selling stock, a business, or property. Investors have 180 days from realizing a gain to reinvest it into a Qualified Opportunity Fund, which in turn invests in real estate or businesses located in one of the 8,700-plus designated low-income census tracts.</p><p>The headline benefit is the back-end: hold the QOF investment for at least 10 years and any appreciation in the fund is permanently excluded from capital gains tax. The original deferred gain is a separate matter and is recognized on its statutory schedule. Because the rules are date-sensitive and have evolved, Opportunity Zone investing should always be coordinated with your CPA.</p></div>
<div class="csec"><span class="kicker">How it works</span><hr class="rule"/><div class="csteps"><div class="cstep"><div class="no">01</div><h3>Realize a capital gain</h3><p>From any source — real estate, stock, a business sale. Only the gain (not the full proceeds) needs to be reinvested.</p></div><div class="cstep"><div class="no">02</div><h3>Invest within 180 days</h3><p>Roll the gain into a Qualified Opportunity Fund within 180 days of realizing it.</p></div><div class="cstep"><div class="no">03</div><h3>Fund deploys into the zone</h3><p>The QOF invests in qualifying real estate or operating businesses within designated Opportunity Zones, typically development or value-add projects.</p></div><div class="cstep"><div class="no">04</div><h3>Hold 10 years, exit tax-free</h3><p>After a 10-year hold, appreciation in the QOF investment is excluded from capital gains tax on exit.</p></div></div></div>

<div class="csec"><span class="kicker">Benefits</span><hr class="rule"/><div class="cgrid2"><div class="citem"><h3>Deferral of the original gain</h3><p>Tax on the rolled-in gain is deferred under the program's schedule, keeping more capital invested up front.</p></div><div class="citem"><h3>Tax-free appreciation</h3><p>The signature benefit: after 10 years, the QOF's own gains can be excluded from federal capital gains tax.</p></div><div class="citem"><h3>Any gain qualifies</h3><p>Unlike 1031, the gain need not come from real estate — stock or business-sale gains qualify too.</p></div><div class="citem"><h3>Community impact</h3><p>Capital is directed to designated communities, aligning return potential with revitalization.</p></div></div></div>
<div class="csec"><span class="kicker">Considerations &amp; risks</span><hr class="rule"/><div class="cgrid2"><div class="citem risk"><h3>Long, illiquid hold</h3><p>The 10-year requirement makes QOFs a long-term, illiquid commitment; early exit forfeits the core benefit.</p></div><div class="citem risk"><h3>Development risk</h3><p>Many QOFs pursue ground-up or value-add projects, which carry execution, lease-up, and cost risk.</p></div><div class="citem risk"><h3>Date-sensitive rules</h3><p>Deferral timing and program provisions are set by statute and have changed; verify current rules with your CPA.</p></div><div class="citem risk"><h3>Concentration</h3><p>Zone-eligible geographies and project types are constrained, which can limit diversification.</p></div></div></div>
