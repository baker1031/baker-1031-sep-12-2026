# UPREIT vs. DownREIT: What's the Difference?

[Home](baker1031.html) · [Back to All Insights](insights.html)

721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read

I see “UPREIT” used as shorthand for nearly every 721 transaction. Usually that is close enough. But when a contributor is evaluating units, conversion rights, allocations, or tax protections, the difference between an UPREIT and a DownREIT deserves more precision.

Both are REIT structures that can accept property tax-deferred under Section 721 in exchange for partnership units. The key difference is structural. An UPREIT holds the portfolio in one operating partnership. A DownREIT places contributed property in a separate, lower-tier partnership. The first is standard and generally simpler; the second is less common and more layered.

The tax benefit begins in the same place, but mechanics, unit economics, conversion features, and administration can diverge. This article explains what owners are actually entering when they encounter either structure.

## The UPREIT structure

An [UPREIT](721-exchange-guide.html), or umbrella partnership REIT, puts the REIT at the top of a single operating partnership. The REIT issues shares and controls the operating partnership as general partner. The operating partnership holds the full portfolio of real estate.

An owner contributing property receives [OP units](op-units-explained.html) in that one partnership. The REIT and all contributing owners are partners in the same operating partnership. Every contributed asset goes into that umbrella entity, and each contributor owns units in it.

That single-partnership structure is the defining characteristic of an UPREIT. It produces uniform units and generally consistent terms for eventual conversion to REIT shares. Most REITs that accept 721 contributions use this structure, which is why it is the default framework for a 721 exchange.

## The DownREIT structure

A DownREIT uses a different arrangement for contributed property. Rather than placing every property in one umbrella operating partnership, the REIT forms or uses a separate partnership at a lower tier. The contributor’s property goes into that partnership, where the REIT and contributor are partners.

The REIT can therefore own property at multiple levels. It may own some assets directly or through its own entities and hold contributed properties in separate lower-tier partnerships. The contributor owns units in the specific lower-tier partnership—DownREIT units—not in one portfolio-wide operating partnership.

The DownREIT is less common and more complex. Its multiple-partnership structure is used in particular circumstances, rather than as the ordinary 721 framework.

> An UPREIT holds the portfolio in one umbrella operating partnership. A DownREIT uses separate lower-tier partnerships for contributed property. The difference is one partnership versus multiple partnerships.

## The principal structural differences

In an UPREIT, one operating partnership holds all properties and all contributors receive units in that partnership. Units are generally uniform, and conversion to REIT shares is generally governed by consistent terms.

In a DownREIT, each lower-tier partnership can have its own economics and conversion features. A contributor’s units can therefore be partnership-specific and less uniform. The layered structure introduces more arrangements to administer, more ownership levels to understand, and potentially different rights among contributors.

First-order thinking sees both structures as a contribution for units and concludes they are economically interchangeable. Second-order thinking asks which partnership holds the asset, who shares the economics, how income, gain, and liabilities are allocated, what conversion terms apply, and which tax protections attach to the contributor’s particular unit interest.

## Tax treatment: same core deferral, different complexity

Both structures can support the core Section 721 result: property is contributed to a partnership for partnership units, and gain is generally deferred. The fundamental benefit is the same in an UPREIT and a DownREIT.

The DownREIT’s layers can make tax mechanics more involved. Income, gain, and liability allocations may have to be traced through multiple partnerships. The lower-tier partnership’s own tax treatment, conversion mechanics, tax protection, and interaction with the REIT’s other holdings can add complexity beyond a single operating partnership.

The same applies to safeguards for built-in gain. Tax protection arrangements intended to address a taxable partnership sale, and terms for converting units to shares, may differ in a DownREIT. This does not mean a DownREIT lacks Section 721 deferral. It means the owner needs a more specific review by a CPA and attorney.

## When each structure is used

The UPREIT is the usual model. Most REITs accepting 721 contributions have an umbrella operating partnership, and most DST-to-REIT transitions also produce standard UPREIT OP units.

A DownREIT may be used when an UPREIT is not already in place or is not suitable for the deal. For example, a REIT that traditionally owns assets directly but wants to accept an owner’s property tax-deferred may form a separate partnership for that asset rather than restructure the entire company as an UPREIT. Particular deal terms or existing REIT arrangements can also support a DownREIT.

The owner normally does not choose between the two structures. The REIT and the transaction determine it. The owner’s task is to understand the specific unit terms and how the structure affects conversion, allocation, protection, and administration.

## What the difference means to a property owner

Most owners will encounter a standard UPREIT. The usual 721 explanation—one operating partnership, OP units, consistent conversion terms—will apply.

If the owner encounters a DownREIT, the core benefit remains a tax-deferred property contribution for partnership units. But the structure requires closer attention. The owner should understand the lower-tier partnership, the partnership-specific economics, conversion features, liability and income allocations, tax-protection terms, and the relationship between the lower-tier entity and the REIT.

The more complex structure does not automatically make the opportunity unsuitable. It calls for clear documentation and experienced advice. The REIT’s quality, the contributor’s objectives, and the complete deal terms generally matter more than the label alone.

## Key Takeaways

- Both UPREITs and DownREITs can defer gain under Section 721 when property is contributed for partnership units.
- An UPREIT uses one operating partnership for the entire portfolio and generally has uniform OP units.
- A DownREIT uses separate lower-tier partnerships for contributed properties, resulting in partnership-specific units and more intricate administration and tax mechanics.
- The UPREIT is the common default. A DownREIT is a situational alternative, often where an umbrella structure is not already available.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between an UPREIT and a DownREIT?

An UPREIT holds all properties in one operating partnership beneath the REIT, and contributors receive units in that partnership. A DownREIT uses a separate lower-tier partnership for contributed property, with the REIT owning properties at multiple levels. The UPREIT is a simpler single-partnership model; the DownREIT is a more complex multi-partnership model. Both can support 721 deferral.

### Do both UPREITs and DownREITs allow 721 exchanges?

Yes. Both allow a property owner to contribute property to a partnership for partnership units under Section 721, generally deferring gain. The core deferral is the same. The difference is the partnership structure that holds the asset and issues the units.

### What is a DownREIT?

It is a REIT arrangement in which a lower-tier partnership holds the contributor’s property. The REIT and contributor are partners in that entity, while the REIT can hold other properties directly or in other entities. The contributor receives units in the particular lower-tier partnership, not portfolio-wide UPREIT units.

### Why is a DownREIT more complex?

It has multiple ownership levels and partnership-specific units. Economics and conversion features can differ among partnerships, and allocations of income, gain, and liabilities can be more involved. The tax-protection and conversion mechanics may also be specific to the lower-tier partnership. A CPA and attorney should evaluate those details.

### When is a DownREIT used instead of an UPREIT?

It may be used where the REIT does not already have an UPREIT structure or where a specific transaction supports a separate contributed-property partnership. For example, a REIT that owns property directly may use a DownREIT to accept a tax-deferred contribution without restructuring its full portfolio. UPREITs remain the common default.

### Are the tax benefits the same in both?

The core Section 721 deferral is generally available in both. A DownREIT can involve more complicated allocations, lower-tier tax treatment, conversion terms, and tax-protection mechanics, however. The benefit is similar; the execution can be more involved.

### Which structure will I encounter?

Most likely an UPREIT. It is used by most REITs that accept 721 contributions and is the normal framework for DST-to-REIT 721 transitions. A DownREIT appears in particular circumstances and should prompt a closer review of its specific terms.

### Are DownREIT units different from UPREIT units?

They can be. UPREIT units are in one operating partnership and generally convert to REIT shares on consistent terms. DownREIT units are tied to a specific lower-tier partnership, so their economics and conversion features may differ and can be more complex. Review the exact terms before contributing.

### Does a DownREIT affect deferral or step-up?

The basic Section 721 deferral and potential step-up at death can apply in both structures. The specifics of deferral, conversion, and protection may be more complicated in a DownREIT. Current advice from the investor’s CPA and attorney remains necessary.

### Should I avoid a DownREIT because it is complex?

Not automatically. It can offer the same central contribution-for-units benefit as an UPREIT. Complexity calls for care and professional guidance, not automatic rejection. Evaluate the particular REIT, lower-tier partnership, terms, and fit with the owner’s goals.

### Does the structure affect conversion to REIT shares?

It can. UPREIT units generally convert on standardized terms from the single operating partnership. DownREIT units may have partnership-specific conversion terms that are less uniform. Both may allow eventual conversion, but the owner should understand the actual unit agreement.

### Why do not more REITs use DownREITs?

The single-operating-partnership UPREIT model is simpler and more standardized. A DownREIT adds administrative and tax complexity without providing a benefit in most situations. It is generally reserved for cases where the umbrella partnership is not in place or is not suitable.

### Should the structure affect my 721 decision?

The main decision remains whether the 721 and the REIT fit the owner’s objectives. Both structures can defer gain. A DownREIT’s added complexity should receive more diligence, but the REIT’s quality, unit terms, conversion rights, and overall fit are usually more important than structure alone.

### Do most DST-to-REIT transitions use UPREITs or DownREITs?

Most use UPREITs. When a REIT acquires DST property in a 721 exit, DST investors generally receive units in the REIT’s single operating partnership. DownREITs are less common for this path.

### Is “UPREIT” sometimes used loosely for both?

Yes. In casual use, “UPREIT conversion” can describe 721 exchanges broadly, including a transaction technically structured as a DownREIT. Strictly, they are distinct: an UPREIT has one operating partnership; a DownREIT has separate lower-tier partnerships. Clarify the exact structure when its terms matter.

## Glossary

**UPREIT:** A REIT holding its portfolio through one umbrella operating partnership.

**DownREIT:** A REIT using separate lower-tier partnerships for contributed property.

**Operating Partnership:** The single partnership in an UPREIT that holds the portfolio.

**Lower-Tier Partnership:** A distinct partnership in a DownREIT that holds contributed property.

**OP Units:** Units in an UPREIT’s principal operating partnership.

**DownREIT Units:** Units in a DownREIT’s particular lower-tier partnership.

**Single-Partnership Structure:** The UPREIT’s one-OP framework.

**Multi-Partnership Structure:** The DownREIT’s multiple-partnership framework.

**Section 721:** The provision that can defer gain in either contribution structure.

**Umbrella Structure:** The UPREIT ownership hierarchy.

**Layered Structure:** The DownREIT’s multiple ownership levels.

**Uniform Units:** UPREIT units with consistent terms.

**Tax Complexity:** The more intricate allocation and mechanics issues in a DownREIT.

**Default Structure:** The common UPREIT arrangement.

**Situational Structure:** A DownREIT used for particular circumstances.

**Conversion Features:** Unit-to-share terms that can differ in a DownREIT.

| Factor | UPREIT | DownREIT |

| Structure | REIT owns property through one umbrella operating partnership | REIT forms a separate partnership for specific contributors |
| OP units received | Units in the REIT’s main operating partnership | Units in a subsidiary or affiliated partnership |
| Tax deferral on contribution | Yes, under Section 721 | Yes, under Section 721 |
| Prevalence | The standard, most common structure | Less common; used for specific deals |
| Complexity | Simpler, single partnership | More complex, multiple partnerships |

## Which should you choose?

The owner typically does not choose the structure; the REIT does. The UPREIT is the dominant arrangement and the one most contributors encounter. A DownREIT appears in circumstances the REIT structures around its existing partnerships.

Either way, focus on the same practical questions: how units are valued, when and on what terms units convert to shares, how gain is deferred, and what the partnership agreement says about rights and protections.
