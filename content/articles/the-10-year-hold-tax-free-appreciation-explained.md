# The 10-Year Hold: Tax-Free Appreciation Explained

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**Opportunity Zone Funds** · **Baker 1031 Research** · **Updated June 2026** · **16 min read**

I have been thinking about how often a tax benefit gets discussed as if it were an investment result. In Opportunity Zone investing, the 10-year hold is the part that can make that mistake tempting. It can exclude appreciation in a Qualified Opportunity Fund (QOF) investment from tax. It cannot make a weak asset stronger, make an illiquid position liquid, or erase the original deferred gain.

The first-order view is simple: hold a QOF for a decade and the gain on its growth can be tax-free. The second-order view is harder and more useful. The exclusion has value only if the investment appreciates, the fund and investor satisfy the program requirements, the investor can carry an illiquid position through the full holding period, and the rules apply as expected when the exit arrives.

That distinction matters because deferral of the original gain is temporary: the gain is eventually recognized. The 10-year exclusion concerns the QOF investment's later growth. It can permanently remove tax on that appreciation, a benefit that no other common deferral tool offers on the new investment's growth. The rules are time-sensitive and evolving, so a tax advisor should confirm the rules and the expected treatment for any particular facts.

## How the 10-year exclusion works

The requirement is a holding period of at least 10 years. After 10+ years, an investor who sells a QOF investment can elect to treat the investment's basis as equal to its fair market value on the sale date. If sale price and stepped-up basis are equal, there is no taxable gain on the appreciation. In plain English, the growth generated by the OZ investment over the decade-plus hold can escape capital-gains tax.

This election applies to the QOF investment's own appreciation, not to the original gain that was contributed and deferred. That original gain has its own recognition date. The separate mechanics of that temporary deferral are addressed in our [Opportunity Zone tax-benefits guide](opportunity-zone-tax-benefits-deferral-and-10-year-exclusion.html).

Hold the QOF interest for less than 10 years and this exclusion is unavailable; appreciation is taxable. That is why the 10-year hold is not a small technical condition. It is the central trade: a patient, long-term commitment in exchange for potential tax-free growth.

## Basis step-up to fair market value

Normally, an investment's basis starts with what an investor paid, subject to adjustments. Taxable gain is generally sale price minus that basis. The 10-year QOF election changes the calculation at exit: it steps the basis up to the investment's fair market value at the sale date. The sale price then equals the stepped-up basis, leaving no taxable gain on the appreciation.

This is not the same as the basis step-up at death under Section 1014. The 10-year QOF election is elective, available during the investor's lifetime after the required hold, and specific to QOF investments. Without it, the appreciation would ordinarily remain subject to capital-gains tax.

That is the technical heart of the benefit. It is an elective, lifetime basis step-up to fair market value at sale, rather than a blanket exemption for everything that went into the fund.

## A long-term growth example

Consider an illustrative example only. It is not a prediction, and actual results vary. An investor contributes a $1,000,000 capital gain to a QOF and holds the investment for 10+ years. Assume it grows to $2,500,000, producing $1,500,000 of appreciation.

With a valid 10-year election, the basis steps up to the $2,500,000 fair market value at sale. The $1,500,000 appreciation is therefore excluded from tax. In a taxable investment, that same $1,500,000 appreciation would be exposed to capital-gains tax and could cost several hundred thousand dollars in tax. The QOF investor keeps the full appreciation if the investment has actually performed as assumed and the election is available.

The original $1,000,000 deferred gain is still recognized at its set date. This example does not say that the original gain vanishes. It shows why the treatment of future appreciation can matter so much over a long hold. The actual figures depend on investment performance, applicable tax rates, and the rules in effect. Run the numbers with a CPA rather than treating an illustration as an outcome.

## What happens if you sell early

Selling before the 10-year mark gives up the tax-free-appreciation exclusion. The appreciation on the OZ investment becomes taxable, and the investor has forfeited the program's signature feature.

An early sale or other disposal can also be an inclusion event. It may accelerate recognition of any remaining original deferred gain if that gain has not already been recognized at its set date. Our explanation of the relevant timing is available in [Opportunity Zone gain recognition: the 2026 deadline and beyond](opportunity-zone-gain-recognition-the-2026-deadline-and-beyond.html).

There is a practical constraint as well: QOF interests are generally illiquid. An investor may not be able to exit early on acceptable terms even if the desire to sell arises. A QOF should therefore not be funded with capital that may be needed before the 10-year point. The tax cost and the liquidity risk both belong in the decision before capital is committed.

## Key Takeaways

- Hold a QOF investment for at least 10 years and elect fair-market-value basis at sale to exclude the QOF investment's appreciation from tax.
- This is an elective, lifetime QOF basis step-up, distinct from a step-up at death.
- A well-performing, long-held investment can produce meaningful after-tax value, but the original deferred gain is still recognized at its set date.
- A sale before 10 years leaves appreciation taxable and defeats the long-term commitment the benefit requires.

## Planning your 10-year exit

Mark the holding-period date from the investment date and plan to hold through the full 10 years. That sounds obvious, but an exit plan that begins in year ten is usually late. The holding period needs to be part of the underwriting from the start.

Then understand the exit mechanics for the particular fund. Value may be realized through sale of the QOF interest or through a fund sale of its assets followed by the applicable election. Some funds have provisions or expected timelines for a post-10-year exit. The permanent program commonly called OZ 2.0 supports continued holding, but the structure of a specific fund remains important. See [Opportunity Zone tax benefits after 2026](opportunity-zone-tax-benefits-after-2026-what-changed.html) for the program changes.

Finally, coordinate the tax work. The original deferred gain's recognition and the 10-year election at exit are separate events that should be planned with a CPA. Timing, mechanics, and the investor's own tax position all matter.

## Considerations and caveats

The exclusion makes appreciation tax-free. It does not create appreciation. A flat investment has little or no appreciation to exclude; a declining investment can still be a poor result despite favorable tax treatment. Investment merits come first. The tax benefit is relevant only after that question is answered.

The strategy also requires a full 10-year hold in an illiquid investment. It depends on the QOF and investment meeting program requirements and on current rules continuing to apply as expected. The permanent program supports the 10-year exclusion, but rules and implementation can change. And the original deferred gain is still taxed at its recognition date.

These are not reasons to ignore the potential benefit. They are reasons to frame it accurately: a contingent benefit tied to performance, duration, compliance, and law—not a guarantee.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the 10-year hold benefit?

The 10-year hold benefit, also called the 10-year exclusion, allows an investor who holds a QOF investment for at least 10 years to exclude that investment's appreciation from tax. At sale, the investor elects fair-market-value basis, leaving no taxable gain on the appreciation. It applies to new QOF growth, not the original deferred gain. No other common deferral tool offers tax-free growth on the new investment in this way.

### How does the basis step-up work?

The election makes the QOF investment's basis equal to fair market value on the sale date. Ordinarily, taxable gain is sale price less the investor's basis. With sale-date FMV as basis, sale price equals the stepped-up basis and the appreciation has no taxable gain. It is an elective, lifetime QOF election, unlike the step-up at death.

### Does the 10-year exclusion eliminate tax on my original gain?

No. The exclusion concerns appreciation in the OZ investment after it is made. The original deferred gain is recognized at its set date. Under OZ 1.0, that date is December 31, 2026; under OZ 2.0, the source describes a rolling 5 years from investing. The original gain is deferred and eventually taxed; the QOF appreciation may be tax-free after the required hold.

### Can you show the long-term growth math?

For illustration only, a $1,000,000 gain invested in a QOF could grow to $2,500,000 after 10+ years. The $1,500,000 appreciation would be tax-free if the 10-year election applies because basis becomes $2,500,000 at sale. A taxable investment could owe capital-gains tax on the same $1,500,000, potentially several hundred thousand dollars. The original $1,000,000 deferred gain still has its own recognition date, and no number in this illustration is a prediction.

### What if I sell before 10 years?

You give up the appreciation exclusion, so growth becomes taxable. A sale can also be an inclusion event that accelerates any remaining original deferred gain, and a QOF may be hard to sell because it is generally illiquid. Do not invest capital that may be needed before the 10-year mark.

### How do I plan my 10-year exit?

Track the date at which the investment reaches 10 years, understand whether value will be realized through sale of the QOF interest or fund asset sales, and coordinate the original-gain recognition and exit-time election with a CPA. Fund-specific exit provisions and timelines matter.

### Does the 10-year benefit require the investment to appreciate?

Yes. The exclusion applies to appreciation, so it has little or no value if the investment is flat or declining. Evaluate the investment on its merits, not on tax treatment alone.

### Is the 10-year exclusion guaranteed?

No. It depends on a full 10-year hold, actual appreciation, fund and investor compliance with program requirements, and rules applying as expected. Confirm current rules with a CPA. It is a potentially valuable result, not a certainty.

### Is the 10-year exclusion still available under OZ 2.0?

Yes. The source describes 2025 legislation as making the Opportunity Zone incentive permanent and preserving the 10-year exclusion. Other details, including the deferral period and zone designations, changed between OZ 1.0 and OZ 2.0. Regulations and implementation are evolving, so confirm the current details for a particular investment and timing.

Yes. Ten years is a minimum holding period for this benefit, not a mandatory sale date. The original program included guidance on making the basis-step-up election through a later cutoff, and the permanent program supports continued holding. An investor can generally hold longer and elect the step-up at sale, subject to then-current rules and timing details that should be confirmed with a CPA.

## Glossary

**10-Year Hold:** The holding period that unlocks tax-free appreciation.

**10-Year Exclusion:** Exclusion of the OZ investment's appreciation from tax.

**Tax-Free Appreciation:** QOF investment growth that escapes tax after 10 years.

**Basis Step-Up to FMV:** The election that makes basis equal sale-date fair market value.

**Fair Market Value:** The sale-date value to which basis steps up.

**Elective Step-Up:** The lifetime, QOF-specific basis step-up, rather than a step-up at death.

**Original Deferred Gain:** The reinvested gain recognized at its set date.

**Recognition Date:** The date on which the original deferred gain is taxed.

**Inclusion Event:** An event, such as an early sale, that triggers gain recognition.

**Early Sale:** A sale before 10 years that forfeits the exclusion.

**Holding Period:** Time from investment to sale; 10+ years is required.

**Exit Mechanics:** How value is realized after the 10-year hold.

**Appreciation:** Investment growth made tax-free by the exclusion.

**Illiquidity:** A QOF's limited salability during the hold.

**Permanent Program:** OZ 2.0, which continues the 10-year exclusion.

**Long-Term Commitment:** The patient hold this strategy requires.
