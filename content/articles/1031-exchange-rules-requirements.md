# 1031 Exchange Rules: The Requirements You Must Follow

I keep seeing investors focus on one attractive piece of a 1031 exchange—the deferred gain—while treating its requirements as paperwork. The requirements are the investment. A missed procedure can make the entire sale taxable; a value shortfall can make part of it taxable.

First-order thinking is, “Find a replacement inside 180 days.” Second-order thinking is, “Does the property qualify, will the exact same taxpayer buy, did I set up the QI before cash changes hands, and do both the equity and debt ledgers balance?” The rules are demanding because they interlock. Full deferral needs all of them working together.

## The Five Pillars of a Valid Exchange

A valid exchange has five core requirements: both properties are like-kind U.S. real property held for investment or business; the same taxpayer sells and buys; the replacement has equal or greater value with all equity reinvested and debt replaced; a qualified intermediary holds proceeds so the seller never receives them; and the 45-day and 180-day deadlines are met.

The QI and deadlines are procedural validity rules. Like-kind, same taxpayer, and value determine whether the exchange qualifies and whether deferral is complete or taxable boot remains. Section 1031 has applied only to real property since the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, and the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act made that real-property limitation permanent. In 2026, start by confirming that the asset is qualifying real property.

## The Like-Kind Property Requirement

Like-kind is broad for U.S. investment or business real estate. It means the character of real property, not matching type, grade, or quality. An apartment building can be exchanged for raw land, a warehouse for retail, farmland for an office building, a rental house for a [DST interest](dst-guide.html), or even perpetual oil and gas royalties treated as real property.

Personal-use primary residences and true vacation homes do not qualify. Nor do dealer inventory and fix-and-flip stock, foreign real estate in exchange for U.S. property, partnership interests, or personal property and equipment after 2017. If the asset's real-property character is uncertain, resolve that with counsel first.

## The Held-for-Investment Requirement

The property must be held for investment or productive business use. Intent and actual use matter; a property bought to flip in the ordinary course is dealer inventory even if it looks like real estate.

There is no statutory minimum holding period. Many advisers use at least a year and two tax years as a practical guideline to demonstrate investment intent, but the governing standard remains facts and circumstances. A quick relisting or an interest acquired only to flip raises scrutiny. Document leasing, holding, and business use. A genuine rental conversion can become eligible over time; a rental converted to personal use can lose eligibility.

## The Same-Taxpayer Rule

The taxpayer selling the relinquished property must acquire the replacement. The name on title, or the tax-disregarded entity behind it such as a single-member LLC, needs to be consistent on both sides.

Partnerships and multi-member LLCs are common complications. The partnership, not its individual partners, is the taxpayer. Partners cannot simply take their shares and conduct separate exchanges within the partnership transaction. A drop-and-swap before sale or swap-and-drop after sale may be considered, but both carry timing and holding risks and need advance counsel.

Correctly structured revocable trusts and disregarded entities can preserve same-taxpayer status. Consistency is the rule: whoever sells must buy.

## The Equal-or-Greater-Value Rule

Full deferral has three linked requirements: acquire replacement property worth at least as much as the relinquished property; reinvest all net equity; and replace debt paid off on the relinquished property.

For a $600,000 sale with $400,000 equity and $200,000 debt, full deferral requires at least $600,000 of replacement value, the full $400,000 reinvested, and at least $200,000 of new debt or additional cash. The equity and debt ledgers both need to balance.

Buy cheaper and keep the difference and that is cash boot. Reduce debt without contributing cash and that is mortgage boot. The rule is the gateway to understanding partial tax in an otherwise valid exchange.

## Key Takeaways

- Full deferral requires equal-or-greater value, all equity reinvested, and all debt replaced.
- Equity and debt are separate ledgers and both must balance.
- Shortfalls are taxable boot.

## The Debt-Replacement Requirement

Debt paid off on the relinquished property must be matched by new debt or added cash. If $300,000 of old debt becomes only $200,000 of new debt, the $100,000 liability reduction is mortgage boot unless the investor adds $100,000 of cash.

A leveraged DST can supply pre-arranged non-recourse debt at the trust level. The beneficial interest includes proportionate leverage without a new loan application or personal guarantee. That can be useful for retirees or investors with changing income, but it remains a security and needs suitability analysis.

## The Qualified Intermediary Requirement

A [qualified intermediary](insights.html) must hold sale proceeds between the relinquished sale and replacement purchase. The investor cannot receive or control the funds. See the broader [1031 exchange guide](1031-exchange-guide.html) for the transaction sequence.

Constructive receipt is the issue: access to proceeds through a personal account or the ability to draw on them counts even if no money is spent. A QI's segregated account and direct closing disbursement prevent that control.

Engage the QI before closing, preferably while the sale agreement is negotiated. Engaging one after closing is too late and is a common fatal error. Since QIs hold the whole proceeds and are lightly regulated, look for segregated qualified accounts, dual authorization, fidelity bonding, and appropriate insurance.

## The 45- and 180-Day Deadlines

Both clocks begin on the relinquished sale date and run concurrently. Within 45 calendar days, deliver a signed written replacement identification to the QI. Within 180 days, close on identified replacement property—or by the tax-return due date including extensions if that is earlier.

Identification can use the 3-property rule, the 200% rule, or the rarely used 95% exception. The 3-property rule names up to three properties of any value. The 200% rule allows more than three if combined value is no more than 200% of what was sold. The 95% exception allows any number only if at least 95% of identified value is acquired.

Days include weekends and holidays. There is no requested-extension or grace period. An identified fast-closing DST backup can protect against a primary deal that fails late.

## Avoiding Boot

Boot is non-like-kind value received and is taxable up to the gain. Cash boot is sale equity not reinvested. Mortgage boot is debt relief not offset by new debt or added cash.

Consider a $600,000 sale with a $200,000 mortgage and $400,000 equity. A $500,000 replacement with a $100,000 loan leaves $100,000 of cash boot and $100,000 of mortgage boot—roughly $200,000 of taxable boot. A $600,000 replacement with a $200,000 loan, all equity reinvested, produces zero boot and full deferral.

| Scenario | Result | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All equity reinvested and all debt replaced | Full deferral | Equal-or-greater value on both ledgers |
| Some cash kept | Cash boot | Unreinvested equity is taxable |
| Less debt and no added cash | Mortgage boot | Debt relief is value received |
| Cheaper replacement | Taxable shortfall | Replacement is below relinquished value |

## What Disqualifies a 1031 Exchange

Constructive receipt, a late QI, missed 45-day or 180-day deadline, non-like-kind or personal/dealer/non-real property, same-taxpayer mismatch, and vague, unsigned, or wrongly delivered identification can disqualify the entire exchange. Related-party exchanges can also unwind when their holding rule is breached. These are strict procedural issues, so address them before the sale.

## Related-Party Exchange Rules

Direct exchanges with related parties are allowed, but both parties generally must hold the received properties for two years. If either disposes within that period, the original exchange can be retroactively disqualified and deferred gain becomes taxable.

Indirect arrangements—such as acquiring through a QI from a related party where that party cashes out—receive close IRS scrutiny. Exceptions for death, involuntary conversion, or transactions not principally tax-motivated are narrow. Related parties include spouses, siblings, ancestors, lineal descendants, and entities in which the taxpayer owns more than 50%. Use counsel.

## Special Situations and Edge Cases

A vacation home may qualify only when genuinely investment-held and rented with personal use within safe-harbor limits. Inherited property often has a date-of-death step-up, which can leave little gain to defer. A former residence turned rental, or a rental turned residence, can move into or out of eligibility and may interact with Section 121.

Mixed-use property can be allocated: the investment portion may use Section 1031 and a personal-residence portion may use the Section 121 home-sale exclusion, up to $250k/$500k of gain, with a reasonable, documented allocation. These are fact-specific structures to review before committing.

## Documentation & Reporting Requirements

Keep the QI exchange agreement, contract assignments, and written identification notice. They establish that the transaction was structured as an exchange from the start.

File Form 8824 with the return for the year the relinquished property was sold. It records exchanged property, dates, values, boot, and carryover basis. Retain both closing statements, QI agreements, settlement and debt records, identification, and basis/depreciation schedules. The CPA should coordinate the reporting and long-term basis tracking.

## Putting the Rules Together

The rules become one repeatable process: confirm qualifying investment real property; engage the QI before sale; sell and start the clocks; identify in writing within 45 days, including a DST backup; acquire equal-or-greater value within 180 days with all equity reinvested and debt replaced; then report on Form 8824.

A QI is required. A CPA handles tax math and reporting. An experienced independent advisor can source and vet replacement property and coordinate deadlines. Their combined work is modest compared with the tax from one failed rule.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the requirements for a 1031 exchange?
Like-kind U.S. investment/business real property, same taxpayer, QI/no receipt, 45-day written identification, 180-day closing, and equal-or-greater value with equity reinvested and debt replaced for full deferral.

### What is the same-taxpayer rule?
The taxpayer or tax-disregarded entity selling must buy. This is why partnerships and multi-member LLCs require special planning.

### Do I have to replace debt?
For full deferral, yes: replace paid debt with new debt or equivalent added cash. Unreplaced debt is mortgage boot; a leveraged DST may provide qualifying replacement leverage.

### Is there a holding period?
No statutory minimum. A year and two tax years is a common practical guideline, but investment intent and facts control.

### What is equal-or-greater value?
Replacement must equal/exceed sale value, full equity must be reinvested, and debt must be replaced. Shortfalls create boot.

### Can I exchange across property types?
Yes. U.S. investment real estate is broadly like-kind: apartments, land, warehouses, retail, farmland, offices, DSTs, and certain perpetual royalties can exchange.

### What disqualifies an exchange?
Receipt, late QI, deadline failure, nonqualifying property, taxpayer mismatch, invalid identification, or related-party holding breach can do so.

### Can LLC partners do separate exchanges?
Not directly within the partnership exchange. Drop-and-swap or swap-and-drop may be considered but bring timing and holding risk and require counsel.

### Do I need a QI?
Yes. A QI holds proceeds and prevents constructive receipt; engage it before closing.

### Are the rules different in 2026?
Core rules continue. The 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act made permanent the 2017 real-property-only framework; deadlines, identification, taxpayer, and value rules remain.

## Glossary

- **Like-Kind Property:** Broad category of U.S. investment or business real property.
- **Held for Investment:** Investment/business use rather than property held primarily for sale.
- **Same-Taxpayer Rule:** Seller must be replacement buyer.
- **Equal-or-Greater-Value Rule:** Full-deferral value, equity, and debt test.
- **Cash Boot:** Unreinvested sale equity.
- **Mortgage Boot:** Debt relief not offset by debt or cash.
- **Qualified Intermediary (QI):** Required independent proceeds holder.
- **Constructive Receipt:** Access or control over proceeds that disqualifies exchange.
- **Dealer Property:** Sale inventory, such as flips, not eligible for 1031.
- **Drop-and-Swap:** Pre-sale tenancy-in-common distribution to partners, with timing risk.
- **3-Property Rule:** Up to three replacement properties of any value.
- **200% Rule:** More properties only within 200% of relinquished value.
- **Leveraged DST:** DST with pre-arranged non-recourse debt.
- **Form 8824:** IRS exchange and carryover-basis report.
- **Identification Notice:** Written, signed QI replacement notice within 45 days.
- **Related Party:** Close family or an entity controlled more than 50%, subject to two-year rule.
- **Section 121 Exclusion:** Home-sale exclusion up to $250k/$500k applicable to residence portions where eligible.
