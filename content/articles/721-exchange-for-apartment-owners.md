# 721 Exchange for Apartment Owners

I keep hearing apartment owners describe a kind of success problem: the buildings have created wealth, but they also create constant work. Turnover, repairs, leasing, tenants, financial administration—and the call that comes at 3 a.m.—can become too much, especially after decades of ownership.

Baker 1031 Research updated this 721 Exchange guide in June 2026. It is a 16 min read. A 721 exchange can transition apartments, directly or through a DST bridge, into passive multifamily REIT ownership. The intended benefits are tax deferral, a broader apartment portfolio, distributions without active management, and estate-planning flexibility.

First-order thinking sees an exit from apartment management. Second-order thinking asks whether the owner is exchanging a familiar set of buildings for a suitable REIT, how the deferred tax and recapture are treated, whether liquidity fits future needs, and whether passive ownership is actually the desired endpoint. These questions should be answered before the contribution, not after it.

## Why apartment owners use 721 exchanges

Apartment ownership is management-intensive. Tenants, maintenance, turnover, and everyday operations require attention. A [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html) can move the owner from direct apartment management into passive REIT ownership, which can be attractive to an owner who is aging or simply ready to stop being a landlord.

Apartments also tend to develop substantial low-basis gain from appreciation and depreciation. A sale can trigger the four-layer tax stack, including meaningful [depreciation recapture](depreciation-recapture-real-estate.html). The 721 can defer that tax and preserve pre-tax wealth in the REIT rather than selling first and reinvesting only the after-tax amount.

Concentration and estate planning are further reasons. An owner with a few apartments can turn exposure to specific buildings into exposure to a broader portfolio. OP units can be easier to divide among heirs, and a step-up at death can potentially remove deferred gain under current law. Passivity, deferral, diversification, and estate planning are the combination that makes a 721 relevant to many apartment owners.

## From active apartments to passive REIT

Before the exchange, the owner is responsible for leasing, tenant issues, maintenance, turnover, finances, and every other feature of direct apartment ownership. After the exchange, the owner holds [OP units](op-units-explained.html) in a multifamily REIT. The REIT manages its apartment communities, and the unit holder receives distributions without performing property management.

That is the central change. The owner keeps exposure to apartments but owns it through a professionally managed portfolio. The change from active landlord to passive investor can be especially meaningful for someone whose main reason for selling is no longer the properties themselves, but the work that comes with them.

The transition does not mean an owner leaves the apartment asset class. A multifamily REIT can keep the owner invested in apartments while spreading exposure across more communities, markets, tenants, and management systems than one or a few direct properties can provide.

## Multifamily REITs as the destination

Multifamily REITs own and operate apartment communities. They are a natural destination for an apartment owner who wants to remain in a familiar sector while moving from one or a few buildings into a diversified apartment portfolio.

The investor can gain geographic and property-level diversification inside the apartment sector. Instead of relying on a specific property or tenant base, the investor has exposure to many communities and markets within the REIT. That does not make the investment safe or guarantee distributions, but it changes the concentration profile.

A multifamily REIT may take an apartment directly if it wants that specific asset. More often, the route is through the DST bridge: first exchange direct apartments into a multifamily DST, then reach the multifamily REIT through a later 721 exit. In either case, the goal is passive, professionally managed apartment exposure.

## Deferring the apartment gain

Apartments are depreciated over 27.5 years. Long-term owners can therefore have substantial accumulated depreciation. A taxable sale can subject that depreciation to recapture, potentially up to 25%, in addition to capital gain, [NIIT](net-investment-income-tax.html), and state tax. A low-basis apartment can carry a substantial four-layer liability.

The 721 can defer capital gain and recapture. Instead of losing a third or more of gain to tax at sale, the owner can move the full pre-tax value into the multifamily REIT, with the embedded tax deferred. That is particularly significant where many years of depreciation have created meaningful recapture exposure.

The potential estate result is different from an immediate sale. If OP units are held until death, a step-up may eliminate the deferred gain, including recapture, for heirs under current law. The specific outcome is tax- and fact-dependent and needs CPA advice; it is not a guarantee.

## Estate planning for apartment owners

Direct apartments can be hard to divide among several heirs. OP units are divisible. An owner can hold the units, receive distributions during life, and leave separate units to multiple heirs rather than requiring a decision about which heir receives a building or whether the family sells it.

The possible step-up at death adds a tax element. Heirs may receive units with the prior deferred gain eliminated under current law. This combination—divisibility and a possible step-up—can address the common estate problem of passing appreciated, hard-to-partition apartments to multiple people.

The estate plan must still address the family’s full circumstances, legal documents, and current tax rules. A 721 is a tool within an estate plan, not the entire plan.

## Key takeaways

- Apartment owners may use a 721 to leave management, defer a large low-basis gain and recapture, diversify, and plan an estate.
- The core transition is active landlording to passive multifamily REIT ownership, with distributions but no direct management.
- A multifamily REIT can keep the investor in apartments while providing diversified, professional ownership.
- The 721 defers apartment gain, including 27.5-year depreciation recapture; OP units can divide among heirs and may receive a step-up.

## The DST-then-721 path for apartments

A direct contribution requires a multifamily REIT to want the owner’s specific apartments. That often is not feasible for typical individual properties. The practical route can be a 1031 exchange into a multifamily DST structured for a later 721 exit, followed by the DST’s acquisition by a multifamily REIT.

This DST-then-721 path keeps the owner in apartments through both steps: multifamily DST first, then a multifamily REIT. It can create a tax-deferred route to passive apartment exposure even where no REIT wants a direct contribution of the original building.

The path adds structural, securities, sponsor, and timing considerations. It should be evaluated as a full sequence, not merely as a promise of a future 721 result.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do apartment owners use 721 exchanges?

They use them to move away from tenant, maintenance, and turnover work; defer low-basis gain and depreciation recapture; diversify beyond a few apartments; and plan for divisible, potentially step-up-eligible wealth for heirs.

### What does the 721 exchange do for apartment owners?

It can move the owner from direct apartment management into OP units of a multifamily REIT. The REIT manages the apartments and the investor receives distributions, staying invested in apartments in a passive, diversified form while deferring gain.

### What is a multifamily REIT?

It is a REIT that owns apartment communities. For an apartment owner, it can provide a diversified portfolio of multifamily property across markets while keeping exposure to the asset class the owner knows.

### Does the 721 exchange defer apartment depreciation recapture?

Yes. Apartments depreciate over 27.5 years, and recapture can be up to 25% on a sale. A 721 can defer recapture along with capital gain, NIIT, and state tax. A potential step-up can eliminate deferred gain for heirs under current law.

### How does estate planning work for apartment owners with a 721?

The owner can hold OP units, receive distributions, and pass divisible units to heirs instead of leaving them hard-to-divide buildings. A step-up at death may erase deferred gain and recapture under current law, subject to actual facts and legal advice.

### Can I 721 my apartments directly into a REIT?

Possibly, if a multifamily REIT wants the specific apartments. Often that is not feasible. Many owners instead use a 1031 into a multifamily DST structured for a 721 exit, followed by a multifamily REIT acquisition of the DST.

### What is the DST-then-721 path for apartments?

It is a 1031 exchange into a multifamily DST, followed later by a 721 exit when the DST is acquired by a multifamily REIT. The owner moves from direct apartments to passive DST exposure and then to REIT OP units, remaining in the apartment sector throughout.

### Will I still be invested in apartments after a 721 exchange?

Yes, when the destination is a multifamily REIT. The owner exchanges one or a few direct buildings for a diversified, professionally managed apartment portfolio held through the REIT.

### How much tax can apartment owners defer?

It depends on gain, basis, depreciation, state, and the owner’s facts. Long-held apartments can have low basis and substantial depreciation recapture, so the four-layer tax can often be a third or more of gain. The CPA should calculate the actual amount.

### Is a 721 exchange better than a 1031 for apartment owners?

It depends on the goal. A 1031 keeps direct apartment ownership, control, and future-exchange flexibility. A 721 favors passive multifamily REIT ownership, diversification, possible liquidity, and estate planning. A 1031 into a DST can also provide passivity without a 721 endpoint.

### Can I do a 721 exchange with a small apartment building?

A direct contribution is often difficult because REITs generally seek larger, institutional-quality assets. A multifamily DST can pool the investment with others and later provide a path to a multifamily REIT. That can make the DST bridge more workable for smaller owners.

### Will I lose the depreciation benefits I had on my apartments?

Direct apartment depreciation deductions end when direct ownership ends. OP-unit distributions may have tax efficiency from underlying REIT depreciation, including a return-of-capital component. Prior recapture remains deferred in the units and may be subject to a later trigger or potential step-up. The CPA should explain the individual tax effects.

### Is the 721 exchange common among apartment owners?

It is an established strategy for apartment owners ready to step back, especially older owners with appreciated low-basis property and estate goals. It is not appropriate for every owner, but its passivity, deferral, diversification, and estate benefits match common apartment-owner objectives.

## Glossary

### Apartment Owner

A multifamily owner, often a candidate for a 721 exchange.

### Multifamily REIT

A REIT holding apartment portfolios and a common 721 destination.

### Active to Passive

The transition from hands-on landlording to passive REIT ownership.

### Management Intensity

The demands of operating apartments, a reason an owner may consider a 721.

### 27.5-Year Depreciation

The apartment depreciation period that can create recapture.

### Depreciation Recapture

Tax, potentially up to 25%, on apartment depreciation; it can be deferred by a 721.

### Low Basis

A small basis and therefore large gain in long-held apartments.

### Four-Layer Tax Stack

Capital gain, recapture, NIIT, and state tax that a 721 can defer.

### Diversification

Spreading exposure across a REIT’s apartment portfolio.

### OP Units

The REIT partnership units apartment owners receive and can divide among heirs.

### Step-Up in Basis

The death-time reset that can erase deferred apartment gain under current law.

### Divisibility

The ability to divide units more easily among heirs than buildings.

### DST-then-721 Path

Reaching a multifamily REIT through a multifamily DST.

### Multifamily DST

A DST holding apartments that can bridge to a multifamily REIT.

### Passive Distributions

Hands-off income from a multifamily REIT, subject to the REIT’s terms and performance.

### Tired Landlord

An apartment owner ready to stop managing property and a common 721 candidate.
