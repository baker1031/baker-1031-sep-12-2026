# The 721 Exchange (UPREIT) for DST Investors


[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**Definitive Guide · 2026 · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 26 min read**

I keep returning to the point at which a tax-deferral plan becomes a decision about permanence. A 721 exchange lets a 1031 investor trade a Delaware Statutory Trust for a stake in a large, professionally managed REIT while keeping tax deferred. It is powerful, and it is permanent.

First-order thinking focuses on the tax-free move. Second-order thinking asks what follows: which exit remains available, when the tax is actually recognized, whether the investor controls conversion timing, and whether the REIT itself deserves the capital. This guide covers the two-step path, the benefits and trade-offs, and written versions of the source decision, tax, and readiness tools.

This guide accompanies our [DST guide](dst-guide.html), where the 721 appears as an exit strategy.

## The essentials

- A 721 exchange swaps real estate, often a DST, for **OP units** in a REIT operating partnership, tax-deferred under Section 721.
- It is typically a **two-step path** for 1031 investors: 1031 into a DST, then the REIT acquires the DST and the investor receives OP units.
- It is a **one-way move**. OP units cannot be 1031'd; gain is deferred rather than erased; and **converting OP units to REIT shares is taxable**, although heirs can still receive a step-up at death.

## 01 · What a 721 Exchange (UPREIT) Is

“UPREIT” means Umbrella Partnership REIT. It is the structure in which a REIT holds properties through an operating partnership, or OP. Section 721 provides that contributing property to a partnership for a partnership interest is not a taxable event. When an investor contributes real estate, or a DST interest acquired by the REIT, to the OP, the investor receives **operating partnership units** instead of cash and the built-in gain carries forward, deferred.

OP units economically mirror REIT shares: they pay the same distributions, track the same value, and can typically be converted into actual REIT shares later on the investor's timetable. A 721 exchange, named for Section 721, therefore allows a contribution of real estate to a REIT operating partnership for OP units while deferring gain, much as a 1031 does.

For a DST investor, it is a common way to graduate from a single trust into a diversified, institutional REIT. Unlike a 1031, though, it is a one-way door. Once the investor holds OP units, the investor cannot 1031 again.

## 02 · The Two-Step DST Path

An investor generally cannot 1031 directly into OP units because OP units are not eligible 1031 replacement property. The usual path is two steps:

1. **Gain.** The investor begins with appreciated investment real estate, or proceeds from it, and a capital-gains bill the investor prefers to defer rather than pay.
2. **1031 → DST.** Within the **45/180-day** clock, the investor completes a 1031 exchange into an UPREIT-eligible Delaware Statutory Trust. Gain is deferred and the investor becomes a passive owner of institutional real estate.
3. **Hold.** The investor holds the DST and receives monthly distributions while the sponsor executes the business plan. The gain remains fully deferred.
4. **REIT acquires.** When the REIT elects to bring the DST into its operating partnership, the 721 step begins. The sponsor and REIT set the timing, not the investor.
5. **OP units.** The DST interest becomes operating partnership units under Section 721. They mirror REIT shares in value and income; the built-in gain remains deferred, but future 1031 exchanges are no longer available.
6. **Exit.** The investor can hold units for income and deferral, convert to REIT shares for liquidity and a taxable event, or hold until death so heirs receive a stepped-up basis.

## 03 · The Benefits

- **Continued tax deferral.** Section 721 defers gain when OP units are received, so the 1031 deferral continues.
- **Real diversification.** The investor moves from one DST property into a large REIT portfolio across many assets and markets.
- **Professional management.** The structure offers institutional scale and is fully passive.
- **A path to liquidity.** Conversion to publicly traded REIT shares can provide liquidity unavailable in raw real estate and DSTs; shares can be sold piecemeal.
- **Estate planning.** OP units are easy to divide among heirs, and a step-up in basis at death can erase deferred gain.

## 04 · The Trade-offs

- **It is one-way.** OP units are not like-kind replacement property and cannot be exchanged again through a 1031. A DST, by contrast, lets the investor or heirs continue exchanging.
- **Deferred, not eliminated.** Built-in gain carries into OP units and comes due when the investor finally sells.
- **Conversion is taxable.** Exchanging OP units for REIT shares triggers deferred gain, although investors can often convert incrementally to spread tax.
- **Timing and control matter.** Most conversions are optional after a holding period, but some DST-to-721 structures are designed for mandatory conversion. The specific deal should be read closely.
- **Distributions are REIT income.** Distributions are taxed as REIT dividends rather than the partly sheltered cash flow a depreciating DST can provide.

## 05 · OP Units vs. REIT Shares vs. DST

| Feature | DST interest | OP units (721) | REIT shares |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tax deferral | Yes, until exit | Yes, while held | No — taxable on sale |
| Future 1031 eligible | Yes | No | No |
| Liquidity | Low (**5–10 yr**) | Low–moderate | High, if traded |
| Step-up at death | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Income type | DST distributions | OP distributions | REIT dividends |

General comparison only; specific terms vary by sponsor and REIT. Converting OP units to REIT shares is a taxable event.

## 06 · Should You Take the 721?

The decision usually comes down to a direct question: are you done exchanging? If the investor wants to keep the 1031 option alive, a DST preserves it. If the investor is ready to settle into a diversified REIT for income and eventual liquidity, a 721 can deliver that outcome.

The following written decision tool preserves the four source questions and their possible paths:

1. **Do you still want to keep doing 1031 exchanges in the future?**
   - “No—I’m ready to stop and simplify” points toward a 721.
   - “Yes—I want to keep exchanging” points toward remaining in the DST or 1031 cycle.
   - “I want out of real estate entirely” points toward a cash-out.
2. **What matters most now?**
   - Diversification into a large REIT and eventual liquidity point toward a 721.
   - Keeping the 1031 option open while deferring points toward the DST or 1031 cycle.
   - Access to cash points toward a cash-out.
3. **What are the estate plans?**
   - Simplicity for heirs—units now and a step-up later—points toward a 721.
   - Heirs who may want to keep 1031-ing real estate point toward the DST or 1031 cycle.
   - A plan to spend the capital in life points toward a cash-out.
4. **How does a one-way move with no more 1031 exchanges feel?**
   - Being comfortable with REIT scale and liquidity points toward a 721.
   - Valuing flexibility points toward the DST or 1031 cycle.
   - Treating the issue as irrelevant because capital will be cashed out points toward a cash-out.

### The possible paths

**Take the 721 UPREIT.** This can fit an investor ready to stop exchanging, seeking REIT-scale diversification and a path to liquidity, and valuing simplicity for heirs. The trade-off is that it is one-way: there is no future 1031, and later conversion of OP units to REIT shares is taxable.

**Stay in the DST / 1031 cycle.** This can fit an investor who values flexibility and wants to preserve the ability to exchange again, including for heirs who may keep deferring through a step-up. The trade-off is continued illiquidity and the work of finding replacement DSTs. See the [DST guide](dst-guide.html).

**Cash out.** This can fit an investor seeking liquidity now and willing to pay deferred tax. The trade-off is recognition of deferred gain and depreciation recapture now.

## 07 · What Happens to Your Tax

The 721 calculus depends on how the investor eventually exits. Holding OP units keeps gain deferred. Converting to REIT shares and selling recognizes tax. Holding until death can give heirs a step-up, under current law, that may result in no tax on the built-in gain.

The source tax tool used a built-in deferred-gain range from **50000** to **5000000**, in **50000** increments, with a default of **500000** or **$500,000**. Its estimated tax-rate range ran from **15%** to **40**, in **0.2** increments, with a default of **23.8%**. The written calculation is:

`tax on convert & sell = built-in deferred gain × estimated tax rate`

For the default **$500,000** gain at a **23.8%** estimated rate, holding OP units shows **$0** current tax on the built-in gain; converting and selling produces the calculated tax; holding until death shows **$0** on that built-in gain, subject to the step-up assumption. The source tool's lesson is simple: holding keeps the gain deferred, converting and selling recognizes it, and only the step-up erases it. Distributions are taxed separately as REIT dividends, and appreciation after the 721 adds to the gain.

## 08 · Are You Ready? Mandatory vs. Optional

Before committing, the investor should understand whether the DST's conversion to OP units is optional or mandatory. The source readiness check contains six items; the first three are non-negotiable:

1. **Must understand:** A 721 is generally one-way; the investor cannot 1031 out of OP units.
2. **Must understand:** Gain is deferred, not eliminated.
3. **Must understand:** Converting OP units to REIT shares is a taxable event.
4. The investor has checked whether the DST's 721 conversion is optional or mandatory. Mandatory conversions remove control over timing.
5. The investor is comfortable with the REIT portfolio, leverage, and distribution history.
6. The investor has an estate plan using the step-up at death if deferral is long-term.

The source check scores readiness from **0/6**. At **6/6**, the investor is well prepared but should confirm specifics with an advisor and offering documents. At **4/6** or **5/6**, the investor is almost ready and should settle remaining points before committing. Below **4/6**, the investor is not ready yet and should work through core trade-offs first. If any of the three required points is missing, understand those first.

## 09 · The Process & Timeline

1. **1031 into a DST** structured with a future 721 or UPREIT option, often called an UPREIT-eligible DST.
2. **Hold the DST** and receive distributions while the sponsor executes the business plan.
3. **The REIT operating partnership acquires the DST.** The sponsor or REIT, not the investor, sets timing.
4. **Receive OP units** under Section 721 while gain remains deferred.
5. **Hold, convert, or pass on.** Keep units for income, convert to REIT shares for taxable liquidity, or hold until death for the step-up.

## 10 · Taxes & Estate Planning

Holding OP units defers gain indefinitely; distributions are taxed as REIT dividends. Conversion is the tax moment: turning OP units into REIT shares is a recognition event. Investors may convert in tranches across several years to stay in lower brackets.

The most tax-efficient outcome is often never converting during life: hold OP units, collect income, and let heirs inherit with a stepped-up basis that wipes out deferred gain. The trade-off with a DST is that heirs cannot keep 1031-ing, but they do not have to manage a rolling exchange strategy either.

## 11 · Risks & Due Diligence

The investor is underwriting a REIT rather than a single building. Evaluate portfolio quality, leverage, distribution coverage and history, management, and whether shares are publicly traded or non-traded, which determines real liquidity. Read whether the 721 conversion is optional or mandatory, identify the holding period, and model tax from a future conversion before assuming the transaction is “tax-free.” A zero-tax move into an underperforming REIT is still a poor investment.

## 12 · Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I 1031 exchange out of OP units later?

No. Operating partnership units are not eligible 1031 replacement property, so a 721 is effectively a one-way move. This is the central trade-off with staying in a DST, which preserves the 1031 option for the investor and heirs.

### Is converting OP units to REIT shares taxable?

Yes. Conversion to REIT shares, or redemption for cash, is a taxable event that triggers deferred gain. Many investors convert incrementally over several years to manage tax.

### Does the 721 eliminate my tax?

No. It defers tax. Built-in gain carries into OP units and is recognized when the investor sells or converts. It disappears only through the step-up in basis received by heirs at death.

### Do I have to convert, or can I just hold?

Usually, an investor can hold OP units indefinitely and collect distributions. But the specific deal must be checked: some DST-to-721 structures are designed for mandatory conversion, which removes control over tax timing.

### How is this different from staying in a DST?

A DST keeps 1031 options open: at full-cycle, the investor can exchange into another DST or property. A 721 trades that flexibility for REIT diversification, professional management, and a path to liquidity, at the cost of never 1031-ing again. See the [DST guide](dst-guide.html) for that path.

## 13 · Glossary

**721 Exchange**  
Contributing property to a REIT operating partnership for OP units, tax-deferred under IRC Section 721.

**UPREIT**  
Umbrella Partnership REIT: a REIT that holds property through an operating partnership.

**Operating Partnership (OP) Units**  
Partnership interests that mirror REIT shares in value and distributions and can later convert to shares.

**UPREIT-Eligible DST**  
A DST structured so the REIT can later acquire it and issue OP units to investors.

**Conversion**  
Exchanging OP units for REIT shares, a taxable event that recognizes deferred gain.

**Mandatory vs. Optional**  
Whether a DST-to-OP-units conversion is forced on a schedule or left to the investor's choice.

**Step-Up in Basis**  
The reset of basis to fair-market value at death, which can erase deferred gain for heirs.
