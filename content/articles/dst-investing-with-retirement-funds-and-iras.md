# DST Investing With Retirement Funds & IRAs

[Home](baker1031.html) › Insights › DST Investing With Retirement Funds & IRAs

**Delaware Statutory Trusts · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read**

I start with the distinction that changes the whole analysis: an IRA buys a DST with cash. It does not complete a 1031 exchange. A self-directed IRA can hold fractional income-producing real estate, but the account's existing tax treatment means there is no capital-gains tax inside it to defer.

First-order thinking sees passive income inside a retirement account. Second-order thinking tests UBIT from leverage, custodial administration, long illiquidity, RMDs, and the different consequences of Roth and traditional accounts. This is educational information, not tax, legal, retirement, or investment advice. [Our complete 2026 DST guide](dst-guide.html) gives broader context.

## DSTs in a Self-Directed IRA

An SDIRA can hold alternative assets including DSTs, unlike most standard brokerage IRAs, which generally limit holdings to stocks, bonds, and funds. The IRA—not the individual—is [the legal owner of the DST interest](holding-a-dst-in-an-llc-or-trust.html). Distributions enter a traditional IRA tax-deferred or a Roth IRA tax-free, subject to applicable rules.

The DST is a cash purchase with IRA funds, never a 1031 exchange. There is no relinquished property, qualified intermediary, 45-day or 180-day clock, or 1031 debt/equity requirement. The DST supplies real-estate exposure and potential income, not 1031 deferral.

## Cash Investors vs. 1031 Investors

A [1031 investor](1031-exchange-guide.html) exchanges appreciated property through a [qualified intermediary](what-is-a-qualified-intermediary.html), within deadlines and exchange rules, into a DST as [tax-deferred replacement property](how-to-find-1031-replacement-property-fast.html). A cash investor simply buys an interest for income and diversification. An IRA is always in the cash-investor camp: it needs no 1031 tax deferral because the account is already tax-advantaged.

## UBIT and Leveraged DSTs

Leverage creates the central IRA tax issue. Income attributable to non-recourse mortgage debt can be Unrelated Debt-Financed Income (UDFI), subject to Unrelated Business Income Tax (UBIT) inside an IRA. For example, a leveraged DST at 50% loan-to-value may expose roughly half of the income to UBIT, paid from the IRA through Form 990-T.

A debt-free, all-cash DST creates no debt-financed income and therefore no UDFI or UBIT. UBIT applies to both Roth and traditional IRAs. Many sponsors offer debt-free DSTs for tax-advantaged accounts, but a CPA and custodian should confirm the offering and tax treatment.

## Key Takeaways

- An SDIRA can buy a DST with cash; it is not a 1031 exchange.
- IRA investors are cash investors, free of 45- and 180-day exchange deadlines.
- Leverage can create UDFI and UBIT; a debt-free DST avoids that issue.
- A specialized self-directed custodian, sufficient liquidity, and a retirement timeline compatible with a long illiquid hold are essential.

## Custodian Requirements and Suitability

A mainstream brokerage IRA generally cannot hold a DST. A self-directed custodian holds the interest in the IRA's name, processes subscription and funding, receives distributions, and handles administration, including Form 990-T and UBIT payment when applicable. Custodian fees vary and should be compared; its process must work within an offering's subscription timeline.

DSTs are illiquid, often held five to seven years, and have little secondary market. A younger investor may accept that; a traditional-IRA owner who needs RMD cash may not. Maintain liquid assets elsewhere in the IRA or across IRAs to fund RMDs because a DST may not distribute enough and cannot be readily sold. Accreditation, real-estate risk, fees, and no-guarantee conditions apply just as they do outside the IRA.

Traditional IRA withdrawals are generally ordinary income and traditional accounts have RMDs. Roth qualified withdrawals are tax-free and a Roth has no lifetime RMDs for the original owner, which can suit a long-hold illiquid DST. Neither Roth nor traditional avoids UBIT from a leveraged DST.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I hold a DST in an IRA?

Yes, usually through an SDIRA. The IRA owns the beneficial interest and receives distributions. It buys with account cash, not a 1031 exchange, and should use a custodian that permits DSTs. Consider UBIT if the DST is leveraged.

### Can I do a 1031 exchange inside an IRA?

No, and none is needed. IRA assets already grow tax-deferred in a traditional IRA or tax-free in a Roth. The IRA buys alternatives with cash and has no QI, 45-day, or 180-day requirements.

### What is UBIT and how does it affect a DST in an IRA?

UBIT can tax UDFI from a leveraged DST. Debt-financed income can be taxable to an IRA despite the account's usual advantages. A 50% LTV DST can expose approximately 50% of income. The custodian may file Form 990-T and pay UBIT from the IRA.

### How can I avoid UBIT in a DST IRA investment?

Choose an all-cash, debt-free DST. No mortgage means no UDFI and no UBIT. This can be a natural IRA fit because an IRA does not need 1031 debt replacement. Confirm details with the custodian and CPA.

### What is a self-directed IRA custodian?

A specialist holding alternative assets such as real estate, private placements, and DSTs. It holds the interest for the IRA, processes cash subscription, receives distributions, handles reporting and UBIT payments, and charges administrative fees.

### Is a DST a good investment for a retirement account?

Possibly for an accredited investor with a long horizon and liquid assets elsewhere. It provides passive real estate and diversification but is illiquid for years, subject to risk and fees, and cannot be sold easily to fund a need or RMD.

### Can I use a Roth IRA to invest in a DST?

Yes. Roth qualified withdrawals are tax-free and original owners have no lifetime RMDs, potentially fitting a long-hold DST. A Roth still owes UBIT on UDFI from leveraged holdings, so all-cash property is the avoidance route.

### What's the difference between a cash investor and a 1031 investor?

A cash investor buys a DST for income and diversification. A 1031 investor exchanges property-sale proceeds through a QI under deadlines and debt/equity rules to defer gain. An IRA is always a cash investor.

### Do I need to be an accredited investor to hold a DST in my IRA?

Yes. The individual beneficial owner generally must satisfy accreditation and the broker-dealer must complete suitability review, including financial facts, goals, risk, liquidity, and retirement timeline.

### How are RMDs affected by a DST in a traditional IRA?

Illiquidity can complicate RMD funding. A DST may not provide enough cash and cannot be easily sold. Keep liquid IRA assets available; RMDs can often be aggregated across IRAs. Coordinate with the CPA and custodian.

### Why hold a DST in an IRA rather than personally?

An IRA offers tax-advantaged real-estate exposure and diversification from stocks and bonds; Roth funds can grow tax-free without lifetime RMD pressure. Personal DST ownership can instead serve as a 1031 replacement for property-sale gain. They are distinct uses.

### What fees are involved?

The DST has its normal offering, asset-management, property-management, and disposition costs. The SDIRA custodian adds setup, annual administration, transaction, and possible Form 990-T fees. Review the all-in cost.

### Can I move an existing IRA into a DST?

Yes. A direct trustee-to-trustee transfer or correctly handled rollover can move funds into an SDIRA, which then subscribes with cash. Follow transfer rules to avoid taxable distribution, and confirm debt-free structure and suitability.

### Can my IRA invest alongside a personal 1031 exchange?

Yes, but they are separate transactions and taxpayers. Personal exchange proceeds follow 1031/QI rules; IRA cash follows SDIRA and UBIT rules. Do not commingle funds in one investment. Coordinate CPA, QI, and custodian.

## Glossary

**Self-Directed IRA (SDIRA)** — IRA holding alternative assets, including DSTs.  
**Delaware Statutory Trust (DST)** — A trust holding fractional, income-producing real estate interests.  
**Cash Investor** — Buyer of a DST with cash, without a 1031.  
**1031 Investor** — Investor using DST to defer gain from a property sale.  
**UBIT** — Unrelated Business Income Tax that can apply inside an IRA.  
**UDFI** — Debt-financed income from a leveraged investment.  
**Debt-Free DST** — All-cash DST avoiding UDFI and UBIT.  
**Leveraged DST** — DST with non-recourse mortgage debt.  
**SDIRA Custodian** — Custodian holding alternatives in an SDIRA.  
**Form 990-T** — Return reporting and paying IRA UBIT.  
**Traditional IRA** — Tax-deferred IRA with taxable withdrawals and RMDs.  
**Roth IRA** — Tax-free qualified withdrawals and no lifetime original-owner RMDs.  
**RMD** — Required minimum distribution from a traditional IRA.  
**Accredited Investor** — Individual meeting income or net-worth requirements.  
**Loan-to-Value (LTV)** — Mortgage debt divided by property value.  
**Suitability Review** — Assessment that a DST fits the investor.
