# 1031 Exchange Glossary of Terms

I keep seeing a familiar problem in exchange planning: the investor understands the broad goal, but a term in the paperwork changes the outcome. “Boot,” “basis,” “EAT,” and “constructive receipt” are not decoration. They describe who holds money, how gain carries forward, and where a transaction can fail.

This is a plain-English reference to the vocabulary of a 1031 exchange. Use it alongside the deeper guides in the series. It groups the core ideas first, answers common questions, then provides a searchable term-by-term glossary.

First-order thinking is, “I only need the headline rule.” Second-order thinking is, “Which definition affects the taxpayer, the calendar, the cash flow, the asset, and the risk in this particular exchange?” The second question is usually the one that protects capital.

## Core concepts: boot, basis, and like-kind

**Boot** is taxable cash, unreplaced debt, or other non-like-kind value received in an exchange. **Basis** is the tax investment in property: original cost plus improvements minus depreciation. In an exchange, basis generally carries into the replacement property. **Like-kind** is broad for real estate: almost any U.S. investment real property is like-kind to another.

**Realized gain** is total sale gain—proceeds less adjusted basis. **Recognized gain** is the amount actually taxed, generally boot up to realized gain. **Carryover basis** is the mechanism that sends the old basis into the replacement and preserves the deferred gain.

## The parties: QI, EAT, and same taxpayer

A [qualified intermediary (QI)](insights.html) holds proceeds so the investor avoids **constructive receipt**, meaning access to or control over the funds. An **exchange accommodation titleholder (EAT)** parks title in a reverse or improvement exchange under a **qualified exchange accommodation arrangement (QEAA)**.

The **same-taxpayer rule** requires the seller of the relinquished property and the buyer of the replacement to be the same taxpayer. A **disregarded entity**, such as a single-member LLC, is generally treated as its owner. A partnership is a separate taxpayer, which can complicate matters if partners want different outcomes.

## Property, time, structures, and tax

The **relinquished property** is sold to begin the exchange; the **replacement property** is acquired to complete it. **Fee-simple** ownership is direct whole ownership. **Fractional** ownership can be held through a DST or TIC. **Dealer property** held for sale, such as inventory or flips, does not qualify. A **leasehold** with **30+ years** remaining, including options, can be like-kind to a fee interest. Both properties must be **held for investment** or business use.

The **45-day identification period** and **180-day exchange period** run in calendar days from closing. The **3-property rule**, **200% rule**, and **95% exception** govern written identification. A backup identification—often a DST—can protect against a failed direct purchase. The **tax-return-date trap** can shorten the **180-day** window for late-year sales without an extension.

A DST and a TIC are fractional replacement structures. The DST is the more common current form. A **721 exchange/UPREIT** contributes property to a REIT operating partnership for [OP units](op-units-explained.html); it is a separate, generally one-way deferral route that may be used after a DST’s full cycle. DSTs often use **non-recourse debt** to replace leverage without a new personal loan application.

Tax terms deserve equal attention. [Depreciation recapture](depreciation-recapture-real-estate.html), or unrecaptured Section 1250 gain, is taxed up to **25%**. The [net investment income tax (NIIT)](net-investment-income-tax.html) is a **3.8%** surtax for higher earners. A step-up in basis at death can eliminate deferred gain for heirs. Section **121** addresses primary-home gain; Section **1033** addresses involuntary conversions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does boot mean in a 1031 exchange?

Boot is non-like-kind value received, usually cash retained or debt not replaced. It is taxable up to gain even if the rest of the exchange is valid.

### What is basis in a 1031 exchange?

Basis is original cost plus improvements minus depreciation. It carries into the replacement property and preserves deferred gain.

### What is an EAT?

An exchange accommodation titleholder temporarily parks property in reverse and improvement exchanges under the IRS safe harbor in Rev. Proc. **2000-37**.

### What is the difference between a DST and a TIC?

Both are fractional replacement structures. A DST is passive, sponsor-managed, and often has pre-arranged debt. A TIC is direct deeded co-ownership with voting rights and more financing complexity. DSTs are the more common modern choice.

### What does like-kind mean?

For real estate, it concerns the nature of property as real estate, not type or grade. Almost any U.S. investment real property can be exchanged for another.

### What is constructive receipt?

It is access to or control over sale proceeds, even without physically holding cash. It disqualifies an exchange, which is why the QI holds funds.

### What is carryover basis?

It is the relinquished property’s adjusted basis carried into the replacement. It keeps deferred gain alive because gain is value less basis.

### What is a 721 exchange or UPREIT?

It contributes property, often a full-cycle DST interest, to a REIT operating partnership for OP units under Section **721**. It defers gain but is generally a one-way door: the investor cannot ordinarily 1031 back into direct real estate.

### What is depreciation recapture?

It is gain attributable to prior depreciation, often unrecaptured Section 1250 gain taxed up to **25%**. A qualifying 1031 exchange defers it.

### What is the same-taxpayer rule?

The taxpayer or disregarded entity selling must acquire the replacement. Matching title is crucial and can complicate partnership and multi-member LLC exchanges.

### What is a qualified intermediary?

It is an independent party that holds exchange proceeds and documents the transaction so the investor never has constructive receipt. It must be engaged before closing.

### What are the identification rules?

They are the **3-property rule**, **200% rule**, and **95% exception**. They govern how replacement property is identified in writing within **45 days**.

### What is non-recourse debt?

It is debt secured only by property without personal liability. A leveraged DST can use non-recourse debt to replace old leverage without a new personal guarantee.

### What is a step-up in basis?

It resets basis to fair-market value at death. It can eliminate deferred gain in a 1031 chain for heirs and is central to swap-till-you-drop planning.

### What is Section 121?

It is the primary-home exclusion that can exclude up to **$250,000** of gain, or **$500,000** for married couples. It is separate from Section 1031 investment-property treatment.

### What is a full-cycle DST?

It is a DST that has completed its hold and sold property, returning capital and gain. Investors may take taxable proceeds, complete another 1031, or in some cases roll into a REIT through Section 721.

### What is realized versus recognized gain?

Realized gain is total proceeds minus adjusted basis. Recognized gain is the part taxed—boot. A fully deferred exchange can have realized gain and no recognized gain.

### What is a sponsor in a DST?

The sponsor acquires property, structures the DST, and manages it. Sponsor track record, fees or load, alignment, and integrity are central to diligence.

### What is a private placement memorandum?

The PPM is the private-placement disclosure document for accredited investors. It describes property, structure, risks, fees, and projections; serious diligence begins with its risk factors.

### What is an accredited investor?

It is an investor meeting SEC income or net-worth thresholds—generally more than **$1 million** net worth excluding a primary residence, or qualifying income. Accredited status is required for private placements such as DSTs.

### What is a drop-and-swap?

It is a distribution of TIC interests to partners before sale so each holds direct real property and may complete a separate exchange. It has timing and holding-period risk and needs counsel.

### What is the napkin test?

It is a rough full-deferral check: buy equal or greater value and reinvest equal or greater equity while replacing debt. If both lines stay equal or rise, boot is generally avoided. It is not a substitute for the rules.

### What is unrecaptured Section 1250 gain?

It is the technical name for real-property depreciation recapture, taxed at a maximum **25%**. It can be the largest layer on a long-held rental, and a 1031 can defer it.

### What is a qualified opportunity fund?

It is a different deferral vehicle that can defer and potentially reduce capital gains invested in opportunity zones. It can accept gain from any source and, under the source description, may make a decade of appreciation tax-free.

### What is bridge financing in a reverse exchange?

It is short-term funding for the up-front replacement purchase before sale proceeds exist. It is repaid when the relinquished property sells and proceeds flow through the exchange.

### What is a 1031 exchange company?

The term can refer to a QI that holds funds, an advisor or broker-dealer that sources replacement property, or a sponsor that creates DSTs. Those roles are different, and an independent advisor is not the same as a sponsor.

### What does same taxpayer mean for an LLC?

A single-member LLC is generally disregarded, so the owner can exchange its property. A multi-member LLC or partnership is a separate taxpayer, so the entity—not individual members—must exchange.

### Where can I find definitions for other 1031 terms?

Use the deeper guides in this series and Baker 1031’s Learning Center. A QI and CPA can explain how a term applies to the actual transaction.

## Complete Glossary

- **1031 Exchange:** A like-kind exchange under IRC Section 1031 that defers capital-gains tax on investment real property.
- **Boot:** Taxable cash, unreplaced debt, or non-like-kind property received in an exchange.
- **Cash Boot:** Sale equity not reinvested into replacement property; taxable.
- **Mortgage Boot:** Debt relief not offset by new debt or cash; taxable.
- **Basis:** Tax investment in property—cost plus improvements minus depreciation.
- **Adjusted Basis:** Basis after adjustments such as improvements and depreciation.
- **Carryover Basis:** Relinquished basis carried into replacement property.
- **Realized Gain:** Total sale gain—proceeds minus adjusted basis.
- **Recognized Gain:** Gain actually taxed—boot up to realized gain.
- **Like-Kind:** Property of the same real-estate nature; broad for real property.
- **Relinquished Property:** Property sold to begin an exchange.
- **Replacement Property:** Like-kind property acquired to complete an exchange.
- **Qualified Intermediary (QI):** Required party holding proceeds and documenting the transaction.
- **Constructive Receipt:** Access to or control over proceeds that disqualifies the exchange.
- **Exchange Accommodation Titleholder (EAT):** Entity parking property in reverse and improvement exchanges.
- **Qualified Exchange Accommodation Arrangement (QEAA):** The safe-harbor agreement under which an EAT holds parked property.
- **Same-Taxpayer Rule:** Seller and replacement buyer must be the same taxpayer.
- **Disregarded Entity:** Entity, such as a single-member LLC, treated as its owner for tax.
- **45-Day Identification Period:** Window from closing to identify replacement property in writing.
- **180-Day Exchange Period:** Window from closing to acquire replacement property.
- **3-Property Rule:** Identify up to three replacement properties of any value.
- **200% Rule:** Identify more than three properties within **200%** of relinquished value.
- **95% Exception:** Identify any number if at least **95%** of identified value is acquired.
- **Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** Trust issuing passive fractional real-property interests usable as replacement property.
- **Tenants-in-Common (TIC):** Direct deeded fractional co-ownership of real property.
- **721 Exchange / UPREIT:** Contribute property to a REIT operating partnership for OP units, deferring gain.
- **OP Units:** Operating-partnership units from a 721 exchange, convertible to REIT shares.
- **Non-Recourse Debt:** Property-only debt without personal liability, typical of DSTs.
- **Full-Cycle:** DST property sale at the end of its hold, returning capital to investors.
- **Depreciation Recapture:** Prior-depreciation gain taxed up to **25%** as unrecaptured Section 1250 gain.
- **Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT):** **3.8%** federal surtax on investment income for higher earners.
- **Step-Up in Basis:** Basis reset to fair-market value at death, eliminating deferred gain for heirs.
- **Section 121:** Primary-residence home-sale exclusion, not a 1031.
- **Section 1033:** Gain deferral for involuntary conversions such as condemnation or casualty.
- **Dealer Property:** Real estate held primarily for sale; ineligible for 1031.
- **Held for Investment:** Required investment or business purpose, rather than personal use or sale.
- **Reverse Exchange:** Acquire replacement before selling through a parking structure.
- **Improvement Exchange:** Use exchange funds to build or improve replacement within **180 days**.
- **Form 8824:** IRS form reporting a like-kind exchange and carryover basis.
- **Tax-Return-Date Trap:** Late-year return due date can shorten the **180-day** window.
- **Cash Boot (restated):** Taxable sale equity not reinvested into replacement.
- **Realized vs. Recognized Gain:** Total sale gain versus the taxable boot portion.
- **Equal-or-Greater-Value Rule:** Full deferral requires replacement equal to or greater than relinquished value with debt replaced.
- **Identification Notice:** Written, signed replacement list delivered to QI within **45 days**.
- **Backup Identification:** Fast-closing option, often a DST, identified to protect the deadline.
- **Loan-to-Value (LTV):** Debt divided by property value; a measure for matching leverage.
- **Sponsor:** Firm creating and managing a DST or TIC program.
- **Sponsor Load:** Total fractional-program fees and costs as a percentage of the raise.
- **Private Placement Memorandum (PPM):** The private-placement offering disclosure document.
- **Accredited Investor:** SEC income or net-worth qualified investor eligible for DST-like private placements.
- **Rev. Rul. 2004-86:** IRS ruling treating a DST interest as direct real-property ownership for 1031 purposes.
- **Rev. Proc. 2000-37:** IRS reverse and improvement exchange parking safe harbor.
- **Rev. Proc. 2002-22:** IRS guidance for TIC rather than partnership co-ownership.
- **Drop-and-Swap:** Pre-sale TIC distribution allowing partners to exchange separately.
- **Swap-and-Drop:** Post-exchange interest distribution carrying timing risk.
- **Related Party:** Family or controlled entity subject to a **two-year** holding rule.
- **Mortgage Boot (restated):** Taxable debt relief not replaced by new debt or cash.
- **Net-Lease (NNN):** Tenant pays taxes, insurance, and maintenance, often under a long lease.
- **Credit Tenant:** Strong, often investment-grade tenant supporting reliable rent.
- **Single-Tenant Concentration:** Risk of depending on one tenant for all property income.
- **Cap Rate:** Net operating income divided by value.
- **Depreciation:** Building-cost deduction over **27.5** residential or **39** commercial years.
- **Unrecaptured Section 1250 Gain:** Real-property recapture portion of gain, taxed up to **25%**.
- **Cost Segregation:** Study accelerating early depreciation deductions and later recapture.
- **Installment Sale:** Payment-over-time sale that can spread recognition as a 1031 alternative.
- **Qualified Opportunity Fund:** Opportunity-zone fund that can defer and reduce eligible capital gains.
- **Bridge Financing:** Short-term funding for an up-front reverse-exchange purchase.
- **Build-to-Suit Exchange:** Improvement exchange used to construct investor specifications.
- **Forward (Delayed) Exchange:** Sell relinquished property first, then acquire replacement within deadlines.
- **Simultaneous Exchange:** Both exchange legs close on the same day; now rare.
- **Improvement / Construction Exchange:** Use exchange funds to build or renovate replacement within **180 days**.
- **Reverse Exchange (restated):** Acquire replacement before selling through an EAT parking structure.
- **Parked Property:** EAT-held property during a reverse or improvement exchange.
- **Master Lease:** Lease arrangement sometimes used for fractional or DST operations.
- **Net Operating Income (NOI):** Property income after operating expenses; cap-rate numerator.
- **Rent Escalation:** Scheduled lease rent increases.
- **Absolute-Net Lease:** Tenant pays virtually all costs, including structure and roof.
- **Going-In Yield:** Initial distribution rate or cap rate at acquisition.
- **Suitability Determination:** Assessment that a private placement is appropriate for an investor.
- **Regulation D:** SEC private-placement framework for accredited investors.
- **Broker-Dealer:** Registered firm offering securities such as DSTs; Baker 1031’s is Aurora Securities, member FINRA/SIPC.
- **FINRA / SIPC:** Securities-firm oversight and investor-protection organizations.
- **Percentage Depletion:** A deduction, commonly **15%** for oil-and-gas royalty owners, sheltering royalty income.
- **Mineral / Royalty Interest:** Perpetual oil-and-gas interest that can be real property eligible for 1031.
- **PV-10:** Present value of future proved-reserve net revenue discounted at **10%**.
- **Involuntary Conversion:** Loss by condemnation, casualty, or theft eligible for Section 1033 deferral.
- **Condemnation / Eminent Domain:** Government public-use taking with compensation; a Section 1033 trigger.
- **Net Sale Price:** Sale price minus selling costs; basis for equal-or-greater-value target.
- **Selling Costs:** Commissions and closing costs reducing net proceeds and gain.
- **Reinvestable Equity:** Proceeds available after debt payoff and selling costs.
- **Effective Tax Rate:** Total tax divided by total gain across applicable layers.
- **Constructive Improvements:** Improvements completed and paid within **180 days** that count toward replacement value.
- **Three-Property Rule:** Identification method allowing up to three properties of any value.
- **200% Rule (restated):** Any number of properties whose combined value is at most **200%** of relinquished value.
- **95% Rule:** Any-number identification if at least **95%** of identified value is acquired.
- **Direct Deeding:** Title passes between actual buyer and seller while QI holds only exchange funds.
- **Exchange Agreement:** Contract between taxpayer and QI governing the exchange.
- **Assignment of Contract:** QI assignment into purchase and sale agreements enabling exchange mechanics.
- **Qualified Escrow Account:** Restricted exchange-fund account preventing taxpayer access.
- **Like-Kind (restated):** Nearly all U.S. investment real property is like-kind by nature or character.
- **Held for Investment (restated):** Required purpose for relinquished and replacement property; excludes personal use and dealer property.
- **Dealer Property (restated):** Inventory held primarily for sale and ineligible for 1031.
- **Vacation-Home Safe Harbor:** Rev. Proc. **2008-16** rental-use conditions for vacation-property exchange qualification.
- **Clawback:** State rule, such as California’s, taxing deferred gain on a later taxable out-of-state replacement sale.
- **Mortgage Boot (additional definition):** Taxable gain when replacement debt is below relinquished debt and cash does not offset the gap.
- **Cash Boot (additional definition):** Taxable proceeds the exchanger takes instead of reinvesting.
- **Fair Market Value:** Price on which willing buyer and seller agree; used in equal-or-greater-value analysis.
- **Replacement Period:** **180-day** window, or earlier return due date, to close replacement property.
- **Identification Period (restated):** First **45 days** after closing for formal replacement identification.
- **Tax-Deferred vs. Tax-Free:** A 1031 carries gain into basis; only a step-up at death or the QOZ **10-year** rule makes appreciation tax-free.
- **Form 8824 (restated):** Sale-year IRS form for reporting a like-kind exchange.
- **Errors-and-Omissions Insurance:** Coverage a reputable QI carries for handling mistakes.
- **Fidelity Bond:** Bond protecting exchange funds from QI misappropriation.
- **Carryover Basis (restated):** Adjusted relinquished basis that transfers to replacement and preserves deferred gain.
