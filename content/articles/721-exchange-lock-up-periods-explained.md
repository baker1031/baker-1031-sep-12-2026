# 721 Exchange Lock-Up Periods Explained

I keep seeing a 721 exchange discussed as though OP units can be turned into cash the next day. They usually cannot. The investor may receive distributions immediately, but conversion to REIT shares is generally restricted by a lock-up, which is a real liquidity constraint, not a footnote.

A lock-up, also called a holding period, is the defined time after a 721 contribution when OP units cannot yet be converted into REIT shares. It is often around one year, but it depends on the operating partnership agreement and the specific REIT.

First-order thinking sees a temporary wait. Second-order thinking asks whether the owner has other liquidity for that period, whether the holding requirement supports tax structure, what the later conversion terms say, and whether the investor truly wants a long-term passive REIT position. A liquidity plan needs to be built before the lock-up begins.

## What a lock-up period is

A lock-up period is the time after receiving [OP units](op-units-explained.html) during which conversion to REIT shares is unavailable. In a [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html), the operating partnership agreement and the REIT’s terms generally require the contributor to hold units before exercising the conversion right.

During the lock-up, the investor remains a unit holder. The units can earn distributions, the gain remains deferred, and the investor retains exposure to the REIT portfolio. What the investor cannot do is convert units into shares for conversion-based liquidity.

The lock-up is a standard OP-unit feature. It delays, but does not necessarily remove, the conversion right. It is the first gate in the liquidity timeline.

## Why lock-up periods exist

One reason is tax structure. A meaningful holding period can support treatment of the transaction as a genuine partnership contribution rather than a disguised sale. That rationale supports the Section 721 deferral framework and explains why immediate conversion can be problematic.

Another reason is stability. The REIT and its operating partnership receive contributed capital and property. A lock-up reduces the chance that contributors immediately convert and exit, which supports the partnership’s capital structure and orderly operation.

Finally, the lock-up reflects the long-term nature of OP units. A 721 exchange is generally a commitment to REIT ownership, not an immediate flip to cash. The tax, structural, and investment-horizon reasons fit together.

## Typical lock-up durations

There is no universal period. A typical lock-up is around 1 year, but a specific REIT may require longer—sometimes 2 years or more—or use another structure. The partnership agreement controls the actual duration.

The investor should check the offering’s exact period, because it marks the earliest possible conversion date. A one-year term permits conversion only after that year; a longer term means a longer wait. There may also be ongoing terms after lock-up governing how frequently or how much can be converted.

The number that matters is in the actual agreement, not a general description of the strategy.

## What you can and can't do during lock-up

During lock-up, the investor can hold units, receive distributions, keep the gain deferred, and retain the economic exposure to the REIT portfolio. [OP unit distributions](insights.html) can continue as income during this period.

The investor cannot convert units to REIT shares. Without conversion, there is no conversion-to-share liquidity. The investor has income from distributions but cannot access principal through the usual share-conversion route.

For a long-term investor focused on income, deferral, and a possible step-up, that may be acceptable. For an investor who expects to need capital soon after the transaction, it is a material limitation.

## Planning around the lock-up

The first rule is simple: do not depend on conversion liquidity during the lock-up. If cash may be needed soon after the contribution, keep other liquidity sources available. Distributions are not the same thing as access to principal.

Determine the specific duration from the offering, put the first possible conversion date on the liquidity calendar, and evaluate other conversion terms as well. The owner should consider reserves, expected expenses, estate needs, and the tax that later conversion will trigger.

For an investor with no near-term cash need, the lock-up often is not a concern because the intention is to hold for income, continued deferral, and potentially a step-up at death. It is most important for investors who may need early capital.

## Key takeaways

- A lock-up, or holding period, prevents conversion of OP units to REIT shares for an initial period after a 721 exchange.
- It supports tax structure, REIT stability, and the longer-term nature of OP-unit ownership.
- It is often around 1 year but can be 2 years or more, depending on the offering and partnership agreement.
- Investors can receive distributions and maintain deferral during lock-up but cannot use conversion liquidity; plan other cash sources accordingly.

## After the lock-up

When the lock-up ends, the conversion right becomes available, subject to other terms. The investor can [use conversion and redemption windows](721-exchange-liquidity-windows-and-redemption-programs.html) to turn OP units into REIT shares. Conversion generally triggers deferred gain.

For a public REIT, converted shares can be sold in the market. For a [non-traded REIT](insights.html), converted shares may rely on a redemption program. The investor can also decide not to convert: continue holding, receive distributions, defer gain, and hold toward a potential step-up.

The end of the lock-up enables choice. It does not require conversion. The partnership agreement may still control frequency, amount, notice, or other mechanics after the initial period.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a lock-up period in a 721 exchange?

It is the period after OP units are issued when the investor cannot yet convert them into REIT shares. The investor holds units, receives distributions, and retains gain deferral, but cannot use conversion-based liquidity.

### Why do OP units have a lock-up period?

The period supports tax treatment by helping distinguish a genuine contribution from a disguised sale, supports REIT and partnership stability by reducing immediate exits, and reflects the long-term nature of the investment.

### How long is the lock-up period?

It varies. Often it is around 1 year, but it can be 2 years or more. The operating partnership agreement and offering terms set the actual period, so investors should verify those documents.

### What can I do during the lock-up period?

You can hold the units, receive distributions, maintain tax deferral, and keep exposure to the REIT portfolio. You cannot convert units to REIT shares, so the principal cannot be accessed through the normal conversion route.

### Can I access any liquidity during the lock-up?

You can receive distributions as income. You cannot access conversion-to-share liquidity because conversion is unavailable. If principal liquidity is needed during lock-up, other cash sources are required.

### What happens after the lock-up period ends?

The conversion right becomes available, subject to other terms. You may convert units to shares, triggering gain, and then sell public shares or use a non-traded REIT redemption program. You may also simply continue holding units.

### Does the lock-up period matter if I'm holding long-term?

Usually less. An investor holding for distributions, deferral, and a possible step-up may not intend to convert during the lock-up anyway. It matters most to someone who needs early liquidity.

### How do I plan around the lock-up period?

Do not rely on conversion liquidity during the period. Confirm the specific duration, arrange other liquidity for foreseeable needs, and plan the date and tax effect of later conversion. The lock-up should be part of the investor’s cash-flow plan.

### Is the lock-up period a reason not to do a 721 exchange?

Not usually by itself. It is a standard temporary feature and often manageable for a long-term holder. It can be a significant issue if the investor needs capital shortly after contribution. The answer depends on the liquidity timeline.

### Are there terms governing conversions after the lock-up?

Possibly. The REIT may set conversion frequency, minimum or maximum amounts, notice requirements, and other procedures. Check the specific agreement for both the initial lock-up and post-lock-up mechanics.

### Do I still earn income during the lock-up period?

Yes. Distributions can continue during lock-up. The period restricts conversion to shares, not the investor’s status as an income-earning unit holder. Distributions are subject to the REIT’s performance and terms.

### Can the lock-up period be waived or shortened?

Generally not at the individual investor’s option. It is typically a fixed term in the partnership agreement. Some structures may include limited death or hardship exceptions, but investors should assume the full stated period applies unless the specific documents say otherwise.

### Does the lock-up affect the deferral or the step-up?

No. It affects when conversion is permitted. Gain remains deferred while units are held during and after lock-up, and a possible step-up at death is unaffected by the lock-up. It is a liquidity-timing feature, not a reduction of the tax benefits.

## Glossary

### Lock-Up Period

The time after receiving OP units before conversion to shares is allowed.

### Holding Period

Another name for the lock-up period.

### Conversion Right

The right to convert units into shares after lock-up.

### Operating Partnership Agreement

The agreement setting lock-up and conversion terms.

### Distributions

Income earned during and after lock-up.

### Deferral

Gain deferral that continues while units are held.

### Conversion Liquidity

Liquidity obtained by converting units to shares, unavailable during lock-up.

### Disguised Sale

A transaction characterization the lock-up helps avoid.

### Stability

The REIT’s interest in retaining contributed capital, supported by the lock-up.

### Typical Duration

Often around one year, but varying by offering.

### Post-Lock-Up Flexibility

The choice to hold or convert after lock-up ends.

### Conversion Terms

Additional conditions governing conversions after lock-up.

### Liquidity Timeline

The investor’s timing of cash needs relative to the lock-up.

### Long-Term Holder

An investor for whom the lock-up may be a non-issue.

### Early Liquidity

Cash needed soon after the exchange, which lock-up limits.

### Other Liquidity Sources

Cash sources to use if needed during lock-up.
