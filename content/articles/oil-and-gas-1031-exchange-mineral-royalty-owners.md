# 1031 Exchange for Oil & Gas: How Mineral & Royalty Owners Defer Taxes

[Home](baker1031.html)

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

*Mineral & Royalty · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 9 min read*

I keep seeing mineral owners evaluate a sale as though the only choice is to take the cash or keep the asset. There is often a third question: whether the interest sold can be exchanged into another qualifying real-property interest before tax erodes the capital available for the next decision.

Section 1031 is not limited to apartment buildings. Mineral and royalty interests are generally treated as real property, so an oil and gas owner can defer capital gains by exchanging into like-kind real estate, including a royalty DST. The tax is deferred, not erased. That difference deserves careful attention.

For an owner, the value locked in a mineral or royalty position may come with capital gains and depletion recapture on sale. Section 1031 can defer that tax when the interest qualifies as real property under federal tax law. For the broader framework, read [our complete guide to mineral rights and royalty 1031 exchanges](mineral-rights-1031-guide.html).

First-order thinking says that a 1031 exchange protects value because it delays tax. Second-order thinking asks whether the new asset is worth owning after commodity concentration, operator exposure, liquidity, operating obligations, timing pressure, and the price paid for tax deferral are all included. Deferral can preserve capital. It cannot turn a weak replacement property into a sound investment.

## What Is a 1031 Exchange for Oil & Gas?

A 1031, or like-kind, exchange lets an owner sell investment or business real property and reinvest the proceeds in other like-kind real property without recognizing the capital gain at the time of sale. The cost basis carries into the replacement property. Gain is generally recognized only when the owner later sells without completing another exchange.

This matters because the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act limited Section 1031 to **real property**. Personal property and equipment no longer qualify. Long-standing federal authority generally treats an interest in minerals in place as an interest in real estate, so a royalty owner in the Permian can, in principle, exchange into an apartment building, raw land, a Delaware Statutory Trust, or [another producing mineral interest as replacement property](mineral-rights-as-replacement-property-1031.html), and defer the gain.

The mechanics are the same as any real-estate exchange. The owner cannot receive the proceeds. A [qualified intermediary](1031-exchange-timeline.html) must hold them, and the identification and closing deadlines are strict. The added work is diligence: confirm that both the interest being sold and the interest being acquired qualify as like-kind real property.

## Which Oil & Gas Interests Qualify as Real Property

The central distinction is between a continuing, or perpetual, interest in minerals in place and a right that ends after a term of years or a fixed quantity of production. The better case is an interest that lasts for the economic life of the reserve.

A perpetual **royalty interest** is usually the cleanest fit. It is a non-cost-bearing, non-operating share of production that continues for the life of the property, and the IRS has long treated such interests as real property. An **overriding royalty interest (ORRI)** was specifically held to be real property eligible for like-kind exchange in Revenue Ruling 72-117. A **working (operating) interest** can also be real property. [Revenue Ruling 68-331 treated a leasehold in a producing oil lease](irs-rulings-tax-court-oil-gas-like-kind-exchanges.html), extending until the deposit is exhausted, as like-kind to a fee ranch. But a working interest carries drilling costs and operating liability that a royalty does not.

The harder cases are term-limited interests. A royalty carved out for a set number of years, a production payment ([which the Code treats as a loan rather than a real-property interest](do-production-payments-qualify-1031-exchange.html) under IRC §636), and other finite carve-outs tend to fail. State law also matters. Most producing states treat minerals as real property, but definitions are not uniform. An interest that clearly qualifies in Texas may need closer analysis elsewhere. See [Can You 1031 Exchange Mineral Rights?](can-you-1031-exchange-mineral-rights.html) and [Are Oil & Gas Royalties Eligible for a 1031 Exchange?](are-oil-gas-royalties-eligible-1031-exchange.html) for a closer look at that line.

| Interest Type | 1031 Eligibility | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perpetual royalty interest | Eligible | Continuing, cost-free interest in real property for the life of the reserve |
| Fee mineral interest | Eligible | Ownership of minerals in place; real property in producing states |
| Overriding royalty (ORRI) | Usually | Real property per Rev. Rul. 72-117, but tied to the lease — confirm duration |
| Working (operating) interest | Usually | Real property per Rev. Rul. 68-331, but cost-bearing and operational |
| Term royalty / carve-out | At risk | Finite duration generally fails the perpetual-interest test |
| Production payment | No | Treated as a loan under IRC §636, not a real-property interest |

## Benefits: Tax Deferral, Diversification & Passive Income

The headline benefit is that capital otherwise headed to federal and state [capital gains tax](capital-gains-tax-on-real-estate.html), the 3.8% [net investment income tax](net-investment-income-tax.html), and depletion recapture can remain invested and compounding.

For many owners, the more useful question is what the exchange allows them to own next. A concentrated, single-basin royalty position is exposed to an operator’s drilling schedule and commodity conditions. An exchange can move that exposure into a diversified mineral pool or institutional real estate through a DST without triggering tax at the time of the move.

One example from the firm’s coverage is **Resource Royalty 27**, a debt-free, direct-title mineral and royalty DST spanning roughly **553 net royalty acres** across **12 properties** in the Anadarko Basin in Oklahoma and the northern Permian Basin in Texas. It is structured as exchange-eligible replacement property. Well-capitalized producers operate the assets, so investors carry no drilling capital expenditure. That description is not a substitute for underwriting; it simply illustrates a type of replacement structure an exchanger may evaluate.

## Key Takeaways

- Mineral and royalty interests are generally treated as real property, so their sale proceeds can be reinvested through a 1031 exchange.
- Perpetual royalty interests are the cleanest fit; term interests and production payments usually do not qualify.
- The exchange can move an owner from a concentrated, commodity-linked position into diversified or passive real estate — including royalty-pool DSTs — with the gain deferred.

## The 45- and 180-Day Timeline at a Glance

A 1031 exchange runs on two hard deadlines starting the day the relinquished interest closes:

- **45 days:** Identify replacement property in writing.
- **180 days:** Close on the replacement property.

These deadlines are non-extendable. Missing either generally fails the exchange and makes the gain taxable. The 45-day period is especially tight in minerals because sourcing a qualifying mineral or royalty package can take time.

That is why some oil and gas exchangers identify a securitized backup, such as a mineral or real-estate DST that can close in days. A slow direct acquisition then does not have to cause the exchange to fail. The [qualified intermediary](what-is-a-qualified-intermediary.html) must be engaged **before** the sale closes. Once the owner has received the proceeds, the exchange is over.

## Is a 1031 Exchange Right for Mineral Owners?

An exchange tends to make the most sense when the owner has meaningful embedded gain, wants to remain invested in real assets, and has a clear reason to defer—diversification, a shift to passive income, or a plan to hold until a [step-up in basis](1031-exchange-step-up-in-basis-at-death.html) at death wipes out the deferred gain for heirs.

It may make less sense when the basis is already high, including recently inherited minerals with a stepped-up basis and little gain to defer, or when the owner needs cash.

Eligibility is a fact-specific legal question involving the form of the interest, its duration, and state law. It should be decided with qualified counsel and a CPA, not from an article. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you do a 1031 exchange with oil and gas interests?

Yes. Mineral and royalty interests are generally treated as real property under federal tax law, so sale proceeds can be reinvested through a Section 1031 like-kind exchange to defer capital gains. The interest must qualify as real property—typically a perpetual interest in minerals in place rather than a term-limited right—and the owner must use a qualified intermediary and meet the 45- and 180-day deadlines.

### What oil and gas interests qualify for a 1031 exchange?

Perpetual royalty interests, ORRIs, and working interests that continue until the reserve is exhausted are generally treated as like-kind real property. Term royalties, production payments treated as loans, and other finite carve-outs usually do not qualify. The determination is fact-specific and varies by state.

### Can I 1031 exchange mineral rights for an apartment building?

Generally, yes. Like-kind treatment for real property is broad. A qualifying mineral or royalty interest can be exchanged for nearly any other U.S. investment real estate, including multifamily, net-lease property, raw land, or a DST interest, and vice versa. Value and debt must be matched to achieve full deferral.

### Did the 2017 tax law change 1031 for oil and gas?

The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act limited Section 1031 to real property, ending like-kind treatment for personal property and equipment. Oil and gas royalty and mineral interests continue to qualify because they are treated as real property; the longstanding treatment in rulings such as Rev. Rul. 72-117 was preserved.

### How long do I have to complete an oil and gas 1031 exchange?

The standard deadlines apply: identify replacement property in writing within 45 days of the sale and close within 180 days. Both periods run from the closing date and cannot be extended, other than limited federal disaster relief.

### Do I need a qualified intermediary for a mineral rights exchange?

Yes. The owner cannot take possession of the proceeds. A qualified intermediary holds the funds and manages the exchange documentation, including mineral-specific paperwork such as deeds and division orders. Engage the QI before the sale closes.

## Glossary

### 1031 Exchange

A like-kind exchange under Internal Revenue Code Section 1031 that defers capital gains tax when investment or business real property is exchanged for other like-kind real property.

### Like-Kind Property

For Section 1031 purposes after 2017, U.S. real property held for investment or business use. Most real estate is like-kind to most other real estate, regardless of asset type.

### Mineral Interest

Ownership of the minerals (oil, gas, and other resources) in place beneath a tract of land, including the right to extract them or lease that right to others. Generally treated as real property.

### Royalty Interest

A non-operating, cost-free share of production (or its value) from a mineral property, free of drilling and operating expense. A perpetual royalty is generally a real-property interest.

### Overriding Royalty Interest (ORRI)

A royalty carved out of a working interest that is tied to the lease rather than to the underlying mineral estate. Held to be a real-property interest eligible for like-kind exchange in Rev. Rul. 72-117.

### Working Interest

The operating interest under an oil and gas lease — the right to drill and produce — which bears its share of costs and liabilities. Real property, but cost-bearing, unlike a royalty.

### Production Payment

A right to a specified quantity or value of production for a limited period. Treated as a loan (a financing) under IRC §636, so it generally does not qualify as like-kind real property.

### Qualified Intermediary (QI)

An independent party that holds the exchange proceeds and documents the transaction so the taxpayer never has actual or constructive receipt of the funds. Required for a deferred 1031 exchange.

### Boot

Cash or non-like-kind value received in an exchange — including debt that is not replaced. Boot is taxable even within an otherwise valid 1031 exchange.

### Depletion

A deduction that recovers an owner's investment as a mineral reserve is produced. It reduces the owner's cost basis over time, which affects the gain recognized on a later sale.
