# Swap-and-Drop 1031 Exchanges Explained

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

I keep seeing partnership exchanges treated as though the property sale is the only decision. It is not. When partners want to separate, the order in which they separate can become the risk that matters most.

A partnership is the taxpayer. That simple fact makes it hard for individual partners to take different paths inside one exchange. Two techniques are used when partners want to divide eventually: drop-and-swap and swap-and-drop. They solve the same problem in reverse order, and they do not carry the same timing risk.

The first-order view is that either structure can let a partnership exchange and later give partners more independence. The second-order view is about the record created by the timing: who held what, when, for what investment purpose, and whether a later distribution looks like a separate decision rather than part of a prearranged exchange. This is educational information, not tax, legal, accounting, or investment advice. Qualification and tax results are fact-specific; an investor should work with experienced tax counsel and a CPA before acting.

## What swap-and-drop means

Swap-and-drop reverses the order of the [more familiar drop-and-swap](drop-and-swap-1031-exchanges-explained.html). The partnership does the [1031 exchange](1031-exchange-guide.html) first—the swap—and distributes interests to the partners afterward—the drop.

In this structure, the partnership sells the relinquished property and acquires replacement property as the partnership. It remains the taxpayer throughout the exchange. Only after the exchange is complete does it distribute interests in the replacement property, or otherwise restructure, so that partners can hold their own interests and eventually go separate ways.

The sequence is concrete. First, the partnership sells the relinquished property and exchanges into replacement property. It holds the new property. Then, after the exchange, it distributes the replacement property as tenant-in-common interests or uses another restructuring so each partner can own an individual interest.

That order is the essential distinction. In a drop-and-swap, the drop comes before the sale, so partners hold direct interests during the exchange and exchange individually. In a swap-and-drop, the drop comes after the exchange, so the partnership holds the property through the exchange and partners separate later. The latter keeps the exchange at the partnership level and addresses separation afterward.

## Drop-and-swap vs. swap-and-drop

The techniques differ in the moment at which the partners separate. A drop-and-swap distributes the property to partners before sale. Each partner can then make an individual exchange, choose a replacement, or cash out. A swap-and-drop keeps the partnership together through the exchange. The partnership acquires a shared replacement, then later separates the partners.

That produces different practical profiles. Drop-and-swap provides maximum flexibility when partner goals diverge. One partner may exchange into one asset, another may select a different replacement, and another may choose cash. But the pre-sale drop creates a held-for-investment question: did each partner hold the distributed interest for investment long enough before sale?

Swap-and-drop is less flexible at the exchange itself because the partnership acquires the replacement as a unit. In exchange, it avoids a pre-sale distribution. Its held-for-investment issue is later: did the partnership hold the replacement property for investment long enough before distributing it? A quick post-exchange distribution can invite an IRS argument that the partnership never genuinely held the replacement for investment, or that the distribution was part of the exchange plan from the outset.

So both techniques have a timing concern. In drop-and-swap, it is pre-sale holding by the partners. In swap-and-drop, it is post-exchange holding by the partnership. Neither structure makes the concern disappear. They place it in different parts of the timeline.

## Timing considerations

Timing is the center of a swap-and-drop. Once the partnership completes its exchange and acquires replacement property, it should hold that property for a meaningful period before distributing interests to the partners. A distribution immediately after the exchange can weaken the position that the partnership held the replacement for investment and can appear prearranged.

There is no bright-line required holding period. Still, the longer the partnership holds the replacement before distribution, the stronger the case that the partnership made a real investment decision and that the subsequent distribution was a separate decision. A meaningful period across a tax year or longer is sometimes suggested, but it is not a safe-harbor rule. Counsel must assess the actual facts.

This is where swap-and-drop can be more workable for some partnerships. A sale may be imminent, leaving no realistic chance to distribute property to partners well before the sale and establish a meaningful pre-sale holding period. In that case, the partnership may be able to complete an entity-level exchange, hold the replacement after closing, and separate later. Other partnerships may find that the pre-sale holding period is easier to support. The right answer turns on the facts, not a label.

Patience is therefore not an administrative detail. It is part of the substance. The partnership needs enough time and genuine investment use to support the position that the post-exchange holding was real.

## Documentation & risk

Swap-and-drop attracts IRS scrutiny just as drop-and-swap does. The main risk is that the IRS treats the post-exchange distribution as part of a prearranged plan, concludes that the partnership did not genuinely hold replacement property for investment, or argues that the structure in substance circumvents the same-taxpayer rule.

Documentation should address the partnership's genuine investment holding of the replacement, the meaningful time between the exchange and the distribution, and the separate character of each partner's later decision. The partnership-level exchange itself is straightforward. The attention belongs on the post-exchange holding and the later distribution: was the replacement held as an investment rather than as a momentary stop before a planned division?

Both structures carry real risk. Neither is a do-it-yourself technique, and neither is risk-free. When done carefully—with adequate post-exchange holding, genuine investment use, and good documentation—a swap-and-drop can be defensible. When done carelessly, it can be difficult to support. Experienced tax counsel should structure the timing and documentation to withstand examination.

## Choosing an approach

The decision starts with the partners' goals. If partners need different replacement properties at the time of sale, drop-and-swap offers the more flexible path because each partner can exchange individually or cash out. Its cost is the pre-sale holding question, which can be difficult if the sale is already close.

If partners can remain together in the same replacement through the exchange and separate afterward, swap-and-drop can keep the exchange cleaner at the partnership level. Its cost is a post-exchange holding period before distribution.

Counsel should consider whether partners need different replacements or can accept a shared one; whether a pre-sale or post-exchange holding period is easier to establish; the timing of the sale; the partners' relationship and objectives; and the risk tolerance of the group. A variation may be appropriate in some circumstances. Neither technique is universally better.

### Key Takeaways

- Swap-and-drop has the partnership exchange first at the entity level and distribute interests later; it is the reverse of drop-and-swap.
- The techniques differ in when partners separate: before the exchange for drop-and-swap, after it for swap-and-drop.
- Both have held-for-investment risk, but drop-and-swap focuses on pre-sale holding and swap-and-drop focuses on post-exchange holding.
- The decision should be made early with experienced tax counsel, based on partner goals, a feasible holding period, the timeline, and the risk profile.

## Practical execution of swap-and-drop

Execution begins with a standard partnership-level exchange. The partnership engages a [qualified intermediary](what-is-a-qualified-intermediary.html), sells the relinquished property, identifies and acquires replacement property, and keeps the gain deferred if the exchange requirements are met. The partnership, rather than its individual partners, handles that entire exchange.

Next comes the holding period. The partnership holds and operates the replacement property as an investment for a meaningful period. Only after adequate holding does it distribute replacement property to partners as tenant-in-common interests or otherwise restructure. Each partner can then hold, sell, or pursue a future exchange with an individual interest.

The distribution must be documented as a genuine post-exchange decision. The partnership needs to have held the replacement for investment in the interim. That discipline—exchange first, hold, then distribute—is the central practical feature of swap-and-drop.

The advantage is that the exchange itself is a clean, uncomplicated partnership transaction with no pre-sale drop to examine. The trade-off is clear too: partners stay together in the replacement through the exchange and for the holding period. A partner who wants an entirely different replacement at the time of sale has less flexibility under this approach.

## Which situations favor swap-and-drop

Swap-and-drop can fit when partners agree on the replacement at the time of the exchange but expect to separate later. The partnership can exchange into a particular replacement, or set of replacements, together, then divide interests later. For that group, a partnership-level exchange can be a natural fit.

It can also fit when a drop-and-swap's pre-sale timing is infeasible. If the sale is imminent and there was not enough time to make a pre-sale distribution and establish a meaningful partner holding period, a last-minute drop can create its own risk. The partnership may instead exchange as a unit and plan a later distribution after a defensible post-exchange holding period.

Some partnerships and advisers also prefer the clean exchange itself. The complexity is shifted from a pre-sale distribution to a deliberately planned post-exchange distribution. That does not remove the risk; it changes where the record needs to be strongest. The fit is situation-specific and should be evaluated against the partnership's facts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a swap-and-drop 1031 exchange?

It is a technique in which the partnership first sells relinquished property and acquires replacement property in a 1031 exchange at the entity level. It then distributes interests in the replacement to its partners. It is the reverse of drop-and-swap and keeps the partnership as taxpayer through the exchange before partners separate.

### How is swap-and-drop different from drop-and-swap?

The difference is the timing of the distribution. Drop-and-swap distributes property before sale, letting partners exchange individually. Swap-and-drop has the partnership exchange first and distribute afterward. Drop before versus drop after the exchange is the essential distinction.

### Which technique lets partners choose different replacements?

Drop-and-swap. Because partners hold direct interests before the sale, each can pursue an individual replacement or cash out. Swap-and-drop keeps them in a shared partnership replacement through the exchange, so it provides less flexibility for entirely different replacements at that time.

### What is the timing concern with swap-and-drop?

It is the post-exchange holding period. After acquiring the replacement, the partnership should hold it for a meaningful period before distribution. A distribution too soon can prompt an IRS argument that the partnership did not genuinely hold the property for investment or that the distribution was prearranged. There is no bright-line period; more time can strengthen the position.

### Where is the held-for-investment concern located in each technique?

For drop-and-swap, it is before sale: whether partners held distributed interests long enough before selling. For swap-and-drop, it is after the exchange: whether the partnership held replacement property long enough before distribution. Both need adequate holding periods to be defensible.

### Does swap-and-drop attract IRS scrutiny?

Yes. The IRS can challenge a post-exchange distribution as prearranged and question whether the partnership truly held the replacement for investment. Documentation of genuine holding, meaningful timing, and a later separate decision can mitigate the concern, but both techniques carry real risk and need experienced counsel.

### How do I document a swap-and-drop?

Document that the partnership held replacement property for investment after the exchange, not merely before a planned distribution. Preserve the timing between exchange and distribution and the facts showing that the later distribution was a separate, genuine decision. The exchange is clean at the partnership level; the record should focus on the post-exchange holding and distribution.

### What's the advantage of swap-and-drop over drop-and-swap?

The exchange itself is a standard partnership exchange without a pre-sale drop to scrutinize. That can avoid drop-and-swap's pre-sale timing complexity. The trade-off is that partners must stay together through the exchange and separate later, giving up some flexibility for different replacements at sale.

### How do I choose between the two techniques?

Consider partner goals, whether partners need different or shared replacements, which holding period is easier to support, the relative risks, and the timeline. Experienced tax counsel should assess the facts. Neither approach is always best.

### Can the partnership use a DST in a swap-and-drop?

Yes. A partnership can exchange into a DST or another replacement property at the entity level, then distribute interests later. DSTs can also be individual partners' replacements in a drop-and-swap. The structure and any offering must be reviewed for suitability with the appropriate professionals.

### Is swap-and-drop a do-it-yourself technique?

No. It has real IRS-scrutiny, holding-period, documentation, and substance risks. Experienced tax counsel and a CPA should guide the structure. A partnership should not attempt it without professional guidance.

### When should I plan a partnership exchange separation?

Well before the exchange. Selecting a technique and establishing adequate timing and documentation require early planning. Both approaches require patience, whether the relevant holding period comes before sale or after the exchange.

### When does swap-and-drop fit better than drop-and-swap?

It can fit when partners agree on the replacement at exchange but expect to separate later; when an imminent sale made a pre-sale drop infeasible; or when the partnership prefers a clean partnership-level exchange with a deliberately planned later distribution.

### Can swap-and-drop handle partners who want different replacements?

Less easily. The partnership exchanges as a unit into a shared replacement, and partners separate afterward. If partners need different replacement properties at the time of sale, drop-and-swap is generally the better fit.

### Why might advisers prefer swap-and-drop's clean exchange?

The exchange is a standard partnership transaction with no pre-sale drop to scrutinize. The complexity moves to the post-exchange distribution, where it can be planned and documented deliberately. The trade-off is that partners remain together through the exchange. The preference depends on the facts and the adviser's judgment.

## Glossary

**Swap-and-Drop:** A technique in which the partnership exchanges first and distributes interests to partners afterward.

**Drop-and-Swap:** The alternative that distributes property to partners before sale so each exchanges individually.

**Swap:** The 1031 exchange, done first at the partnership level in a swap-and-drop.

**Drop:** The distribution of interests to partners after the exchange in a swap-and-drop.

**Entity-Level Exchange:** An exchange completed by the partnership itself.

**Tenant-in-Common (TIC) Interest:** A direct fractional interest that can be distributed to partners in the drop step.

**Held for Investment:** The requirement that the partnership hold replacement property for investment after the exchange.

**Post-Exchange Holding:** The period the partnership holds replacement property before distribution; it is central to swap-and-drop defensibility.

**Same-Taxpayer Rule:** The requirement that the taxpayer selling relinquished property acquire replacement property.

**Partnership Exchange Problem:** The separation difficulty created because a partnership is the single taxpayer.

**Substance Over Form:** The principle the IRS may use to challenge a prearranged post-exchange distribution.

**IRS Scrutiny:** IRS attention to the timing and substance concerns in both techniques.

**Holding Period:** The time an asset is held; in swap-and-drop, post-exchange holding supports investment characterization.

**Tax Counsel:** The experienced attorney who structures a defensible swap-and-drop.

**Replacement Property:** Property the partnership acquires in the swap and may later distribute in the drop.

**Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** A possible replacement property at the partnership level in swap-and-drop or at the partner level in drop-and-swap.
