# 721 Exchange Minimum Investment & Suitability


[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read

I keep seeing owners treat the size of their property as the whole eligibility test. It is not. With a 721 exchange, the harder work often begins after the equity value gets someone’s attention.

Because a 721 exchange involves securities in the form of OP units, it commonly calls for accredited-investor status, a suitability review, and minimum investment amounts in the underlying offerings. That includes [DSTs in the bridge path](can-you-1031-then-721-dst-bridge-strategy.html) and REIT interests. This guide covers the accreditation requirement, the review, the minimums, what suitability assesses, and who qualifies.

The first-order view is that an owner with substantial appreciated real estate will probably clear the financial threshold. The second-order view asks whether that owner can bear illiquidity, a one-way structure, market and valuation risk, and dependence on the REIT’s performance. The second question is what makes the review useful. This is educational information, not tax, legal, or investment advice.

## Accredited investor requirement

[721 exchanges](721-exchange-guide.html) commonly involve securities: [OP units](op-units-explained.html), plus DST or REIT offerings in the usual paths. Those offerings are generally made to [accredited investors](accredited-investor-requirements.html). An accredited investor meets income or net-worth thresholds established in securities regulations.

Under the current SEC Rule 501(a) standard, as of 2026 and subject to future amendment, an individual generally qualifies with income over $200,000 in each of the two most recent years, or $300,000 jointly, or net worth over $1 million excluding a primary residence. Certain professional certifications can also qualify. DST and REIT interests in these paths are typically available only to accredited investors.

That makes accreditation a gate, not a marketing label. The offerings may be private or non-traded and are limited to investors presumed to have the resources and sophistication to evaluate and bear the risks. If you are not accredited, a DST or securitized REIT route into a 721 exchange will generally not be available. A financial professional verifies the status early.

## The suitability review

Accreditation is necessary in the typical securities path, but it does not answer every question. A suitability review is the financial professional’s assessment, through the broker-dealer, of whether the 721 exchange and the specific offering are appropriate for the investor. Securities can be recommended only when suitable.

The review considers financial situation, investment objectives, risk tolerance, time horizon, liquidity needs, other holdings, and the characteristics of the strategy. It is a substantive assessment, not a checkbox. Its focus includes illiquidity, the one-way nature of the structure, and the underlying risks. An advisor is required to decline a recommendation if the facts do not fit.

That is investor protection in practical form: a determination that a 721 exchange is not appropriate can prevent an unsuitable recommendation.

## Minimum investment amounts

The DST and REIT securities involved in a 721 exchange generally have [minimum investment amounts](dst-minimum-investment-and-accreditation-requirements.html). Each offering sets its own amount, often at a level appropriate for accredited investors and sometimes in the tens of thousands of dollars or more.

For someone exchanging appreciated property worth seven figures, the minimum is rarely the binding constraint; the exchange amount commonly exceeds it. The point is to access an offering in meaningful amounts, not to exclude the typical exchanger. Specific minimums still depend on the DST or REIT being considered, and the financial professional can provide them.

## What suitability assesses

The review starts with the investor’s financial situation: income, net worth, assets, and the overall picture. It then considers objectives such as income, growth, diversification, and estate planning. Those facts help determine whether a 721 exchange serves the stated purpose.

Risk tolerance means both ability and willingness to bear illiquidity, the one-way structure, market and valuation risk, and reliance on the REIT’s performance. Time horizon and liquidity needs matter because this is a long-term, illiquid holding; the assessment needs to confirm that the investor does not need cash the investment cannot provide. Other holdings complete the picture.

## Who qualifies

So [who qualifies for a 721 exchange](who-should-consider-a-721-exchange-investor-profiles.html) through the usual securities paths? Accredited investors for whom the strategy is also suitable. Both tests must be met: the accreditation threshold and the suitability fit.

Owners considering a 721 exchange often have substantial appreciated real estate, so their income and equity may clear the accreditation threshold. Investors whose goals include passivity, diversification, or estate planning and who can live with the risks may also pass the review. But qualification is never automatic. Some owners do not meet the accreditation standards; others need liquidity the investment cannot provide or have goals or risk limits that do not fit.

### Key Takeaways

- A 721 exchange involves securities, so it typically requires accredited-investor status based on income or net-worth thresholds.
- A financial professional’s suitability review assesses the investor’s situation, objectives, risk tolerance, and needs.
- Underlying offerings have minimum investments; they are generally not a barrier for typical 721 exchangers with substantial property values.
- Qualification requires both accreditation and suitability. Many candidates qualify, but not all do.

## Preparing for the suitability process

Preparation makes the process more orderly. To verify accreditation, investors usually provide income or net-worth documentation, such as tax returns, W-2s, financial statements, or third-party verification. The financial professional will identify the documentation required for the particular offering.

For the review, be ready to discuss financial circumstances, investment objectives, risk tolerance, time horizon, liquidity needs, and other holdings. Clear reasons for considering a 721 exchange and candid facts about the situation allow the professional to assess fit accurately.

Working with a knowledgeable financial professional through a broker-dealer is the practical step. That professional handles verification and the review, and can keep the exchange on track if it is suitable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to be an accredited investor for a 721 exchange?

Almost always. Sponsors generally limit these private securities offerings to accredited investors. An owner with appreciated investment property and meaningful equity may already qualify, but verification is the first step.

### What is the suitability review?

It is the broker-dealer process in which a financial professional determines whether the 721 exchange and the particular offering are appropriate. The review examines financial situation, objectives, risk tolerance, time horizon, liquidity needs, and other holdings. It is substantive rather than ceremonial and protects investors from unsuitable recommendations.

### What does the suitability review assess?

It assesses income, net worth, assets, investment objectives such as income, growth, diversification, and estate planning, the ability and willingness to bear illiquidity, the one-way nature, market and valuation risk, time horizon, liquidity needs, and other holdings. The purpose is a complete assessment of appropriateness before the strategy is recommended.

### Is there a minimum investment for a 721 exchange?

Yes. DST and REIT offerings have minimums that vary by offering and can be tens of thousands of dollars or more. For most exchangers with substantial appreciated property, the exchange amount exceeds the minimum. A financial professional can identify the minimum for each offering under consideration.

### Who qualifies for a 721 exchange?

The usual securities paths require an accredited investor for whom the strategy is suitable. Many owners with substantial appreciated property meet the financial thresholds and have objectives aligned with passivity, diversification, or estate planning, but some do not meet accreditation or do not fit the strategy. Both accreditation and suitability are required.

### How do I prove I’m an accredited investor?

Proof usually consists of income or net-worth records: tax returns, W-2s, financial statements, or statements showing assets and liabilities excluding the primary residence. A CPA, attorney, or verification service may provide third-party verification. The financial professional specifies the documentation required and confirms the applicable threshold before access to the offering.

### What if I’m not an accredited investor?

The typical 721 routes through DSTs and securitized REIT offerings may not be available. A standard 1031 exchange into property bought directly, which does not involve securities, may be an alternative. Discuss alternatives with an advisor; accreditation is a gate for the securities-based paths.

### Why is a suitability review required?

Securities regulations require a recommendation to be suitable for the investor. A securities-based 721 exchange can involve illiquidity, a one-way structure, market and valuation risk, and REIT dependence, which do not fit every investor. The review protects the investor by testing fit before a recommendation is made.

### Can I fail the suitability review?

Yes. If the investor needs liquidity the investment cannot provide, cannot bear the risks, or has goals that do not align, the review can find the strategy unsuitable and it should not be recommended. That outcome is a safeguard, not a defect.

### How do I prepare for the suitability process?

Gather income or net-worth records for accreditation and be prepared to discuss financial facts, objectives, risk tolerance, time horizon, liquidity needs, and other holdings openly. Clarity about why the 721 exchange is being considered and candor about circumstances help the professional determine fit.

### How does Baker 1031 help with these requirements?

Baker 1031 helps with accreditation verification, the suitability review, minimum-investment explanations, and the surrounding requirements. REIT units, DST interests, and related securities are offered through Aurora Securities, member FINRA/SIPC, and any recommendation follows a suitability review. The strategy is recommended only when it fits the investor’s circumstances and goals.

### How do I know if I meet the accredited-investor thresholds?

The definition includes income above the applicable recent-year threshold or a net-worth threshold excluding the primary residence, along with other qualifying criteria such as certain professional certifications. The rules can be checked against current sources or with a financial professional. Owners of substantial appreciated real estate often meet the net-worth test, but the professional confirms the result.

### Does the suitability requirement protect me?

Yes. It is an investor-protection measure that helps prevent an illiquid, risky, or misaligned strategy from being recommended to the wrong investor. A capable financial professional uses it as a genuine fit test and should recommend against the exchange when it is not appropriate.

### Are there ongoing requirements after the 721 exchange?

Accreditation and suitability primarily apply at entry to the securities offering. Afterward, the investor holds OP units and may later hold REIT shares. Ongoing considerations include K-1 tax reporting, holding and conversion decisions, and monitoring the investment rather than recurring accreditation or suitability reviews. A later DST or REIT securities investment has its own review.

### Does the minimum investment apply to my whole exchange or per offering?

It typically applies per offering. Each DST or REIT has its own minimum. If an exchange is divided among multiple offerings for diversification, each allocation must meet its offering’s minimum. For typical exchangers with substantial property values, meeting several minimums is generally not a problem; an advisor helps allocate the exchange accordingly.

### Can a trust or entity be the accredited investor?

Yes, under applicable criteria. A trust may qualify with sufficient assets and a sophisticated trustee, and an entity may qualify with sufficient assets or when it is owned entirely by accredited investors, among other conditions. A financial professional and attorney should confirm the entity’s qualification and the same-taxpayer-type considerations for the exchange.

## Glossary

- **Accredited Investor:** An investor meeting income or net-worth thresholds for securities offerings.
- **Suitability Review:** The assessment of whether the 721 exchange fits the investor.
- **Securities:** The OP units, DSTs, and REIT interests involved in a 721 exchange.
- **Minimum Investment:** The offering’s minimum investment amount.
- **Income Threshold:** An accreditation criterion based on recent income.
- **Net-Worth Threshold:** An accreditation criterion based on net worth excluding a primary residence.
- **Broker-Dealer:** The firm through which securities and the suitability review occur.
- **Investment Objectives:** The goals assessed in the suitability review.
- **Risk Tolerance:** The investor’s capacity to bear risk, assessed in the review.
- **Liquidity Needs:** The investor’s need for liquidity, assessed against the investment’s illiquidity.
- **Time Horizon:** The investor’s investment timeframe, assessed in the review.
- **Accreditation Verification:** Confirmation that the investor meets accredited-investor thresholds.
- **Appropriateness:** Whether the strategy fits the investor; the suitability determination.
- **Financial Professional:** The advisor conducting the accreditation and suitability process.
- **Qualification:** Meeting both accreditation and suitability for a 721 exchange.
- **Documentation:** The records that verify accreditation and inform suitability.
