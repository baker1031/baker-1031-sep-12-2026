# 721 Exchange Closing Documents Explained

That is when the broad idea of a 721 exchange becomes a set of long-term rights, restrictions, obligations, and tax protections. The documents are not a closing formality. They are the investment after closing.

## The key documents overview

A [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html) typically relies on four core agreements. The contribution agreement transfers property to the operating partnership for [OP units](op-units-explained.html). The tax protection agreement addresses the investor’s deferred gain. The partnership agreement governs the operating partnership and the investor’s rights as a unit holder. A registration rights agreement, often used for a publicly traded REIT, can govern conversion of units to shares and registration of those shares for sale.

Together, these agreements describe what the investor contributes and receives, how deferred gain is protected, the rights attached to units, and the path to conversion or liquidity. They create the full framework for the exchange. One document cannot answer for another: economics sit principally in the contribution agreement, tax protection in its own agreement, ownership rights in the partnership agreement, and share-sale rights in registration rights.

Because the 721 exchange is generally permanent, the agreements deserve the same attention as the real estate itself. They govern the investment for the long term, rather than merely recording a completed transaction.

## The contribution agreement

The contribution agreement is the foundation of the 721 exchange. It effects the contribution of property to the operating partnership in exchange for OP units. It identifies the [property that qualifies for the contribution](721-exchange-eligibility-what-property-qualifies.html), establishes the agreed value, sets the number of units received, and describes the transfer mechanics under Section 721.

It also usually includes representations and warranties about the property and parties, closing conditions, and other transaction terms. In practical terms, this is the primary contract for what the investor gives and what the investor gets. Value and units received are the core economic outcome.

The review should confirm that value, unit count, transfer terms, representations, and conditions are the agreed terms. The contribution agreement is the heart of the deal: it defines the property value, OP units, transfer mechanics, and the economic result of the exchange.

## The tax protection agreement

The tax protection agreement protects deferred or built-in gain against a premature trigger. If the operating partnership later sells contributed property in a taxable way, the investor’s deferred gain could be recognized. The agreement addresses that exposure by having the REIT or partnership protect the investor, typically through a covenant not to make a taxable sale for a period or through compensation if a triggering sale occurs.

The details determine the strength of the protection. Important terms include the protected period, the form of protection, a covenant not to sell, a make-whole payment, debt maintenance, and the specific conditions under which protection applies. A longer protected period and a stronger form of commitment reduce the owner's exposure. A weak agreement leaves greater exposure.

This agreement should be reviewed and negotiated with the attorney because it directly affects the security of tax deferral. It is not enough to know that a tax protection agreement exists. The investor needs to understand how long protection lasts, what the partnership must do, and what remedies follow a triggering sale.

## The partnership agreement

The partnership agreement is the operating partnership’s governing document. It defines the investor’s rights and obligations as an OP unit holder, generally a limited partner. It addresses management of the partnership, usually by the REIT as general partner; distributions; conversion rights from units to shares; governance rights; and other terms of ongoing ownership.

Once the investor receives OP units, the investor is subject to this agreement. It determines the terms of distributions, the availability and timing of conversion, the investor’s limited governance rights, and the operation of the partnership. It is the contract governing the ownership after the contribution agreement has done its work.

Review distribution provisions—how distributions are determined and paid—alongside conversion provisions, including when units can be converted to shares and any lock-up. The investor should also understand the limited partner protections and the scope of the general partner’s authority. These are the provisions that define what OP units actually entitle the holder to receive and do.

## The registration rights agreement

A registration rights agreement is common where the exchange involves a publicly traded REIT. After conversion of OP units to REIT shares, the shares may need to be registered with the SEC before they can be sold freely in the market. The agreement gives the investor rights to have converted shares registered and therefore can enable their sale.

That affects liquidity. Without registration rights, converted shares may be restricted and harder to sell. With registration rights, the investor has a path to registration and free sale, subject to the agreement’s terms. The key terms determine when and how the investor can demand or participate in registration.

For a non-traded REIT, liquidity often operates through a redemption program rather than the sale of registered public shares, so registration rights can be less central. For a traded-REIT 721 exchange, however, registration rights are a meaningful part of the eventual liquidity path.

## Key takeaways

- A 721 exchange commonly involves a contribution agreement, tax protection agreement, partnership agreement, and registration rights agreement.
- The contribution agreement effects the property-for-units exchange and sets value, units, and transfer terms.
- The tax protection agreement helps protect deferred gain from a premature trigger and is a central document to negotiate.
- The partnership agreement governs unit-holder rights; registration rights can support sale of converted shares.

## Reviewing the documents

Given the importance of these agreements, review them with an attorney before [the closing stage of the 721 exchange](insights.html). They define the investor’s ownership, rights, tax protections, and liquidity for the long term. An attorney can explain provisions, determine whether the documents reflect agreed terms, and advise on concerns or negotiation.

The review has four connected parts. First, economic terms: the contribution agreement’s property value and unit count. Second, tax protection: the duration and quality of protection. Third, ownership rights: distribution, conversion, and governance provisions in the partnership agreement. Fourth, liquidity: registration rights or redemption terms.

A 721 exchange is [generally a one-way door](insights.html). After the property is contributed and units are issued, unfavorable terms or inadequate protection can be difficult to correct. Thorough pre-closing review and negotiation are risk controls, not excess paperwork.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What documents are involved in a 721 exchange?

The main four are the contribution agreement, tax protection agreement, partnership agreement, and often a registration rights agreement. Together, they establish the property contribution and OP units, protection of deferred gain, rights as a unit holder, and potential conversion/share-sale liquidity. Review them with an attorney before closing.

### What is the contribution agreement?

It is the foundational contract that contributes property to the operating partnership for OP units. It sets the property, agreed value, unit count, and transfer mechanics, along with representations, warranties, and closing conditions. It is the primary transaction document and establishes the core economics of what the investor gives and receives.

### What is the tax protection agreement?

It protects deferred gain from being triggered prematurely if the operating partnership sells the contributed property in a taxable way. The REIT or partnership may promise not to make a taxable sale for a period or may compensate the investor if a triggering sale occurs. Its protected period and form of protection determine the strength of the deferral protection, so it should be reviewed and negotiated with counsel.

### What does the partnership agreement govern?

It governs the operating partnership and the investor’s rights as a limited partner: management, usually by the REIT as general partner; distributions; conversion rights and lock-up; governance rights; and other ownership terms. An OP unit holder is subject to it, so it defines distributions, conversion, and the ongoing role as a partner.

### What is a registration rights agreement?

It is common for traded-REIT exchanges and governs the ability to have converted REIT shares registered for sale. OP units may convert into shares that need SEC registration for free market sale. The agreement can provide rights to registration and affect when and how liquidity is available. For a non-traded REIT, redemption programs often matter more than registration rights.

### Why are the closing documents important?

They define long-term ownership, rights, protection, and liquidity in a generally irreversible exchange. The contribution agreement sets economics; the tax protection agreement protects deferral; the partnership agreement defines unit-holder rights; and registration rights can support liquidity. They are the governing terms of the investment, not formalities.

### Should I have an attorney review the documents?

Yes. The agreements define ownership, rights, protection, and liquidity, and a 721 exchange is generally irreversible. An attorney can confirm agreed terms, explain legal and tax provisions, and negotiate concerns before closing. Legal review is particularly important for tax protection and partnership terms.

### What should I focus on in reviewing the documents?

Review the contribution agreement’s property value and units; the tax protection agreement’s protected period and strength; partnership distribution, conversion, and governance rights; and registration rights or redemption terms that define the liquidity path. Those are the economic, tax, ownership, and liquidity considerations.

### Can I negotiate the documents?

Some terms can be negotiated. Property value in the contribution agreement and the protected period or protections in the tax protection agreement commonly matter most. Partnership and registration-rights agreements may be more standardized, especially at established REITs, but they still warrant full understanding and discussion with counsel.

### What happens if there's an issue with the documents after closing?

It may be hard to fix. The investor has contributed property and accepted units under the agreements, and the transaction is generally irreversible. That is why problems such as unfavorable terms or inadequate protection should be identified and addressed through review and negotiation before closing.

### Are there other documents beyond the main four?

Possibly. A full closing package can include deed or property-transfer documents, subscription documents for units, lender or other consents, disclosure materials from the REIT, and closing certificates. The four main agreements are the core, but the attorney and advisors should review the complete package.

### Should I keep copies of the closing documents?

Yes. Keep complete copies of the contribution agreement, tax protection agreement, partnership agreement, registration rights agreement, and supporting documents for the long term, including digital backups. They establish ownership, rights, protection, tax basis, conversion choices, and estate-transfer information that may remain relevant for years.

### How long does the document process take?

It varies. Drafting, reviewing, and negotiating can take weeks, especially where property value, tax protection, debt, or other structural issues are complex. The document process follows diligence and valuation and leads to closing. Counsel manages drafting and review, and the pace depends on the transaction and negotiation.

## Glossary

### Contribution Agreement

The document effecting the contribution of property for OP units.

### Tax Protection Agreement

The document protecting deferred gain from a premature trigger.

### Partnership Agreement

The document governing rights as a unit holder.

### Registration Rights Agreement

The document supporting registration of converted shares for sale.

### Property Value

The agreed value in the contribution agreement that sets the number of units.

### Built-In Gain

The deferred gain protected by the tax protection agreement.

### Protected Period

How long tax protection lasts.

### Make-Whole Payment

A form of tax protection compensating for a triggered gain.

### Distribution Provisions

The partnership agreement’s terms governing distributions.

### Conversion Provisions

The partnership agreement’s terms for converting units to shares.

### Limited Partner

The investor’s role as a unit holder under the partnership agreement.

### General Partner

The REIT that manages the partnership.

### Representations and Warranties

The assurances made in the contribution agreement.

### Closing Conditions

The conditions that must be met for the exchange to close.

### SEC Registration

Registration of converted shares for free sale through registration rights.

### Legal Review

The attorney’s examination of the documents before closing.
