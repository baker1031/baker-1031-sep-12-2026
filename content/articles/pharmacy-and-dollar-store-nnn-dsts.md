# Pharmacy & Dollar-Store NNN DSTs

*Delaware Statutory Trusts · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read*

I keep noticing how quickly “defensive retail” becomes shorthand for “safe retail.” It comes up whenever someone sees a pharmacy or dollar-store lease: prescriptions and everyday goods feel less exposed to a weak economy, and a long lease can make the income look comfortably fixed.

The first-order view is simple: one needs-based tenant, one long lease, and a stream of rent with very little landlord work. The second-order reality is equally simple, but more important: one tenant can also mean one point of failure. If that tenant stops paying, closes the store, or declines to renew, the income can fall to zero.

A pharmacy or dollar-store NNN DST is a Delaware Statutory Trust that owns a building leased to one retail tenant on a triple-net, or NNN, basis. The tenant pays property taxes, insurance, and maintenance in addition to rent. Under IRS Revenue Ruling 2004-86, fractional beneficial interests in a qualifying DST are treated as like-kind real property for a 1031 exchange. That can make the structure a passive replacement option for an exchanger, but it does not turn projected distributions into guaranteed income or remove the risks of an illiquid, fee-bearing security. Baker 1031 does not provide tax or legal advice.

For the broader structure, see the [in-depth DST pillar guide](dst-guide.html).

## Single-Tenant Net-Lease Appeal

In a [triple-net (NNN) lease](net-lease-nnn-dsts-explained.html), the tenant covers taxes, insurance, and maintenance along with rent. The DST therefore does not bear those operating costs or the accompanying day-to-day management burden. One tenant and one lease can be a clean, largely passive ownership arrangement, which is why it appeals to [1031 exchangers seeking truly passive ownership](1031-exchange-into-a-dst-passive-option.html).

A long lease to a creditworthy tenant can produce contractual rent for years. Scheduled escalations can add modest growth. That creates a cash-flow profile often called bond-like: predictable rent, few property-level landlord duties, and income that is easier to project than income from a property with multiple changing leases and variable operating expenses.

That comparison has limits. “Bond-like” describes a cash-flow pattern, not a government guarantee or a promise of principal. The rent still depends on a single corporate tenant performing under a single lease, and the DST interest is not a readily tradable bond.

## Pharmacy & Dollar-Store Tenants

Pharmacies sell prescriptions, health products, and convenience items. Prescriptions in particular are generally non-discretionary and tied to healthcare needs rather than the usual business cycle. Pharmacy chains have historically operated thousands of locations, often under long net leases, so they are familiar single-tenant categories.

Dollar stores serve a different part of retail. They sell household essentials, consumables, and low-cost goods. Demand for value-oriented retail can hold up, and may rise, when household budgets are tight. That gives the category a counter-cyclical feature and has supported large national store footprints, often in convenient locations and under long net leases.

Those traits explain the interest in both categories: needs-based demand can support the tenant’s ability to continue paying rent through different economic conditions. Still, the business model is the appeal, not a guarantee from any particular company. A healthy category does not ensure that a specific chain, guarantor, or store will remain healthy.

## Lease Length & Rent Bumps

Single-tenant net-lease retail commonly begins with a long lease term, often a decade or more, and may include renewal options that extend occupancy. The original term matters, but the remaining committed term matters more to an investor deciding how much contractual income is still in place. A longer remaining term can reduce near-term re-leasing risk and make the income feel more fixed.

Rent bumps are contractual increases in rent. They can take the form of a fixed percentage increase every few years, annual escalations, or a step-up when the tenant exercises a renewal option. They can help rent keep pace with inflation and can affect both the property’s income and its value. The schedule—how large the increase is and how often it occurs—can materially shape the return profile and resale appeal.

The useful discipline is to read the actual lease rather than assume that “long-term” or “NNN” answers the question. A long term with no escalations has different economics from a long term with recurring increases, and renewal options are not the same as committed years.

## Key Takeaways

- A single-tenant NNN DST can provide a passive, bond-like income profile because the tenant pays taxes, insurance, and maintenance in addition to rent.
- Pharmacy and dollar-store categories are favored for needs-based, recession-resilient demand, but category strength is not a company guarantee.
- Long leases and periodic rent bumps can lock in contractual income and increase it gradually over time.
- Single-tenant concentration is the defining risk. Tenant credit, lease term, escalations, location, and store performance all deserve close review.

## Single-Tenant Concentration Risk

All the income in a single-tenant property comes from one tenant. If that tenant fails to pay, goes dark, or does not renew at lease end, the building can move from full occupancy to full vacancy and income can drop to zero until the space is re-leased or the property is sold. A multi-tenant center has other rents to cushion one departure. A single-tenant property does not.

Tenant-credit risk follows directly. A downgrade, financial distress, bankruptcy, or a weak guarantor can jeopardize the rent. Retail-disruption risk matters as well. Consolidation, competition, changing shopping behavior, online pharmacy, e-commerce, and store-closure programs can affect a needs-based chain and an individual location. A pharmacy or dollar store can have useful demand characteristics and still close a particular store.

The DST structure adds its own conditions. DST interests are generally illiquid, carry fees, are offered only to accredited investors after suitability review, have limited active investor management, and do not guarantee distributions. The analysis should begin with the fact that this is a concentrated exposure inside a longer-term private-placement structure.

## Evaluating the Offering

Start with the tenant’s credit because the full income stream depends on it. Review the financial condition and credit rating of the tenant and, where relevant, the parent company standing behind the lease. An investment-grade-quality tenant is generally more likely to honor its obligations than a weaker or unrated tenant, but no rating or guarantee eliminates risk. It matters whether the lease is backed by a parent, a subsidiary, a franchisee, or another entity.

Then review the lease and the store. How many firm years remain? What renewal options exist? When do rent bumps occur, and at what rate? What do the store’s sales and profitability suggest about closure risk? Location and local demographics affect both the operating store and the building’s possible re-leasing or resale value if the tenant leaves.

The DST itself requires equal attention: sponsor record, fees, projected hold period, projected distributions, property-level debt, and any non-recourse financing. Projected distributions are not guaranteed. A projected hold is not a certainty. The aim is not to find a label that makes the asset safe; it is to understand the lease, the guarantor, and the structure that will determine the outcome.

## Net-Lease DSTs in a 1031 Exchange

After an appreciated investment property is sold, a 1031 exchanger generally has **45 days** to identify replacement property and **180 days** to close. A [qualified intermediary](what-is-a-qualified-intermediary.html) must be used so the exchanger does not receive the proceeds. A pharmacy or dollar-store DST can often be identified and closed quickly because the real estate and financing are already inside an existing offering. Its single lease can also make the economics relatively straightforward to describe on the [45-day identification](1031-exchange-identification-rules.html) list.

Replacement value and debt still matter. A leveraged NNN DST may use non-recourse financing to help replace debt from the relinquished property, supporting full deferral when the exchanger needs debt replacement. A debt-free DST can make sense when there is no relinquished debt to replace, but taking cash or failing the debt-and-value analysis can create mortgage [boot](what-is-boot-1031-exchange-how-to-avoid.html).

Concentration can be addressed at the portfolio level, though not erased. An exchanger may use a pharmacy or dollar-store DST as one piece of a diversified replacement allocation, along with net-lease DSTs leased to different tenant categories or [DSTs in other property sectors](single-family-rental-and-build-to-rent-dsts.html). Spreading exposure across tenants, sectors, sponsors, and geographies can reduce reliance on one store, one tenant, or one market while keeping capital in a 1031 framework. The total value and debt-replacement requirements still have to be met, and an advisor and CPA should confirm the facts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a pharmacy or dollar-store NNN DST?

It is a Delaware Statutory Trust that owns a building leased to one pharmacy or dollar-store operator on a triple-net basis. The tenant pays rent plus property taxes, insurance, and maintenance. Investors own fractional beneficial interests, which Revenue Ruling 2004-86 treats as like-kind real property for a 1031 exchange. That can provide passive, income-producing real-estate ownership and capital-gain deferral when the exchange is properly structured. The appeal is long-lease, needs-based, bond-like income; the central risk is that one tenant’s vacancy can mean no income. It is an illiquid, fee-bearing security for accredited investors after suitability review, and distributions are never guaranteed.

### What does triple-net (NNN) mean?

NNN means the tenant is responsible for real-estate taxes, building insurance, and maintenance in addition to rent. In a single-tenant DST, that shifts variable operating costs and much of the management burden away from the landlord. A gross lease instead leaves operating costs with the landlord; single- and double-net arrangements put only some expenses on the tenant. NNN can make net income easier to project, but the actual lease controls and “net lease” terms can vary, so confirm the offering’s exact structure.

### Why are pharmacies and dollar stores favored as net-lease tenants?

They sell necessities. Pharmacies serve prescriptions, health products, and convenience needs; dollar stores sell everyday household goods at value-oriented prices. Prescription demand is often tied to healthcare rather than the business cycle, and value retail can hold up or rise when budgets tighten. Large chains have operated many locations under long net leases. The favorable traits describe the categories, not a guarantee that an individual chain or store will perform.

### How long are the leases on these properties?

Initial terms are often a decade or more and may have renewal options. The remaining firm term, not just the original term, is important because it tells the investor how long contractual rent is committed. Leases commonly include scheduled escalations, such as fixed increases every few years or at renewal. Review both the remaining years and the precise escalation and option terms in the offering documents.

### Are these DSTs 1031-eligible?

Generally, qualifying DST interests can be used as replacement property because Revenue Ruling 2004-86 treats the beneficial interest as like-kind real property. A proper exchange can defer capital-gains and depreciation-recapture tax. A leveraged DST’s non-recourse financing may help replace relinquished-property debt. The exchanger must still use a QI, identify within **45 days**, close within **180 days**, and meet the other 1031 requirements. Confirm the tax treatment and timing with a tax advisor.

### What is single-tenant concentration risk?

It is the risk that all income depends on one tenant and one lease. If that tenant stops paying, closes the store, or declines renewal, the building can become fully vacant and income can fall to zero until it is re-leased or sold. There is no multi-tenant diversification cushion. The risk is compounded by credit deterioration, distress or bankruptcy, chain consolidation, competition, and retail-disruption trends.

### Are pharmacy and dollar-store DSTs really safe because the categories are defensive?

No. Needs-based categories can reduce some demand risk, but a DST owns one specific property leased to one specific tenant. The tenant’s credit can weaken; a store can underperform or close even when the chain remains healthy; online pharmacy, competition, consolidation, and nonrenewal can matter. The DST is also illiquid, fee-bearing, dependent on a sponsor, and has no guaranteed distributions. Defensive is not the same as risk-free.

### Why are these called bond-like investments?

The description refers to contractual rent from a long lease to a creditworthy tenant, with the tenant covering NNN expenses and periodic rent bumps providing modest scheduled growth. The pattern resembles a bond coupon. But it is not a U.S. Treasury: payment depends on a corporate tenant, there is default and vacancy risk, the interest is illiquid, the property can lose value, and return of principal is not guaranteed.

### What are rent bumps and why do they matter?

Rent bumps are contractual increases: a fixed percentage every few years, an annual escalation, or a step-up at renewal. They can prevent rent from remaining flat over a long lease, partly offset inflation, and influence income, resale value, and the return profile. Their frequency and size matter. Review the lease schedule because a property with limited or no escalations may have a flatter income path.

### How do I evaluate the tenant’s credit?

Review the entity actually responsible for the lease: its rating, financial strength, profitability, retail trajectory, and the quality of any guarantee. A stronger, investment-grade-quality tenant generally presents less payment risk than a weaker or non-rated tenant, but it remains a single-tenant exposure. A corporate-parent guarantee is different from a subsidiary or franchisee obligation. The category alone is not enough; examine the particular tenant and guarantor.

### How long do these DSTs typically last?

The projected hold is commonly about five to seven years, although the actual period depends on the sponsor’s business plan and market conditions. During the hold, the DST collects rent and may distribute net income, which is projected rather than guaranteed. At sale, investors receive a share of proceeds and may take cash and recognize gain, begin another 1031 exchange into a DST or other like-kind property, or use another available alternative. Remaining lease term at the time of sale can affect value and salability. There is little or no secondary market, so investors should plan to hold for the full period.

### Does a single-tenant net-lease DST use debt?

Some offerings are leveraged and others are debt-free. A leveraged DST has non-recourse mortgage debt that can help an exchanger replace relinquished debt; leverage can increase both return and risk. A debt-free DST avoids refinancing and interest-rate exposure and may have a lower-risk profile, but it does not replace old debt and may offer more modest projected returns. If the tenant leaves a leveraged property, debt service still remains. Match the structure to debt-replacement needs and assess how leverage compounds concentration risk.

### Who can invest in these DSTs?

They are generally Regulation D private-placement securities for accredited investors. Accredited status is commonly based on income above **$200,000** individually or **$300,000** jointly in each of the past two years with an expectation of the same, or net worth above **$1 million** excluding a primary residence, among other criteria. A broker-dealer performs a suitability review of finances, objectives, liquidity needs, and risk tolerance. DSTs are not publicly traded, can have minimum investments and a subscription process, and are intended for investors who can accept a longer illiquid hold.

### How can I diversify single-tenant concentration risk in a 1031 exchange?

An exchange can be split across several DSTs. A pharmacy or dollar-store NNN DST can be paired with other tenant categories or property sectors such as industrial, multifamily, medical, or grocery-anchored retail. Diversifying sponsors and geographies can add further risk spreading. If one store goes dark, only a portion of the replacement portfolio may be affected rather than all of it. Diversification cannot eliminate risk, and the allocation must still satisfy total replacement value and debt requirements; coordinate the mix with an advisor and CPA.

## Glossary

**Single-Tenant NNN DST:** A DST that holds a building leased to one tenant on a triple-net basis.

**Triple-Net (NNN) Lease:** A lease under which the tenant pays taxes, insurance, and maintenance.

**Needs-Based Tenant:** A tenant selling necessities such as prescriptions or everyday goods.

**Bond-Like Income:** Steady, predictable contractual rent that resembles a bond coupon, without a bond guarantee.

**Single-Tenant Concentration Risk:** The risk that one tenant’s vacancy ends all property income.

**Tenant Credit:** The financial strength of the tenant or guarantor backing the lease.

**Retail-Disruption Risk:** The risk that closure trends or changes in retail make a location unviable.

**Lease Term:** The remaining committed length of the tenant’s lease.

**Renewal Option:** The tenant’s right to extend the lease beyond its initial term.

**Rent Bump (Escalation):** A scheduled contractual increase in rent.

**Corporate Guarantee:** A parent company’s backing of a store’s lease obligation.

**Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** A trust that holds real estate in 1031-eligible fractional interests.

**Rev. Rul. 2004-86:** The IRS ruling treating qualifying DST interests as like-kind real property.

**Accredited Investor:** An investor who meets applicable income or net-worth thresholds for Regulation D offerings.

**Suitability Review:** A broker-dealer assessment of whether a DST fits the investor.

**Non-Recourse Debt:** Property-level DST mortgage debt that may help replace relinquished-property debt.
