# Property Types

[Back to Home](baker1031.html)

Baker 1031 Research &middot; Updated June 2026

I have been thinking this week about how a 1031 replacement can look straightforward on a spreadsheet and then become a very different investment once the tenant, lease, and operating demands come into view. The asset type is not a label. It is a set of trade-offs that needs to fit the investor’s income needs, time horizon, and tolerance for uncertainty.

First-order thinking treats like-kind property as interchangeable because it may qualify for a 1031 exchange. Second-order thinking asks what actually creates the cash flow, what can interrupt it, and how much management or illiquidity the owner is accepting. This directory is a starting point for that work.

## 1031 Replacement Property Types

A 1031 exchange or [DST](delaware-statutory-trusts.html) can be backed by very different real estate, and the asset type drives the income, the risks, and the work involved. Explore each property type we help accredited investors evaluate, from widely traded sectors like multifamily and net lease to specialized niches like data centers, marinas, and mineral royalties.

Replacement property for a 1031 exchange or DST falls into three broad groups: income-first sectors (multifamily, net lease, self-storage, industrial, healthcare and government-leased), operating-heavy assets (hotels, senior living, student housing, data centers and marinas), and land or resource interests (raw land plus oil, gas and mineral royalties) that produce little or no current income. All are like-kind for a 1031 exchange, but they carry very different income, risk and management profiles.

Low rents, month-to-month leases, and demand that holds up in a downturn give it a profile no other property type quite matches.</p><span class="read">Why self-storage holds up →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-industrial.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Industrial</h3><p>Industrial real estate rode the rise of e-commerce from a sleepy corner of the market to a favored asset. Long net leases to logistics tenants and tight supply near cities are why exchangers took notice.</p><span class="read">Inside industrial DSTs →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-small-bay-industrial.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Small-Bay Industrial</h3><p>Small-bay industrial is the unglamorous corner of the warehouse boom: compact units leased to dozens of local businesses, high rents per foot, and almost no new construction. It is also a newer DST niche, and worth understanding on its own terms rather than as a smaller version of big-box.</p><span class="read">Understand small-bay industrial →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-healthcare.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Healthcare &amp; Medical</h3><p>Healthcare real estate rents to doctors, surgery centers and clinics that are slow to move and aging demographics that keep coming. That mix is why this corner of the market can pay more than the core sectors and asks investors to underwrite a tenant they may not know.</p><span class="read">Review healthcare real estate →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-senior-living.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Senior Living</h3><p>Senior housing is supported by demographics that are already locked in, one of the more durable demand stories we track. It also sits on a spectrum from passive real estate to an operating business, and where a deal lands on that line changes everything about owning it.</p><span class="read">Look at senior living deals →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-student-housing.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Student Housing</h3><p>Student housing looks like apartments and earns like apartments, until you read the leases. By-the-bed terms, parental guarantees, and an August turn every year make it its own animal.</p><span class="read">How student housing differs →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-office.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Office</h3><p>Office is the one sector where we tell most exchangers to slow down. The headwinds are real, the pricing reflects them, and the gap between a building that works and one that does not has rarely been wider.</p><span class="read">Read our take on office →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-hospitality.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Hospitality &amp; Hotel</h3><p>A hotel earns more like a business than a building with a lease: it sells its rooms one night at a time. That reshapes everything about how it earns, how it swings, and how it fits a 1031 exchange.</p><span class="read">How hotels earn their yield →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-government-leased.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Government-Leased (GSA)</h3><p>A government-leased building collects rent from a tenant backed by the full faith and credit of the United States, so the credit risk most investors worry about is largely removed. The trade-off is rent that often escalates slowly, if at all, leases the government can decline to renew, and a DST interest that is not itself insured or guaranteed by any government entity.</p><span class="read">See government-leased (GSA) offerings →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-life-sciences.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Life Sciences</h3><p>Life science buildings are laboratories first and offices second. The specialized build-out makes tenants sticky and re-tenanting slow, and right now a supply wave has pushed vacancy up in several clusters.</p><span class="read">Explore life science space →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-data-centers.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Data Centers</h3><p>Data centers are the buildings the digital economy runs on. AI and cloud demand have made them one of the most sought-after property types, but power, capital, and obsolescence keep all but the largest investors out.</p><span class="read">What drives data center demand →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-marina.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Marina</h3><p>A marina is the rare real-estate asset that earns its keep two ways at once: as scarce waterfront land and as a small operating business. That dual nature is why it has tended to carry some of the higher yields among the property types we follow, though higher yields also reflect higher risk.</p><span class="read">How marinas earn income →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-land.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Land</h3><p>Almost every property type is bought for the rent. Land is the exception. It pays nothing while you hold it, and the entire case rests on what it might be worth when someone develops the area around it.</p><span class="read">The case for land →</span></div></a>

<a class="ptcard" href="property-type-oil-gas-royalties.html"><div class="cb"><span class="cat">Property Type</span><h3>Mineral &amp; Royalty Interests</h3><p>Mineral and royalty interests are one of the more misunderstood 1031 replacements. The yields draw attention, but the structure, a share of revenue with no costs and no control, is what an investor needs to understand first.</p><span class="read">How mineral royalties work →</span></div></a>

Every property type is like-kind for a 1031 exchange, but they are not interchangeable. Income-first sectors such as net lease and healthcare trade hands-on management for contractual rent. On the other end, operating-heavy assets like hotels and senior living carry <a href="due-diligence.html">more upside and more volatility</a> — and land, which produces no current income at all, is really a different kind of bet. We help match the asset to your goals, timeline, and tax picture, then point you to the <a href="investments.html">vetted offerings on our shelf</a>. We generally ask exchangers to slow down and underwrite these sectors carefully rather than reach for headline yield.</div></details>

<details><summary>Can raw land be a 1031 replacement property?</summary><div class="ans">Yes. Raw land is like-kind for a 1031 exchange, but it produces no current income while you hold it, so the case rests on future value rather than cash flow.</div></details>
