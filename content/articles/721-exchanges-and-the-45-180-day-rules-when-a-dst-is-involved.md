# 721 Exchanges and the 45/180-Day Rules When a DST Is Involved


[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read**

I keep seeing the same timing question around the DST bridge: does the 721 need to happen inside the familiar 45- and 180-day 1031 windows? No. But the distinction matters because one missed deadline in the first step can undo a plan that is intended to last for years.

First-order thinking sees a single “1031-then-721” strategy. Second-order thinking separates the two transactions. The **45/180-day** rules apply to the first step, a 1031 exchange into a DST; they do not apply to the second step, a 721 exit into a REIT. This guide explains where the deadlines apply, how to manage them, and why the later exit is open-ended.

## The 45/180 rules apply to the 1031 step

The core rule is simple: the **45/180-day deadlines apply to the 1031 step into the DST, not the 721 step into the REIT**. A [1031 exchange](1031-exchange-guide.html) into a DST is a like-kind exchange: the relinquished property and the DST interest are both real property for this purpose. It is therefore governed by 1031 rules, including [45-day identification](1031-exchange-identification-rules.html) and the 180-day completion deadline.

Within **45 days** after selling relinquished property, the investor must identify the DST, and any backups, in writing. Within **180 days**, the investor must close on the DST. A [qualified intermediary](what-is-a-qualified-intermediary.html) and normal identification rules are part of that first step. It is a standard 1031 exchange and is deadline-bound.

## The DST step has the deadlines

The **45-day identification** deadline requires written identification of the specific DST and the investor's interest within 45 days of selling the relinquished property. DSTs are commonly used for their speed and certainty; describing a specific DST interest is straightforward.

The **180-day completion** deadline requires closing on the DST within 180 days. Because DST offerings are pre-packaged and fast-closing, they can close in days. That can make the deadline more manageable than a direct-property acquisition, even though both deadlines still apply.

> The DST step has the 45/180-day deadlines, but the DST's speed and certainty can make them straightforward to meet. That is part of why DSTs are well suited to the bridge strategy.

## The 721 exit has no such deadlines

The second step, the 721 exit into the REIT, has no **45/180-day** deadlines. The REIT acquires the DST property and converts the investor's DST interest into [OP units](op-units-explained.html). That is a property contribution to a partnership under Section 721, not a like-kind exchange, so 1031 deadlines do not apply.

The timing is open-ended. The exit occurs when the REIT acquires the DST property under the DST/REIT structure and sponsor's plan. It can follow a planned timeline, such as a target number of years, or an option exercised at the sponsor's discretion. Unlike the DST step, it can occur years later and has no statutory 180-day clock.

## Why this matters

The deadline distinction matters first for planning the DST step. The investor must direct deadline management toward identifying the DST within 45 days and closing within 180 days.

Second, it sets expectations for the 721 exit. There is no fixed statutory timing. An investor should not wait for an “180-day” 721 exit; there is none. The exit occurs on the DST/REIT schedule, potentially years after the DST acquisition.

Third, it clarifies the strategy's time structure: a deadline-bound entry into the DST followed by an open-ended transition into the REIT. Knowing which stage carries time pressure prevents confusion and supports realistic planning.

## Managing the 1031 deadlines into the DST

The deadline-related work in the bridge strategy is managing the DST step as any 1031 exchange:

1. Engage a qualified intermediary before selling.
2. Select and identify the DST, along with any useful backups, in writing within **45 days**.
3. Close on the DST within **180 days**.

Best practice is to select the DST before or soon after selling, make sure it is structured for the intended 721 exit, use its fast closing to meet the 180-day window, and identify backup DSTs if helpful. Because DSTs close quickly and with certainty, the main task is selecting the right DST and identifying it on time; closing is generally reliable.

## Key Takeaways

- In the 1031-then-721 strategy, **45/180-day** deadlines apply to the first step, the 1031 into the DST, not to the later 721 exit.
- The DST step has standard 1031 deadlines, but its speed and certainty can make them straightforward to meet.
- The 721 exit is a partnership contribution rather than a like-kind exchange. It has no such deadlines and its timing is open-ended.
- Manage the DST step through the QI, 45-day identification, and 180-day closing; afterward, the 721 exit proceeds on the DST/REIT schedule.

## The open-ended 721 exit timing

After the deadline-bound DST step, the 721 exit follows the DST/REIT structure. It may use a planned target timeline or a sponsor option. The structure, not a statutory deadline, determines timing.

Before investing, understand the expected timing and certainty of the exit. Is there a planned exit timeline, or only an option? How certain is it? Since no deadline forces the REIT acquisition, the investor needs realistic expectations about how and when the REIT transition could occur. The DST step is deadline-bound and generally manageable; the exit is open-ended and dependent on structure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do the 45/180-day deadlines apply to a 721 exchange?

Not to a standalone 721 exchange, because it is a partnership contribution rather than a like-kind exchange. In the 1031-then-721 DST bridge, the deadlines apply to the first step, the 1031 into the DST. The 721 exit into the REIT has no such deadline and is open-ended.

### Which step has the deadlines in the 1031-then-721 strategy?

The first step: the 1031 into the DST. It is a like-kind exchange and must identify the DST within **45 days** of sale and close within **180 days**. The 721 exit is a partnership contribution, not a like-kind exchange, so it has no 45/180-day deadlines.

### Why doesn't the 721 exit have deadlines?

The exit is the REIT acquisition of DST property and conversion of the DST interest into OP units under Section 721. It is a partnership contribution rather than a 1031 exchange. The exit follows the DST/REIT structure and sponsor plans—planned timeline or option—and can happen years after the DST step.

### How do I meet the deadlines for the DST step?

Engage a qualified intermediary before sale, identify the DST and backups in writing within 45 days, and close on the DST within 180. DSTs are pre-packaged and can close in days, so selecting a DST structured for the 721 exit and identifying it within the window makes the closing step reliable.

### Are the DST-step deadlines hard to meet?

Generally, no. The deadlines still apply, but DSTs are pre-packaged, fast-closing, and certain. Identification is simple because the investor names the specific DST, and closing can occur well within 180 days. That can be less stressful than finding and closing a direct property.

### When does the 721 exit happen?

It happens on the DST/REIT structure's schedule when the REIT acquires the DST property. It may follow a planned target number of years or a sponsor option. The timing is open-ended and structure-driven, not deadline-bound. The investor should understand expected timing and certainty before investing.

### Why does the deadline distinction matter?

It directs planning toward the deadline-bound DST entry and establishes realistic expectations for the open-ended REIT transition. The investor must identify within 45 days and close within 180, but does not await an “180-day” 721 exit. The distinction clarifies the strategy's timeline.

### Can I miss the 721 exit deadline?

No. There is no statutory 721 exit deadline to miss. The deadlines that can be missed are in the 1031 into the DST: failure to identify within 45 days or close within 180 would fail the 1031 step. The 721 exit proceeds on the structure's schedule.

### Does the open-ended 721 exit create uncertainty?

Some. Timing depends on the DST/REIT structure and sponsor plans, so an investor may not know exactly when the REIT transition occurs. Understand whether the exit follows a planned timeline or an option and how likely it is. Setting realistic expectations for the non-deadline-driven timing addresses the uncertainty.

### Is managing the deadlines easier with a DST than a direct property?

Yes, for the 1031 DST step. DSTs are pre-packaged and fast-closing, and can close in days. A direct-property replacement can be difficult and uncertain to find and close within 45/180 days. DST speed and certainty reduce deadline stress in the first step.

### How does Baker 1031 help with the deadlines?

We help manage the DST step by identifying a DST structured for a 721 exit within 45 days and closing within 180, and help investors set expectations for the open-ended exit. We coordinate with the QI on deadline mechanics and the CPA on tax across both steps.

### Does identifying a DST count toward the three-property rule?

Yes. Identifying a DST in the 1031 step counts as an identified property under the standard identification rules: the three-property rule, allowing up to three properties of any value, or the **200%** rule. The investor can identify a primary DST plus properties or DSTs as backups by describing each specific DST and interest.

### If the 721 exit takes years, am I still deferred the whole time?

Yes. After the 1031 into the DST, gain is deferred under Section 1031 and remains deferred during the DST holding period, however long the exit takes. When the 721 exit occurs, deferral continues under Section 721 in the OP units. Open-ended timing does not interrupt continuous deferral.

### What if no 721 exit ever happens after my DST 1031?

The investor remains a DST investor with gain deferred through the 1031. The DST eventually resolves through a property sale, which can trigger gain unless the investor completes another 1031. This is why the DST's 721-exit structure and certainty should be understood before investment. If REIT ownership is the goal, choose a DST whose 721 exit is reasonably assured. If the exit does not materialize, the outcome is a standalone DST, not REIT ownership.

## Glossary

**45-Day Identification**  
The 1031 deadline to identify the DST, applying to the DST step.

**180-Day Completion**  
The 1031 deadline to close on the DST, applying to the DST step.

**1031 Step**  
The first, DST-entry step governed by deadlines.

**721 Step (Exit)**  
The second, REIT-entry step with no deadlines.

**DST Step**  
The deadline-bound 1031 exchange into the DST.

**Like-Kind Exchange**  
The 1031 transaction into the DST with deadlines.

**Partnership Contribution**  
The 721 transaction into the REIT without deadlines.

**Open-Ended Timing**  
The 721 exit's non-deadline-driven schedule.

**Qualified Intermediary**  
The party handling the DST step's 1031 mechanics.

**Identification**  
Naming the DST within 45 days as required for the DST step.

**DST Speed**  
The fast closing that eases the 180-day deadline.

**1031-then-721 Strategy**  
The two-step path with deadlines only in step one.

**Exit Timing**  
When the 721 exit occurs, structure-driven rather than deadline-bound.

**Exit Certainty**  
How assured the 721 exit is and what must be understood upfront.

**Backup DST**  
An additional identified DST used as insurance in the DST step.

**Deadline Pressure**  
Time pressure present in the DST step and absent from the 721 exit.
