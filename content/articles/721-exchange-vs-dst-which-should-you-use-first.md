# 721 Exchange vs. DST: Which Should You Use First?

I keep hearing “DST or 721?” as if they were substitutes. The first-order question is which vehicle to pick. The second-order answer is often sequence: a DST is generally the 1031-eligible entry point, while a 721 exchange is the later transition to REIT ownership.

## Key Takeaways

- A DST and a 721 exchange are often sequential rather than competing choices: DST first, 721 second.
- The DST is the 1031-eligible entry point; the 721 is the later transition into REIT ownership.
- Use a DST alone for passive real estate and 1031 flexibility; pursue the 721 when REIT ownership is the destination.

## They're not either/or

A [DST](dst-guide.html) and a [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html) usually have different roles. A DST is a passive, 1031-eligible real-estate investment. A 721 contribution exchanges property for REIT operating-partnership [OP units](op-units-explained.html). In a common DST bridge strategy, the investor first completes a [1031 exchange](1031-exchange-guide.html) into a DST within 45 days for identification and 180 days for closing, and later, when a REIT acquires the DST property, receives OP units through a 721 exchange. DST first; 721 second.

## The DST's role

A Delaware Statutory Trust holds real estate and, under IRS guidance, a DST interest can be treated as a direct interest in real property for 1031 purposes. An owner can exchange into a DST, defer gain under Section 1031, and gain fractional, passive exposure to institutional real estate. The sponsor manages the property. A DST can be the entire objective: passive, tax-deferred real estate that remains 1031-eligible when it resolves.

The DST is the entry point because an investor cannot 1031 directly into a REIT. It can stand alone or be chosen as a vehicle structured for a later 721/UPREIT exit.

## The 721's role

The 721 is the transition into REIT ownership. In the bridge strategy, the REIT later acquires the DST property and the DST interest becomes REIT OP units. This can carry the deferred gain from direct property through the DST and into the units under Section 1031 followed by Section 721.

The REIT can provide broader portfolio diversification, scale, a path to liquidity through convertible units, potential income distributions, and estate-planning liquidity through divisible units. It also creates a generally one-way move: OP units are not 1031-eligible real property, and conversion to shares is taxable.

## The typical sequence

First, sell direct property and use a 1031 exchange into a DST within the identification and closing deadlines. If the REIT destination is intended, the DST must be structured for a 721 exit. Later, on the DST/REIT's schedule, the REIT acquires the DST property and the investor receives OP units. The deferral is carried from property, to DST, to OP units. The later 721 event may occur years after the DST investment.

## When to use a DST alone

Use a DST alone when passive, tax-deferred real estate—not REIT ownership—is the goal. A DST can provide passivity and diversification across its holdings while retaining future 1031 flexibility when it resolves. It fits an owner who values that flexibility and does not want the one-way commitment, REIT performance dependence, or taxable conversion path of OP units.

## When the 721 is the goal

Pursue the DST bridge when REIT ownership is the intended destination: broader portfolio diversification, potential liquidity from convertible units, and REIT scale. The DST is then the necessary 1031-eligible means, not the endpoint. A direct 721 of an individual property is possible in principle but often impractical because a REIT must want that particular property; the DST bridge is the more practical route for many owners.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I use a DST or a 721 exchange?

Usually both may be sequential: DST first as the 1031-eligible investment, then 721 when the DST is acquired by a REIT. Use a DST alone when passive real estate is the goal; pursue the 721 when REIT ownership is the goal.

### Which comes first, the DST or the 721?

The DST. You cannot 1031 directly into a REIT, but you can 1031 into a DST. The 721 follows later when a REIT acquires the DST property and converts the DST interest to OP units.

### Why can't I 721 directly instead of using a DST first?

It is possible in principle, but a REIT would need to want an investor's particular property. That is often impractical. A DST structured for a 721 exit creates a bridge to the REIT.

### When should I use a DST alone?

When passive, tax-deferred real estate and continuing 1031 flexibility are the goals, rather than REIT ownership. A DST can later resolve into another 1031 exchange.

### When is the 721 the goal?

When REIT diversification, unit liquidity, and scale are the desired destination and the investor accepts the one-way commitment. Choose a DST structured for its 721 exit.

### Is the DST-then-721 sequence tax-deferred?

Both steps can be tax-deferred: gain is deferred under Section 1031 into the DST and under Section 721 into OP units. It is generally recognized only on a later taxable conversion or disposition, unless held until death.

### Can I decide later whether to do the 721?

The DST selected largely determines the path. A DST structured for a 721 exit is generally acquired on the REIT's schedule; depending on the DST terms, that 721 exit can be an option rather than an obligation. A standalone DST remains a DST and can be 1031 exchanged at resolution. Decide the intended goal when selecting the DST.

### Does using a DST alone keep my 1031 options open?

Yes. On resolution, a DST can generally be exchanged into another property or DST. OP units are not 1031-eligible, so the 721/REIT path forecloses that flexibility.

### What if I am not sure about REIT ownership?

A DST can supply passive, tax-deferred real estate. If REIT ownership may be desired, select a DST with a 721-exit option only after understanding the commitment and timing.

### Is a single DST as diversified as a REIT?

Usually not. A DST often holds one property or a small portfolio; a REIT generally holds a broader portfolio across properties, markets, and types. Multiple DSTs can also diversify exposure.

### How do I decide between a DST alone and the 721?

Choose DST alone for passive real estate and 1031 flexibility. Choose the DST-then-721 path for REIT diversification, potential liquidity, and scale, accepting the one-way nature.

### Is a DST more liquid than OP units?

Both are limited. A DST is generally illiquid until resolution, which can be a 1031-able event. OP units can convert to shares after a holding period, but conversion is taxable; a traded REIT can then provide market liquidity, while other REIT structures can be more constrained.

### Can I split my exchange between a DST and direct property?

Potentially. A 1031 exchange can use multiple replacements, including passive DST interests and active direct property, if identification and value rules are met.

### Does using a DST first delay reaching the REIT?

Yes. The DST must be held until its property is acquired on the DST/REIT's schedule, potentially years. The bridge is often the practical route, but expected timing should be understood before choosing it.

## Glossary

- **Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** A trust holding real estate in fractional interests that can qualify for 1031 treatment.
- **DST Bridge:** A 1031 investment in a DST intended to later enter a REIT through a 721 exit.
- **Sequential Steps:** DST first as the 1031 entry point, then 721 as the REIT transition.
- **721 Exchange:** Tax-deferred contribution of property to a REIT operating partnership for OP units.
- **OP Units:** Operating-partnership units received in the 721 transition.
- **UPREIT:** REIT structure using an operating partnership to receive property contributions.
- **1031-Eligible:** Ability to serve as like-kind real property in a 1031 exchange.
- **One-Way Move:** Loss of future 1031 flexibility after receipt of OP units.
- **Section 1031:** Tax rule for like-kind real-property exchanges.
- **Section 721:** Tax rule for nonrecognition on partnership contributions.
- **Passive Ownership:** Ownership without direct property-management responsibility.
- **Institutional Real Estate:** Larger professionally managed real estate accessed through the DST or REIT.
- **Liquidity:** DST resolution-based 1031 choice versus taxable OP-unit conversion to REIT shares.
- **DST Resolution:** Sponsor sale of DST property, producing a later exchange decision.
- **721 Exit:** REIT purchase of DST property that can create a 721 event.
- **DST Alone:** Passive, tax-deferred real estate without a 721 transition.
- **1031 Flexibility:** Ability to make another 1031 exchange after DST resolution.
- **Standalone DST:** A DST intended to remain a DST rather than bridge to a REIT.
- **REIT Ownership:** Passive ownership through OP units or REIT shares.
- **Continuous Deferral:** Gain remaining deferred through the Section 1031 and Section 721 steps.
- **Diversification:** Spreading exposure across multiple properties, markets, or property types through a DST or REIT portfolio.
- **Destination:** Intended endpoint: DST real estate or REIT ownership.
- **Suitability Review:** Broker-dealer assessment of fit for the investor.
- **Direct 721:** A direct contribution of individual property to a REIT, often impractical.
