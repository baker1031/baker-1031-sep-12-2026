# Qualified Intermediary Requirements for Mineral Rights Exchanges

*Mineral & Royalty · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read*

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

I keep coming back to the point in a mineral exchange where a small administrative miss can carry the largest consequence: the path of the proceeds. The first-order view is that a qualified intermediary is paperwork between closing and the next purchase. The second-order reality is that the QI is the separation that keeps a sale from becoming taxable, while division orders and late royalty checks create extra places for that separation to fail.

A qualified intermediary is required in a 1031 exchange, and minerals bring their own paperwork. This guide covers the QI's role, royalty checks and division orders, choosing a QI with oil-and-gas experience, and avoiding the constructive-receipt error that ends an exchange.

Every deferred 1031 exchange needs a qualified intermediary. In a mineral exchange, the basic job is unchanged: an independent party holds the sale proceeds so the taxpayer never receives them. But mineral deeds, assignments, division orders, and the timing of royalty checks create added work that a real-estate-only QI may not expect. An experienced QI can keep a stray royalty check or poorly handled division order from jeopardizing the exchange.

The QI is only one piece of the broader process described in [our complete 2026 guide to mineral rights and royalty 1031 exchanges](mineral-rights-1031-guide.html).

## Why you can't touch the sale proceeds

The foundation is simple and unforgiving: the taxpayer cannot have actual or constructive receipt of the sale proceeds. If the money reaches you, or you can control it, the IRS treats the transaction as a taxable sale rather than an exchange. That is not a later paperwork fix. Touching the proceeds is the most common way an otherwise valid exchange is destroyed.

An independent [qualified intermediary](what-is-a-qualified-intermediary.html) stands between the taxpayer and the money. At closing, the buyer sends funds directly to the QI. The QI holds them in a segregated account during the exchange and uses them to acquire the replacement property. The funds never go through your hands or an account you control. That is what lets the transaction be treated as an exchange rather than a sale followed by a reinvestment.

Constructive receipt is the quieter risk. A taxpayer can be deemed to receive money without ever cashing a check if funds are set aside, available, or under that taxpayer's control. The QI arrangement must put the money truly beyond your reach for the entire exchange. The deferral rests on that separation.

## What a QI does in a mineral exchange

Before the sale closes, the QI prepares the exchange agreement and assignment documents and is assigned into the purchase and sale contract. At closing, it receives proceeds into a segregated qualified escrow or trust account. It holds them during the identification and exchange periods, disburses them for the replacement purchase, and documents the exchange.

For minerals, those steps have to fit oil-and-gas conveyances. The relinquished interest moves through a mineral deed or assignment, rather than a standard real-estate deed, and a direct-mineral replacement is acquired the same way. The QI's documents must fit those instruments. If the replacement is a [DST](diversified-1031-portfolio-minerals-plus-dsts.html), the transaction is a securities purchase through a broker-dealer that the QI funds—a different process it must coordinate.

The QI is a neutral custodian and documenter, not the person advising you what to buy or [how the minerals are valued](how-to-value-mineral-rights-1031-exchange.html). Those are jobs for your [advisor and CPA](role-of-your-cpa-in-a-1031-exchange.html). Its narrow task is to keep funds beyond reach, document the exchange correctly, and disburse properly. With minerals, that means comfort with conveyances and income details specific to oil and gas.

## Handling division orders and royalty checks

Division orders and royalty checks are the distinctive mineral issue. A division order tells an operator each owner's decimal interest in production and where payment should go. When a mineral or royalty interest is sold, it must be updated to redirect payments to the buyer. The timing matters: production before closing generally belongs to the seller; production afterward belongs to the buyer.

Royalty checks often arrive a month or two after the production they represent. A check for pre-closing production can therefore arrive long after the sale has closed. It generally remains the seller's ordinary income, but its route matters. Having it move through the QI as exchange proceeds, or having exchange-related amounts flow to the seller, can create confusion or constructive-receipt risk. The QI, CPA, and operator paperwork must line up.

A mineral-experienced QI will establish instructions before closing. It will distinguish pre-closing royalty income—yours and taxable as income—from sale proceeds, which must go to the QI, and will make sure the division-order transition does not direct exchange funds to you. Sorting trailing income before closing is essential.

> Royalty checks arrive on a lag. Pre-closing production can show up after a sale, and routing those checks incorrectly can create an avoidable constructive-receipt problem.

## Choosing a QI experienced in oil & gas

QIs are lightly regulated; there is no federal licensing regime. That makes the diligence yours. Confirm that the QI uses segregated qualified escrow or trust accounts rather than operating cash, carries a fidelity bond for employee theft and errors-and-omissions insurance for mistakes, and has a long, clean operating record. Funds may be held for months and are irreplaceable if mishandled or lost.

Then ask directly about oil-and-gas exchanges. Has the QI handled mineral conveyances and assignments? Does it understand division orders and trailing income? Can it coordinate a DST replacement? A firm that works only on conventional real estate may miss the issues that matter. Membership in the Federation of Exchange Accommodators and adherence to its standards is a positive professionalism signal.

The cheapest QI can be a false economy. Its fee difference is small next to the tax at stake and the risk of lost funds or a failed exchange. Put fund security and relevant experience ahead of price. Evasiveness about custody, control, or mineral-exchange experience is sufficient reason to choose someone else.

## Avoiding constructive receipt

The discipline is to keep proceeds genuinely beyond your reach. Engage the QI before the sale closes, and have closing instructions send proceeds directly to its account—not to you, your attorney's trust account, or any account you control. The assignment and instructions must be in place before closing, not afterward.

Do not take or direct exchange funds during the exchange period. Funds stay with the QI and may be used only for the replacement purchase under the exchange agreement. You cannot borrow against them, direct them to another purpose, or ask the QI to release them mid-exchange.

For minerals, separate trailing royalty income from exchange funds. Route pre-closing checks under the QI's and CPA's instructions, keep them separate from sale proceeds, and do not let exchange-related amounts come to you. Early QI engagement, proceeds outside your control, and clean income handling make constructive receipt avoidable. One slip can still end the exchange.

## Key Takeaways

- A qualified intermediary is mandatory—you must never have actual or constructive receipt of sale proceeds.
- The QI keeps proceeds beyond your reach, documents the exchange, and disburses for the replacement.
- Minerals add division-order transitions and trailing royalty checks that must not blur into exchange funds.
- Choose a QI for segregated escrow, bonding, E&O coverage, and oil-and-gas experience, not its lowest fee.

## The QI's place in your team

The QI is one member of the mineral-exchange team. It holds funds and documents the exchange, but does not advise what to buy, value minerals, or model tax. Your advisor sources and vets the replacement; your CPA handles basis, gain, recapture, and Form 8824; counsel handles conveyances, title, and entity issues. The QI is central as custodian, but it is not a substitute for those roles.

That boundary matters. A QI will not determine whether an interest is a qualifying perpetual real-property interest or whether value matching will fully defer gain. Those are CPA and advisor questions. The QI keeps mechanics clean; the rest of the team tests whether the exchange is sound and worthwhile.

Minerals require the team to coordinate closely. The QI's trailing-income instructions, CPA basis work, attorney conveyances, and advisor replacement sourcing must fit together. An experienced QI at the custodial center helps the exchange move smoothly despite the added complexity.

## The QI through the exchange timeline

Before listing or signing a sale contract, engage the QI and sign the exchange agreement. This is when you also settle the treatment of trailing income and the division-order transition. The QI may be engaged after the sale contract is signed but must be in place before closing—never afterward.

At the relinquished closing, the QI is assigned into the contract and buyer funds go to its segregated account instead of you. The mineral deed or assignment conveys the interest to the buyer. From that date, the [45-day identification](1031-exchange-identification-rules.html) and 180-day exchange clocks run while the QI holds funds untouched.

At replacement closing, the QI disburses funds for a direct mineral purchase by mineral deed or a DST purchase through the broker-dealer. It documents the exchange for Form 8824 and handles trailing checks under the agreed instructions, keeping pre-closing income separate from exchange proceeds. Early engagement, relinquished closing, and replacement closing are the three points that make the QI's function clear.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a qualified intermediary required for a mineral 1031 exchange?

Yes. Every deferred 1031 exchange, including a mineral exchange, requires an independent QI to hold sale proceeds so the taxpayer never takes actual or constructive receipt. Without a QI, it is a taxable sale, not an exchange. The QI must be engaged before closing; there is no fix afterward.

### What does a qualified intermediary do?

It prepares exchange documents, is assigned into sale and purchase contracts, receives proceeds into a segregated account at closing, holds them during the exchange, and disburses them to acquire the replacement property. It is the neutral custodian and documenter that keeps funds beyond your reach.

### What is constructive receipt?

It means being deemed to receive proceeds without physically holding them because the funds are set aside, available, or controlled by you. It disqualifies an exchange just as actual receipt does, which is why funds must remain beyond your reach with the QI.

### How are royalty checks handled in a mineral exchange?

Checks for pre-closing production often arrive after a sale because royalty payments lag. They generally belong to the seller as ordinary income, not exchange proceeds, and must follow QI and CPA instructions so they do not blur into sale proceeds or create constructive receipt. Set this up before closing.

### What is a division order and why does it matter?

A division order confirms each owner's decimal production interest and payment instructions. On sale, it must redirect payments to the buyer. Its timing relative to closing determines who owns which payments, so the transition requires coordination.

### Can I use any qualified intermediary for minerals?

You can, but avoid one without oil-and-gas experience. Mineral conveyances, division orders, trailing royalty checks, and DST funding are details a real-estate-only QI may not anticipate. Choose demonstrated mineral-exchange experience and strong fund security.

### How do I know my funds are safe with a QI?

Confirm segregated qualified escrow or trust accounts rather than commingled firm cash, fidelity bonding for theft, E&O insurance for mistakes, a long clean operating history, and Federation of Exchange Accommodators membership. Because funds may be held for months, security is decisive.

### When must I engage the QI?

Before closing. The QI must be assigned into the contract and closing instructions must send proceeds to its account before closing. If proceeds reach you first, you have taken receipt and the exchange is disqualified with no fix.

### Can the QI advise me on what minerals to buy?

No. It is a neutral custodian and documenter, not a replacement-property adviser, mineral valuer, or tax modeler. Your advisor and CPA perform those functions.

### What happens if the proceeds touch my account by mistake?

The exchange is generally disqualified because you have received the funds, and there is typically no post-closing cure. Prevention is the protection: engage the QI before closing and direct proceeds to it, never to an account you control.

### How is a DST replacement funded through the QI?

A DST is acquired as a securities purchase through a broker-dealer. The QI uses proceeds it holds to fund that disbursement and complete the exchange. A QI experienced with DST replacements can coordinate this process.

### Does the QI charge a large fee?

Typically no. A QI generally charges a modest flat fee per exchange, sometimes with a small additional-property charge. It is small relative to tax at stake and the cost of a mishandled exchange. Do not save a small fee at the expense of security and mineral experience.

### Can my attorney or CPA act as my qualified intermediary?

Generally no. Recent agents, including an attorney, accountant, or broker, are disqualified from acting as QI so the QI remains independent. Your attorney and CPA have essential roles, but a separate independent QI must hold the funds.

### What happens to the QI's account if the exchange fails?

If a replacement cannot be acquired in time, the QI returns funds under the exchange agreement and the transaction becomes a taxable sale for that year. Funds remain protected in the segregated account, but the deferral is lost.

### Does using a QI cost me access to my proceeds during the exchange?

Yes, by design. You cannot access or control proceeds while they are held for the exchange; they are deployed only for the replacement purchase. That temporary lack of access is the mechanism that keeps the deferral valid.

### How do I verify a QI's track record with minerals?

Ask how many oil-and-gas exchanges it has handled, seek mineral-exchange references, and confirm its knowledge of conveyances, division orders, trailing income, and DST funding. Also check custody controls and FEA membership. Vague answers are a reason to keep looking.

## Glossary

**Qualified Intermediary (QI).** The independent party that holds exchange proceeds so the taxpayer never takes actual or constructive receipt.

**Constructive Receipt.** Being deemed to receive proceeds without physically holding them, if they are available to or controlled by you; it disqualifies the exchange.

**Qualified Escrow Account.** A restricted, segregated account where exchange funds are held beyond the taxpayer's reach.

**Exchange Agreement.** The contract between the taxpayer and QI documenting and governing the exchange.

**Assignment.** The QI's assignment into sale and purchase contracts, a required exchange-mechanics step.

**Division Order.** An operator's document confirming each owner's decimal interest in production and payment instructions.

**Trailing Income.** Royalty checks for pre-closing production that arrive after sale and must be routed carefully.

**Mineral Deed.** The instrument conveying a mineral or royalty interest in place of a standard real-estate deed.

**Fidelity Bond.** Insurance protecting client funds against misappropriation by the QI or its employees.

**Errors-and-Omissions (E&O) Insurance.** Coverage protecting against mistakes in handling an exchange.

**Federation of Exchange Accommodators (FEA).** The trade body setting standards and certification for qualified intermediaries.

**Segregated Account.** An account holding client funds separately from the QI's operating cash.

**Delaware Statutory Trust (DST).** A securitized real-property interest that can be the QI-funded replacement in an exchange.

**Closing Instructions.** Directions ensuring proceeds go to the QI rather than the taxpayer at closing.

**Same-Taxpayer Rule.** The taxpayer who sells relinquished property must also acquire the replacement.

**Broker-Dealer.** A firm registered to offer securities such as DSTs; Baker 1031's is Aurora Securities, member FINRA/SIPC.
