# 721 Exchange Glossary of Terms

[Home](baker1031.html) › Insights › 721 Exchange Glossary of Terms

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read**

I keep seeing a familiar mistake: someone understands the headline “tax deferral,” but not the vocabulary that tells them what they actually own, what they can convert, and what control they have surrendered. The 721 exchange and UPREIT world has its own language—Section 721, OP units, operating partnership, carryover basis, conversion, the step-up, the DST bridge, and more.

First-order thinking treats the terms as labels. Second-order thinking uses them to trace risk, tax timing, liquidity, and decision rights. This glossary explains the key terms by theme: core 721 terms, UPREIT structure terms, tax terms, OP-unit and liquidity terms, process terms, and DST-bridge terms. It is meant to build understanding from the foundations through the more advanced concepts.

## Core 721 terms

A **721 exchange**, also called an **UPREIT conversion**, is the contribution of real estate to a REIT's operating partnership in exchange for partnership units, tax-deferred under Section 721. **Section 721** is the Internal Revenue Code section providing that no gain or loss is recognized when property is contributed to a partnership for a partnership interest. It is the legal basis for the deferral.

An **UPREIT**, or umbrella partnership real estate investment trust, is a REIT that owns properties through an operating partnership, making tax-deferred 721 contributions possible. The **contribution** is the act of transferring property to that operating partnership for units. **Tax deferral** is the postponement of capital-gains tax that the transaction achieves.

These are the foundation terms: a 721 exchange or UPREIT conversion, Section 721 as the deferral provision, the UPREIT as the enabling structure, the contribution as the transfer, and tax deferral as the benefit. Together, they describe contributing real estate to a REIT operating partnership for units on a tax-deferred basis.

## UPREIT structure terms

The **operating partnership (OP)** is the partnership beneath the REIT that directly owns and operates the real estate. It is the entity to which property is contributed and in which the investor receives units. The **REIT** is the parent entity, often publicly traded, that controls the operating partnership as its general partner and is owned by shareholders.

The **general partner** is the controlling partner of the operating partnership—the REIT that manages it. **Limited partners** are non-controlling partners, including unit holders, with economic interests but no control. A **DownREIT** is a variation that uses separate lower-tier partnerships for contributed property rather than one umbrella operating partnership. It is more complex than a standard UPREIT.

The essential hierarchy is the REIT as parent and general partner, the operating partnership as owner of the real estate, and the investor as a limited partner holding OP units. Those structure terms explain the organization being entered: operating partnership, REIT, general partner, limited partner, and the DownREIT variation.

> Learn the structure: the REIT, as parent and general partner, sits over the operating partnership that owns the real estate; the investor becomes a limited partner holding OP units. That hierarchy is at the heart of every 721 exchange.

## Tax terms

**Carryover basis** is the tax basis of the contributed property carried into the [OP units](op-units-explained.html). It embeds the deferred gain—the difference between the units' value and the investor's low basis. **Deferred gain** is gain not recognized at contribution and carried in the units. The [**step-up in basis**](1031-exchange-step-up-in-basis-at-death.html) at death resets the heirs' basis to fair market value and can erase that deferred gain.

[**Depreciation recapture**](depreciation-recapture-real-estate.html) is the tax, up to **25%**, on prior depreciation. A 721 defers it by carrying it in the units, and conversion triggers it. The **four-layer tax stack** is the combined tax a 721 can defer: capital gains, recapture, [NIIT](net-investment-income-tax.html), and state tax. A **tax protection agreement** protects deferred gain from being triggered by a partnership sale. **Built-in gain** is the deferred gain associated with contributed property.

These tax terms explain the mechanics and the benefit. Carryover basis defers gain; deferred and built-in gain preserve it; the step-up can eliminate it; and a tax protection agreement seeks to safeguard it. They also show why deferral is not forgiveness before a step-up or other recognized outcome.

## OP unit and liquidity terms

**OP units**, or operating partnership units, are the investor's ownership interest in the operating partnership, received in a 721 exchange. They pay distributions and are convertible into REIT shares. **REIT shares** are the corporate, often traded ownership interests of the REIT into which OP units convert. **Distributions** are the income paid on OP units, comparable to REIT dividends.

**Conversion** exchanges OP units for REIT shares after a lock-up and triggers the deferred gain, making it a taxable liquidity step. The **lock-up period**, or holding period, is the time before conversion becomes available, often around a year. A **redemption program** is a non-traded REIT's mechanism for buying back shares and is the non-traded liquidity path. The **Schedule K-1** is the partnership tax form that reports the unit holder's share of partnership income.

The terms describe both the holding and access to value: OP units are the holding, distributions are its income, and conversion or redemption are ways to access value. The lock-up identifies the initial wait; the K-1 identifies the tax form. The key point is that conversion can provide liquidity, but it is taxable.

## Process and party terms

The **contribution agreement** is the document that effects the 721 exchange: property for units. **Valuation** determines the property's value and, therefore, the units received. A **suitability review** assesses whether the 721 exchange fits the investor's circumstances; it is required because the transaction involves securities. An **accredited investor** meets income or net-worth thresholds and is typically required for the securities involved.

The **sponsor** is the firm behind the REIT or DST. The **CPA** handles tax aspects. The **attorney** handles legal structuring and documents. The **financial advisor**, working through a broker-dealer, handles suitability and securities. Together, these professionals form the investor's 721 exchange team.

The contribution agreement, valuation, suitability review, accredited-investor status, sponsor, CPA, attorney, and advisor describe both the transaction and its participants. They show how the exchange is executed and by whom.

## Key Takeaways

- Core terms: 721 exchange or UPREIT conversion, Section 721, UPREIT, contribution, and tax deferral.
- Structure terms: operating partnership, REIT, general and limited partner, and DownREIT.
- Tax terms: carryover basis, deferred gain, step-up, depreciation recapture, tax protection agreement, and built-in gain.
- OP-unit and liquidity terms: OP units, REIT shares, distributions, conversion, lock-up period, redemption program, and Schedule K-1, plus process terms and the DST bridge.

## The DST bridge terms

A **Delaware Statutory Trust (DST)** is a 1031-eligible passive real-estate vehicle that can act as a bridge to a REIT. The **DST bridge**, or **1031-then-721**, is the two-step strategy of completing a 1031 exchange into a DST and later a 721 exchange into a REIT. The **721 exit**, or **UPREIT exit**, occurs when the REIT acquires the DST property and converts the investor's interest into OP units.

The **step-transaction doctrine** is the IRS principle that can collapse bridge steps when they are too integrated, which requires the steps to be genuine and independent. **Continuous deferral** is the gain remaining deferred across both steps: Section 1031 and then Section 721.

Together, DST, DST bridge or 1031-then-721, 721 exit, step-transaction doctrine, and continuous deferral explain the common route to REIT ownership and its tax considerations. The bridge takes an investor from direct property through a DST and then to a REIT; the step-transaction doctrine is the key consideration along that path.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a 721 exchange?

It is the tax-deferred contribution of real estate to a REIT operating partnership for partnership units, or OP units, under Section 721. Also called an UPREIT conversion, it moves an investor from direct property ownership into REIT ownership through OP units, defers capital-gains tax, and can provide diversification, passivity, liquidity through convertible units, and estate benefits. It is the foundational term: a tax-deferred conversion of property into REIT units.

### What is Section 721?

It is the Internal Revenue Code section providing that no gain or loss is recognized when property is contributed to a partnership for a partnership interest. It is why a contribution of property to a REIT operating partnership for OP units can be tax-deferred. The “721 exchange” takes its name from this foundational tax provision.

### What is an UPREIT?

An umbrella partnership real estate investment trust is a REIT that owns its properties through an operating partnership rather than directly, enabling tax-deferred 721 contributions. The REIT-over-operating-partnership-over-real-estate structure lets owners contribute property for units on a tax-deferred basis. Most REITs accepting 721 contributions are UPREITs.

### What are OP units?

Operating partnership units are the investor's ownership interest in the REIT operating partnership after a 721 exchange. They pay distributions comparable to REIT dividends, represent a stake in the REIT portfolio, and can convert into REIT shares after a lock-up. They are the investor's tax-deferred REIT ownership and the form that real-estate wealth takes in the strategy.

### What is carryover basis?

It is the property's tax basis carried into OP units, embedding the deferred gain. The low basis becomes the basis in the units, preserving the difference between the units' value and that basis. Carryover basis defers rather than forgives gain; it carries the gain to a later recognition event unless a step-up erases it.

### What is the step-up in basis?

It resets the heirs' basis in OP units to fair market value at death and can erase deferred gain. If units are held until death, heirs may inherit them with a stepped-up basis, allowing a conversion or sale with little income tax on the prior appreciation. This estate benefit can turn the 721's deferral into permanent elimination of gain.

### What is conversion?

It is the exchange of OP units for REIT shares after the lock-up. It can provide liquidity, because shares in a traded REIT are tradable, but it triggers deferred gain and is taxable. Investors may convert gradually to manage tax or hold units to continue deferral toward a step-up.

### What is the lock-up period?

It is the time after receiving OP units during which conversion into REIT shares is unavailable—often around a year, though it varies by REIT. During the lock-up, the investor holds units, earns distributions, and keeps the gain deferred. After it, conversion and its liquidity path become available.

### What is the DST bridge?

It is the two-step strategy, also called 1031-then-721: first a 1031 exchange into a DST and then a 721 exchange into a REIT. The DST, which is 1031-eligible, bridges the gap because an investor cannot 1031 directly into a REIT. When the REIT later acquires the DST, the 721 exit provides OP units. It is a common practical route to tax-deferred REIT ownership.

### What is the operating partnership?

It is the partnership beneath the REIT in an UPREIT structure that directly owns and operates the real estate. In a 721 exchange, the investor contributes property to it, receives OP units, and becomes a limited partner. The REIT controls the operating partnership as general partner. It is the entity the investor transitions into and the key structural component of an UPREIT.

### What are distributions?

They are income paid on OP units, comparable to the dividends paid to REIT shareholders, and reflect the unit holder's share of income from the REIT portfolio. They provide passive income from the REIT's real estate without management work and are taxed under partnership rules through the K-1. Distributions are the continuing income from a 721 exchange while units are held.

### What is a tax protection agreement?

It is an agreement negotiated in a 721 exchange under which the REIT or operating partnership agrees to protect the investor's deferred built-in gain from a taxable sale of contributed property. It may promise not to sell the property taxably for a period or compensate the investor if it does. The agreement safeguards deferral against a premature trigger, and its protected period and form of protection determine how well it works.

### What is the 721 exit (or UPREIT exit)?

It is the DST-bridge step in which the REIT acquires the DST property and converts DST investors' interests into REIT OP units. After an investor 1031s into a DST, the 721 or UPREIT exit is the second step that reaches the REIT under Section 721. Its timing is not always guaranteed; it depends on the DST and REIT structure, so the expected exit deserves attention.

### What is a DownREIT?

It is a REIT-structure variation using separate lower-tier partnerships for contributed property instead of one umbrella operating partnership. The contributed property goes into a specific lower-tier partnership and the investor holds units in it. It is more complex than a standard UPREIT and used in specific circumstances, such as a non-UPREIT REIT accepting a contribution. Both structures can allow tax-deferred 721 contributions.

### What is the four-layer tax stack?

It is the combined tax that a sale of appreciated real estate would trigger and a 721 can defer: federal capital-gains tax, up to **20%**; depreciation recapture, up to **25%**; the **3.8%** net investment income tax (NIIT); and state income tax. Together, these often total a third or more of gain. The phrase is shorthand for the full tax burden that a 721, like a 1031, postpones—not only capital gains but recapture, NIIT, and state tax.

### What is an accredited investor?

An accredited investor meets income or net-worth thresholds defined by securities regulations, such as income above a specified level or net worth above a specified level excluding a primary residence. Accreditation is typically required for the securities offerings used in 721 exchanges, including OP units, DSTs, and REIT interests. Most owners of substantial appreciated real estate meet the thresholds, and a financial professional verifies accreditation.

### What is a suitability review?

It is the financial professional's broker-dealer-based assessment of whether a 721 exchange and its specific offering suit the investor's financial situation, objectives, risk tolerance, time horizon, and liquidity needs. It is required because the strategy involves securities and is intended to prevent an unsuitable recommendation. Every securities-based 721 exchange includes a suitability review before it is recommended.

## Glossary

**721 Exchange**  
Contributing real estate to a REIT's operating partnership for units, tax-deferred.

**Section 721**  
The code section providing nonrecognition on partnership contributions.

**UPREIT**  
An umbrella partnership REIT that enables 721 contributions.

**Operating Partnership**  
The partnership owning the REIT's real estate.

**OP Units**  
The investor's interest in the operating partnership, received in the exchange.

**REIT Shares**  
The REIT's corporate ownership, convertible from OP units.

**Carryover Basis**  
The property's basis carrying into the units and embedding the gain.

**Deferred Gain**  
The unrecognized gain carried in the units.

**Step-Up in Basis**  
The death-time reset erasing the deferred gain.

**Depreciation Recapture**  
The tax on prior depreciation, deferred by the 721.

**Conversion**  
Exchanging units for shares and triggering the gain.

**Lock-Up Period**  
The wait before converting units to shares.

**Distributions**  
The income paid on OP units.

**Tax Protection Agreement**  
The agreement protecting deferred gain.

**DST Bridge**  
The 1031-then-721 route to a REIT through a DST.

**Schedule K-1**  
The partnership tax form for OP-unit income.
