# How a 721 Exchange Works: Step-by-Step Process

[Home](baker1031.html) · [Back to All Insights](insights.html)

721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read

I find that a 721 exchange appears more mysterious than it is until its sequence is separated into decisions. At its core, an owner contributes property to a REIT operating partnership and receives partnership units in return without current gain recognition. The surrounding choices are where the work is: suitability, a willing REIT, value, documents, tax protections, and the future decision to hold or convert units.

The complete process goes from an initial assessment through ownership of OP units. It can deliver passive income, portfolio diversification, potential liquidity, continued deferral, and possible estate-planning value. It also usually means giving up direct control and the ability to 1031 the contributed position again. This guide follows each step and the safeguards that make the structure work.

## Step 1: assess suitability and find a willing UPREIT

The process begins with an assessment of whether a [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html) fits the owner’s objectives. The owner should be seeking REIT ownership’s potential diversification, passive management, potential liquidity, and estate-planning benefits—and be comfortable giving up control of the property and the ability to use another 1031 exchange on the contributed position.

This assessment belongs with a financial professional and the owner’s CPA. First-order thinking sees deferral and a higher-level REIT portfolio. Second-order thinking asks whether the owner is prepared for a generally one-way move into a partnership interest, the illiquidity of OP units, the tax cost of conversion, and the loss of direct property control.

The owner must also find a REIT that is both an UPREIT and willing to acquire the specific property. An UPREIT has an operating partnership; not every REIT does, and not every UPREIT wants every property. The asset has to fit the REIT’s acquisition criteria, portfolio strategy, quality standards, and current needs.

This can happen through a direct match, where the REIT wants the owner’s property. More commonly, an investor reaches the REIT through a [DST structured for a potential 721/UPREIT exit](from-dst-to-reit-721-rollup.html): the investor first holds a DST interest and later receives OP units when the REIT acquires the DST property. Without both suitability and a willing UPREIT, there is no viable 721 exchange.

## Step 2: value the property and determine the OP units

The second stage determines the economics. The owner and the UPREIT agree on a contribution value for the property, generally through negotiation informed by appraisals and property financials. That value determines how many [OP units](op-units-explained.html) the owner receives.

The unit calculation is direct: agreed property value divided by OP-unit value. The unit value is generally tied to the REIT’s share price or net asset value because OP units are convertible into shares. For example, a property valued at $5,000,000 and OP units valued at $50 each would produce 100,000 OP units.

This is not a trivial arithmetic exercise. The property valuation determines the owner’s stake in the operating partnership. The owner has an interest in maximizing the units received for the property; the REIT has an interest in acquiring the property at a sound value. A fair, supportable valuation is the economic heart of the transaction.

> The agreed property value divided by the unit value determines the owner’s number of OP units—and therefore the ownership stake received in the operating partnership.

## Step 3: make the contribution

The third stage is the Section 721 contribution. The owner transfers the property to the operating partnership, and the partnership issues the determined number of OP units. This is the [Section 721 transaction](section-721-exchange-tax-code-behind-upreit-conversions.html) that generally defers gain.

The legal mechanics include the contribution agreement, property transfer documents, OP-unit issuance documentation, the partnership agreement, and often a tax protection agreement. Attorneys and advisers handle the contribution terms, title transfer, and the intended Section 721 treatment. The owner becomes a limited partner holding OP units; the property becomes part of the operating partnership’s REIT portfolio.

The contribution is the moment of transition. The owner no longer owns the direct property. They own a partnership interest in the portfolio. Gain is generally deferred, but the underlying built-in gain is still present and needs to be tracked through basis records and later decisions.

## Step 4: hold OP units

After contribution, the investor holds OP units. The units represent an economic interest in the REIT’s diversified portfolio and generally entitle the holder to distributions comparable to REIT dividends. The investor receives passive income while the deferred gain remains deferred because they have not converted or sold the units.

The REIT manages the portfolio. The OP-unit holder no longer handles property operations or management responsibilities. Units can be held indefinitely, so holding is not merely waiting for a later sale; it is the period when the investor may receive income, portfolio diversification, and continued tax deferral.

There is typically a conversion holding period, often around one year, before units can be exchanged for REIT shares. The owner may hold far longer. Many investors hold units for income and potential estate-planning value. If units are held until death, a step-up in basis can generally eliminate deferred gain for heirs under current rules.

## Step 5: convert to shares or continue holding

After the applicable holding period, the investor typically may convert OP units to REIT shares, often one-for-one. In some structures, units may be redeemed for cash. A public REIT’s shares can be traded for cash. Conversion creates a path to liquidity, but it is generally a taxable event that triggers the deferred gain on the units converted.

The investor’s basic choice is to convert for liquidity and accept tax, or to continue holding for income and continued deferral. Conversion can occur gradually. An investor can convert and sell portions of the position over time as cash is needed, spreading taxable gain across years, rather than converting everything at once.

The other choice is to hold indefinitely. That retains the income and deferral and may lead to a step-up in basis at death. Many investors combine the approaches: they hold most units long term and convert only the amount needed for liquidity. The investor is managing timing, not removing the tax consequence.

## Tax protection and documentation

Tax protection and documents matter throughout the process. A tax protection agreement can be negotiated as part of the contribution. It is intended to protect the contributor’s deferred built-in gain if the operating partnership sells the contributed property in a taxable transaction during an agreed protected period. It addresses the risk of a later partnership sale unexpectedly triggering the contributor’s tax.

The terms must be negotiated: the protection period and form of protection can vary. A tax protection agreement is important to many contributors because it provides a contractual response to a tax risk that otherwise may arise after the owner no longer controls the property.

Core documents include the contribution agreement, tax protection agreement where applicable, OP-unit issuance records, property-transfer documents, and the partnership agreement that governs the investor’s rights as a limited partner. These establish the units, rights, protection, and structure. The owner should retain complete records long term because the deferral, basis, and partner rights persist for years.

The Section 721 structure, partnership agreement, documentation, and tax protection all require experienced specialists: a CPA, an attorney, and a securities-licensed adviser.

## Timing and the DST route

The timing of a direct 721 exchange is driven by the parties: a willing UPREIT, valuation negotiation, diligence, and documents. A contribution can take weeks or months. Unlike a 1031 exchange, a stand-alone 721 contribution does not carry a statutory 45-day identification or 180-day completion deadline because it is a partnership contribution rather than a like-kind exchange.

If the owner reaches REIT ownership through the 1031-then-721 DST route, the situation differs. The initial 1031 into a DST is subject to the 45-day and 180-day rules. The later 721/UPREIT exit of the DST is not. Holding after contribution is open-ended.

## Key Takeaways

- **Step 1:** Confirm suitability and find a willing UPREIT, directly or through a DST structured for a future 721 exit.
- **Step 2:** Value the property and calculate OP units as property value divided by unit value.
- **Step 3:** Transfer the property to the operating partnership for OP units under Section 721, generally deferring gain.
- **Steps 4–5:** Hold units for distributions and continuing deferral, or convert to shares for liquidity in a generally taxable transaction. Holding until death may allow a step-up in basis.
- Tax protection agreements and complete contribution, transfer, unit, and partnership records protect the deferral and the investor’s rights.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the steps of a 721 exchange?

The usual steps are: assess suitability and find a willing UPREIT; value the property and determine the OP units; transfer the property to the operating partnership in the Section 721 contribution; hold units and receive distributions; then either convert units to shares for liquidity, generally triggering tax, or continue holding for deferral and a possible step-up at death. Tax protection and documentation matter across the process.

### How do I find an UPREIT for a 721 exchange?

The REIT must have an operating partnership and must want the specific property. The owner and advisers seek a direct match with an UPREIT whose portfolio criteria fit the asset, or use a DST structured for a later 721/UPREIT exit. Not every REIT accepts every property. A securities-licensed adviser can help identify suitable opportunities after assessing the owner’s situation.

### How is property valued in a 721 exchange?

The owner and UPREIT agree on a value using negotiation, appraisals, and property financials. The agreed property value and the unit value, typically related to share price or net asset value, determine the unit count. A $5,000,000 property at $50 per unit produces 100,000 units. The valuation sets the economic stake the owner receives and should be fair and supportable.

### What happens in the contribution step?

The owner transfers property ownership to the operating partnership, which issues the agreed OP units. This is the Section 721 transaction and generally defers gain. It uses contribution, transfer, unit-issuance, partnership, and often tax-protection documentation. Afterward, the property is in the REIT portfolio and the owner holds a limited-partner interest in the operating partnership.

### What do I do after receiving OP units?

The investor holds them, receives distributions comparable to REIT dividends, and remains a passive investor while gain continues to be deferred. Units can be held indefinitely. After a holding period that is often around one year, the owner can typically convert units for liquidity if desired. Holding is the period in which income, deferral, diversification, and potential estate benefits accrue.

### How do I convert OP units to REIT shares?

After the applicable holding period, the owner typically requests conversion through the REIT’s process, often on a one-for-one basis, or may be able to redeem units for cash in some structures. Public REIT shares can be sold for cash. Conversion is generally taxable and triggers deferred gain on converted units. Owners can convert gradually over time or only as cash is required.

### What is a tax protection agreement?

It is a negotiated contribution agreement feature that protects the contributor from premature recognition of deferred built-in gain if the operating partnership sells the contributed property in a taxable manner during a protected period. The terms, duration, and form of protection are negotiated. An attorney typically negotiates it because it is a key safeguard for contributors.

### How long does a 721 exchange take?

It varies with the search for a willing UPREIT, valuation, diligence, negotiation, and documents. The contribution itself can take weeks or months. There are no fixed 45-day or 180-day statutory deadlines for a direct 721 exchange. After contribution, holding OP units has no set end date.

### Does a 721 exchange have 45- and 180-day deadlines?

No. Those deadlines belong to Section 1031 like-kind exchanges. A 721 is a contribution to a partnership and is not subject to the 45-day identification and 180-day completion rules. If a 721 is reached through a 1031 into a DST, the preceding 1031 follows the 45/180 rules, while the later 721 exit does not.

### What documentation is involved in a 721 exchange?

The documents include the contribution agreement, tax protection agreement if negotiated, OP-unit issuance documents, property-transfer documents, and the partnership agreement. They establish the transfer, units, partner rights, tax protection, basis, and structure. The owner should keep complete copies because the legal and tax effects continue for years.

### Do I need professionals for a 721 exchange?

Yes. The structure involves partnership tax treatment, basis, tax protections, a potential step-up, legal documents, property transfer, and securities. The owner should use an experienced CPA, attorney, and securities-licensed adviser. The transaction should not be undertaken without that support.

### Can I reach a 721 exchange through a DST?

Yes. In a 1031-then-721 path, the investor first completes a 1031 exchange into a DST, deferring gain and gaining passive real-estate ownership. Later, when a REIT acquires the DST property through a 721/UPREIT exit, the DST interest becomes OP units. If this future transition matters, confirm before investing that the DST is structured for it.

### Can I contribute property with a mortgage on it?

Often, but debt adds complexity. The operating partnership may assume the debt or take the property subject to it. Partnership liability allocations can affect tax outcome and basis. A reduction in the contributor’s liability share can in some cases be a deemed distribution with tax consequences. A CPA and the REIT structure must analyze existing debt before contribution.

### What if I change my mind after contributing?

The transaction generally cannot be reversed tax-free. OP units are partnership interests, not real property, so they cannot be 1031-exchanged out. Converting to shares or cash is generally taxable. The remaining choices are to hold the units for income and continued deferral, convert for liquidity, or hold until death for a possible step-up. This is why the initial suitability assessment matters.

## Glossary

**721 Exchange Process:** The sequence from assessment through holding or converting OP units.

**Suitability Assessment:** Determining whether a 721 exchange fits the owner’s objectives before proceeding.

**UPREIT:** An umbrella-partnership REIT with an operating partnership that can receive the property.

**Property Valuation:** Determining the property’s contribution value to set the OP units received.

**OP Unit Determination:** Calculating units by dividing property value by unit value.

**Contribution:** Transfer of property to the operating partnership in exchange for OP units.

**Section 721:** The code provision that generally makes the contribution tax-deferred.

**Holding Period:** The time before OP units can typically convert to shares.

**Distributions:** Income paid on OP units while the investor holds them.

**Conversion:** A generally taxable exchange of OP units for REIT shares.

**Tax Protection Agreement:** A negotiated agreement intended to protect deferred gain from premature triggering.

**Contribution Agreement:** The agreement that effects the Section 721 transaction.

**Partnership Agreement:** The agreement governing the limited partner’s rights.

**Limited Partner:** The OP-unit holder’s role in the operating partnership.

**1031-then-721 Path:** Reaching OP units through a DST’s later UPREIT exit.

**Built-In Gain:** Deferred gain that a tax protection agreement can address.
