# Opportunity Zone Deadlines and Program Timeline

<article class="article">
<p class="lead">Opportunity Zone investing is governed by a set of important dates and deadlines — and because the program has recently changed significantly (made permanent, with new rules and zones under the 2025 OZ 2.0 legislation), keeping track of the timeline is more important than ever. Key dates include the 180-day window to invest a gain, the date the deferred gain is recognized (which differs between the original and revised program), and the program's designation and transition dates (the new zone map and the overlap period). Missing a deadline (like the 180-day window) can forfeit benefits, and the timing affects your tax outcomes. This guide lays out the key Opportunity Zone dates, when deferred gain is recognized, the program's permanence and transitions, how timing affects your benefits, and how to stay current. Critically, the rules are time-sensitive and evolving — verify the current law with your tax advisor.</p>
<h2 id="key-dates">Key Opportunity Zone dates</h2>
<p>Several key dates govern <a href="opportunity-zones-guide.html">Opportunity Zone</a> investing. The 180-day investment window — you have 180 days from realizing a capital gain to invest it into a QOF (with flexible start dates for pass-through gains). This is the deadline to act to earn the OZ benefits for a gain.</p>
<p>The deferred-gain recognition date — the date your deferred original gain is recognized (taxed): under the original program (OZ 1.0), December 31, 2026 (a fixed date); under OZ 2.0 (for post-2026 investments), a rolling 5 years from investing. The 10-year mark — <a href="opportunity-zones-at-the-ten-year-mark.html">the point, 10 years after investing, at which you qualify for the tax-free exclusion</a> of the OZ investment's appreciation.</p>
<p>And program designation dates — the current zone map (in effect, with designations running through the end of 2028) and the new zone map (effective January 1, 2027, under the permanent program). So the key dates include the 180-day window, the recognition date, the 10-year mark, and the zone-designation dates. Key Opportunity Zone dates — the 180-day investment window, the deferred-gain recognition date (December 31, 2026 for OZ 1.0; rolling 5 years for OZ 2.0), the 10-year mark for the exclusion, and the zone-designation dates (current through 2028, new map January 1, 2027) — govern OZ investing. Tracking them is essential. Understanding the key dates frames the timeline. Key OZ dates include the 180-day investment window, the deferred-gain recognition date, the 10-year exclusion mark, and the zone-designation dates — all essential to track.</p>
<h2 id="when-recognized">When deferred gain is recognized</h2>
<p>The deferred-gain recognition date is a critical timing point that differs between the program versions. Under the original program (OZ 1.0), <a href="opportunity-zone-gain-recognition-the-2026-deadline-and-beyond.html">all deferred gains are recognized on a fixed date of December 31, 2026</a>. So OZ 1.0 investors recognize (and pay tax on) their deferred original gain at the end of 2026, regardless of when they invested. This fixed date is now imminent (from the perspective of mid-2026).</p>
<p>Under the revised program (OZ 2.0, for investments made after December 31, 2026), the deferral is a rolling 5 years from the investment date — the deferred gain is recognized 5 years after you invest (or upon an earlier inclusion event). So OZ 2.0 investments have an individualized recognition date (5 years out), rather than a single fixed date.</p>
<p>So when your deferred gain is recognized depends on the program version: a fixed December 31, 2026 for OZ 1.0, or a rolling 5 years for OZ 2.0. This affects when you'll owe the tax on the original gain (plan for it). When deferred gain is recognized — December 31, 2026 (fixed) for OZ 1.0 investments, or a rolling 5 years from investing for OZ 2.0 (post-2026) investments — is a critical timing point differing by program version. It determines when you owe the original gain's tax. Understanding it shows the recognition timing. The deferred gain is recognized December 31, 2026 (OZ 1.0) or a rolling 5 years after investing (OZ 2.0) — plan for the tax at the applicable date.</p>

<h2 id="sunset-extensions">Program permanence &amp; transitions</h2>
<p>The original Opportunity Zone program had a sunset, but the 2025 legislation made the program permanent — a major change. Under the original program (OZ 1.0), the incentive was temporary (with the deferral recognition date of December 31, 2026, and zone designations set to expire), creating a sunset that loomed over the program.</p>
<p>The <a href="opportunity-zones-2-0.html">2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which ushered in OZ 2.0</a>, made the Opportunity Zone incentive permanent, establishing a recurring cycle of zone designations (updating the zone map roughly every 10 years) — eliminating the prior sunset. So the program is now a permanent feature, not a temporary one set to expire.</p>
<p>The transition involves a new zone map (effective January 1, 2027, with governors nominating from mid-2026) overlapping the current map (which runs through the end of 2028) for a period. So the program moved from a sunsetting temporary incentive to a permanent one, with a zone-map transition underway. Program permanence &amp; transitions — the original program's sunset (temporary incentive, expiring designations) replaced by the 2025 legislation making OZ permanent (recurring designations), with a new zone map (effective January 1, 2027) overlapping the current map (through 2028) — mark a major change. The sunset is gone; transitions are underway. Understanding this shows the program's evolution. The OZ program, once a sunsetting temporary incentive, is now permanent (under the 2025 OZ 2.0 law), with a zone-map transition (new map January 1, 2027, overlapping the current through 2028).</p>
<h2 id="timing-benefits">How timing affects your benefits</h2>
<p>Timing affects your OZ benefits in several ways. The 180-day window — missing it forfeits the OZ benefits for a gain (you must invest within the window). So meeting the investment deadline is essential to earning any benefits.</p>
<p>The recognition date — when your deferred gain is recognized (and taxed) affects your tax planning (you'll owe the tax then, so plan for the liability). For OZ 1.0 investors, the December 31, 2026 date means a near-term tax bill on the deferred gain. The 10-year mark — you must hold from your investment date to the 10-year mark to earn the tax-free exclusion (timing your hold to reach 10 years is essential to the marquee benefit).</p>
<p>And the program version (OZ 1.0 vs. OZ 2.0) — the timing of your investment (before or after December 31, 2026) determines which rules apply (the recognition timing, the available benefits). So timing affects whether you get benefits (the 180-day window), when you owe tax (the recognition date), whether you get the exclusion (the 10-year hold), and which rules apply (the program version). Timing determines whether you earn benefits (180-day window), when you owe tax (recognition date), whether you get the exclusion (10-year hold), and which rules apply (program version).</p>
