# 1031 Exchange for Land and Vacant Lots

I keep seeing owners treat a long-held parcel as if it were only a number on a balance sheet. Land can appreciate for years without producing a dollar of income. Selling it may unlock cash, but it can also turn a dormant gain into a current tax bill.

First-order thinking is that land is easy because it is undeveloped. Second-order thinking asks whether it was held as investment rather than dealer inventory, whether a usable replacement can be found inside the calendar, and whether the next asset improves income or diversification. A 1031 exchange can turn dormant appreciation into productive real estate, but only after those questions are answered.

## Does Vacant Land Qualify?

Yes, if it is held for investment or business use. Vacant land is real property and is like-kind to virtually all U.S. investment real estate: apartment buildings, net-lease stores, DST interests, farmland, and other land. The replacement need not be another parcel.

The controlling issue is intent. Land held for appreciation or business use can qualify. Land held primarily for sale does not.

## The Held-for-Investment Requirement

There is no statutory minimum holding period. Holding for years, no development-and-sale activity, and investment treatment on tax returns support the case. A quick resale, frequent sales, or subdivision and marketing of lots points toward dealer property.

Land often produces no income, so the proof of investment intent is usually the holding pattern, the absence of dealer activity, and the tax record. Document those facts.

## From Dormant Land to Income Property

The practical appeal is simple: exchange appreciated, non-income land into a rental, net-lease property, or diversified DST. Rather than pay tax and reinvest the remainder, the owner can deploy the full exchange equity into assets producing rent. One concentrated parcel can also be divided among several income properties or DST interests.

## Land Into a DST

A Delaware Statutory Trust can work for an owner who wants income without management. It is passive, professionally managed, and can close in days. That speed can matter because sourcing an appropriate land replacement inside the [45-day identification period](1031-exchange-identification-rules.html) can be difficult.

A DST can convert non-income, illiquid land into diversified income-producing real estate without a management burden. It is sold only to [accredited investors](insights.html) through a [private placement memorandum](how-to-review-a-ppm.html). It is also a speculative, illiquid security, not a substitute for underwriting.

## Key Takeaways
- Investment land is broadly like-kind to qualifying real estate.
- It must be investment or business property, not subdivided dealer inventory.
- Land can become direct income property or a passive, fast-closing DST.

## The Dealer-Property Pitfall

Dealer property is the major land-exchange risk. A parcel subdivided into lots and marketed for sale, or land bought and resold frequently, may be inventory held primarily for sale. That treatment makes the gain ordinary income and makes the property ineligible for a 1031.

The same parcel may qualify when passively held for appreciation and fail when developed and sold as lots. If development, subdivision, or repeated sales are involved, have the CPA assess the specific parcel before it is sold.

## Raw Land, Farmland, and Development

Raw land held for appreciation qualifies. Entitlements, subdivision, or infrastructure undertaken with the intent to sell can shift the analysis toward dealer status. A 1031 can be used to move into raw land held for investment or out of developable land before development; an improvement exchange may matter if acquired land is being built on in the exchange window.

Farmland and agricultural land held for investment or farm use also qualify. Since **2017**, only land and buildings qualify; equipment, livestock, and other personal property must be handled separately to avoid [boot](insights.html). A retiring farmer may exchange real farmland into passive income property.

## Calendar, Valuation, and Examples

Land follows the usual **45-day** identification and **180-day** completion periods, but parcels can be scarce and slow to transact. Search before sale and identify backups. A DST can be the primary replacement or a deadline backstop.

Value sets the equal-or-greater-value target. Raw land is generally valued through comparable sales, location, and development potential rather than cap rate and cash flow. A qualified appraisal supports the sale, replacement sizing, and full-proceeds reinvestment.

Consider an investor who bought **40** acres for **$100,000** **fifteen** years ago and now owns land worth **$600,000**. A **$600,000** net-lease replacement can convert the parcel into rent while deferring the **$500,000** gain. A diversified DST can instead spread the exact sale proceeds across institutional properties. A retiring farmer can exchange farm real property into DSTs while separately handling equipment and other personal property. These are illustrative examples, not projections.

## Is a Land Exchange Right for You?

It can fit if you own appreciated investment land and want income, diversification, or another real-estate position. It is a weak fit for dealer or personal-use land, or high-basis land with little gain. Do not force a poor parcel purchase merely to meet the calendar; a DST may offer a better way to preserve the exchange while evaluating direct property.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does vacant land qualify for a 1031 exchange?

Yes, when held for investment or business use, not primarily for sale. It may be exchanged into land, income property, or a DST.

### Can I exchange land for a rental property?

Yes. It is a common way to convert non-income appreciation into cash flow, directly or through a passive DST.

### What disqualifies land from a 1031 exchange?

Dealer inventory and personal-use land. Subdivision-and-sale activity or frequent resale can create ordinary-income dealer treatment.

### Can I exchange land into a DST?

Yes. A DST can close quickly, provide passive professionally managed income, and solve the problem of finding land in **45 days**.

### Is subdivided land eligible for a 1031?

Generally not where lots were subdivided to sell. Passive appreciation without subdivision-and-sale activity can qualify; confirm the facts with the CPA.

### Does farmland qualify for a 1031 exchange?

Yes, for investment or farming use. Since **2017**, only its real property qualifies; equipment and livestock are separate.

### How do I prove land is held for investment?

Use the holding period, passive purpose, tax-return treatment, and absence of subdivision-and-sale activity.

### Can I exchange land for land?

Yes, but appropriate parcels may be scarce and slow to close. Identify a DST backup before the deadline.

### Can I use a 1031 to move into land for development?

Yes for investment land, but later development-and-sale can create dealer status. Coordinate development timing with the CPA.

### Why is land hard to exchange within 45 days?

Land inventory can be scarce and transactions slow, which makes the **45/180-day** schedule tight. A fast DST can reduce that risk.

### Can I exchange land that has a homesite on it?

Mixed personal and investment use requires a documented allocation. The personal portion generally does not qualify and may use Section **121**.

### Does exchanging land defer all my gain?

It can when replacement real property is equal or greater in value, all equity is reinvested, and debt is replaced. Debt-free land generally requires full-proceeds reinvestment.

### Can a farmer retire by exchanging farmland into a DST?

Yes. Real farmland can become passive DST income with gain deferred; equipment and personal property remain outside the exchange.

### What is the biggest mistake in land exchanges?

Dealer characterization is first; failing to find a replacement in time is second. Planning, a CPA review, and a DST backup address both.

### Is a land exchange right for me?

It can be compelling for appreciated investment land to be converted to income or diversification, not for dealer, personal-use, or high-basis land.

### How is land valued for a 1031 exchange?

Comparable sales, location, and development potential are central. An appraisal sets a defensible sale and equal-or-greater-value target.

### Can I exchange appreciated land into a net-lease property?

Yes. A net lease can turn dormant land into low-management rent from a creditworthy tenant. A net-lease DST is the passive diversified version.

### Do I need an appraisal for a land exchange?

It is not strictly required, but is strongly advisable for a defensible value, replacement sizing, and transaction support.

### Can I exchange land in one state for property in another?

Yes for U.S. investment real property. California-like state clawback rules can apply, so check state treatment with the CPA.

### What replacement options work best for landowners?

Direct rentals or net-lease assets create cash flow; DSTs offer passive diversified income and speed. A direct property plus DST sleeve is another option.

### Is exchanging land into a DST a good idea?

It can be for an accredited investor seeking income without management and a quick close. It remains a speculative, illiquid security offered through a PPM.

### How much of my land’s gain can I defer?

The entire gain when all proceeds enter equal-or-greater-value real property and debt is replaced. A shortfall is taxable boot.

### Can I exchange inherited land?

Yes if held for investment. The date-of-death step-up may leave little immediate gain, but later appreciation can make an exchange useful.

### Does my land need to produce income to qualify?

No. Appreciation-only land can qualify if investment intent is clear and it is neither personal use nor dealer property.

### Can I exchange land for multiple properties?

Yes. One parcel can be divided among several direct replacements or DSTs to diversify a concentrated position.

### What happens to my land’s low basis in an exchange?

It carries into the replacement. The deferred gain is recognized on a later taxable sale or may be affected by a step-up at death.

### Is now a good time to exchange appreciated land?

That depends on goals, replacement quality, and market conditions. The tax deferral is consistent; timing is an investment decision for the owner and advisers.

## Glossary

- **Held for Investment:** Property held for investment or business rather than sale.
- **Dealer Property:** Subdivided lots or other inventory held for sale; ordinary income and ineligible.
- **Income Property:** Real estate producing rental or operating income.
- **Like-Kind:** Broad U.S. investment-real-estate category.
- **Subdivision:** Dividing land into lots; lot sales can create dealer status.
- **Raw Land:** Undeveloped land eligible when held for investment, not development-and-sale.
- **Farmland:** Agricultural investment or farm-use land; bundled equipment does not qualify.
- **Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** Passive, fast-closing fractional replacement.
- **Investment Intent:** Income or appreciation purpose shown by holding pattern.
- **45-Day Identification Period:** Replacement-identification window.
- **Boot:** Taxable cash, debt relief, or personal property received.
- **Section 121 Exclusion:** Home-sale exclusion potentially relevant to a personal homesite.
