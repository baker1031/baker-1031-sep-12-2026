# Who Should Consider a 721 Exchange? Investor Profiles

[Home](baker1031.html) › Insights › Who Should Consider a 721 Exchange? Investor Profiles

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read**

I start with fit, because a 721 is not a universal solution. It can be an excellent match for an owner who wants passivity, estate planning, diversification, or a route to liquidity. It can be a poor match for the owner who wants control and ongoing 1031 flexibility.

First-order thinking begins with tax deferral. Second-order thinking matches the structure to the investor's goals and circumstances. The useful profiles are the tired landlord, estate planner, concentrated owner, and liquidity seeker. The active investor who values control and flexibility belongs on the other side of the line.

## The tired landlord

The tired landlord has grown weary of managing rental property: tenants, maintenance, vacancies, and the proverbial 3 a.m. phone call. The owner wants to keep real-estate capital working for income and tax deferral without continuing the operational work.

A [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html) can move this owner into passive REIT ownership. By contributing property for [OP units](op-units-explained.html), the former landlord receives distributions from the portfolio while the REIT handles property management. The investor leaves day-to-day operations behind and keeps a real-estate investment on a tax-deferred basis.

This profile often includes aging owners, owners focused on business, family, or travel, and anyone simply done with landlording. For this investor, passivity is the central benefit. The tired landlord is one of the clearest and most common 721 candidates.

## The estate planner

The estate planner wants to transfer appreciated real-estate wealth to heirs efficiently, divide the inheritance fairly, and simplify the estate. The focus is not merely current income. It is what heirs receive and what tax burden travels with the asset.

The 721 can support that goal in two ways. Under current law, a [step-up in basis](1031-exchange-step-up-in-basis-at-death.html) at death can erase deferred gain for heirs. OP units can also divide cleanly among several heirs, unlike one building that may force joint management or a sale.

An older owner with appreciated property and multiple heirs may therefore defer gain during life, leave unit interests at death, and provide a divisible inheritance. The estate planner is a strong 721 profile because the potential step-up and divisibility directly address wealth-transfer goals.

## The concentrated owner

The concentrated owner has much of their wealth in one property or a small group of properties. That concentration makes the outcome dependent on a single tenant, local market, property type, or sector. A tenant departure or market decline can affect a large share of wealth.

The 721 can turn that single-property position into a stake in the REIT portfolio across many properties, markets, and types. The investor exchanges concentrated exposure for diversified exposure, which can reduce the risk that one property's performance determines the entire outcome.

This profile is common among owners who built wealth in one large asset and want to protect it through diversification, especially when a large loss would be harder to recover from. Diversification is the reason the concentrated owner can be a strong 721 candidate.

## The liquidity seeker

The liquidity seeker wants a path to access wealth over time for retirement income, expenses, or opportunities. A single building is illiquid and generally cannot be sold in pieces. The owner values the ability to draw on capital gradually.

OP units can generally be converted to REIT shares, which for a public REIT may be sold for cash. That gives the investor a path to liquidity unavailable through an all-or-nothing property sale. The holder can convert gradually and manage the tax associated with conversion.

This profile can include retirees planning to draw down real-estate wealth or owners who want an option to access cash without selling an entire property. The liquidity is a path, not immediate tax-free cash, but convertible units can be a useful fit for investors valuing gradual access.

## Who should NOT consider a 721 exchange

The active investor who values control is the clearest non-fit. This owner wants to make property decisions, manage assets, and direct investment strategy. The passivity that benefits the tired landlord is a drawback for this investor because the REIT controls the portfolio.

Also poorly suited is the investor who values flexibility: the ability to keep exchanging property, reposition among assets, or return to direct real estate. The 721 is one-way because OP units are not 1031-eligible. For these owners, a [1031 exchange](1031-exchange-guide.html) that keeps them in direct real estate may be a better match.

Investors uncomfortable with REIT dependence, the securities nature of the units, complexity, or the commitment to REIT ownership should also be cautious. Control and flexibility are the things these investors value, and those are the things the 721 gives up.

## Key Takeaways

- The tired landlord fits the 721's passivity and can stop managing while retaining real-estate exposure.
- The estate planner fits its potential basis step-up and divisibility for wealth transfer.
- The concentrated owner and liquidity seeker fit its diversification and convertible path to cash.
- The active investor who values control and flexibility should generally not consider a 721; a 1031 is usually more suitable.

## Finding your profile

Start with the question of which profile fits. Are you ready to stop managing? Focused on heirs? Too concentrated in one asset? Seeking access to wealth over time? One or more affirmative answers can indicate that 721 benefits align with the investor's goals.

The contrary test matters just as much. Does the investor want management control and the ability to continue exchanging real estate? If so, the 721's passivity and one-way nature are unlikely to fit, and direct real estate through a 1031 may be more appropriate.

Many owners fit multiple positive profiles. A tired landlord can also be an estate planner and a concentrated owner; the combined alignment may strengthen the case. Others clearly fit the active-investor profile. Working through the goals and circumstances with professional guidance is how to test fit honestly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who should consider a 721 exchange?

Owners fitting the tired-landlord, estate-planner, concentrated-owner, or liquidity-seeker profile may be candidates. Those profiles align with passivity, potential step-up and divisibility, diversification, and a path to liquidity. Active investors valuing control and flexibility generally should not use it.

### What is the “tired landlord” profile?

It is an owner tired of tenants, maintenance, vacancies, and active management but still wanting real-estate income and investment exposure. A 721 can replace management work with passive REIT distributions while deferring gain. It is a common candidate profile.

### Is a 721 exchange good for estate planning?

It can be. OP units may receive a basis step-up at death that erases deferred gain for heirs under current law, and they divide more easily among several heirs than a single property. Those features can serve tax-efficient, clean wealth-transfer goals.

### Should a concentrated owner do a 721 exchange?

It can be a strong fit. A concentrated owner can exchange a single property for exposure to a diversified REIT portfolio across properties, markets, and types, reducing reliance on one asset. The investor must still evaluate the REIT and trade-offs.

### Who should NOT do a 721 exchange?

Active owners who want control over decisions and flexibility to keep exchanging under Section 1031. Also be cautious if the investor is uncomfortable with REIT dependence, securities, complexity, or commitment. These owners may be better served by direct real estate and a 1031.

### What is the “liquidity seeker” profile?

It is an owner wanting a path to access wealth over time for retirement, expenses, or opportunities. Convertible OP units can provide a route to REIT shares and cash, especially for a public REIT, though conversion is taxable and liquidity is not immediate.

### Can I fit multiple profiles?

Yes. An aging owner may be a tired landlord, estate planner, and concentrated owner at once. More than one matching profile can strengthen the 721 case because multiple benefits align with the investor's objectives.

### How do I know if I'm a good candidate?

Identify goals and profile. One or more of tired landlord, estate planner, concentrated owner, and liquidity seeker can support fit. The active investor who values control and flexibility generally indicates a non-fit. Professional guidance can help make the assessment honestly.

### Is the 721 exchange right for younger investors?

It can be, but it is often more suitable for owners later in their investment journey who want to step back, diversify, plan estates, or access liquidity. Younger owners focused on building, managing, acquiring, and exchanging direct property often value the control and flexibility the 721 gives up. Individual goals matter more than age alone.

### What if I value both income and not managing?

That is essentially the tired-landlord profile. The 721 can provide distributions from a diversified REIT portfolio without property-management work, preserving tax deferral while held. It can be a strong fit for an owner who wants income but is done operating property.

### Should I consult a professional about my fit?

Yes. A financial advisor addresses suitability and fit, a CPA addresses tax, and an estate attorney addresses estate objectives. The decision is significant and individual. Good guidance includes being candid when the 721 is not the right strategy.

### Do I need to be an accredited investor for a 721 exchange?

Generally yes. OP units and related REIT securities are typically offered to accredited investors who meet income or net-worth thresholds and pass suitability review. The DST-bridge route also generally involves securities available to accredited, suitable investors.

### Can a 721 exchange fit a charitable-giving goal?

Potentially. OP-unit divisibility and a possible basis step-up can interact with philanthropic and estate strategies. The analysis is specialized and depends on goals and unit terms, so coordinate charitable planning with the estate attorney and tax advisor.

### What if I'm unsure which profile I fit?

That is common. Investors may have mixed goals or be uncertain whether they value passivity more than control or diversification more than flexibility. Take time to work through priorities with an advisor, CPA, and attorney, and compare alternatives such as a 1031. There is no need to rush a generally irreversible decision.

## Glossary

**Investor Profile**  
A type of investor characterized by goals and circumstances.

**Tired Landlord**  
An owner ready to stop managing property, fitting the 721's passivity.

**Estate Planner**  
An owner focused on wealth transfer, fitting the 721's estate benefits.

**Concentrated Owner**  
An owner heavily in one property, fitting the 721's diversification.

**Liquidity Seeker**  
An owner wanting a path to cash, fitting the 721's liquidity.

**Active Investor**  
An owner valuing control and flexibility, generally not fitting the 721.

**Passivity**  
Hands-off ownership, the benefit for the tired landlord.

**Step-Up in Basis**  
The death-time reset, the benefit for the estate planner.

**Divisibility**  
Easy division of units among heirs, an estate benefit.

**Diversification**  
Spreading risk across a portfolio, the benefit for the concentrated owner.

**Liquidity Path**  
The convertible units' route to cash, the benefit for the liquidity seeker.

**Control**  
Decision-making over real estate, valued by the active investor.

**Flexibility**  
The ability to keep exchanging, valued by the active investor.

**Suitability Review**  
The assessment of whether the 721 fits your profile.

**Fit**  
The alignment of the 721 exchange with your goals and profile.

**Wealth Transfer**  
Passing real estate wealth to heirs, the estate planner's goal.
