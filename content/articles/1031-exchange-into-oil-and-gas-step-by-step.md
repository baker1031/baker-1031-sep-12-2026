# 1031 Exchange Into Oil & Gas: A Step-by-Step Guide

I have been thinking about how often the tax structure gets more attention than the replacement asset. That tendency gets sharper when a real-estate investor considers oil and gas. The five 1031 steps are familiar. The market for qualifying mineral interests often is not.

An exchange into oil and gas follows the same basic sequence as an exchange into a building: engage a qualified intermediary, sell the relinquished property, identify replacements within **45 days**, close within **180 days**, and report the transaction on Form 8824. The difference is that minerals add sourcing, title, characterization, trailing-income, and depletion questions to a fixed calendar.

First-order thinking is that a mineral or royalty interest offers a new source of income and tax deferral. Second-order thinking asks whether the interest is actually qualifying real property, whether the price and commodity exposure are acceptable, who is running the assets, how liquid the investment is, and whether the transaction can close on time. The exchange mechanics cannot repair a poor replacement decision.

For broader context, see the [pillar guide on 1031 exchanges for minerals and royalties](mineral-rights-1031-guide.html).

## Before you start: confirm the plan

Begin by deciding why oil and gas belongs in the replacement plan. The source identifies several possible reasons: income that may be partly sheltered by depletion, diversification away from real estate, or a repositioning from one mineral interest into another. Then decide what kind of replacement you are prepared to own: direct producing royalties, a diversified royalty-pool DST, or a mix.

This choice changes the work. Direct interests require deal flow, specialized diligence, and enough time to close. A DST is a turnkey, fast-closing structure. Investors who settle the strategy before the relinquished sale make the **45-day** period a selection window instead of a frantic search.

Confirm eligibility on both sides before the formal steps begin. The relinquished asset must be investment real property. The replacement oil-and-gas interest must have the required perpetual real-property character. A fee mineral interest or perpetual royalty is a cleaner fit. A production payment and the equipment component of a working interest do not qualify. A tax adviser familiar with minerals should review the actual conveyance, especially for a direct purchase.

Build the team early: a [qualified intermediary](insights.html) comfortable with oil and gas, a CPA to model the tax and file the return, and an advisor to source and vet interests and coordinate deadlines. Mineral exchanges reward specialized experience. Preparation is what makes the formal process routine.

## Step 1 — Engage a qualified intermediary

The QI must be engaged before the relinquished-property sale closes. It is an independent party that receives the proceeds so the taxpayer never has actual or constructive receipt. If money reaches the investor, an attorney's trust account, or an account the investor controls, the exchange is disqualified with no fix.

The practical sequence is to sign an exchange agreement and assign the QI into the purchase-and-sale contract before closing. Closing instructions then send the proceeds to the QI's segregated qualified escrow account. Because the funds are irreplaceable if mishandled, the source says to check for segregated accounts, fidelity bonding, and errors-and-omissions insurance. Oil-and-gas experience and familiarity with trailing-income issues matter too.

Early engagement also keeps the documentation clean. The most common mistake is closing a sale first and asking about a 1031 afterward. By then, the essential step has already been missed.

## Step 2 — Sell the relinquished property

Once the QI is in place, sell the relinquished real estate or mineral interest. The proceeds go to the QI rather than to the taxpayer. The closing date is day zero for both hard deadlines: the **45-day** identification period and the **180-day** closing period begin together, and neither can be paused.

For relinquished real estate, the familiar sale work includes prorated rents, security deposits, and mortgage payoff. For relinquished minerals, pay particular attention to pre-closing production income that arrives later. Those trailing royalty checks must be routed under the QI's instructions so they do not become receipt of exchange funds. The sale contract should also reflect exchange intent and the QI assignment.

There is a late-year timing issue to review with the CPA. The due date for that year's tax return can arrive before the **180th** day and shorten the available closing window. Filing an extension for that return restores the full **180 days**. It is an administrative detail with real consequences.

## Step 3 — Identify oil and gas replacement interests

Within **45 days** of the sale, identify replacement interests in writing. The signed notice must unambiguously describe every candidate and be delivered to the QI, not to an agent. In oil and gas, this is often the tightest part of the process because the market can be fragmented and opaque. Finding, valuing, and vetting a qualifying interest is difficult to do from scratch after the sale.

Choose the identification rule before attempting to widen the list.

- **3-property rule:** up to three replacement properties of any value.
- **200% rule:** any number of properties with total value up to **200%** of the relinquished value.
- **95% rule:** available but rarely used.

For an oil-and-gas exchange, the source favors a disciplined pattern: identify the primary target—whether a direct interest or royalty-pool DST—and at least one fast-closing backup, usually a DST, under the 3-property rule. After day **45**, nothing new can be added. A direct mineral purchase may be slow or uncertain to close, so an identified DST backup can keep the exchange from failing when a primary interest falls through.

> Sourcing qualifying minerals inside **45 days** is the hardest step. Identify a primary target and a fast-closing DST backup before the window closes, because the list cannot be expanded afterward.

## Step 4 — Close within 180 days

Close on the identified replacement interests within **180 days** of the original sale, using the QI's escrowed proceeds to fund the acquisition. A newly discovered interest cannot replace the original list on day 120. Direct mineral transactions can require specialized title and conveyance work, so the calendar can tighten faster than expected.

Full deferral also depends on the value and debt calculation. Acquire replacement interests of equal or greater value than the net sale price and reinvest all equity. Minerals are often unencumbered, so an owner exchanging unencumbered minerals may have no debt to replace. An investor exchanging out of mortgaged real estate must replace that debt with financing or cash to avoid mortgage [boot](insights.html). Cash kept or value not replaced is boot and taxable up to the gain.

If a direct deal threatens to miss day **180**, a previously identified royalty-pool or real-estate DST can generally close in days. Once the QI's funds are fully deployed into identified replacements inside the window, the exchange is complete and the gain is deferred, subject to the remaining reporting requirements.

## Step 5 — Report the exchange on Form 8824

Report the exchange on **Form 8824**, Like-Kind Exchanges, with the tax return for the year of the relinquished sale. The form records the properties, dates, values, boot, deferred and recognized gain, and the new basis. Even a fully deferred exchange must be reported. The deferral is claimed through this filing; it is not automatic.

For oil and gas, the form also captures taxable components such as boot received, the equipment portion of a relinquished working interest, and the carryover basis that governs later depletion or depreciation. The CPA prepares the form and the related tax work. Involving the CPA from the outset is preferable to introducing the exchange at filing time.

Keep a clean record: QI agreement, closing statements for both legs, written identification, valuations, and any eligibility opinion. A mineral exchange may attract more scrutiny than a routine real-estate swap. Documentation demonstrating qualifying interests and correct mechanics is important protection.

## The mineral-specific wrinkles to watch

Oil-and-gas replacements bring several specialized issues. Sourcing and diligence are the largest. Unlike buying a building, a mineral purchase may require analysis of reserves and decline curves, operator review, clear-title confirmation, and a way through a fragmented market. Those are demanding tasks inside the exchange windows. A royalty-pool DST can ease this burden because the sponsor has already handled the underlying sourcing and diligence.

Characterization is the next issue. On the replacement side, confirm a perpetual real-property interest rather than a production payment, a short-dated term interest, or nonqualifying equipment within a working interest. The same analysis applies when exchanging out of minerals. A tax adviser's review of the conveyances can prevent the costly mistake of using a nonqualifying interest.

Then there are trailing income and depletion. Royalty proceeds may arrive on a lag and need to be routed carefully through the QI. After acquiring replacement minerals, qualifying owners may generally claim depletion. The source notes that percentage depletion is often **15%** of gross income for qualifying owners, and that carryover basis affects the calculation. These items are manageable, but they are reasons to prepare earlier and use a team with appropriate experience.

## Choosing your replacement interests

The core choice is direct interests, a royalty-pool DST, or a combination. Direct producing royalties can offer control and no DST fees. They also require sourcing infrastructure, specialized diligence, and the ability to close before the **180-day** deadline. Without a diversified basket, they may leave the investor concentrated in specific wells and operators.

A royalty-pool DST packages diversified, pre-vetted royalty interests in a turnkey, fast-closing form. The source states that it qualifies under Revenue Ruling **2004-86**, diversifies across wells and basins, generally passes through depletion, and can close in days. The tradeoffs are fees, passivity, and illiquidity. For many real-estate investors unfamiliar with mineral markets, the source describes the DST as the practical route and a reliable backup.

A blended approach can pair a direct interest the investor specifically wants with a DST that adds diversification and closing certainty. The selection should fit the investor's expertise, preference for control or convenience, desired commodity exposure, and confidence that the investment can actually fund on time. The best replacement is the one that advances the objective and can be acquired within the deadline.

## Common pitfalls and how to avoid them

The first pitfall is waiting until after the sale to start the search. The mineral market can leave a late starter without viable identified interests by day **45**. Decide on the strategy, line up candidates, and have a DST backup in view before the relinquished property closes.

The second is buying a nonqualifying interest: calling a production payment or short-dated term interest a royalty, or overlooking equipment in a working interest. That can void deferral or create unexpected tax. A tax adviser should characterize the interest before commitment. The third is mishandling constructive receipt or trailing income, which QI involvement and clear instructions are intended to prevent.

The fourth is a value or debt shortfall: buying down in value, failing to reinvest all equity, or not replacing debt. A defensible valuation and a value-matching calculation with the CPA before closing help prevent accidental boot. Early preparation and an experienced team address each of these risks.

### Key takeaways

- The five steps are familiar: QI before selling, sell, identify in **45 days**, close in **180 days**, and file Form **8824**.
- The difficult part is sourcing and diligencing qualifying minerals under the clock. Preparation and an identified DST backup matter.
- Confirm a qualifying perpetual real-property interest and route trailing royalty income through the QI.
- Match value and debt for full deferral. A royalty-pool DST can be a practical turnkey replacement and backup.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the steps to do a 1031 exchange into oil and gas?

Engage a QI before the sale closes, sell the relinquished property, identify replacement interests within **45 days**, close within **180 days**, and report the exchange on Form **8824**. The mechanics are the same as any 1031; the mineral-specific challenge is sourcing and vetting qualifying interests under the clock.

### Can I 1031 exchange real estate into oil and gas?

Yes. A qualifying perpetual mineral or royalty interest is real property and may be like-kind to relinquished real estate. Clean perpetual royalties and a royalty-pool DST are the clearer routes; working interests carry additional equipment and operating issues.

### Why do I need a qualified intermediary?

An independent QI must receive the proceeds before closing so the taxpayer never has constructive receipt. If the money reaches the taxpayer, the exchange is disqualified without a fix. Engage a QI before sale, prefer mineral-closing experience, and confirm fund security such as segregated escrow and bonding.

### How hard is it to identify minerals within 45 days?

It is harder than identifying a building because the market is fragmented and opaque and the work includes sourcing, valuation, and eligibility review. Begin before the sale and identify a fast-closing royalty-pool DST as a primary option or backup under the 3-property rule.

### What can I exchange into—direct minerals or a DST?

Either. Direct interests offer control and no DST fees, but demand sourcing, diligence, and speed. A royalty-pool DST is turnkey, diversified, and fast closing under Revenue Ruling **2004-86**, but has fees and passivity. Some exchangers use a direct interest plus a DST backup.

### What is Form 8824?

It is the IRS Like-Kind Exchanges form filed with the return for the year of the relinquished sale. It reports the properties, dates, values, boot, deferred and recognized gain, and new basis. A fully deferred exchange still needs the filing, generally prepared by the CPA.

### Do I have to replace debt when exchanging into minerals?

If mortgaged real estate is relinquished, replace that debt with financing or cash to avoid mortgage boot. Unencumbered minerals generally have no debt to replace. Full deferral also requires equal-or-greater value and reinvestment of all equity; a shortfall is taxable boot.

### How are trailing royalty checks handled?

Production proceeds for a pre-closing period may arrive after the sale. They should be routed under the QI's instructions so they do not become receipt of exchange funds. Decide who receives trailing income before closing.

### What happens if I cannot close a direct deal in time?

An identified fast-closing royalty-pool or real-estate DST backup can close in days, preserving a valid exchange if the primary direct deal collapses late in the **180-day** window. This is why the source treats a backup as standard insurance in oil-and-gas exchanges.

### How is the replacement mineral interest's eligibility confirmed?

A tax adviser reviews the conveyance to confirm a qualifying perpetual real-property interest, not a production payment treated as debt under §**636**, a short-dated term interest, or the equipment portion of a working interest. Confirm this before committing.

### Does depletion follow the minerals I exchange into?

Once replacement minerals are owned, qualifying owners can generally claim depletion. The source notes percentage depletion is often **15%** of gross royalty income. Carryover basis from the exchange affects the calculation, which the CPA handles with the new depletion schedule.

### When should I start the process?

Before listing or signing anything. Settle the strategy, confirm eligibility, engage the QI before closing, and identify possible replacements and a backup before the sale funds. Earlier involvement from the tax adviser, QI, and advisor leaves more options open.

### Do I need a separate team for an oil-and-gas exchange?

Not a separate team, but a specialized one. The usual QI, CPA, and advisor should ideally understand mineral characterization, valuation, and trailing income. A QI familiar with mineral closings and an advisor who can source suitable interests make the process more manageable.

### Can I exchange out of minerals and into other minerals?

Yes. The same five steps apply when both legs are oil and gas: sell a qualifying perpetual interest and exchange into another qualifying interest or a royalty-pool DST. Confirm both sides' character and watch the working-interest equipment carve-out.

## Glossary

- **Qualified Intermediary (QI):** The independent party that holds exchange proceeds so the taxpayer never has constructive receipt.
- **Relinquished Property:** The real estate or mineral interest sold to begin the exchange.
- **Replacement Interest:** The qualifying oil-and-gas or other real-property interest acquired to complete the exchange.
- **Constructive Receipt:** Access to or control over proceeds that disqualifies an exchange.
- **45-Day Identification Period:** The window after the sale to identify replacement property in writing.
- **180-Day Exchange Period:** The window after the sale to close on replacement property.
- **3-Property Rule:** An identification method allowing up to three replacement properties of any value.
- **Form 8824:** The IRS form reporting a like-kind exchange for the year of the relinquished sale.
- **Boot:** Cash or non-like-kind value received in an exchange; taxable up to the amount of gain.
- **Mortgage Boot:** Taxable gain from replacing less debt than was paid off, unless offset with cash.
- **Royalty-Pool DST:** A DST holding diversified mineral royalty interests as turnkey 1031 replacement property.
- **Perpetual Interest:** An open-ended interest with the durational character of a real-property fee estate; it qualifies for 1031.
- **Production Payment:** A right to a set sum or volume of production, treated as debt under §**636** and not qualifying.
- **Depletion:** A deduction sheltering part of mineral income; percentage depletion is often **15%** of gross income.
- **Trailing Income:** Production proceeds for pre-closing periods that arrive after the sale and must be routed carefully.
- **Carryover Basis:** The adjusted basis of the relinquished property that transfers to the replacement, preserving deferred gain.
