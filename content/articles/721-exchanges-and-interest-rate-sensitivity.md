# 721 Exchanges and Interest-Rate Sensitivity


[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Investments · Updated June 2026 · 12 min read

I keep seeing daily REIT prices mistaken for a new kind of risk. A 721 owner moving into a traded REIT may see rate risk every day, but a directly owned building carried much of the same exposure without sending a daily price.

REITs are sensitive to interest rates. Rates affect borrowing costs, the appeal of distributions relative to bond yields, and capitalization rates used to value real estate. Rising rates can pressure REIT values, especially traded share prices, and can affect distributions and costs. This is a characteristic to understand, not a forecast of rates. See [how interest rates affect REIT returns](how-interest-rates-affect-reit-returns.html), [non-traded REITs](721-exchanges-and-non-traded-reits.html), and the [721 exchange guide](721-exchange-guide.html).

The first-order view is that rising rates can lower a REIT share price. The second-order view asks about debt structure, rent growth, leverage, holding period, and the difference between visible daily volatility and underlying economic exposure. A REIT with growing income may partially offset rate effects over time, but there is no assurance it will.

## Why REITs are interest-rate sensitive

REITs use debt. Higher rates raise the cost of new and refinanced debt, pressuring profitability. Rates also affect valuation: higher rates can raise capitalization rates and lower property values, while lower rates can have the reverse effect.

REIT distributions also compete with bond yields. When bond yields rise, a REIT distribution can look relatively less attractive to income-seeking investors, which can pressure traded REIT share prices. Borrowing costs, yield competition, and cap rates are the main channels linking rates to REIT performance.

## The effect on REIT values

For a publicly traded REIT, rate changes can move the share price and the value associated with OP units. Rising rates can pressure price through yield competition and cap-rate effects; falling rates can support it. The fluctuation is visible daily.

A non-traded REIT’s NAV can also be affected through the value of its real estate, though the effect is less visible because NAV is not market-priced daily. In either case, REIT ownership carries market and valuation risk. A REIT investment can lose value, including the entire investment, and there is no assurance of recovery.

## The effect on distributions

Higher rates can increase borrowing costs and reduce income available for distributions. Distributions are not guaranteed, may be modified or suspended, and may be return of capital, including from offering proceeds or borrowings.

The income effect can be more muted than the value effect. Rents may continue or grow with inflation, and fixed-rate debt can insulate a REIT from higher borrowing costs. The outcome depends on the particular REIT’s rent growth and debt structure.

## Rate sensitivity vs. direct real estate

Direct real estate is also rate-sensitive: rates affect mortgage costs, capitalization rates, valuations, and the broader market. A traded REIT merely marks the exposure to market every day. A building’s value is generally seen only in an appraisal or a sale.

The difference is therefore largely visibility and liquidity, not necessarily the amount of underlying rate exposure. Direct property can feel less volatile because it lacks a daily price.

## Key Takeaways
- REITs are rate-sensitive through borrowing costs, yield competition, and cap-rate effects.
- Rising rates can pressure traded share prices and non-traded NAVs, although the latter move less visibly.
- Rates can pressure distributions, but rent growth and fixed-rate debt can cushion the effect.
- Direct real estate is also rate-sensitive; daily pricing makes REIT exposure more visible.

## Managing rate risk

Rates cannot be controlled, but a REIT can be selected with rate resilience in mind. Consider fixed-rate versus floating-rate debt, reasonable leverage, and sectors or portfolios with rent-growth potential. Excess leverage can increase rate sensitivity and contribute to [REIT underperformance](721-exchange-risks-reit-underperformance.html). [Inflation](reits-and-inflation-hedge-or-headwind.html) can affect rent growth as well.

Time horizon matters. Rate effects can be cyclical; a long-term investor may ride through cycles, while a short-term investor is more exposed to value swings. Investors focused on income may be less affected by price movements until they need to sell or convert.

## The long-term view

Short-term rate movements can move REIT values, but long-term performance is also shaped by real-estate fundamentals: income, appreciation, and rent growth. Income growth may offset some rate pressure over time. That does not remove risk.

For a 721 owner holding for income, tax deferral, and a possible step-up, a long horizon may be appropriate. The point is not to dismiss rate sensitivity; it is to put short-term price changes beside fundamentals and actual liquidity needs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why are REITs sensitive to interest rates?

They use debt, distributions compete with bond yields, and rates affect cap rates and real-estate values. These channels affect value and potentially income for a 721 investor holding a REIT interest.

### How do interest rates affect my REIT holding’s value?

Rising rates can pressure traded share prices; falling rates can support them. Non-traded NAVs are also affected through property valuation but not priced daily. This is market and valuation risk.

### Do rising rates reduce REIT distributions?

They can raise borrowing costs and pressure distributions, but rents may grow and fixed-rate debt may cushion the effect. Distributions are not guaranteed and can be reduced, suspended, or include return of capital.

### Is direct real estate also interest-rate sensitive?

Yes. Rates affect financing and cap rates for direct property. The main difference is that traded REITs display the effect in a daily price while direct property does not.

### Does the 721 exchange increase my interest-rate exposure?

Not necessarily in underlying economic terms, but it can make the exposure more visible when the destination is a traded REIT. A non-traded REIT has less visible NAV movement, though rate exposure remains.

### How can I manage rate risk?

Consider fixed-rate debt, reasonable leverage, rent-growth potential, a long holding period, and an income focus. These steps can position for resilience but cannot remove rate risk.

### Should I worry about rate-driven value swings?

They matter most when you need to sell or convert. A long-term holder may focus more on real-estate fundamentals than short-term rate moves, while recognizing that values can decline.

### Can REITs offset rate effects over time?

Growing rents and income can partially offset rate effects and support value and distributions over time. The result depends on the REIT; it is not assured.

### Does Baker 1031 predict interest rates?

No. It does not base recommendations on rate forecasts. It helps owners understand rate sensitivity and evaluate resilience in the selected REIT.

### How does Baker 1031 help with rate considerations?

It explains value and distribution effects, direct-property comparison, debt structure, rent growth, long horizon, and income focus. Securities are offered through Aurora Securities following suitability review.

### Do all REITs have the same interest-rate sensitivity?

No. Sensitivity differs by fixed- versus floating-rate debt, leverage, sector, lease structure, rent growth, and portfolio characteristics. The details require review.

### Should I avoid a 721 exchange when rates are rising?

Not necessarily. Rate predictions are unreliable, and a 721 exchange is a long-term decision driven by the owner’s goals rather than short-term rate movements. Rising rates can pressure REIT values, but a long-term holder can ride through rate cycles. The strategy’s potential benefits—deferral, diversification, passivity, and estate planning—do not depend on rate timing. If a 721 fits the investor’s goals, consider the REIT’s rate resilience and quality rather than making the decision from a rate forecast. Baker 1031 helps assess the owner’s situation, not time rates.

### Does rising-rate-driven value decline affect my distributions?

Not directly. A rate-driven decline in REIT value or share price does not itself reduce distributions; distributions depend on operating income, including rents, rather than market value. Income can continue while value declines. Rising rates can still affect distributions indirectly if higher costs pressure operating income. Distributions can therefore be relatively resilient to value swings, but they are not guaranteed.

## Glossary

- **Interest-Rate Sensitivity:** The effect of rates on REIT value and income.
- **Borrowing Costs:** Debt costs that rise with refinancing and new borrowing rates.
- **Yield Competition:** Competition between REIT distributions and bond yields.
- **Capitalization Rate:** A valuation rate that can rise with rates and pressure property values.
- **Traded REIT:** A REIT with daily market pricing.
- **Non-Traded REIT:** A REIT with periodic NAV rather than a daily market price.
- **NAV:** Net asset value, the estimated value of a non-traded REIT.
- **Rate-Driven Volatility:** Value fluctuation associated with rate moves.
- **Distribution Resilience:** Relative ability of distributions to withstand rate pressure.
- **Fixed-Rate Debt:** Debt that can insulate a REIT from rising rate costs.
- **Leverage:** Debt use that can increase rate sensitivity.
- **Rent Growth:** Income growth that can offset rate effects.
- **Long-Term View:** Focus on fundamentals over short-term rate swings.
- **Direct Real Estate:** Property with rate exposure that is less visibly priced.
- **Visibility Difference:** Daily REIT pricing versus infrequent property valuation.
- **Rate Risk:** The unremovable exposure to changes in rates.
