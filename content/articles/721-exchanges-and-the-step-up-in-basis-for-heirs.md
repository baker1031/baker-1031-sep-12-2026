# 721 Exchanges and the Step-Up in Basis for Heirs


[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read**

I keep coming back to the question an estate plan ultimately has to answer: what arrives in the hands of the next generation? In a 721 exchange, the answer can be OP units with income, convertibility, real-estate exposure, and a stepped-up basis that may erase the deferred gain.

First-order thinking focuses on deferral during the owner's life. Second-order thinking asks what heirs inherit, what basis they receive, whether they can make their own decisions, and which taxes remain separate. This [estate-planning benefit of a 721 exchange](721-exchange-tax-benefits-deferral-diversification-estate-planning.html) is the point at which the owner’s deferred gain can be erased for heirs.

## What heirs inherit

When an owner holds [OP units](op-units-explained.html) until death, heirs inherit the units: their stake in the REIT operating partnership, its distributions, convertibility to REIT shares, and underlying real-estate exposure. They become the new partners and holders of the unit rights.

Critically, they inherit the units with a [stepped-up basis](721-exchange-estate-planning.html). Their basis resets to fair market value at the owner's death instead of the owner's low carryover basis. The owner's embedded deferred gain is therefore erased, leaving heirs with units at current value and a clean basis. This is the central estate-planning result of the [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html).

## How the step-up works for heirs

Under the general rule for inherited property, an asset included in a decedent's estate receives a basis equal to its date-of-death value. The heirs' basis in OP units is therefore the units' fair market value at death, not the owner's historical basis. The difference represented by the owner's deferred gain is eliminated.

The practical effect is that an heir can convert units to REIT shares or sell them shortly after inheritance with little or no income tax on prior appreciation. The owner’s deferred gain and prior appreciation are erased by the step-up. Heirs can access the value without carrying the income-tax burden deferred by the owner.

> Heirs inherit OP units with basis stepped up to date-of-death value. They can convert or sell with little or no income tax on the prior appreciation that the owner had deferred.

## Heirs' options after inheriting

Heirs can hold inherited OP units, continue receiving distributions, and retain real-estate exposure. They can convert units to REIT shares; because of the step-up, the conversion normally produces little gain. For a public REIT, they can then hold or sell shares for cash.

They can also convert and sell, or redeem where available, to realize value with the step-up minimizing tax. The choice is flexible: hold for income, convert for liquidity, or sell. The step-up minimizes income tax on the previous appreciation across those choices.

## Dividing among multiple heirs

OP units divide cleanly among multiple heirs in a way a single property often cannot. An owner can bequeath specific numbers of units to each heir, giving every heir a distinct, stepped-up portion. This avoids a forced sale or conflicted shared ownership of one real-estate asset.

Each heir can decide independently whether to hold for income, convert for liquidity, or sell. One heir might hold units while another converts and sells. This independent management reduces the conflicts that can arise when several heirs own one property together. Divisibility is a major family advantage of OP units.

## The estate tax consideration

The step-up addresses income tax on deferred gain. **Estate tax is separate.** OP-unit value is included in the decedent's estate and may be subject to estate tax depending on estate size relative to the exemption. [Estate liquidity for heirs](721-exchanges-and-estate-liquidity-for-heirs.html) is therefore a related planning concern.

A large estate might owe estate tax on unit value even though the step-up eliminates income tax on the owner’s deferred gain. Estate-tax planning through exemptions, trusts, or gifting strategies is separate from step-up planning and should be handled with an estate attorney. Owners should plan for both the income-tax benefit and potential estate-tax exposure.

## Key Takeaways

- Heirs inherit OP units with basis reset to date-of-death value, erasing the owner's deferred gain.
- The step-up can let heirs convert or sell with little or no income tax on prior appreciation.
- Heirs can hold for income, convert for liquidity, or sell; the step-up minimizes tax in each case.
- OP units divide cleanly among multiple heirs; estate tax remains a separate consideration from the income-tax step-up.

## Communicating the plan to heirs

Owners using a 721 for estate planning should explain what heirs will inherit, how the step-up benefits them, and the options to hold, convert, or sell. This helps heirs manage an otherwise unfamiliar asset.

Communication also lets the owner coordinate heirs with estate professionals. Heirs should know to work with their CPA and advisors on basis, K-1 reporting, and their choices. This discussion can be part of the broader estate plan and supports a smooth, understood transition.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What do my heirs inherit from a 721 exchange?

They inherit OP units: a stake in the REIT operating partnership, distributions, convertibility to shares, and real-estate exposure, with a stepped-up basis equal to date-of-death value. They become new holders able to hold for income, convert for liquidity, or sell with the step-up minimizing tax. It is a valuable, income-producing, divisible inheritance.

### How does the step-up benefit my heirs?

It resets their basis to date-of-death value and erases the owner's deferred gain. Heirs can convert or sell with little or no income tax on prior appreciation. They can access value or keep the investment without the deferred income tax the owner carried.

### What can my heirs do with inherited units?

They can hold units and receive distributions, convert to REIT shares and then hold or sell shares, or sell or redeem to realize value. The step-up minimizes tax on prior appreciation, giving them flexibility to retain or access the investment.

### Do OP units divide among multiple heirs?

Yes. An owner can leave specific numbers of units to each heir. Every heir receives a distinct stepped-up portion and decides independently whether to hold, convert, or sell. This avoids the shared-property conflicts that arise when heirs inherit a single building.

### Will my heirs owe income tax on inherited units?

Generally little or none on prior appreciation because basis resets to date-of-death value. They may owe tax on appreciation after inheritance and on distributions earned while holding. Their CPA handles their tax reporting.

### Does the step-up eliminate all taxes for my heirs?

It eliminates income tax on the owner’s deferred gain and prior appreciation, but estate tax is separate. Unit value may be included in the owner’s estate and taxed if it exceeds the applicable exemption. Estate planning is distinct from the step-up.

### What is the difference between the step-up and estate tax?

The step-up is an income-tax rule resetting heirs' basis and erasing deferred gain. Estate tax is a separate tax on estate value, including units, if the estate exceeds the exemption. A large estate might owe estate tax even when the step-up removes income tax on gain.

### Should I tell my heirs about the 721 exchange plan?

Yes. Explaining units, the step-up, and heir options prepares them to manage the inheritance. It also lets the family coordinate with CPAs and advisors as part of broader estate discussions.

### What if I want my heirs to keep units long-term?

They can hold inherited units indefinitely, receive distributions, and retain exposure to the REIT investment. The step-up gives them a fresh basis but does not require a sale. Communicating this intention and the units’ value can help.

### Is the step-up for OP units the same as for other inherited assets?

Generally, yes. OP units can receive the same date-of-death step-up as other inherited capital assets, such as real estate or stocks, under current law. This is why units can be effective estate-planning holdings: they are income-producing, divisible, and step-up eligible.

### Do my heirs need to act quickly after inheriting the units?

Not necessarily. Heirs can take time to hold, convert, or sell. Selling relatively soon after inheritance, while value remains close to stepped-up basis, can minimize additional gain, but there is generally no immediate requirement. They should consult their CPA and advisors.

### What if I gift OP units during life instead of at death?

Lifetime gifts generally do not receive a step-up; the recipient receives carryover basis with deferred gain intact. A gift can have other estate-tax purposes, such as removing future appreciation from an estate, but sacrifices the income-tax step-up. The estate attorney and CPA should weigh that trade-off.

### Does the step-up apply if units are held in a trust?

It depends on trust structure. Assets included in the estate at death, such as those in a revocable living trust, generally receive a step-up. Assets held in certain irrevocable trusts outside the estate may not. Trust design should align with step-up and estate-tax goals.

## Glossary

**Step-Up in Basis**  
Resetting heirs' basis to date-of-death value and erasing gain.

**Heirs**  
Those inheriting OP units and benefiting from the step-up.

**Date-of-Death Value**  
Fair market value at death and the heirs' new basis.

**Deferred Gain**  
The owner's untaxed gain erased for heirs by the step-up.

**Carryover Basis**  
The owner's low basis replaced by the step-up for heirs.

**OP Units**  
The income-producing, convertible inherited interest.

**Divisibility**  
Clean division of units among multiple heirs.

**Conversion**  
An heir exchanging units for shares with little tax due to the step-up.

**Distributions**  
Income heirs earn while holding inherited units.

**Estate Tax**  
A separate tax on estate value, distinct from step-up treatment.

**Estate-Tax Exemption**  
The threshold below which estate tax does not apply.

**Section 1014**  
The code section providing step-up in basis at death.

**Income Tax**  
The tax the step-up eliminates on deferred gain for heirs.

**Independent Management**  
Each heir managing divided units independently.

**Wealth Transfer**  
Passing units to heirs, helped by step-up and divisibility.

**Communication**  
Explaining the plan to heirs for a smooth transition.
