# What Is an UPREIT? Umbrella Partnership REIT Basics

[Home](baker1031.html) › Insights › What Is an UPREIT? Umbrella Partnership REIT Basics

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read**

I start with the layer that makes a 721 possible. An UPREIT does not hold its real estate directly. It holds property through an operating partnership, and that partnership is the place where an owner can contribute property for units on a tax-deferred basis.

First-order thinking sees a [real estate investment trust](reits-guide.html). Second-order thinking sees the ownership hierarchy: REIT above, operating partnership below, real estate inside it, and contributors holding units rather than a deed. That structure is the framework for the 721 exchange.

## What is an UPREIT?

An UPREIT is an umbrella partnership real estate investment trust. It owns its real estate indirectly through an operating partnership rather than directly. The REIT is the parent entity; the operating partnership is the “umbrella” beneath it, holding and operating the property portfolio.

The REIT is generally the operating partnership's general partner and majority owner, so it controls the OP. Other parties, including property contributors, can hold limited-partner interests through [OP units](op-units-explained.html).

The partnership layer distinguishes an UPREIT from a traditional REIT. It lets owners contribute property to the OP for units. Section 721 can defer gain on that partnership contribution, allowing the REIT to acquire real estate while the contributor defers tax. The [UPREIT](721-exchange-guide.html) is therefore the structure that makes a 721 exchange possible.

## The umbrella partnership structure

The hierarchy is straightforward:

```text
REIT — issues shares; usually general partner and majority OP owner
  └─ Operating Partnership (OP) — owns and operates the real estate
└─ Real estate portfolio
```

The OP's partners include the REIT and limited partners such as contributors who received OP units in a 721 exchange. The REIT controls the partnership and the real estate. Unit holders have an economic interest: distributions and typically conversion to REIT shares, but not operating control.

The properties sit one layer below the REIT, inside the OP. Contributors receive partnership units rather than REIT shares directly, which is why their transaction is a Section 721 contribution rather than a property sale to the REIT. This REIT-over-OP-over-real-estate architecture is the key enabler of tax-deferred 721 contributions.

## How property owners join an UPREIT

An owner joins by contributing property to the operating partnership for OP units: the 721 exchange. Rather than selling the property to the REIT for taxable cash, the owner contributes it to the OP. The partnership issues units representing the agreed property value, and Section 721 generally defers gain at contribution.

The UPREIT must be willing to accept the property, which needs to fit its portfolio and criteria. The parties value the property to determine units. Once completed, the property joins the REIT portfolio and the former owner becomes a limited partner with diversified REIT exposure.

After joining, the owner holds OP units, receives distributions, and continues to defer gain. Units can generally be converted to REIT shares or cash after a holding period, but conversion recognizes tax. The move therefore replaces direct property ownership with tax-deferred operating-partnership ownership and a later path to REIT shares.

## OP units: your stake in the partnership

OP units are the contributor's stake in the OP. They represent the value of contributed property and give the holder an economic claim on the REIT's portfolio. They participate in distributions generally comparable to REIT dividends and expose the holder to the portfolio's economic results.

The units are typically convertible into REIT shares, often one-for-one, after a holding period. That creates a path to traded REIT shares and their liquidity. The economic exposure can resemble that of REIT shares, but the tax treatment differs: holding OP units defers gain; conversion to shares recognizes it.

OP units are thus a tax-deferred form of REIT ownership. They bridge contributed property to potential REIT shares, preserving deferral while held. A holder can convert for liquidity or hold until death, when a possible step-up in basis may apply under current law.

## UPREIT vs. traditional REIT

| Structure | How it holds property | Can a property owner contribute tax-deferred? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traditional REIT | Directly, on its own balance sheet | Generally no; sale to the REIT is taxable |
| UPREIT | Through an operating partnership | Yes, through a Section 721 contribution to the OP |

A traditional REIT buys and owns property directly, and investors own it through shares. There is no OP layer and therefore no partnership mechanism for an owner to contribute property tax-deferred.

An UPREIT has the operating partnership beneath the REIT. That additional layer is what permits 721 contributions. For public investors buying shares in the market, the distinction usually has little effect. For a property owner seeking a tax-deferred contribution, it is decisive: only an UPREIT with an appropriate operating partnership can accept the property through this structure.

## Key Takeaways

- An UPREIT owns real estate through an operating partnership rather than directly.
- The structure is REIT over operating partnership over real estate, enabling tax-deferred Section 721 property contributions.
- Owners join by contributing property to the OP for OP units.
- Unlike a traditional REIT, an UPREIT can accept property tax-deferred through its operating partnership.

## Why UPREITs exist

UPREITs solve a specific acquisition problem. REITs want property from long-term owners with substantial embedded gain, but a taxable cash sale can deter those owners. The OP gives the REIT another form of consideration: units issued for a tax-deferred contribution rather than cash for a sale.

For REITs, OP units function as acquisition currency. Issuing units can expand the portfolio by attracting sellers with large gains who would not accept taxable cash. For property owners, the same mechanism supplies a 721 exchange: a tax-deferred transition into diversified, passive, potentially more liquid REIT ownership with possible estate-planning advantages.

The mutual benefit explains why UPREITs became widespread. REITs can grow through unit-based acquisitions, while owners receive an alternative exit. Neither benefit removes the need to assess the REIT, tax facts, and loss of direct control.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an UPREIT?

An umbrella partnership REIT: a REIT that owns property through an operating partnership. The REIT is the controlling, share-issuing parent; the OP owns property. This permits owners to contribute property to the OP for units under Section 721, a capability a direct-owning traditional REIT generally lacks.

### What does the “umbrella partnership” refer to?

It is the operating partnership below the REIT that owns and operates real estate. The REIT sits above as general partner and majority owner. Contributors can be limited partners. This layer under the REIT is what enables tax-deferred property contributions.

### How does an UPREIT differ from a traditional REIT?

A traditional REIT holds property directly and generally cannot accept a tax-deferred property contribution. An UPREIT holds property through an OP, which permits the Section 721 contribution. The distinction matters mainly to owners contributing property, not ordinary public shareholders.

### How do I join an UPREIT?

Contribute property to the OP in exchange for OP units. The UPREIT must accept the property, and valuation determines the units issued. The owner becomes a limited partner with diversified REIT exposure while gain remains deferred under Section 721.

### What are OP units in an UPREIT?

They are operating-partnership units received for contributed property. They represent an economic interest in the portfolio, entitle holders to distributions, and are generally convertible to REIT shares after a holding period. Holding units defers gain; conversion recognizes it.

### Why do UPREITs exist?

They let REITs acquire gain-laden property without forcing a taxable sale. OP units attract sellers by preserving deferral. For owners, the same structure creates a 721 exchange and tax-deferred REIT ownership. That mutual benefit is why the format is common.

### Does the REIT control the operating partnership?

Yes, typically. The REIT is usually general partner and majority owner, so it controls the OP and property. Unit-holding contributors are limited partners with passive economic interests: distributions and potential share conversion, but no management control.

### Can any REIT accept my property in a 721 exchange?

No. The REIT needs an operating partnership—an UPREIT—and it must accept the particular property as fitting portfolio criteria. Traditional direct-owning REITs cannot provide the tax-deferred contribution. Some owners reach an UPREIT through a DST structured for a 721 exit.

### Are OP units the same as REIT shares?

They are economically similar but legally different. Units are interests in the OP that holds property; shares are interests in the REIT that controls the OP. Units are often convertible one-for-one into shares. The tax difference is critical: holding units defers gain; conversion recognizes it.

### Is investing in an UPREIT passive?

Yes. The REIT manages the OP and portfolio. Unit holders receive a passive economic interest and distributions, with no property-management responsibility or control. The passivity is a benefit for owners leaving management and a trade-off because outcomes depend on REIT decisions.

### Do public investors interact with the UPREIT structure?

Usually not directly. Most public investors buy REIT shares and own the REIT, indirectly participating in the OP without using the contribution mechanism. The distinction matters when a property owner wants to contribute real estate tax-deferred.

### Can a DST be UPREIT'd into a REIT?

Yes. Many DSTs have a potential UPREIT exit: an affiliated or sponsoring REIT later acquires the DST property and investor interests become OP units through a 721 contribution. This is the common 1031-then-721 path. Not all DSTs offer it, so confirm the structure before investing if the future REIT exit is important.

### Are publicly traded and non-traded UPREITs different?

Both use an operating partnership, but share liquidity and valuation differ. Publicly traded UPREIT shares trade on an exchange, so converted units may become liquid market-priced shares with market volatility. Non-traded shares are not listed; liquidity is more limited, often through discretionary periodic redemption programs, and valuation is less transparent.

## Glossary

**UPREIT**  
An umbrella partnership REIT, owning real estate through an operating partnership.

**Operating Partnership (OP)**  
The “umbrella” partnership beneath the REIT that owns the real estate.

**Umbrella Structure**  
The REIT-over-OP-over-real-estate hierarchy of an UPREIT.

**OP Units**  
Operating partnership units held by contributors, their stake in the partnership.

**General Partner**  
The controlling partner (the REIT) of the operating partnership.

**Limited Partner**  
A non-controlling partner (like a contributor) holding OP units.

**721 Exchange**  
Contributing property to the OP for units, tax-deferred under Section 721.

**Section 721**  
The code section deferring gain on contributing property to a partnership.

**Traditional REIT**  
A REIT owning property directly, without an operating partnership.

**Acquisition Currency**  
OP units used by REITs to acquire property tax-deferred.

**REIT Shares**  
The corporate ownership of the REIT, convertible from OP units.

**Conversion**  
Exchanging OP units for REIT shares, generally taxable.

**Distributions**  
Income paid on OP units, comparable to REIT dividends.

**Contribution**  
Transferring property to the OP for units, the 721 exchange transaction.

**Embedded Gain**  
The deferred gain on contributed property, carried into the OP units.

**Passive Ownership**  
Holding units or shares with no management role, the REIT managing.
