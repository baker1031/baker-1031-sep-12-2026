# Are Oil & Gas Royalties Eligible for a 1031 Exchange?

I have been thinking about how often a royalty owner hears that an interest is “passive” and immediately assumes it is simple. Eligibility can be comparatively clear while the investment decision remains anything but. Of the principal oil and gas interests, a perpetual royalty is generally the clearest candidate for a 1031 exchange because it conveys a continuing real-property interest. The important next question is where the sale proceeds can go—and which conditions must still be met.

First-order thinking focuses on the tax deferral and the monthly royalty check. Second-order thinking asks whether the interest is truly perpetual, cost-free, and conveyed by deed; whether the 45- and 180-day requirements can be met; and whether the replacement choice introduces a different form of risk. Royalties are the asset most often exchanged in the sector. Royalty eligibility is one piece of the broader framework in [the 2026 mineral rights & royalties 1031 guide](mineral-rights-1031-guide.html).

## How the IRS Treats Oil & Gas Royalties

A royalty is a share of production, or its value, carved out free of the cost of drilling and operating a well. When it is perpetual—continuing for the life of the property—federal tax authority treats it as an interest in real property. Revenue Ruling 72-117 is the frequently cited authority: it held that overriding royalty interests in oil and gas are real-property interests eligible for like-kind exchange treatment.

That character matters because selling a royalty can create capital gain that a 1031 exchange may defer. A qualifying royalty can be exchanged for almost any other U.S. investment real estate, not only another oil and gas interest. The owner is not required to replace minerals with minerals.

## Why Royalties Usually Qualify as Like-Kind

Three characteristics make a royalty the clearest fit. It is usually perpetual, meeting the continuing-interest test. It is non-operating and cost-free, without the operating liabilities that complicate a working interest. And it is conveyed by deed as real property in producing states, giving it the documentary character of real estate.

The qualification needs care with an overriding royalty interest (ORRI). An ORRI is carved from a working interest and tied to the lease rather than the underlying mineral estate; it can expire when the lease expires. Rev. Rul. 72-117 treated ORRIs as real property, but a clearly term-limited override requires closer review with counsel because its duration may weaken the perpetual character needed for 1031 treatment.

Perpetual, cost-free, and conveyed by deed: a royalty interest has the profile Section 1031 rewards.

## Royalty Sale Proceeds and Tax Deferral

Long-held royalties can produce a substantial gain, often enlarged because percentage depletion over the years has reduced basis. Federal [capital gains tax](capital-gains-tax-on-real-estate.html), the 3.8% [net investment income tax](net-investment-income-tax.html), and state tax can create a sizable drag. A 1031 exchange can defer that gain and keep proceeds working in replacement property.

The timing rules remain the standard ones. A [qualified intermediary](what-is-a-qualified-intermediary.html) must hold the proceeds. Replacement property must be identified within 45 days, and the purchase must close within 180 days. To defer the entire gain, acquire equal or greater value and replace debt; otherwise the shortfall is taxable [boot](what-is-boot-1031-exchange-how-to-avoid.html).

## Key Takeaways

- Perpetual royalty interests are generally real property and are among the clearest oil and gas assets to exchange under Section 1031.
- Rev. Rul. 72-117 supports like-kind treatment for overriding royalty interests, although a clearly term-limited override needs closer review.
- Royalty proceeds can be exchanged into U.S. investment real estate, including royalty-pool DSTs, with full deferral when values and debt are matched.

## Replacement Options for Royalty Owners

Because the real-property like-kind standard is broad, a royalty seller has options. Some owners remain in the sector through a diversified royalty pool. Others replace commodity exposure with the steadier cash flow of real estate. A Delaware Statutory Trust can be a middle path and may close quickly enough for the 45-day identification period.

Resource Royalty 27 illustrates the in-sector option described in our coverage: a debt-free, direct-title mineral and royalty DST across roughly 553 net royalty acres in the Anadarko and northern Permian basins. It is described as exchange-eligible replacement property with a statutory depletion allowance sheltering part of its income. Staying in royalties or rotating to diversified real estate does not change the discipline: confirm eligibility, engage a QI early, and identify a backup before the deadline forces a failed exchange. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are oil and gas royalties eligible for a 1031 exchange?

Yes. Perpetual royalty interests are generally real property and are among the clearest oil and gas interests for a like-kind exchange. Revenue Ruling 72-117 supports treating overriding royalty interests as real property eligible for Section 1031 when the standard exchange rules are met.

### Can I exchange a royalty interest for real estate instead of more minerals?

Yes. Like-kind treatment for real property is broad. A qualifying royalty interest can be exchanged for almost any U.S. investment real estate, including multifamily, net-lease property, land, or a DST, rather than only another oil and gas interest.

### Does an overriding royalty interest (ORRI) qualify?

Often, yes. Rev. Rul. 72-117 treated ORRIs as real-property interests. The caution is that an ORRI is tied to a lease and can be term-limited; where it is clearly finite, confirm its perpetual character with counsel before relying on 1031 treatment.

### How is the gain on a royalty sale taxed without an exchange?

A sale generally produces capital gain. Depletion may have reduced basis over time, enlarging the gain, and the owner may also face the 3.8% net investment income tax and state tax. A 1031 exchange defers the gain by reinvesting proceeds in like-kind real property.

### What is the deadline to reinvest royalty sale proceeds?

The standard 1031 deadlines apply: identify replacement property within 45 days of the sale closing and close within 180 days. The qualified intermediary must hold the proceeds throughout.

## Glossary

### Royalty Interest

A cost-free share of oil and gas production, or its value, that continues for the life of the property when perpetual. It is generally treated as real property.

### Overriding Royalty Interest (ORRI)

A royalty carved from a working interest and tied to the lease term. Rev. Rul. 72-117 treated it as a real-property interest, subject to its duration.

### Perpetual Royalty

A royalty lasting for the economic life of the reserve rather than a fixed term—the duration profile that supports like-kind real-property treatment.

### Percentage Depletion

A deduction—15% for many oil and gas royalty owners, subject to limits—that shelters part of royalty income but also reduces basis and can increase gain on a later sale.

### Boot

Cash or non-like-kind value received in an exchange, including unreplaced debt. Boot is taxable even in an otherwise valid 1031 exchange.

### Delaware Statutory Trust (DST)

A trust that holds title to real property, including mineral and royalty pools, and issues fractional beneficial interests that can serve as 1031 replacement property for accredited investors.
