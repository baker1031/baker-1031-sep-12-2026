# 721 Exchange Diversification by Property Sector


[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read**

I keep seeing “diversification” used as if it meant one thing. It does not. In a 721 exchange, the diversification gained depends on the REIT: an apartment REIT can diversify an owner across many apartment properties but leave every dollar exposed to that one sector; a diversified REIT can add exposure to retail, industrial, office, healthcare, hospitality, data centers, and more.

First-order thinking says more properties automatically mean less risk. Second-order thinking asks whether those properties share the same cycle, tenants, demand drivers, and structural pressures. The [broad diversification an UPREIT conversion](insights.html) can offer is real, but its sector mix matters.

## Sector diversification in a 721 exchange

Sector diversification means spreading real-estate exposure across property sectors. A [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html) can lead to single-sector diversification—many properties within one category—or multi-sector diversification across categories. Multifamily, retail, industrial and warehouses/logistics, office, healthcare, hospitality, and data centers each have distinct drivers, cycles, and risks. The REIT’s focus determines the exposure you receive.

## Single-sector vs. multi-sector REITs

A single-sector REIT owns one property type: perhaps apartments, warehouses, or net-lease properties. It may diversify geographically, across properties, and across tenants, but it remains concentrated in that sector. A multi-sector REIT owns several property types, spreading exposure across sectors as well as properties. A downturn in one sector then affects only part of the holding.

Both forms offer property-level diversification. Only a multi-sector REIT adds across-sector diversification. The choice determines whether the 721 diversifies within a sector or across sectors.

## Diversifying across sectors

The direct route to across-sector diversification is a multi-sector REIT; an investor could also hold several sector-specific REITs, although a 721 usually concentrates the conversion in one REIT. A [case study of a rental portfolio converted to REIT units](721-exchange-case-study-rental-portfolio-to-reit-units.html) illustrates the concept.

Different sectors can move differently. Office may face remote-work pressure while retail faces e-commerce competition; other sectors may respond to different demand conditions. Spreading exposure reduces the chance that one sector’s downturn dominates the entire position. That does not remove risk. It reduces sector concentration.

## Why sector diversification matters

Property sectors have distinct demand, supply, financing, and operating cycles. Across-sector diversification can reduce volatility when returns do not move together, but it cannot promise stability or returns. It is a risk-spreading tool, not a substitute for underwriting the REIT, its sponsor, leverage, property quality, valuation, and distribution policy.

The differences are concrete: multifamily responds to housing demand; retail to consumer spending and e-commerce; industrial to logistics and e-commerce; and office to employment and remote work. Sectors can diverge for years, with one outperforming while another struggles. Diversification reduces the chance of being caught entirely in the latter, but broad markets and rates remain systematic risks that sector diversification cannot remove.

## Choosing your sector exposure

Choose broad multi-sector exposure when reducing concentration is the primary goal. Choose focused single-sector exposure when you have a considered view, knowledge, or preference for that sector and accept the concentration. A commercial-property owner may also decide whether to [stay in a familiar sector](721-exchange-for-commercial-property-owners.html) or reduce the exposure already present elsewhere in the portfolio.

For an owner without a strong sector view, a multi-sector REIT is generally the prudent broad-diversification default. An investor who favors industrial because of logistics growth, or multifamily because of housing demand, may choose a single-sector REIT for focused exposure. That still has diversification by property, geography, and tenant, but it accepts the chance of focused gains and focused losses if the chosen sector underperforms.

## Key Takeaways

- A 721’s sector diversification depends on whether the REIT is single-sector or multi-sector.
- A single-sector REIT diversifies across properties in one sector; a multi-sector REIT adds diversification across sectors.
- Sector diversification can cushion concentration in a troubled sector because sectors perform differently.
- Choose broad or focused exposure based on views, familiarity, overall holdings, and risk tolerance.

## Sector concentration to be aware of

Sector concentration can arise even inside a large REIT. Count your direct real estate, DSTs, REIT interests, employment, and other holdings when assessing overall exposure. A familiar sector can be a sensible focused allocation, but familiarity is not diversification. Consider its sector cycle and structural headwinds, including long-term challenges such as remote work, alongside the REIT’s ability to change its mix over time.

There is no blanket sector prediction here. Conditions change, so review current conditions and outlook with advisors rather than rely on past reputations. Be cautious about heavy concentration, especially where office or certain retail face structural pressure. If other holdings are already concentrated in one sector, a different-sector or multi-sector REIT can balance the total exposure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a 721 exchange diversify me across property sectors?

It can. A multi-sector REIT diversifies across sectors; a single-sector REIT diversifies within one sector only.

### What’s the difference between single-sector and multi-sector REITs?

Single-sector REITs can focus on multifamily, industrial, or net-lease property and diversify by property, geography, and tenant within that category. Multi-sector REITs hold several property types and diversify across sectors as well as properties. Both diversify at property level; only multi-sector adds across-sector exposure.

### Why does sector diversification matter?

Sectors have different drivers—housing demand, consumer spending and e-commerce, logistics, employment, and remote work—and can have prolonged divergent performance. Diversification makes an office or retail setback only part of a holding rather than the whole position, though it cannot eliminate real-estate risk.

### How do I diversify across sectors in a 721 exchange?

Transition into a multi-sector REIT, or hold multiple sector-specific REITs where that is available. A 721 itself usually transitions into one REIT.

### Should I choose a single-sector or multi-sector REIT?

For broad diversification without a strong sector view, multi-sector is generally the prudent default. A single-sector REIT can fit an investor with a well-understood favored sector who accepts concentrated exposure, within-sector diversification, and sector-concentration risk. Choose based on views, familiarity, total exposure, and risk tolerance.

### Can I stay in my property’s sector via a 721 exchange?

Yes. A sector-specific REIT can maintain familiar exposure, but it also maintains sector concentration.

### Is a multi-sector REIT always better for diversification?

It is broader for pure across-sector diversification and reduces concentration risk. A single-sector REIT can still fit an owner seeking focused exposure in a sector they understand and believe in, accepting the chance of concentrated outperformance or underperformance. The choice is broad diversification versus focused exposure, not an automatic ranking.

### What sectors should I be cautious about?

Avoid blanket calls. The general caution is over-concentration and sectors facing current structural headwinds. Office and some retail have faced remote-work and e-commerce pressure, but conditions change. Review current sector conditions and outlook with advisors rather than rely on past reputations; no sector is risk-free.

### How does my existing real estate affect the sector choice?

If direct property, DSTs, or other investments already concentrate you in one sector, a single-sector REIT in that same category adds to that concentration. A different-sector or multi-sector REIT may better balance total holdings. Assess the whole portfolio, not the REIT in isolation.

### What property sectors do REITs cover?

REITs may cover multifamily; retail including shopping centers, net-lease retail, and malls; industrial including warehouses, logistics, and distribution; office; healthcare including medical offices and senior housing; hospitality; self-storage; data centers; cell towers, infrastructure, and other specialty sectors. A REIT can be focused on a major sector or span several, allowing an investor to stay familiar or diversify.

### Can sector diversification reduce volatility?

Generally, yes: sectors can move in opposite directions, so multi-sector exposure can smooth overall return and reduce volatility compared with one sector’s swings. It cushions a weak sector but does not remove broad-market or interest-rate risk, so it reduces sector-specific—not all—volatility.

### Is staying in my familiar sector a good idea?

It can be appropriate if it fits a deliberate focused view and risk tolerance. Familiarity alone does not solve concentration risk.

### Does a diversified REIT sacrifice returns for stability?

Not necessarily. A diversified REIT earns the blended result of its sectors, which can be higher or lower than a single sector in a period. A focused REIT can outperform when its sector thrives or underperform when it struggles. Diversification exchanges the chance of one sector’s outsized gain for steadier, less concentrated portfolio-average results; it is a risk-return trade-off, not a required return sacrifice.

### Can the REIT’s sector mix change over time?

Yes. Management may buy, sell, finance, or reposition properties, so review current mix and governance rather than assume it is fixed.

### How many sectors make a REIT well-diversified?

There is no fixed number. Look for meaningful balance across sectors and, within each, across properties, markets, and tenants. A REIT with multifamily, industrial, retail, and office can be broadly diversified when allocations are balanced; one dominated by a single sector is less so even if it names several. Assess breadth, balance, and within-sector diversification holistically.

## Glossary

### Sector Diversification

Spreading exposure across property sectors.

### Property Sector

A real-estate category such as multifamily, retail, industrial, or office.

### Single-Sector REIT

A REIT focused on one property type.

### Multi-Sector REIT

A REIT diversified across multiple sectors.

### Within-Sector Diversification

Diversification across properties in one sector.

### Across-Sector Diversification

Diversification across multiple sectors.

### Multifamily

The apartment sector, a common REIT focus.

### Retail

The shopping and store sector.

### Industrial

The warehouse and logistics sector.

### Office

The office-building sector.

### Sector Concentration

Heavy exposure to one sector, a risk to manage.

### Sector Cycle

A sector’s distinct performance pattern.

### Structural Headwind

A long-term challenge facing a sector, such as remote work.

### Focused Exposure

Concentrated investment in a favored sector.

### Overall Exposure

Total sector concentration across all holdings.

### Diversified REIT

A multi-sector REIT that spreads risk across sectors.
