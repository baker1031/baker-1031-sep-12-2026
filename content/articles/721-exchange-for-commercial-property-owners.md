# 721 Exchange for Commercial Property Owners

I keep noticing that commercial owners often have two separate decisions hiding inside one property. The first is whether to sell or keep managing the building. The second is whether the owner wants continued exposure to that particular commercial sector. A 721 exchange can address both, but only if the owner is comfortable exchanging direct control for a REIT portfolio.

Baker 1031 Research updated this 721 Exchange guide in June 2026. It is a 16 min read. Commercial owners of office, retail, industrial, and other property can use a direct contribution or DST bridge to reach passive, diversified commercial REIT ownership while deferring gain and considering estate planning.

First-order thinking sees the tax deferral and a potential exit from management. Second-order thinking examines the sector chosen, the REIT’s portfolio and sponsor, recapture, conversion tax, liquidity, and whether the owner needs to retain control. The 721 can reposition capital, but it also creates a generally permanent change in the form of ownership.

## Why commercial owners use 721 exchanges

Commercial ownership can require continual work: leasing, tenant relations, building operations, capital improvements, and financial oversight. A 721 can transition an owner from active property work into passive REIT ownership. The holder receives [OP units](op-units-explained.html) while the REIT manages its commercial portfolio.

Long-held commercial property can also create significant low-basis gain through appreciation and depreciation. A sale can trigger capital gain, [depreciation recapture](depreciation-recapture-real-estate.html), [NIIT](net-investment-income-tax.html), and state tax. A 721 can defer those items and leave the owner’s full pre-tax value invested in the REIT rather than paying tax at sale.

Commercial owners may be concentrated in one property, one tenant, one market, or one sector. A REIT can offer broader exposure. An owner may also want to move from a sector of concern, such as office or retail, to an industrial, diversified, or other-sector REIT. The combination of passivity, deferral, diversification, sector repositioning, and estate planning can make the 721 relevant.

## From active commercial to passive REIT

Direct commercial ownership involves leasing, tenant relations, building operations, capital improvements, and finances. Those demands can be especially heavy in multi-tenant or larger property. After a 721, the owner holds OP units in a commercial REIT and receives distributions without managing the buildings.

This is not a departure from commercial real estate so much as a change in how the owner holds it. The REIT manages the property, and the investor has economic exposure to its commercial portfolio. For an owner ready to shed the work but remain invested in the asset class, that is the central appeal.

The owner exchanges direct control for professional management and diversified exposure. Whether that is a benefit depends on the investor’s goals, not simply on the tax result.

## Sector-specific REIT destinations

Commercial REITs can be sector-specific. Office REITs own office buildings; retail REITs own shopping centers, malls, and net-lease retail; industrial REITs own warehouses and logistics assets. Other destinations include healthcare, hospitality, data centers, and diversified commercial portfolios.

An owner can remain in a familiar sector while gaining diversity inside it. For example, an industrial owner can hold an industrial REIT’s broader portfolio instead of one or a few industrial assets. Or the owner can choose another sector or diversified REIT to reposition commercial exposure.

The destination should follow the owner’s outlook, risk tolerance, and objectives. A sector-specific REIT can spread risk across many properties and markets, but it remains exposed to its sector. A diversified or different-sector REIT can change that exposure. Neither choice is automatically better.

## Deferring the commercial gain

Commercial property is generally depreciated over 39 years. A long-term owner may have substantial depreciation recapture, potentially up to 25%, along with capital gain, NIIT, and state tax. For low-basis and [cost-segregated](cost-segregation.html) property, the four-layer tax can be large.

A 721 can defer the capital gain and recapture. Rather than lose a third or more of gain in an immediate sale tax, the owner can transition the pre-tax value into the REIT. This can be particularly meaningful where years of depreciation and cost segregation have added to potential recapture.

If OP units are held until death, a step-up in basis can potentially eliminate deferred gain and recapture for heirs under current law. Tax results depend on the facts, documents, and current law, so the owner’s CPA must calculate the actual implications.

## Estate and diversification benefits

OP units can be divided among several heirs more readily than a commercial building. An owner can hold units, receive distributions, and leave separate units to heirs instead of leaving them one indivisible property or a forced-sale choice. A potential step-up may erase deferred gain under current law.

The diversification benefit is equally material. One or a few direct commercial assets become exposure to a REIT portfolio that may span properties, markets, tenants, and potentially sectors. The portfolio can reduce dependency on a particular building or tenant but does not eliminate commercial real estate risk.

Estate planning and diversification combine with deferral and passivity to make a 721 potentially useful for commercial owners. The owner must still decide whether the structure’s loss of direct control is acceptable.

## Key takeaways

- Commercial owners may use a 721 to leave active management, defer gain and 39-year recapture, diversify, reposition sector exposure, and plan an estate.
- The key transition is active commercial ownership to passive commercial REIT ownership.
- Office, retail, industrial, healthcare, hospitality, data-center, and diversified REITs offer different sector destinations.
- A 721 can defer capital gain and recapture; OP units can divide among heirs and may receive a step-up under current law.

## The DST-then-721 path for commercial

A direct contribution requires a commercial REIT to want the owner’s particular property. That is often not feasible for a typical individual asset. The usual alternative is a DST bridge: a 1031 exchange into a commercial DST in the relevant sector and structured for a later 721 exit, followed by a commercial REIT’s acquisition of the DST.

The owner remains in commercial real estate during both steps: commercial DST first, then commercial REIT. The path can provide passive, diversified REIT exposure where a direct 721 is unavailable. It can also let an owner remain in a sector or reposition to another sector through the destination selected.

The DST and later REIT transaction each have their own terms, risks, sponsor considerations, and securities requirements. The future 721 exit is not guaranteed and must be understood as part of the full decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do commercial property owners use 721 exchanges?

They may use one to leave management, defer a large gain and 39-year depreciation recapture, diversify beyond concentrated properties, reposition sector exposure, and plan an estate with divisible OP units and a possible step-up.

### What does the 721 exchange do for commercial owners?

It can move the owner from leasing, tenant work, operations, and capital improvements into passive OP-unit ownership in a commercial REIT. The REIT manages the portfolio, while the owner receives distributions and retains commercial real estate exposure in a diversified form.

### What commercial REITs can I transition into?

Possible destinations include office, retail, industrial, healthcare, hospitality, data-center, other sector REITs, and diversified commercial REITs. An owner can remain in a sector while diversifying or move to another sector to reposition exposure.

### Does the 721 exchange defer commercial depreciation recapture?

Yes. Commercial property depreciates over 39 years, and recapture can be up to 25% on sale. A 721 can defer recapture along with capital gain, NIIT, and state tax. This can be important for long-held and cost-segregated property. A potential step-up can eliminate deferred gain for heirs under current law.

### Can I reposition my sector exposure with a 721 exchange?

Yes. An owner concerned about office or retail can pursue an industrial, diversified, or other-sector REIT, if suitable. An owner can also stay within a familiar sector while moving from one property to a diversified sector portfolio. The destination is chosen based on the owner’s outlook and objectives.

### How does estate planning work for commercial owners with a 721?

The owner can pass divisible OP units to multiple heirs rather than a difficult-to-divide commercial property. Units held until death may receive a step-up that erases deferred gain and recapture under current law. Individual estate and tax results require professional advice.

### Can I 721 my commercial property directly into a REIT?

Possibly, if a REIT wants the specific asset. Often it does not. Many owners use a 1031 into a commercial DST structured for a 721 exit, followed later by acquisition of the DST by a commercial REIT and issuance of OP units.

### What is the DST-then-721 path for commercial property?

It is a 1031 exchange into a commercial DST, commonly in the relevant sector and structured for a later 721 exit. If the DST is later acquired by a commercial REIT, the owner receives OP units. It is the practical route where a direct contribution is unavailable.

### Will I still be invested in commercial real estate after a 721 exchange?

Yes, if the destination is a commercial REIT. The owner exchanges exposure to one or a few direct assets for exposure to a diversified, professionally managed commercial portfolio, often within the same sector.

### Is a 721 exchange good for office or retail owners given sector concerns?

It can be useful for repositioning. An office or retail owner can select an industrial, diversified, or other-sector REIT to reduce concentration in the sector of concern. Alternatively, a sector REIT can diversify exposure across many properties. The investment still carries sector and REIT risks.

### Is the 721 exchange a good fit for owner-occupied commercial property?

Owner-occupied commercial property has productive-use character, but the owner’s business operations add complexity. The real estate portion can potentially qualify, but the owner often must address separation of the real estate from the operating business. This needs careful advice from the CPA and attorney before pursuing a 721.

### Can I exchange multiple commercial properties via a 721?

Yes. Multiple properties may be contributed directly if a REIT accepts them or, more commonly, exchanged into commercial DSTs that later complete a 721. Multiple assets can be consolidated into one REIT OP-unit holding, simplifying the portfolio and creating diversified exposure.

### Does the type of commercial property affect the 721 exchange?

Yes. Office, retail, industrial, and other property types have different sector REITs, DST availability, market conditions, and feasibility. Those differences affect whether a REIT would accept a direct contribution and what direct or DST-bridge destinations may be suitable.

## Glossary

### Commercial Property

Office, retail, industrial, and other commercial real estate.

### Commercial REIT

A REIT that holds commercial property by sector or in a diversified portfolio.

### Office REIT

A REIT that owns office buildings.

### Retail REIT

A REIT that owns shopping centers and retail property.

### Industrial REIT

A REIT that owns warehouses and logistics property.

### Active to Passive

The transition from commercial property management to passive REIT ownership.

### 39-Year Depreciation

The commercial depreciation period that can create recapture.

### Depreciation Recapture

Tax, potentially up to 25%, on commercial depreciation; a 721 can defer it.

### Sector Repositioning

Moving into a different-sector REIT to change commercial exposure.

### Four-Layer Tax Stack

Capital gain, recapture, NIIT, and state tax that Section 721 can defer.

### Diversification

Spreading exposure across a REIT’s commercial portfolio.

### OP Units

The REIT partnership units commercial owners receive and can divide among heirs.

### Step-Up in Basis

The death-time reset that can erase deferred commercial gain under current law.

### DST-then-721 Path

Reaching a commercial REIT through a commercial DST.

### Commercial DST

A DST holding commercial property that can bridge to a commercial REIT.

### Cost Segregation

Accelerated depreciation that can increase commercial recapture.
