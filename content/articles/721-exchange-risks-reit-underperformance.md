# 721 Exchange Risks: REIT Underperformance

I have been thinking about the part of a 721 exchange that cannot be diversified away by good intentions: after the contribution, the investor's outcome is tied to a REIT that someone else runs. If the REIT performs well, the investor participates. If it performs poorly, distributions and holding value can suffer, and the path out can be costly.

The first-order view is that passive REIT ownership replaces a single direct property. The second-order reality is that it replaces a controllable, concentrated risk with a diversified but less controllable dependence on one manager, one portfolio, and one financial structure. That makes REIT selection the important decision before the exchange, not an afterthought once it is complete.

## Key Takeaways

- REIT underperformance can reduce distributions and holding value because the investor's outcome depends on the REIT.
- Performance depends on the portfolio, management decisions, financial structure, and broader markets, rates, and economy.
- The one-way nature of a 721 exchange makes an underperforming REIT difficult to exit without conversion tax and a sale at a possibly depressed value.
- Careful REIT selection is the primary defense; internal diversification cushions single-property problems but not broad downturns or poor overall management.

## The risk of REIT underperformance

After a [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html), an investor's distributions, holding value, and total outcome depend on the REIT. If the REIT has poor returns, declining value, or reduced distributions, the investor bears that result. Unlike a direct owner, who can influence a property's outcome through management, a passive REIT investor does not control the portfolio.

This is one of the [drawbacks worth weighing](721-exchange-risks-and-drawbacks-to-know.html). The investor has traded control of a direct property's outcome for dependence on the REIT's execution. A well-performing REIT benefits the investor; an underperforming REIT does not become less consequential merely because the investor dislikes the outcome.

The risk is made harder by the one-way nature of the transaction. The investor cannot simply return to direct real estate tax-free if the REIT disappoints. Exiting requires conversion and a taxable sale. Performance dependence plus an expensive exit is the central risk of the structure.

## What drives REIT performance

REIT performance begins with its portfolio: property income, occupancy, and value. Those factors are shaped by real-estate markets, property quality, and tenant performance. It also depends on management's acquisitions, dispositions, financing choices, and strategy. Sound decisions can support results; poor decisions can reduce them.

Financial structure matters as well. Excessive leverage and rising debt costs can pressure a REIT even when the properties themselves are reasonably healthy. Broader conditions matter too: real-estate cycles, supply and demand, sector exposure, interest rates, and the wider economy all affect tenants, demand, borrowing costs, and—in a traded REIT—share prices. Use this guide to [evaluate a REIT using FFO, AFFO, and NAV](how-to-evaluate-a-reit-ffo-affo-and-nav.html) alongside these factors.

Underperformance can arise from weak properties, poor management, financial stress, adverse markets, rising rates, or an economic downturn. These sources sit outside a passive investor's control. The task is not to predict every problem; it is to understand which portfolio, management, financial, and environmental risks the investor is accepting.

## The impact on you

Underperformance can affect income first. A REIT that struggles may reduce distributions, cutting cash flow. That can be particularly significant for an income-dependent investor, including a retiree, and is one reason to consider [who should not do a 721 exchange](who-should-not-do-a-721-exchange.html) before contributing property.

It can also affect wealth. In a traded REIT, underperformance may reduce the share price and the value represented by the investor's units. In a non-traded REIT, it can reduce net asset value (NAV). Either result can reduce net worth and make eventual liquidity less valuable because conversion and sale would occur at a lower value.

The consequence is twofold: lower income and lower holding value. The investor may endure both at the same time and cannot easily escape them. That is why the performance risk needs to be assessed as a capital and income risk, rather than just a measurement in a quarterly report.

## You can't easily exit

The 721 exchange is generally irreversible. An investor cannot tax-free move from an underperforming REIT back into direct real estate. To exit, the investor generally converts OP units into shares, which triggers the deferred gain, and then sells the shares. The sale could be made at a depressed value precisely when the investor most wants to leave.

The exit therefore can carry two costs: conversion tax and sale at a potentially low price. That makes the investor effectively tied to the REIT unless accepting those costs is preferable to remaining invested. The inability to leave a bad situation readily makes upfront REIT selection more important, not less.

## Mitigating through REIT selection

Careful selection before contribution is the primary mitigation. A strong portfolio, capable management, sound financials, and a credible track record do not guarantee performance, but they reduce the likelihood of avoidable weakness. A weak portfolio, poor management, or financial stress raises the chance of underperformance.

The selected REIT should be evaluated before the investor becomes dependent on it. That means reviewing the portfolio, management team, balance sheet and leverage, strategy, distributions, relevant financial measures, and sponsor. Selecting well is the main opportunity to manage this risk because exit becomes difficult afterward.

## Diversification within the REIT

A REIT's internal diversification is a secondary cushion. Because a REIT typically owns many properties, trouble at a single property affects only a portion of the portfolio. This reduces the concentrated, property-specific risk that a direct owner may face with one asset.

It does not protect against systematic risk. A broad market downturn, rising rates, sector-wide weakness, or poor overall management can affect the entire portfolio. Internal diversification therefore reduces single-property risk but does not eliminate REIT-performance risk. It is a useful partial mitigant alongside careful REIT selection, not a substitute for it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the REIT underperformance risk in a 721 exchange?

It is the risk that the REIT produces poor returns, declining value, or reduced distributions and thereby hurts the investor's income, holding value, and total outcome. A passive investor depends on the REIT's performance and cannot easily fix or exit the situation because of the one-way nature of the 721 exchange.

### What causes a REIT to underperform?

Causes can include weak portfolio income, occupancy, or value; poor property quality or tenant performance; poor management decisions about acquisitions, financing, or strategy; excessive leverage or rising debt costs; adverse real-estate markets; rising interest rates; and economic downturns. Underperformance can come from properties, management, financial structure, or the broader environment.

### How would REIT underperformance affect me?

It can reduce distributions and therefore cash flow, especially important to income-dependent investors. It can also reduce a traded REIT's share price or a non-traded REIT's NAV, lowering the value of the holding and the wealth available on a later conversion and sale.

### Can I exit if the REIT underperforms?

Not easily or tax-free. A typical exit requires converting units to shares, recognizing deferred gain, and selling at what may be a depressed value. Those tax and value costs make an exit unattractive and increase the importance of selecting the REIT before commitment.

### How do I reduce the underperformance risk?

The primary mitigation is careful pre-contribution REIT selection: choose a strong portfolio, capable management, sound finances, and a good track record. The REIT's internal diversification can cushion individual-property problems. Both measures reduce risk but do not eliminate it.

### Does the REIT's diversification protect me?

Partially. A diversified portfolio can limit the effect of one property on total results. It does not protect against broad market declines, rising rates, sector problems, or poor overall management. It cushions property-specific risk, not the entire performance risk.

### Is the underperformance risk unique to 721 exchanges?

Performance risk exists in direct property and directly purchased REIT shares too. Direct owners can manage and sell or 1031 exchange their property; direct REIT shareholders can generally sell shares. The distinctive 721 combination is dependence on the REIT plus an exit that is hard to accomplish without tax and possible value loss.

### Should the underperformance risk deter me from a 721 exchange?

Not by itself. It is a real risk that should be managed mainly through selection and weighed against deferral, diversification, passivity, and estate-planning benefits. A strong selection and willingness to accept passive ownership can make the trade sensible; the risk warrants careful selection rather than automatic avoidance.

### What if the whole REIT sector underperforms?

A sector-wide or market-wide downturn can hurt the REIT's value and possibly distributions regardless of its quality. Internal diversification does not remove broad market, rate, or sector risk. The investor remains exposed to real estate's systematic risks after the 721 exchange.

### How does the underperformance risk compare to keeping my property?

Direct property has its own performance risk: a tenant can leave, the local market can decline, or management can be weak. The owner has control and can sell or use a 1031 exchange, but remains concentrated. A REIT trades that concentrated, controllable risk for a diversified but less controllable REIT risk. Neither is risk-free.

### Can I monitor the REIT's performance after the exchange?

Yes, and you should monitor distributions, value, financial reports, and material developments even though exit is difficult. Traded REITs offer public information and market prices; non-traded REITs rely more on periodic reports. Monitoring helps an investor stay informed and decide whether the cost of conversion and sale is warranted if the REIT seriously deteriorates.

### Is some underperformance risk unavoidable?

Yes. Once committed to a REIT, an investor cannot eliminate performance dependence. Good selection and internal diversification can reduce risk, but market downturns, rising rates, and unforeseen events can affect even a well-selected REIT. This partly unavoidable risk is inherent in passive REIT ownership.

### Would a partial 721 exchange reduce the underperformance risk?

Potentially. Contributing only part of a real-estate position and retaining some direct ownership can reduce concentration in a single REIT. It also limits the 721 benefits of passivity and diversification to the contributed portion. Feasibility depends on the facts, so a full versus partial transition should be compared before action.

## Glossary

- **REIT Underperformance:** The REIT performing poorly and harming the investor's outcome.
- **Performance Dependence:** The investor's outcome depending on the REIT's performance.
- **Distribution Cut:** Reduced distributions from underperformance, harming income.
- **Value Decline:** A falling REIT share price or NAV caused by underperformance.
- **One-Way Nature:** The irreversibility that makes the risk hard to escape.
- **Performance Drivers:** The portfolio, management, financials, and broader environment.
- **Management Decisions:** The REIT's choices that affect performance.
- **Leverage:** Debt that can pressure performance when excessive.
- **Systematic Risk:** Broad market risk that diversification does not cushion.
- **Internal Diversification:** A REIT portfolio's ability to cushion single-property risk.
- **REIT Selection:** Choosing a strong REIT, the primary mitigation.
- **Exit Cost:** The conversion tax and possibly low-value sale required to leave an underperforming REIT.
- **Passive Investor:** The investor's role as dependent on the REIT without direct control.
- **Income Impact:** The effect of underperformance on distributions.
- **Wealth Impact:** The effect of underperformance on holding value.
- **Risk Mitigation:** Reducing risk, mainly through selection.
