# Inside Basis vs. Outside Basis in a QOF

I have spent the week looking at QOF questions that sound simple until the tax reporting begins. “What is my basis?” is usually one of them. In a partnership QOF, there are two answers, and confusing them can distort the whole picture.

This guide was updated by Baker 1031 Research in June 2026. It is educational, not tax advice. Inside and outside basis explain the tax mechanics of a Qualified Opportunity Fund (QOF): the starting basis for deferred gain, the 10-year step-up, gain calculation, and the work a CPA must do. Verify the current, technical rules with your CPA.

First-order thinking treats the 10-year Opportunity Zone result as a blanket tax exemption. Second-order thinking separates the original deferred gain from new appreciation, distinguishes the fund's asset basis from the investor's interest basis, and asks whether every required election and adjustment will be recorded correctly.

## Basis basics for QOF investors

Basis is the starting point for gain or loss. In the usual shorthand, it is what you have invested—your cost—and gain on sale equals amount realized minus basis. A higher basis produces a smaller taxable gain; a lower basis produces a larger one.

A deferred-gain QOF starts differently from an ordinary after-tax investment. When a capital gain is rolled into a QOF, tax on that gain is deferred. Because the rolled gain was not taxed when invested, the investor's initial basis in the QOF interest is generally zero, rather than the amount contributed. That zero starting point is central to the later calculations.

## Inside vs. outside basis

For a QOF structured as a partnership, **outside basis** is the investor's basis in the partnership interest—the investor's stake in the fund. **Inside basis** is the partnership's basis in the assets it owns, such as its property. One is at the investor level; the other is at the fund level.

They can differ and do not move in lockstep. The fund's inside basis generally reflects what it paid for the property. A deferred-gain investor's outside basis generally begins at zero, then may change when deferred gain is recognized, through allocated income reported on a K-1, through the investor's share of partnership debt, and through distributions or allocated losses. The running outside basis matters directly to the investor's exit gain.

> Two kinds of basis operate in a partnership QOF: outside basis in your fund interest, which generally starts at zero for deferred gain, and inside basis in the property the fund owns.

## Basis step-up at 10 years

The 10-year result is an elective step-up, not a slogan. After holding a QOF investment for at least 10 years, an investor can elect to step up the basis in the QOF interest to fair market value on sale. Since taxable gain is amount realized minus basis, a basis equal to sale-date fair market value leaves effectively no taxable gain on the QOF investment's appreciation.

That result applies to the **new appreciation** during the hold. It does not erase the original deferred gain. The original gain is recognized and taxed at its recognition date; the step-up is the mechanism that excludes tax on the later growth. For OZ 1.0 investments, the recognition date is fixed at December 31, 2026; for OZ 2.0 investments, it is a rolling 5 years. Confirm the applicable program and dates with your CPA.

## Impact on gain calculation

At the recognition date, the deferred original gain is recognized and taxed. That event also adds to outside basis because the investor has then been taxed on the amount. At a sale after 10+ years, the calculation uses the investor's stepped-up outside basis; the fair-market-value election eliminates taxable gain on appreciation when properly made.

Allocated income and partnership debt affect the running outside basis between those events. Distributions and allocated losses can reduce it. This is why the tax outcome cannot be read from one number on a subscription document. Basis is the thread running through deferral, recognition, and the 10-year exclusion.

## Key Takeaways
- Outside basis is the investor's basis in a QOF interest; inside basis is the partnership's basis in its underlying assets.
- A deferred-gain QOF investor's initial outside basis is generally zero because the rolled gain was not taxed at investment.
- Outside basis can increase at the recognition date and through allocated income and partnership debt; distributions and allocated losses can reduce it.
- After at least 10 years, an election to step up outside basis to fair market value eliminates taxable gain on the QOF investment's appreciation, not the original deferred gain.

## Working with your CPA

This is a CPA-led process. A CPA tracks the zero starting point, recognition-date increase, K-1 income allocations, debt-share adjustments, distributions, and losses that affect outside basis. The CPA also handles the 10-year election, the recognition-date reporting, and the relevant forms.

Form 8949 generally reports the deferral election and related capital-gains schedules. A QOF self-certifies and reports its 90% asset-test compliance on Form 8996. A partnership QOF issues Schedule K-1s for income, deductions, and other items feeding basis tracking. Reporting then continues through the recognition date and a sale after 10+ years. This is not a do-it-yourself tax calculation.

## Common points of confusion

The first mistake is using inside and outside basis as if they were the same number. They are not: one belongs to the fund's property, one belongs to the investor's interest. The second is assuming basis equals the gain contributed. For a deferred-gain QOF, initial outside basis is generally zero because that gain was untaxed.

The third is assuming the 10-year step-up makes every dollar tax-free. It excludes tax on appreciation, while the original deferred gain is recognized and taxed. The fourth is treating basis as static. In a partnership, the figure can change with recognized gain, K-1 income, debt share, distributions, and losses.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between inside and outside basis in a QOF?

Outside basis is your basis in the QOF partnership interest; inside basis is the partnership's basis in its assets. For deferred gain, outside basis generally starts at zero while inside basis reflects the fund's cost in property. Outside basis drives the investor's personal gain calculation and 10-year step-up; inside basis affects the fund's depreciation, asset-level gain or loss, and the items allocated to partners.

### Why is my initial outside basis in a QOF zero?

The gain contributed to a deferred-gain QOF was not taxed at the time of investment. Unlike an ordinary investment funded with after-tax dollars, that deferred gain generally does not create initial outside basis. Without the later step-up, a sale could therefore produce a large gain. Confirm any individual exceptions with your CPA.

### How does outside basis change over the hold?

It can increase when the original deferred gain is recognized and taxed, through allocated income reported on a K-1, and through the investor's share of partnership liabilities. Distributions and allocated losses can decrease it. Recourse versus nonrecourse debt and the partnership agreement can matter, so the CPA should keep the running record.

### How does the 10-year basis step-up work?

After at least 10 years, the investor may elect to step up basis in the QOF interest to its fair market value on the sale date. Amount realized minus a fair-market-value basis produces effectively no taxable gain on the appreciation. It is an election that must be made properly and does not apply to the original deferred gain.

### Does the step-up eliminate tax on my original gain too?

No. The original gain is deferred and later recognized at the recognition date—December 31, 2026 for OZ 1.0 investments, or a rolling 5 years for OZ 2.0 investments. The 10-year step-up addresses new appreciation in the QOF investment, not the earlier deferred gain.

### What is inside basis used for?

Inside basis is used in the partnership's asset-level tax work: depreciation and gain or loss on property sales. It affects the income, deductions, and gains computed by the fund and allocated to investors. In some partnership settings, basis-adjustment mechanisms can align inside and outside basis, but the QOF rules have their own specifics.

### Why does basis matter for my QOF tax outcome?

Taxable gain equals amount realized minus basis. The zero start, recognition-date increase, income and debt adjustments, and 10-year step-up determine the reported gain. A mistake in tracking or in the step-up election can leave appreciation taxable or create an incorrect tax calculation.

### Are these basis concepts only for partnership QOFs?

Inside versus outside basis is specifically a partnership-tax framework, and partnership QOFs commonly use it with K-1 reporting. A corporate QOF has corporate basis rules instead. The general concept of a deferred-gain starting basis and a 10-year appreciation exclusion can apply more broadly, but detailed mechanics and debt-share rules depend on the fund's structure.

### How does partnership debt affect my outside basis?

An investor's share of partnership liabilities can be included in outside basis. Changes in debt or the investor's share can change basis. The result depends on whether debt is recourse or nonrecourse and on the partnership agreement, so it is a technical CPA matter.

### Do I need to understand basis to invest in a QOF?

You do not need to perform the calculations, but it helps to know the framework: generally zero starting outside basis, adjustments over time, and the fair-market-value step-up after 10 years. Your CPA should handle the running basis, elections, forms, and reporting; this educational overview is not a substitute for tax advice.

### What forms are involved in QOF basis and reporting?

The investor generally uses Form 8949 and related capital-gains schedules for the deferral election. The QOF uses Form 8996 for its 90% asset-test compliance. A partnership QOF issues a Schedule K-1. Reporting also addresses the recognition-date inclusion and the later sale and step-up election.

### Can a basis mistake cost me the tax benefit?

Potentially. Failing to make the 10-year election properly can leave appreciation taxable, and an inaccurate basis record can create an incorrect gain calculation. The tax benefit depends on accurate tracking, proper elections, and correct reporting.

### How does the recognition date affect my basis?

When the deferred original gain is recognized and taxed, it adds to outside basis. The investor began with generally zero basis because the gain was untaxed; after recognition, the taxed amount becomes basis. The relevant recognition date depends on the applicable program, so confirm timing with a CPA.

### Is this basis topic something I handle myself or with a professional?

With a professional. Partnership tax rules, debt allocations, income adjustments, the recognition-date increase, the 10-year election, and reporting can materially affect the result. A conceptual understanding is useful; precise execution belongs with a qualified CPA.

## Glossary

**Basis:** What you've invested, used to compute gain or loss.

**Outside Basis:** The investor's basis in their QOF interest.

**Inside Basis:** The partnership's basis in its underlying assets.

**Zero Starting Basis:** A deferred-gain investor's generally zero initial basis.

**Deferred Gain:** The untaxed gain rolled into the QOF.

**Recognition Date:** When the deferred gain is taxed and basis increases.

**Basis Step-Up:** Raising basis to fair market value at the 10-year sale.

**Fair Market Value:** The sale-date value the basis steps up to.

**10-Year Exclusion:** The tax-free result the step-up delivers.

**Amount Realized:** Sale proceeds, minus basis to compute gain.

**Allocated Income:** Partnership income (via K-1) increasing basis.

**Partnership Debt:** A partner's debt share affecting outside basis.

**Schedule K-1:** Partner reporting feeding basis tracking.

**Form 8949:** Where the deferral election is reported.

**Gain Calculation:** Amount realized minus basis, driven by basis figures.

**Suitability Review:** Assessing whether a QOF fits the investor.

## Inside vs. outside basis in a QOF at a glance

How inside basis and outside basis differ for an investor in a Qualified Opportunity Fund:

| Factor | Inside Basis | Outside Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it measures | The fund's basis in its own assets | Your basis in your QOF interest |
| Whose level | Entity (fund) level | Investor level |
| Starting point | Cost of the underlying property | Often zero for deferred-gain investments |
| 10-year step-up | Not the mechanism for the exclusion | Steps up to fair market value at sale |
| Drives | Fund-level depreciation and gain | Your gain or loss on exit |
| Who tracks it | The fund's accountants | You and your CPA |

## Which should you choose?

This is not an either/or choice; both concepts apply at once. Inside basis sits at the fund level and affects the fund's depreciation and gain. Outside basis is the investor's basis in the QOF interest and determines the investor's gain on exit. For an investor, the outside-basis step-up to fair market value after a ten-year hold is the relevant OZ exclusion. Work with a CPA because deferred-gain investments often begin with zero outside basis.
