# 1031 Exchange FAQ

I keep seeing an exchange reduced to a slogan: sell, buy, defer. The slogan is useful, but it hides the parts that determine whether the deferral survives: who receives the money, what is identified, how debt is replaced, and whether the next property is worth owning after the tax benefit is removed.

First-order thinking is “how do I avoid today’s tax?” Second-order thinking is “what is the cost of the calendar, leverage, liquidity, and replacement decision—and does the tax deferral improve an investment that already makes sense?” The questions below are short by design, but the facts behind them are not casual. Confirm your circumstances with a qualified intermediary and your CPA before acting.

## 1031 Exchange Basics

A 1031 exchange lets an investor sell investment real estate and reinvest in like-kind real estate while deferring capital-gains tax. The tax is postponed, basis carries into the replacement property, and exchanges can be repeated over a lifetime. It is a long-standing part of the tax code used by investors of every size. For background, see the [complete guide](insights.html) and the [beginner’s guide](1031-exchange-basics-first-time-investors.html).

## Rules and Deadlines

Four core requirements govern an exchange: like-kind property, the same taxpayer, a [qualified intermediary](insights.html), and equal-or-greater value for full deferral. The calendar adds **45 days** to identify and **180 days** to close, both measured from the sale. See the [rules guide](1031-exchange-rules-requirements.html) and [timeline guide](1031-exchange-timeline.html).

## Intermediary, Boot, and Debt

The QI holds proceeds so the seller does not have constructive receipt. To defer fully, reinvest all equity and replace debt. Cash retained or debt reduced is taxable boot. The mechanics merit planning; see the [QI guide](insights.html) and [boot guide](insights.html).

## Replacement Property and DSTs

Replacement property can be active, such as a building you manage, or passive, including net-lease property, DSTs, tenants-in-common interests, and even qualifying oil-and-gas royalties. DSTs are a popular passive choice because they can close quickly, diversify an exchange, and replace debt without the investor qualifying for a loan. See the [options comparison](1031-exchange-replacement-property-options-compared.html) and [DST guide](1031-exchange-into-a-dst-passive-option.html).

## Taxes and Strategy

A 1031 can defer federal capital gains, [depreciation recapture](depreciation-recapture-real-estate.html), the **3.8%** [net investment income tax](net-investment-income-tax.html), and state tax. In high-tax states, the combined cost can exceed a third of gain. Holding the final property until death may result in a [step-up in basis](1031-exchange-step-up-in-basis-at-death.html), the basis of the “swap till you drop” approach. See the [deferral guide](insights.html).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a 1031 exchange?

It is a like-kind exchange under IRC Section 1031. Reinvesting proceeds from investment property into like-kind real estate defers capital-gains tax and carries basis into the replacement property.

### How long do I have to complete a 1031 exchange?

Identify replacement property in writing within **45 days** of the sale and close within **180 days**. Both periods start at closing and cannot be extended on request, except in limited federal-disaster-relief situations.

### Do I need a qualified intermediary?

Yes. The QI must hold proceeds so the seller never has constructive receipt. Engage the QI before the relinquished property closes. Engaging one after closing is too late and the exchange fails.

### Can I take some cash out of a 1031 exchange?

Yes, but retained cash is taxable boot. A partial exchange is allowed: the reinvested portion defers while cash and unreplaced debt are taxable. Full deferral requires all equity reinvested and debt replaced.

### What property qualifies for a 1031 exchange?

U.S. real property held for investment or business use qualifies: apartments, commercial buildings, land, net-lease property, DSTs, and similar assets. Primary residences, property held for sale, and personal property since **2017** do not qualify.

### Is a 1031 exchange still available in 2026?

Yes. Section 1031 remains available for real property in **2026**. The source states that the **2025** OBBBA made the framework permanent and that no limiting legislation has passed.

### Can I exchange my primary residence?

No. A primary residence is personal-use property. The relevant home-sale rule is Section 121, which may exclude up to **$250k/$500k** of gain. A former home that becomes a genuine rental can become 1031-eligible over time.

### Can I exchange across different property types?

Yes. Like-kind real estate is broad: a rental may be exchanged for an apartment building, commercial property, land, or a DST. The replacement need not match the type sold.

### What is boot?

Boot is non-like-kind value received, usually retained cash or debt not replaced. It is taxable up to realized gain, even in a valid exchange. Reinvest all equity, acquire equal-or-greater value, and replace debt to avoid it.

### Do I have to replace my mortgage?

For full deferral, yes. Debt paid off on the relinquished property must be replaced by new debt or offsetting personal cash; otherwise the shortfall is taxable mortgage boot. A leveraged DST can replace debt without the investor qualifying for a loan.

### How much tax does a 1031 exchange defer?

It can defer federal capital gain of up to **20%**, depreciation recapture of up to **25%**, the **3.8%** NIIT, and state tax ranging from **0%** to over **13%**. The combined rate can exceed a third of gain in a high-tax state.

### Does a 1031 exchange eliminate the tax?

No. It defers the tax during life. The deferred gain comes due after a sale without another exchange. A step-up in basis at death can eliminate it, which is the “swap till you drop” strategy.

### What is a DST?

A Delaware Statutory Trust is passive, fractional ownership of institutional real estate that can qualify as replacement property under Rev. Rul. **2004-86**. DSTs can close quickly, diversify an exchange, and replace debt without loan qualification. They are for accredited investors through a private placement memorandum.

### What are the 45-day identification rules?

You may identify up to three properties of any value under the 3-property rule; more than three up to **200%** of sale value under the 200% rule; or any number if you acquire **95%** of identified value under the 95% exception. Most exchangers use the 3-property rule.

### Can I do a reverse 1031 exchange?

Yes. A reverse exchange permits acquisition before sale, using an exchange accommodation titleholder to park property under the IRS safe harbor in Rev. Proc. **2000-37**. It is more complex and costly than a standard forward exchange.

### Can I use exchange funds to improve a property?

Yes, through an improvement or construction exchange. Exchange funds can build or renovate the replacement, but all work must be completed and paid for within **180 days**. An exchange accommodation titleholder is required.

### What happens if I miss a deadline?

The exchange generally fails, the sale becomes taxable, and the QI returns the funds. There is no grace period. Identifying a fast-closing DST backup can provide useful insurance.

### Can partners in an LLC do separate exchanges?

Not directly. The partnership is the taxpayer and must complete the exchange. Where partners want different outcomes, a drop-and-swap may be considered with advance counsel.

### How is a 1031 exchange reported to the IRS?

Use Form **8824** with the tax return for the year of the relinquished-property sale. It reports the exchange, dates, values, boot, and carryover basis. A CPA handles the filing.

### Can I exchange into oil and gas or minerals?

Yes, when perpetual oil-and-gas mineral and royalty interests qualify as real property. They may be like-kind replacement property and can serve as a higher-yield, depletion-sheltered sleeve.

### What is the same-taxpayer rule?

The taxpayer, or disregarded entity, selling the relinquished property must acquire the replacement. Title must match, which complicates partnerships and multi-member LLCs.

### Is there a holding period for a 1031 exchange?

There is no statutory minimum, but property must be held for investment rather than primarily for sale. A practical guideline is at least a year across two tax years to demonstrate investment intent.

### Can I do a 1031 exchange more than once?

Yes. An investor can exchange repeatedly over a lifetime to trade up, diversify, or move toward passive ownership. Each exchange defers accumulated gain, and a final hold until death may eliminate that gain for heirs.

### What is depreciation recapture?

It is tax on gain attributable to depreciation taken, often the largest tax layer on a long-held rental. It is taxed up to **25%** as unrecaptured Section **1250** gain and is deferred in a 1031 exchange along with capital gain.

### How do I start a 1031 exchange?

Decide whether it fits the goals, estimate deferred tax, engage a QI before selling, and build a replacement shortlist that includes a DST backup. Then sell, identify within **45 days**, close within **180 days**, and report on Form **8824**.

### Can I exchange vacant land?

Yes, if it is held for investment. Investment land can be exchanged into income property or a DST. Land held primarily for sale, including subdivided lots or dealer inventory, does not qualify.

### What is a qualified intermediary’s role?

A QI holds proceeds between sale and purchase, prepares exchange documents, and disburses funds to acquire the replacement. It is a custodian and facilitator, not a tax or investment adviser.

### Can I refinance before or after an exchange?

Refinancing immediately before sale to pull cash can be recharacterized as boot and is risky. Refinancing replacement property after the exchange is complete is generally cleaner. Both are fact-specific; consult the CPA and attorney.

### Does a 1031 exchange work across state lines?

Yes. Property in one state can be exchanged for U.S. real property in another. Watch for state clawback rules, such as California’s, that track deferred gain after an out-of-state exchange.

### What is a 1033 exchange?

Section 1033 provides deferral for gain from involuntary conversion, such as property that is condemned, taken, or destroyed, when compensation is reinvested. It differs from a 1031: no QI is required and replacement periods are much longer.

### How much does a 1031 exchange cost?

QI fees are generally modest: a base fee plus per-property charges. CPA and advisor fees add to cost, but the deferred tax often reaches six figures and can be much larger.

### Is a 1031 exchange worth it?

Usually, when there is meaningful gain and a desire to remain in real estate. It is less compelling when basis is high, cash is needed, or no replacement property is worth owning.

### Can I exchange into a property in an LLC I own?

A single-member LLC is disregarded for tax, so its property may be exchanged by its owner if the same-taxpayer rule is met. A multi-member LLC or partnership is an entity; its interest generally does not qualify and needs advance planning.

### What disqualifies a 1031 exchange?

Constructive receipt, missed deadlines, non-like-kind or personal-use property, a same-taxpayer mismatch, invalid identifications, and violation of related-party holding rules can all disqualify an exchange.

### Who should I have on my 1031 team?

The QI holds funds, the CPA handles tax and Form **8824**, and an independent advisor can source and vet replacement property and coordinate the calendar. The roles rarely overlap.

### Can I exchange a property I’ve owned for a short time?

There is no statutory minimum, but a short hold can suggest dealer intent or a flip rather than investment intent. A year across two tax years is a practical guideline.

### Can I exchange a vacation home?

Only if it is genuinely investment property. It should be rented at fair value, often at least **14 days** a year, with personal use inside strict safe-harbor limits. A property used mostly for personal enjoyment qualifies for neither a 1031 nor Section 121.

### What is the 200% rule?

It allows identification of more than three replacements if their combined value is no more than **200%** of the property sold. Investors may use it to diversify across several properties or DSTs.

### Can I add my own cash to an exchange?

Yes. Personal cash can help reach equal-or-greater value and offset debt relief, avoiding mortgage boot. It also leaves the replacement less leveraged.

### What is a leveraged DST?

It is a DST with pre-arranged non-recourse debt at the trust level. An investor’s interest includes its debt share, replacing old leverage without applying for or guaranteeing a new loan.

### Can I exchange a property with a partner and split it?

Not easily within one exchange because the partnership is the taxpayer. Partners seeking different outcomes sometimes distribute tenancy-in-common interests in a drop-and-swap before sale, but that requires advance counsel.

### Does a 1031 exchange restart depreciation?

Generally, carried-over basis continues on its existing depreciation schedule. Additional basis from a new investment is depreciated separately on a fresh schedule.

### What is the “swap till you drop” strategy?

It is repeated lifetime exchanging, followed by holding the final property until death. A step-up in basis may eliminate accumulated deferred gain for heirs.

### Can I exchange into a REIT?

Not directly. REIT shares are securities, not like-kind real property. An investor may exchange into a DST and later contribute it to a REIT through a **721** UPREIT exchange for OP units. That is a one-way door: it does not permit a later 1031 exchange back into direct real estate.

### What is a partial 1031 exchange?

It is an exchange in which an investor deliberately retains some boot, such as cash, and defers the rest. It is valid when liquidity is wanted and the investor accepts the tax on boot.

### How do I find replacement property fast?

Start before selling, build a shortlist, and identify a fast-closing DST backup that can close in days. An independent advisor with broad coverage and ready inventory may help match the available dollar amount and deadline.

### What is the napkin test?

It is a rule of thumb for full deferral: buy equal or greater in value and equal or greater in equity by reinvesting cash and replacing debt. If both lines stay equal or rise, boot has generally been avoided.

### Can I exchange farmland?

Yes. Farmland held for investment or farming is like-kind real property. Since **2017**, only its real-property component qualifies, so equipment and livestock must be handled separately to avoid boot.

### Does the 1031 exchange apply to commercial property?

Yes. Office, retail, industrial, and hospitality property held for investment or business use can be exchanged into residential property, land, net-lease assets, or DSTs.

### What happens if my replacement deal falls through?

If backups, especially a fast-closing DST, were identified, one can close within the **180-day** period. If the failed property was the only identification and day **45** has passed, the exchange generally fails and the sale becomes taxable.

### Can I do a 1031 exchange on inherited property?

Yes, if held for investment. Inherited property usually has a basis stepped up to date-of-death value, so a prompt sale may create little gain and make an exchange less necessary. Meaningful post-inheritance appreciation can make deferral worthwhile.

### How does a 1031 exchange affect my estate?

Holding the final replacement property until death can pass it to heirs with a stepped-up basis. Passive, divisible property such as DSTs, or a **721** UPREIT, may also ease estate division while deferral continues.

### Can I exchange a property in a self-directed IRA?

An IRA already offers tax-deferred or tax-free growth, so a 1031 exchange is generally unnecessary inside it. 1031 is most relevant to taxable, directly held investment property. Discuss IRA real estate with the CPA and custodian.

### What is a build-to-suit exchange?

It is an improvement or construction exchange used to construct a property or make substantial improvements to specifications with exchange funds. All work must be completed and paid for within **180 days**, with an exchange accommodation titleholder involved.

### Is the 1031 exchange a tax loophole?

No. It is an intentional, long-standing provision of the tax code, dating to **1921**, based on the idea that an investor who continues the real-estate investment has not cashed out. It defers tax during life; it does not create tax avoidance.

### Can I exchange into multiple properties?

Yes. An investor can acquire several replacement properties or DSTs, diversifying across sectors and markets. Their combined value, reinvested equity, and debt assumed must meet the targets for full deferral.

### What is a delayed (forward) exchange?

It is the standard and most common structure: sell first, have the QI hold proceeds, then acquire replacement property in the **45/180-day** windows. “Delayed” distinguishes it from a simultaneous swap or reverse exchange.

### Can I exchange property between family members?

Yes, but related-party exchanges generally require a **two-year** holding period on both sides. Certain structures draw IRS scrutiny, so counsel should plan the transaction to avoid retroactive disqualification after an early disposition.

### How soon should I plan my exchange?

Before listing the relinquished property. Use pre-sale time to test the fit, estimate tax, assemble the QI, CPA, and advisor, and build a replacement list with a DST backup. Early planning is the best protection against a rushed exchange.

### Does the 1031 exchange work for small investors?

Yes. Investors of every size use exchanges, from one rental owner to large portfolios. DST minimums for accredited investors are often **$25,000–$100,000**, which can make a modest exchange more diversified across institutional properties.

### Where can I learn more about 1031 exchanges?

Baker 1031’s Learning Center, this guide series, and its calculators cover rules, deadlines, replacement options, strategy, deferred tax, and exchange targets. Confirm the facts of a particular transaction with a QI and CPA.

### Can I do a 1031 exchange on a vacation home?

Only if it is genuinely investment property rather than personal use. Rev. Proc. **2008-16** provides a safe harbor that requires fair-value rental for a minimum number of days and limits personal use. A genuine investment vacation rental may qualify; a personal-use second home does not.

### What is a reverse 1031 exchange?

It is an arrangement in which replacement property is acquired before the relinquished property sells. Because an investor cannot hold title to both, an exchange accommodation titleholder parks one property under Rev. Proc. **2000-37**, typically within **180 days**. It is more complex and costly, but useful if the right replacement appears first.

### Can I exchange into multiple replacement properties?

Yes. Proceeds can be divided among several replacements by property type or geography if the 3-property rule, **200%** rule, or **95%** exception is met and enough total value and equity are reinvested. One management-intensive asset may be exchanged for several passive assets, including DSTs.

### What happens if I receive cash back at closing?

Cash retained rather than reinvested is boot, taxable up to gain. It includes proceeds pulled out, debt relief not replaced, and other non-like-kind property. Reinvest all equity and replace debt for full deferral; if cash is wanted, plan an intentional partial exchange with the CPA.

### Do 1031 exchange rules differ by state?

Federal deferral applies nationwide, but states differ. Most conform. Pennsylvania has historically differed, and several states, including California, impose clawback reporting that can tax deferred gain after an out-of-state replacement is sold without another exchange. A CPA can apply the relevant state rules.

## Glossary

- **1031 Exchange:** A like-kind exchange deferring capital-gains tax on investment real estate.
- **Qualified Intermediary (QI):** The required party holding exchange proceeds.
- **Boot:** Taxable cash or unreplaced debt received in an exchange.
- **Like-Kind:** The broad category of U.S. investment real estate that qualifies.
- **Partial Exchange:** An exchange where some value is kept as taxable boot and the rest is deferred.
- **45-Day Rule:** Identify replacement property in writing within **45 days** of sale.
- **180-Day Rule:** Close on replacement property within **180 days** of sale.
- **Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** A passive, fractional replacement option for accredited investors.
- **Form 8824:** The IRS form reporting a like-kind exchange.
- **Step-Up in Basis:** The reset of basis at death that can eliminate deferred gain for heirs.
- **Depreciation Recapture:** Gain from prior depreciation taxed up to **25%**, deferred in a 1031.
- **Same-Taxpayer Rule:** The seller and buyer in an exchange must be the same taxpayer.
