# REITs and 1031 Exchanges: Why REIT Shares Don't Qualify

[Home](baker1031.html) · [Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**Filed under:** [REIT](insights.html), [REITs](insights.html), [DSTs](insights.html), and [1031 Exchange](insights.html)  
**By:** Baker 1031 Research  
**Updated:** June 2026 · **16 min read**

I keep encountering the same tempting shortcut: sell real estate, buy REIT shares, call it a 1031 exchange. The desired destination is understandable. The legal character of the asset is the obstacle.

You cannot directly 1031 exchange into REIT shares. This guide explains why REIT shares are not like-kind real property, the DST plus 721 route that can reach a REIT tax-deferred, the tax and liquidity differences between the paths, and how to plan the route.

First-order thinking sees a REIT’s buildings and assumes the share is real estate. Second-order thinking distinguishes an interest in property from a security issued by the entity that owns it. That distinction controls the tax result. For broader vehicle context, see the [private REIT pillar guide](reits-guide.html).

## Why REIT Shares Aren't Like-Kind

Section 1031 defers tax only when investment or business real property is exchanged for like-kind real property. Since the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, the statute is limited to real property; personal property and securities do not qualify.

REIT shares are interests in a corporation or trust. They are securities—personal property—not direct interests in real property. A REIT may own buildings, but its shareholder owns a security issued by the entity. Exchanging relinquished real estate for that security is therefore not like-kind, so a direct 1031 exchange into any REIT share, traded or non-traded, fails.

## The DST + 721 Workaround

There is an indirect path: [the DST-then-721 route](can-you-1031-into-a-reit-the-dst-plus-721-path.html). First, an exchanger moves from relinquished property into a [Delaware Statutory Trust](dst-guide.html). A DST holds real estate and is recognized in Revenue Ruling 2004-86 as like-kind real property for Section 1031 purposes, so the exchange can defer gain.

Later, where the structure provides for it, the DST’s real estate can be acquired by a REIT or its operating partnership in a [721 exchange (also called an UPREIT transaction)](from-dst-to-reit-721-rollup.html). The holder receives [OP units](op-units-explained.html) in the operating partnership, with the contribution tax-deferred under Section 721. The OP units can later convert to REIT shares, but conversion is generally taxable.

The route is not a direct 1031 into shares. It is a 1031 into qualifying DST real estate, followed by a separate 721 contribution for operating-partnership units.

## Direct REIT Investing vs. 1031

Direct REIT investing—buying traded or non-traded shares—is simple. A traded REIT can be liquid and immediate. It is also a fully taxable purchase: if a real-estate sale funded it, the seller pays capital-gains tax before investing after-tax proceeds. There is no 1031 deferral.

The 1031/DST-721 route can defer capital gain from relinquished real estate. The 1031 into the DST starts the deferral; the 721 contribution into OP units continues it. The trade-off is complexity and illiquidity: DST interests and OP units are illiquid, and a later conversion or sale can trigger tax.

Direct is simple and potentially liquid but taxable. DST-721 is complex and illiquid but can defer a real-estate gain. The question is whether a gain exists to defer and whether the investor accepts the structure’s constraints.

## Tax Implications of Each Path

With a direct purchase following a real-estate sale, the seller pays federal and state capital-gains tax and any [depreciation recapture](depreciation-recapture-real-estate.html), then buys shares with after-tax proceeds. Going forward, REIT dividends are generally ordinary income, although eligible dividends can receive the **20%** Section 199A deduction.

With DST-721, the 1031 exchange defers the original gain and the 721 contribution continues the deferral. OP-unit distributions are generally taxed similarly to REIT dividends. The deferred gain remains until a taxable event, notably the sale of OP units or conversion to REIT shares. A [step-up in basis](1031-exchange-step-up-in-basis-at-death.html) at death can potentially eliminate deferred gain for heirs.

Tax deferral is not tax elimination during life. Conversion, sale, and individual circumstances matter, and a CPA and attorney must determine the actual result.

## Key Takeaways

- REIT shares are securities, not like-kind real property; direct 1031 exchange into them is not available, including for non-traded REITs.
- The indirect route is 1031 into a DST recognized as qualifying real property, followed by a 721/UPREIT contribution for OP units.
- Direct REIT investing is simple and potentially liquid but taxable; DST-721 is complex and illiquid but can defer capital gain.
- OP-unit conversion to REIT shares is generally taxable, so tax deferral, liquidity, and timing need planning with advisers.

## Planning Your Route Into a REIT

The DST-721 route may fit someone with appreciated real estate who wants to defer capital gain while transitioning from active property to professionally managed, REIT-style exposure. It requires a DST that offers a 721 option, careful structuring, and professional guidance; the timing of any roll-up is determined by the sponsor or acquiring REIT, not the investor.

Direct REIT investing may fit someone investing cash, someone without a gain to defer, or someone who values simplicity and liquidity more than deferral. Public and non-traded shares have different liquidity and suitability profiles, but neither can be purchased directly through a 1031 exchange.

## REIT vs. DST: The Broader Trade-Offs

A DST is a 1031-eligible, illiquid direct real-estate investment that often holds one or a few properties, has a defined hold, and lacks daily pricing. A REIT, particularly a traded REIT, is a professionally managed, diversified pool of many properties with daily pricing, but its shares are not 1031-eligible. Our guide to [how real estate investment trusts work](reits-explained-how-real-estate-investment-trusts-work.html) explains the REIT structure.

The DST offers 1031 deferral and direct fractional real-estate ownership, with concentration and illiquidity. The REIT offers diversification and, for traded REITs, liquidity, without 1031 entry. DST-721 can join the two: the DST preserves deferral and the 721 can reach REIT OP-unit economics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I do a 1031 exchange into a REIT?

Not directly. REIT shares are securities, not like-kind real property, and Section 1031 has been limited to real property since 2017. A tax-deferred route can use a 1031 exchange into a DST followed by a 721 contribution for REIT OP units, but it is not a direct exchange into shares.

### Why don't REIT shares qualify for a 1031 exchange?

A REIT share is an interest in the entity, not a direct real-property interest. Section 1031 requires like-kind investment or business real property, while securities and other personal property do not qualify. The fact that a REIT owns real estate does not change the tax character of its shares.

### What is the DST + 721 workaround?

First, the exchanger moves into a DST, which holds qualifying real estate under Revenue Ruling 2004-86. Later, where available, the DST real estate is contributed to a REIT operating partnership under Section 721 and the investor receives OP units. Both steps can be tax-deferred; an eventual conversion of OP units to shares is generally taxable.

### What are REIT operating-partnership (OP) units?

They are ownership interests in the partnership through which an UPREIT holds property. In a 721 exchange, a property interest such as a DST interest is contributed for OP units. They generally have REIT-like economic exposure and distributions, but are partnership units rather than shares. A later conversion to shares is treated as a taxable sale.

### Is direct REIT investing different from the 1031 route?

Yes. Direct shares are bought with cash and are fully taxable if funded by the sale of appreciated real estate. DST-721 can defer the original real-estate gain but is more complex and illiquid. The paths differ in tax treatment, mechanics, liquidity, and suitability.

### What are the tax implications of buying REIT shares directly?

If a property sale provides the cash, capital-gains tax, state tax, and depreciation recapture are paid first. The investor then buys shares with after-tax proceeds. REIT dividends are generally ordinary income, with potential Section 199A and return-of-capital treatment. Buying shares with existing cash does not itself trigger tax, but it does not create 1031 deferral.

### What are the tax implications of the DST-721 route?

The 1031 into the DST defers gain and the 721 contribution into OP units continues the deferral. Distributions on OP units are generally taxed like REIT dividends. The gain remains deferred until a taxable sale or conversion, and a step-up in basis at death can potentially eliminate gain for heirs. The mechanics are technical; consult a CPA.

### Is converting OP units to REIT shares taxable?

Generally, yes. The 721 contribution is tax-deferred, but conversion of OP units to actual REIT shares is treated as a sale and can trigger deferred and additional gain. Investors who seek continuing deferral may hold OP units rather than convert, subject to their own planning and professional advice.

### Should I invest in a REIT directly or via the DST-721 route?

The DST-721 route may fit an owner with appreciated real estate who values deferral and accepts complexity, illiquidity, and the requirement for an available 721 option. Direct REIT investing may fit a cash investor or someone who values simplicity and liquidity. No path is universally better.

### What's the difference between a REIT and a DST?

A DST holds one or a few properties, is illiquid, has a defined hold, and can qualify for a 1031 exchange. A REIT is a diversified property pool and a traded REIT has daily pricing and liquidity, but shares are not 1031-eligible. DST-721 can bridge DST deferral to REIT diversification.

### Can I 1031 into a DST and then into a REIT?

Yes, through the DST-721 route, if the DST is structured with a 721 option and its property is later acquired by a REIT operating partnership. The 1031 into DST and 721 into OP units can each defer tax. The sponsor controls timing, and not every DST offers the route.

### Why would I use the DST-721 route instead of just buying a REIT?

It can preserve full pre-tax proceeds by deferring gain from appreciated real estate. Buying a REIT directly after sale requires tax payment first. If there is no gain to defer, direct investment can be simpler and more liquid; the DST-721 advantage is specifically tax deferral for a real-estate seller.

### Are non-traded REITs 1031-eligible?

No. Traded and non-traded REIT shares are both securities, so neither is like-kind real property for Section 1031. The distinction affects liquidity, pricing, fees, and suitability—not 1031 eligibility. A non-traded REIT may participate as the acquiring REIT in a 721 transaction, but the exchange cannot move directly into its shares.

### Which is better for me, a REIT or a DST?

It depends on priorities. A DST may suit a seller seeking 1031 deferral and fractional direct property exposure despite illiquidity and concentration. A REIT may suit someone seeking professional diversification and, if traded, liquidity, accepting the lack of 1031 entry. DST-721 can combine DST deferral with REIT diversification for the appropriate investor.

## Glossary

- **1031 Exchange:** A tax-deferred exchange of like-kind real property.
- **Like-Kind Real Property:** The 1031 requirement, excluding securities like REIT shares.
- **REIT Shares:** Securities (personal property), not 1031-eligible.
- **DST:** Delaware Statutory Trust — 1031-eligible real estate.
- **721 Exchange:** An UPREIT contribution of property for OP units, tax-deferred.
- **UPREIT:** Umbrella partnership REIT structure enabling 721 exchanges.
- **OP Units:** Operating-partnership units received in a 721 exchange.
- **Section 1031:** The Code section, limited to real property since 2017.
- **Section 721:** The Code section allowing tax-deferred property-for-units contributions.
- **Rev. Rul. 2004-86:** The IRS ruling recognizing DSTs as 1031 real property.
- **Direct REIT Investing:** Buying REIT shares — a taxable purchase, no 1031.
- **Tax Deferral:** Postponing the capital-gains tax via 1031/721.
- **OP-Unit Conversion:** Converting OP units to REIT shares — a taxable event.
- **Step-Up in Basis:** Basis reset at death, potentially eliminating deferred gain.
- **Depreciation Recapture:** Tax on prior depreciation, due on a taxable sale.
- **Diversification:** Many-property exposure a REIT offers versus a DST.
