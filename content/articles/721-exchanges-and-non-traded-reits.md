# 721 Exchanges and Non-Traded REITs


[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

721 Exchange · Baker 1031 Research · Updated June 2026 · 16 min read

I keep seeing “no daily price” confused with “no volatility.” A non-traded REIT avoids a flashing market quote; it does not remove valuation, liquidity, fee, or portfolio risk.

Many [721 exchange](721-exchange-guide.html) paths lead to a non-traded REIT. Its shares are not listed on an exchange, so liquidity usually comes from periodic redemption programs, valuation is a periodic NAV estimate, and fees can be significant. These features call for careful due diligence.

The first-order view is an income-oriented REIT without daily market-price swings. The second-order view is whether a redemption program will actually meet a cash need, whether NAV reflects current conditions, how fees affect returns, and whether the sponsor and portfolio justify the limitations.

## What is a non-traded REIT?

A non-traded REIT owns real estate but does not list shares on a public exchange. A publicly traded REIT has market liquidity and real-time pricing; a non-traded REIT has limited liquidity and periodic valuation. A 721 contributor may receive [OP units](op-units-explained.html) that later convert into non-traded REIT shares.

Many 721 exchanges use a DST path and later involve a non-traded REIT, though some use traded REITs. The distinction matters because it changes the investor’s liquidity, valuation, and fee profile.

## Liquidity and redemption programs

Non-traded shares cannot be sold on an open market. Liquidity depends on the REIT’s periodic share-repurchase or redemption program. Requests may be subject to caps, conditions, and REIT discretion; the program can be limited or suspended, especially when many investors request redemption. See [redemption programs and liquidity windows](721-exchange-liquidity-windows-and-redemption-programs.html).

This is not guaranteed liquidity. Investors should not count on immediate or full cash access and should be prepared to hold. Conversion of [OP units into non-traded REIT shares](liquidity-after-721-exchange-converting-op-units-to-reit-shares.html) does not create a public-market sale option.

## Valuation and NAV

Non-traded REITs typically use periodic appraisals and estimates to report net asset value per share. Unlike a market price, NAV is not real-time and may not reflect current conditions or the value an investor could actually realize. Its method and frequency should be understood.

The absence of daily market volatility is therefore a visibility difference, not proof of stable value. Rates can affect the underlying portfolio and NAV; see [interest-rate sensitivity](721-exchanges-and-interest-rate-sensitivity.html).

## Fees

Non-traded REITs can have upfront fees, including commissions and offering costs, as well as management, advisory, and other ongoing fees. Fees vary by offering but can be significant and reduce net returns. Review the full fee structure, not just the distribution rate.

FINRA and other regulators emphasize careful review because limited liquidity, periodic valuation, and potentially high fees can materially affect outcomes.

## Traded versus non-traded REITs

A traded REIT generally offers market liquidity, transparent real-time price discovery, and often lower fees, but daily market volatility. A non-traded REIT avoids that daily price movement but has program-based liquidity, valuation uncertainty, and often higher fees.

Neither is universally right. Liquidity and transparency may favor a traded REIT. An investor who accepts limited liquidity and prefers no daily market quote may consider a non-traded REIT, but only after careful review of the particular offering.

## Key Takeaways
- Non-traded REITs lack exchange-listed shares, so liquidity is generally through limited, discretionary redemption programs.
- NAV is a periodic estimate, not a real-time market price, and may not reflect current conditions.
- Upfront and ongoing fees can be significant and reduce returns.
- Non-traded REITs can offer 721 benefits, but their liquidity, valuation, fees, portfolio, and sponsor require thorough due diligence.

## Due diligence

Review redemption terms, caps, conditions, and history; NAV method and frequency; all fees; portfolio quality; management and sponsor track record and financial health. Review whether distributions are covered by operating income. Some programs may fund distributions from offering proceeds or borrowings, which is not sustainable long term.

A non-traded REIT can lose value and limited liquidity can make exit difficult at a desired price or time. A stated value is not necessarily a realizable value. Future listing on an exchange or acquisition may provide liquidity, but neither is guaranteed and timing is uncertain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a non-traded REIT in a 721 exchange?

It is a REIT whose shares are not exchange-listed. A 721 investor may hold OP units that convert into these shares. Many, but not all, 721 paths involve a non-traded REIT.

### How do I get liquidity from a non-traded REIT?

By requesting redemption under its periodic share-repurchase program. Redemptions are capped, conditional, and discretionary; they can be limited or suspended. There is no open-market sale.

### How is a non-traded REIT valued?

Through periodic NAV estimates based on appraisals and other inputs. NAV is less timely and less market-tested than a traded share price.

### Do non-traded REITs have higher fees?

They often have upfront commissions and offering costs plus management, advisory, and other ongoing fees. Fees vary and can reduce net returns, so review the specific offering.

### Should I choose a traded or non-traded REIT for my 721 exchange?

It depends on priority. Traded REITs favor liquidity and pricing transparency but have daily volatility. Non-traded REITs avoid daily pricing but bring limited liquidity, NAV uncertainty, and often higher fees.

### Why does FINRA caution about non-traded REITs?

Because redemption limits, periodic valuation, and fees can be poorly understood and materially affect outcomes. The caution calls for careful due diligence.

### What due diligence should I do on a non-traded REIT?

Examine redemption terms and history, NAV method and frequency, full fees, portfolio quality, distributions, sponsor and management track record, and financial health.

### Can I lose money in a non-traded REIT?

Yes. The portfolio and NAV can decline, liquidity can be restricted, and the stated value may not equal a realizable exit value.

### Is a non-traded REIT’s liquidity guaranteed?

No. Redemptions are program-based, limited, and discretionary. They may be suspended in adverse conditions or when requests are high.

### How does Baker 1031 help with non-traded REITs?

### Can a non-traded REIT later become traded?

Sometimes a REIT may list or be acquired, but no listing, sale, or timing is guaranteed. Assess it on its current non-traded characteristics.

### Are non-traded REIT distributions reliable?

They are not guaranteed and may be reduced or suspended. Review whether they are covered by operating income rather than offering proceeds or borrowings.

### Is a non-traded REIT a bad choice for a 721 exchange?

Not necessarily. It can provide deferral, diversification, passivity, and estate features while avoiding daily price swings. Its liquidity, valuation, and fee limits require realistic expectations and careful evaluation.

## Glossary

- **Non-Traded REIT:** A REIT with unlisted shares and limited liquidity.
- **Publicly-Traded REIT:** A REIT with market-traded, liquid shares.
- **Redemption Program:** Periodic share repurchase by a non-traded REIT.
- **Net Asset Value (NAV):** Periodic per-share value estimate.
- **Periodic Valuation:** Non-real-time estimate of value.
- **Liquidity Limit:** Cap on redemption requests per period.
- **Valuation Uncertainty:** Lack of a real-time market price.
- **Upfront Fees:** Initial commissions and offering costs.
- **Management Fees:** Ongoing fees reducing net returns.
- **Discretionary Redemption:** Ability to limit or suspend redemptions.
- **Market Volatility:** Daily traded-share price fluctuation.
- **Transparency:** Clarity of value, generally higher for traded REITs.
- **Due Diligence:** Careful pre-investment review.
- **Sponsor:** Manager whose quality should be evaluated.
- **FINRA Caution:** Regulatory warning to review non-traded REITs carefully.
- **OP Units:** 721 units convertible into non-traded shares.
