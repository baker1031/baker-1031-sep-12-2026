# All-Cash/Debt-Free vs. Leveraged DSTs

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**Category:** Delaware Statutory Trusts · **Research:** Baker 1031 Research · **Updated:** June 2026 · **Reading time:** 16 min read

I keep seeing the debt-free-versus-leveraged choice reduced to a yield preference. It starts with a more basic question: what debt did the relinquished property carry? In a 1031 exchange, that answer can determine whether full deferral is even possible.

A debt-free DST owns property outright. A leveraged DST uses non-recourse mortgage debt. Both can be 1031-eligible under Revenue Ruling 2004-86, but they carry different debt-replacement, risk, and return consequences. Distributions are never guaranteed, and the tax math belongs with the investor’s CPA.

## What Debt-Free DSTs Are

A debt-free or all-cash [DST](dst-guide.html) owns the real estate free and clear. Investor equity funds the acquisition, so there is no lender, debt service, refinancing, or interest-rate exposure on a mortgage. Its fractional beneficial interests can remain like-kind real property under Rev. Rul. 2004-86.

The absence of debt can make the structure more resilient in a downturn: there is no loan to refinance or lender to foreclose. The trade-off is potentially more modest returns and, crucially, no replacement of relinquished-property debt.

## When You Need Leverage

If the property sold had a mortgage and the investor wants full deferral, the debt generally must be replaced or made up with additional cash. A leveraged DST’s non-recourse mortgage means the investor’s proportional share of DST debt can replace the paid-off mortgage.

Using only debt-free DSTs without replacing debt or adding cash creates taxable mortgage [boot](what-is-boot-1031-exchange-how-to-avoid.html), even if all cash proceeds were reinvested. An all-cash seller has no debt to replace and can use debt-free DSTs without that boot. The old mortgage drives the need for new leverage.

## Matching Relinquished-Property Debt

For full deferral, replacement value generally meets or exceeds the sold value, and replacement debt meets or exceeds the debt paid off, unless additional cash closes the gap. A leveraged DST has a loan-to-value ratio, and the investor’s proportional debt share counts toward the requirement.

The match need not be one DST or one exact dollar amount. Investors can combine debt-free and leveraged DSTs or add cash. The total replacement debt should at least equal the relinquished debt to avoid mortgage boot. Coordinate the technical figures with a CPA.

## Risk & Return Differences

Debt-free DSTs have no debt service, refinancing, or interest-rate risk. That can mean lower risk and more resilience, but returns may be more modest because leverage does not magnify them.

Leveraged DSTs use borrowed capital. Leverage can increase returns when the property performs and rates cooperate, but it also magnifies losses, adds mandatory debt service, refinancing risk at maturity, and interest-rate risk. In a severe case, the lender can take the property and equity can be lost. These are general characteristics, not promises.

## Key Takeaways
- A debt-free DST has no mortgage, refinancing, or interest-rate risk, but replaces no relinquished debt and may have more modest returns.
- A leveraged DST has non-recourse debt that can replace a relinquished mortgage and can amplify both return and risk.
- Your share of replacement debt should at least match debt paid off, or mortgage boot can be taxable.
- Lead with the debt requirement, then choose the risk profile; blending debt-free and leveraged DSTs may help.

## Choosing the Right Type

First-order thinking asks which DST advertises the better yield. Second-order thinking checks the exchange math first: did the sold property have debt, and must it be replaced for full deferral? If yes, use leverage or add cash. If no, a debt-free DST can fit.

Then consider risk tolerance. Debt-free can fit capital preservation and a lower-risk preference. Leveraged can fit required debt replacement or a willingness to accept more risk for higher potential return. A blend can replace the required debt while moderating overall leverage. Both interests are illiquid, and returns are not guaranteed.

## Leverage and Other 1031 Factors

The choice operates inside the same 1031 timetable: 45 days to identify, 180 days to close, and a [qualified intermediary](what-is-a-qualified-intermediary.html) to hold proceeds. Identify leveraged replacement early if debt must be replaced; discovering a shortfall after identification closes can leave taxable boot.

Multiple DSTs can diversify across sectors and sponsors while blending leverage levels. At the end of a DST hold, a new exchange into a debt-free or leveraged DST can continue deferral; debt-replacement math then resets from the new relinquished position. CPA coordination remains essential.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a debt-free (all-cash) DST?

It owns real estate outright with investor equity and no mortgage. It is 1031-eligible under Rev. Rul. 2004-86, has no refinance or debt-rate risk, but replaces no relinquished debt and may offer more modest returns.

### What is a leveraged DST?

It finances property with non-recourse mortgage debt. That debt can replace relinquished debt but adds debt service, refinancing, rate risk, and magnified loss risk.

### When do I need a leveraged DST?

Usually when the sold property had a mortgage and full deferral is desired. Use leveraged replacement or add equivalent cash; otherwise unreplaced debt is mortgage boot. Confirm numbers with a CPA.

### What is mortgage boot?

It is taxable debt relief from failing to replace paid-off relinquished debt. Match debt through a leveraged DST share or add cash.

### How do I match my relinquished-property debt?

Choose leverage so the proportional replacement debt equals at least what was paid off. Combine DSTs or add cash if useful, with CPA confirmation.

### What are the risk differences between debt-free and leveraged DSTs?

Debt-free removes debt service, refinancing, and rate risk. Leveraged debt can amplify gains and losses and add loan, refinancing, and rate exposure.

### Do leveraged DSTs offer higher returns?

They offer higher potential returns, not a guarantee. Leverage also increases variability and loss risk.

### Can I combine debt-free and leveraged DSTs?

Yes. A blend can meet the debt requirement and tune risk across offerings.

### Are debt-free DSTs always the safer choice?

They reduce leverage-related risk but remain illiquid real estate with tenant, income, fee, value, and distribution risk. They may also create mortgage boot if debt is not replaced.

### What is non-recourse debt in a DST?

The lender’s remedy is generally limited to the property rather than investors’ other assets. It does not eliminate leverage risk: the property and equity can still be lost.

### Are both types 1031-eligible?

Yes, under Rev. Rul. 2004-86. The distinction is debt replacement, and both require a QI plus 45-day identification and 180-day closing compliance.

### How does refinancing risk affect a leveraged DST?

At maturity, higher rates, tighter credit, lower value, or weaker income can force worse terms, an equity paydown, reduced cash flow, or a property loss. Review rate, maturity, and structure.

### Which type should I choose?

Start with debt replacement, then risk tolerance. Debt-free may fit no-debt, capital-preservation cases; leveraged may be required for debt or chosen for potential upside. Blends may help. CPA confirmation is essential.

### How does the 1031 timeline affect the debt-free versus leveraged decision?

Work out value and debt early. Identify the needed leveraged, debt-free, or blended DST by day 45 and close by day 180 through the QI. DSTs can close quickly, but late debt discovery can create boot.

## Glossary

- **Debt-Free (All-Cash) DST:** A DST owning property outright without a mortgage.
- **Leveraged DST:** A DST financed with non-recourse mortgage debt.
- **Non-Recourse Debt:** A loan whose remedy is limited to the property, not investor assets.
- **Debt Replacement:** Replacing relinquished debt to fully defer tax.
- **Mortgage Boot:** Taxable amount from not fully replacing relinquished debt.
- **Loan-to-Value (LTV):** Leveraged DST debt as a share of property value.
- **Refinancing Risk:** The risk a maturing loan must be refinanced on worse terms.
- **Interest-Rate Risk:** The risk rate changes affect leveraged returns.
- **Relinquished Property:** Property sold in a 1031 exchange.
- **Replacement Property:** Like-kind property, here a DST, acquired in the exchange.
- **Full Deferral:** Deferring all gain by replacing both value and debt.
- **Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** A trust holding 1031-eligible fractional real-estate interests.
- **Rev. Rul. 2004-86:** IRS ruling treating DST interests as like-kind real property.
- **Accredited Investor:** Investor meeting Regulation D income/net-worth thresholds.
- **Suitability Review:** Broker-dealer check that a DST fits the investor.
- **Qualified Intermediary:** Party holding exchange proceeds to preserve 1031 deferral.

## Debt-free vs. leveraged DSTs at a glance

| Factor | Debt-Free DST | Leveraged DST |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property-level debt | None | Yes — mortgage on the asset |
| Replaces relinquished debt? | No — suited to no- or low-debt exchanges | Yes — can match debt paid off |
| Risk profile | Lower — no foreclosure or refinancing risk | Higher — leverage amplifies both ways |
| Return potential | Steadier, typically lower ceiling | Higher potential, more variability |
| Sensitivity to rates | Minimal | Refinancing and rate risk at maturity |
| Cash needed | Higher equity for the same exposure | Less equity covers more property value |
| Best when | Capital preservation is priority | Debt must be replaced or more upside is wanted |

## Which should you choose?

A debt-free DST may fit an unleveraged relinquished property and a capital-preservation priority. A leveraged DST is often needed when the relinquished property had a mortgage, unless equivalent cash replaces it, and it brings more potential upside with more risk.
