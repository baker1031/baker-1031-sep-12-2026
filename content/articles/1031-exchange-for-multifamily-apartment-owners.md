# 1031 Exchange for Multifamily & Apartment Owners

I keep seeing multifamily owners reach the same fork: they have built equity through rent growth, appreciation, and amortization, but the building that created it now asks for more management than they want to give. Selling can solve the management problem while creating a substantial tax problem.

First-order thinking is “trade up or cash out.” Second-order thinking asks whether scale, location, management intensity, leverage, tax exposure, and the replacement’s actual quality all move in the right direction. A 1031 exchange keeps capital in real estate while an owner scales, repositions, consolidates, diversifies, or steps back from management.

## Why Multifamily Owners Exchange

Apartments can accumulate substantial equity through appreciation, rent growth, and mortgage amortization. A taxable sale can trigger capital gain and depreciation recapture. An exchange lets the owner redeploy the full equity rather than the after-tax remainder.

Owners also use exchanges to move from a softening market to a growing market, consolidate scattered properties, diversify one concentrated building into several holdings, change from value-add apartments to stabilized assets, or leave active management behind. Tenants, maintenance, turnover, and the familiar **3 a.m.** call are real costs, not just operational details.

## Trading Up in Multifamily

Trading up means exchanging a smaller building for a larger one, or an older management-intensive building for a better-located, newer, or higher-quality property. Deferral preserves equity that would otherwise have gone to tax, adding buying power for the replacement.

Owners may repeat the move: small building to larger building to larger complex. Serial exchanges can compound scale while deferring tax at each stage. The same logic can improve quality as well as size—better tenants, lower maintenance, and stronger appreciation potential are possible reasons to trade up. The replacement still has to be a good investment without relying on the tax benefit.

## Common Multifamily Moves

An owner can change markets toward stronger population, job, and rent growth; diversify one large building across markets and property types; or consolidate several small holdings into one simpler property. An owner can exchange value-add apartments into stabilized lower-management property, or apartments into another investment-real-estate sector.

Those choices have different risk, operating, and liquidity profiles. The exchange provides flexibility; it does not decide which tradeoff is correct.

## Multifamily-to-DST Exchanges

Many apartment owners eventually want real estate’s income, appreciation, and deferral without active management. A multifamily-to-DST exchange trades apartments for passive fractional interests in professionally managed real estate. That can fit an owner who is aging, relocating, tired of management, or simply ready to step back.

One apartment building can be divided across several DSTs, including institutional multifamily and other property types. That may diversify a formerly concentrated holding and provide access to properties an individual owner could not acquire alone. DSTs are securities for accredited investors and require a suitability review. They are illiquid, provide no investor control, and need the same attention to property, leverage, sponsor, price, and cash-flow risk as any other replacement.

## Tax Considerations for Apartment Owners

[Depreciation recapture](depreciation-recapture-real-estate.html) is central. Residential-rental apartment property is depreciated over **27.5 years**. Long-held properties may have substantial prior depreciation, subject to recapture up to **25%** on a taxable sale. A 1031 defers that recapture along with capital gain.

[Cost segregation](cost-segregation.html) can accelerate depreciation by moving components into shorter recovery periods. It improves deductions during ownership but can increase recapture on sale. The exchange defers this enhanced recapture, while the CPA calculates carryover basis and depreciation in the replacement property.

The four-layer stack—capital gains, recapture, [NIIT](net-investment-income-tax.html), and state tax—can total a third or more of gain. That is why the deferral can be particularly valuable for apartment owners with large gains and accumulated depreciation.

### Key Takeaways

- Owners exchange to redeploy equity, reposition, and move from active to passive ownership.
- Trading into larger or better properties can compound through serial exchanges.
- DSTs are the familiar “tired landlord” route to passive real estate, but their illiquidity and suitability matter.
- Recapture, especially after cost segregation, makes full-tax deferral valuable.

## Timing the Multifamily Exchange

Market timing, deadline timing, and personal timing all matter. Owners may sell after a market or property has peaked and redeploy into better growth prospects, but perfect timing is not possible. Retirement, relocation, and estate planning may also prompt the exchange.

The calendar is firm: identify replacements in [**45 days**](1031-exchange-identification-rules.html) and close in **180 days**. Start sourcing before sale, line up channels, and identify a DST backup in a competitive multifamily market. Preparation turns a repositioning into a decision instead of a scramble.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do multifamily owners use 1031 exchanges?

To preserve equity otherwise reduced by tax, reposition through trade-up, market change, diversification, or consolidation, and move from active apartments to passive DST ownership.

### How does trading up work for apartment owners?

Deferred tax leaves full equity available for a larger or better replacement. Many owners do it serially—small building, larger building, larger complex—to compound holdings.

### What is a multifamily-to-DST exchange?

It is a move from actively managed apartments to passive, professionally managed DST interests. It can preserve income, appreciation, and deferral while removing tenants, maintenance, and turnover from the owner’s work.

### Can I exchange my apartment building into multiple properties?

Yes. One building may become several direct replacements or DST interests across markets and property types, reducing concentration risk.

### How does depreciation recapture affect apartment owners?

Apartments use **27.5-year** depreciation. Prior deductions can be recaptured up to **25%** on sale; a 1031 defers that tax along with capital gain.

### Does cost segregation affect my exchange?

Yes. It accelerates depreciation through shorter recovery periods and can increase recapture exposure. The exchange defers that exposure, while the CPA addresses replacement depreciation.

### Can I trade up from a small building to a large complex?

Yes. The deferred tax can increase buying power. Repeating that small-to-large move is a common way to scale tax-deferred.

### Is multifamily a good asset class for exchanges?

Yes. It combines substantial equity and depreciation, evolving ownership goals, active-management demands, and direct/DST replacement options.

### How do I transition from active apartments to passive income?

Exchange the apartments for professionally managed DST interests. The owner remains invested in real estate and can receive income without directly managing apartments.

### What deadlines apply to a multifamily exchange?

Identify in **45 days** and close in **180 days**. Source replacements before selling and identify a DST backup in case direct property does not close.

### Can I exchange apartments for a different property type?

Yes. Apartments may be exchanged for retail, industrial, net-lease, and other investment or business real estate.

### Should I exchange or just sell my apartments?

If the goal is to exit real estate, a taxable sale may fit. If the goal is repositioning or passive ownership in real estate, an exchange preserves more capital by deferring the four-layer tax. Model both with the CPA.

### Can I exchange into a larger building with more debt?

Yes, provided equal-or-greater value and debt requirements are met, or personal cash replaces debt. More leverage can increase potential return and risk.

### What if I co-own apartments with partners?

The partnership is the taxpayer. If all partners exchange, the entity can exchange as a whole. Different goals can require a drop-and-swap or related structuring with attorney and CPA involvement.

### Are apartment DSTs available as replacements?

Yes. Many DSTs hold multifamily property, allowing an owner to stay in a familiar asset class while passive, or to diversify into other sectors.

## Glossary

- **Multifamily:** Apartment buildings and complexes.
- **Trading Up:** Exchanging into a larger or better property with tax deferral as buying power.
- **Serial Exchange:** Repeated exchanges that compound holdings over time.
- **Multifamily-to-DST Exchange:** Active apartments exchanged for passive DST interests.
- **Depreciation Recapture:** Tax up to **25%** on prior depreciation, deferred by an exchange.
- **27.5-Year Depreciation:** Residential-rental-property recovery period.
- **Cost Segregation:** A study accelerating depreciation and increasing recapture exposure.
- **Value-Add:** Apartments requiring renovation and active management.
- **Stabilized Property:** Fully leased, lower-management property.
- **Diversifying:** Exchanging one building into several holdings to spread risk.
- **Consolidating:** Exchanging multiple small properties into one larger property.
- **Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** Passive replacement for an owner stepping back from management.
- **Four-Layer Tax Stack:** Capital gain, recapture, NIIT, and state tax.
- **Carryover Basis:** Basis transferred from apartments to replacement property.
- **Tired Landlord:** Owner ready to shed apartment management, often a DST candidate.
- **Replacement Property:** Property acquired within the exchange deadlines.
