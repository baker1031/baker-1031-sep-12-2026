# The 2026 1031 Exchange Outlook

[Back to All Insights](insights.html)

**Category:** 1031 Exchange · **Research:** Baker 1031 Research · **Updated:** June 2026 · **Reading time:** 7 min read

I keep seeing investors focus on whether the 1031 rules have changed, when the harder question is whether their exchange plan has enough margin for the market around those rules. The statute is stable. Tighter deadlines, choppier financing, and more DST choices are changing the work required to use it well.

The 45-day identification and 180-day closing clocks still govern. What has changed is the time between identifying a replacement and actually getting it closed. That makes a planned fallback—rather than a last-minute search—central to an exchange this year.

## Key Takeaways
- The 2026 rules remain 45 days to identify and 180 days to close, measured from the relinquished closing. Full deferral still requires equal-or-greater value and debt replacement.
- Longer closings and less certain financing increase the odds of missing a deadline, so identify a backup before the clock starts.
- The DST shelf now includes multifamily, industrial, self-storage, healthcare, net-lease, and mineral royalties, offering more ways to match equity and debt.
- A DST can close in days without a financing contingency. It can serve as a backup or absorb boot.
- Engage the qualified intermediary before closing, prepare targets early, and name a primary, a second option, and a DST backup.
- This is not investment advice. DSTs are speculative, illiquid, accredited-only securities and can lose principal.

## What hasn't changed in 2026

A 1031 exchange still defers federal capital gains tax, the 3.8% net investment income surtax, and depreciation recapture when investment or business real estate is exchanged for like-kind replacement property. There are **45 calendar days** after the relinquished closing to identify in writing and **180 calendar days**—or the tax-return due date, if earlier—to acquire the replacement. Both clocks begin at the sale; the 45-day period sits inside the 180-day period.

For full deferral, buy replacement property of **equal or greater value**, reinvest all net equity, and replace debt paid off with new financing or additional cash. A shortfall is taxable [boot](1031-exchange-boot.html). A qualified intermediary, rather than the investor, must hold the proceeds; constructive receipt ends the exchange. See the [1031 exchange guide](1031-exchange-guide.html) and the [day-by-day timeline](1031-exchange-timeline.html).

The 2026 tax law changes involving Opportunity Zones did not change section 1031 for real property held for investment or business use. The issue is not whether the tool survives. It is execution.

## The market conditions that matter for an exchange

Longer diligence, title, and financing cycles mean a replacement that once might have closed in 45 days can drift toward 60 or 75. Meanwhile, the 180-day clock does not move. Rate moves and tighter lending standards make loan approvals slower and less predictable, while some segments have a thinner pipeline of clean deals.

First-order thinking tries to forecast the next quarter and picks one financed target. Second-order thinking builds room for a delayed closing, a lender withdrawal, or a primary that simply disappears. The durable fact is structural: exchanges often fail in the gap between identification and closing. More preparation is a better answer than a market prediction.

Late-year sellers have an added trap. The exchange period ends on the earlier of 180 days or the due date of the return for the sale year, including extensions. A November closing can shorten the runway unless an extension preserves the full 180 days. Map that timeline before the sale. A failed exchange makes the gain taxable in the sale year.

## Identification discipline in a slower market

The 45-day period does not extend because the market is difficult. The available paths are:

| Identification rule | What you may name | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3-Property Rule | Up to three, any value | Simplest and most common |
| 200% Rule | Any number | Combined value cannot exceed 200% of the sale price |
| 95% Rule | Any number, any value | Must acquire at least 95% of identified value |

The 3-property rule is usually the practical route. The 200% rule can support a larger list; the 95% rule is a rare escape hatch with almost no margin for error.

A valid notice is **written, signed, unambiguous, and delivered to the QI by midnight on day 45**. Identify a whole property with a street address or legal description. Identify a DST with the particular trust and the dollar amount or percentage interest. Read the [identification rules](1031-exchange-identification-rules.html), then treat day 40—not day 45—as the internal finish line.

## A deeper DST shelf changes the backup math

A [Delaware Statutory Trust](dst-guide.html) is a passive, pre-packaged, institutionally managed real-estate investment that can accept 1031 proceeds. The menu now extends through self-storage, industrial and logistics, healthcare and medical office, multifamily, net-lease retail, and mineral and royalty interests. That breadth can help match the equity that must be placed and the debt that must be replaced.

For example, a $1,500,000 sale with $600,000 of debt paid off leaves roughly $900,000 of equity to place and $600,000 of debt or additional cash to replace. DSTs have different leverage profiles, so one may roughly match the paid-off debt without requiring a new personal loan—particularly useful when new financing is uncertain.

## Pre-identifying a DST as insurance

A DST often has a low minimum, around $100,000, trust-level financing with no financing contingency, and a subscription process that can close in days. Those features make it a practical third identification alongside a primary and a genuine second option.

Consider a $1,200,000 sale. An investor identifies a financed medical-office purchase, a smaller retail property, and a DST for the full amount. On day 50, the medical-office lender withdraws. Instead of searching from scratch, the investor can subscribe to the already identified DST, place the entire $1,200,000, and close inside 180 days. The DST is not automatically the right investment; it is a planned insurance policy for a financed primary. The mechanics are explained in [this DST exchange guide](1031-exchange-into-dst.html).

## Using a DST to absorb boot

Boot is the portion that does not defer. **Cash boot** is equity removed rather than reinvested. **Mortgage boot** is debt paid off but not replaced. Both are taxable even in an otherwise valid exchange.

If a $1,200,000 sale is followed by a $1,100,000 replacement, the $100,000 shortfall can be taxable. A right-sized DST investment can place that $100,000, reinvest the full proceeds, and keep the gain deferred. In a market where one perfect replacement is harder to find, pairing a main purchase with a DST may hit the target more precisely. See [boot and debt replacement](1031-exchange-boot.html).

## A 2026 exchange playbook

Engage the QI before the relinquished closing. Build the target list while the sale is pending. Vet financing and title on the primary early. Then identify with depth: a real primary, a real second option, and a DST backup that can close regardless of financing.

Deliver the written notice by day 40 and keep the QI, lender, CPA, and closing agent coordinated. The investor who treats the exchange as a scheduled project with a fallback has a better chance of clearing both deadlines.

## Mistakes that are costlier this year

Starting the search after closing, identifying just one property, and relying on new financing without a backup are especially costly when a lender can pull back late. Constructive receipt of proceeds, even briefly, voids the exchange, so the QI must be set before sale.

The deadlines do not flex for a hard market. Only an IRS disaster declaration can postpone them, and that relief is for a real emergency, not a long deal. Also, a broader DST shelf does not make every trust interchangeable. Two trusts that each place $300,000 of equity can have very different leverage, property type, hold period, and sponsor record. Compare loan-to-value and read the private placement memorandum. If the underlying property does not stand on its own merits, tax deferral is not a reason to buy it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Did the 1031 exchange rules change for 2026?

No. The 45-day and 180-day clocks, equal-or-greater-value requirement, reinvestment of all equity, and debt replacement remain. The 2026 Opportunity Zone changes left section 1031 for real property alone.

### What makes a 1031 exchange harder to execute in 2026?

Longer closings and less certain financing. When diligence and loan approval stretch, the identification-to-close gap gets wider while the deadline remains fixed.

### Why pre-identify a DST as a backup?

Its low minimum, lack of financing contingency, and ability to close in days give the investor a completion path if a financed primary fails.

### How does a wider DST shelf help an exchanger?

It can match both equity and debt across self-storage, industrial, healthcare, multifamily, net-lease, and mineral royalties. A leverage profile may replace paid-off debt without a personal loan.

### Can a DST absorb leftover boot?

Yes. A small DST position can reinvest a cash shortfall, and suitable DST leverage may help replace mortgage boot.

### Can the 45-day and 180-day deadlines be extended in a hard market?

No. Only an IRS disaster declaration can postpone them; slow financing and a difficult market do not.

### What is the most important step for a 2026 exchange?

Put the QI in place before sale, then identify depth: a primary, a second option, and a fast-closing DST backup.

## Glossary

- **1031 Exchange:** A like-kind investment or business real-estate swap that defers capital gains, NIIT, and depreciation recapture.
- **Identification Period:** The 45 calendar days after sale for written identification to the QI.
- **Exchange Period:** The 180 calendar days, or earlier tax-return due date, to acquire replacement property.
- **Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** A passive, 1031-eligible fractional interest in institutional real estate that can close quickly without a financing contingency.
- **Boot:** Cash removed or debt not replaced; it is taxable.
- **Qualified Intermediary (QI):** The independent party that holds proceeds and receives identification, preventing constructive receipt.
- **Debt Replacement:** New financing or cash equal to paid-off debt, required for full deferral.
