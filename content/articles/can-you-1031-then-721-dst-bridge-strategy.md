# Can You 1031 Then 721? The DST Bridge Strategy

[Home](baker1031.html) · [Back to All Insights](insights.html)

I hear a version of this question whenever an owner wants a more passive real-estate position without turning a sale into an immediate tax bill: can the owner use a 1031 exchange to get all the way into a REIT?

Not directly. A 1031 exchange cannot deliver REIT shares or [OP units](op-units-explained.html), because neither is like-kind real property. Yet a two-step route can achieve the larger goal. The investor first completes a 1031 exchange into a Delaware Statutory Trust, or DST, then later, if the DST property is acquired by a REIT under a Section 721 transaction, receives OP units. The DST bridges direct property to REIT ownership, with deferral preserved at each step.

The key word is “if.” The path works only when the particular DST is structured for a potential 721/UPREIT exit and that exit materializes. The investor must understand the destination REIT, the sponsor’s role, and both the timing and certainty of the later transaction. This guide explains the bridge, the sequence, and the due diligence required before entering it.

## Yes, but not by going directly into the REIT

An investor can effectively 1031 and then 721 their way to REIT ownership. They cannot do so by completing a 1031 exchange directly into the REIT.

A 1031 exchange requires like-kind real property on both sides. What a REIT offers to an investor is either a REIT share—corporate stock—or an OP unit—a partnership interest. Neither is direct real property. Exchanging a building for REIT ownership would therefore be a taxable transaction rather than a qualifying 1031 exchange.

First-order thinking may focus on the fact that a REIT owns real estate. Second-order thinking focuses on the investor’s legal interest. The investor owns a security or a partnership interest, not the real estate itself. The direct route is foreclosed by that distinction.

The workaround is to use a DST as the intermediate holding. The investor 1031s into a DST, which qualifies as real property for the exchange. Later, the REIT may acquire the DST property through a 721 exchange, converting the DST interest into OP units. The two steps—1031 into the DST and then 721 into the REIT—produce an outcome the direct route cannot.

## Why a direct 1031 into REIT ownership is unavailable

The like-kind requirement requires real property for real property. REIT shares are corporate stock. OP units are partnership interests. Neither is the kind of direct real-property interest a 1031 exchange needs.

That means an owner cannot exchange direct property for REIT shares or OP units and call the result a 1031. The acquisition would be taxable. The rule applies even though the issuer owns a portfolio of real estate.

An owner could, in principle, contribute an individual property directly to a REIT operating partnership in a Section 721 transaction. In practice, that is usually not a workable path for an individual property: the REIT has to want that specific asset, and typical individual properties may not meet the REIT’s portfolio criteria. Thus neither a direct 1031 nor a direct 721 contribution of the typical individual property is an easy way to reach the REIT.

The DST solves the problem because it can be a 1031-eligible replacement interest and can later be part of a structure that the REIT acquires. It is the bridge between an owner’s direct property and the REIT’s operating partnership.

> A direct 1031 into a REIT does not work because REIT shares and OP units are not like-kind real property. A DST is the bridge because it holds real property and can be used in a later 721/UPREIT transaction.

## The DST’s role as the bridge

A [Delaware Statutory Trust](dst-guide.html) holds real estate. Under longstanding IRS guidance, a DST beneficial interest is treated as a direct interest in real property for 1031 purposes. The investor can therefore sell direct property and exchange into the DST interest while satisfying the like-kind requirement.

The DST has a second property that matters here: it may be structured so that the REIT can acquire its underlying property. In a [721 roll-up into the REIT](from-dst-to-reit-721-rollup.html), the REIT’s operating partnership acquires the DST property, and the DST investors receive OP units under Section 721.

The DST is therefore the practical intermediary that serves both requirements. It is 1031-eligible real property, and it can be part of a planned 721 transaction into a REIT. That is why DSTs are central to a strategy intended to reach REIT ownership tax-deferred from direct property.

## How the two-step sequence works

Step one starts with the sale of the owner’s direct property. The owner uses a 1031 exchange to acquire a DST interest, observing the applicable [1031 deadlines](721-exchanges-and-the-45-180-day-rules-when-a-dst-is-involved.html): [identify replacement property within 45 days](1031-exchange-identification-rules.html) and complete the exchange within 180 days. The DST must be one that is structured for a potential 721/UPREIT exit. At the end of this step, the owner has a passive DST interest and gain is deferred under Section 1031.

Step two occurs later, on the timetable of the DST and REIT structure. The REIT’s operating partnership acquires the DST property. The investor’s DST interest is converted into OP units under Section 721, and gain is generally deferred again. Afterward, the owner has an operating-partnership interest tied to the REIT, receives distributions comparable to the REIT’s dividends, and may have a later conversion path to REIT shares.

The sequence is direct property → DST interest → OP units. Gain carries from the original property through the DST and into the units. The deferral continues until the investor converts OP units to shares or cash in a taxable transaction, or potentially never recognizes the gain if the units are held until death and a step-up in basis applies.

The pathway is tax-deferred at both stages: Section 1031 governs the first step and Section 721 the second. The fact that each step is nonrecognition does not mean the investor has unlimited flexibility thereafter; OP units are partnership interests and are generally a one-way commitment away from future 1031 exchange eligibility.

## Confirm the DST’s 721 exit before entering the DST

The most important diligence item is not whether the DST is a qualifying 1031 replacement. It is whether the DST is actually structured for a 721/UPREIT exit.

Not every DST has that feature. Some are standalone DSTs without a planned REIT acquisition. A standalone DST may have merits as a passive, tax-deferred real-estate investment, but it will not deliver the investor to REIT ownership through this strategy.

For the bridge strategy, confirm that an affiliated REIT intends, or has the option, to acquire the DST property and convert investors’ interests into OP units. Review whether the proposed REIT acquisition and unit conversion are part of the structure or plan. Without an expected 721 exit, the DST will not complete the bridge.

The investor should also evaluate the REIT they will ultimately own: its portfolio, quality, and whether it is traded or non-traded. The DST is an intermediate holding; the destination REIT is the long-term investment. Due diligence should cover both.

## Timing and certainty of the 721 exit

The expected timing and certainty of a 721 exit are distinct issues. Some DSTs may have a planned exit timetable, such as a target number of years. Others give the sponsor the option to arrange an exit when it decides, without a fixed date. Reaching REIT ownership may therefore occur on a defined schedule or on an open-ended one.

Certainty can also differ. One structure may make a 721 exit highly likely or contractual. Another may merely preserve it as an option that the sponsor may or may not exercise. The investor should not assume that an anticipated exit is guaranteed.

These differences affect whether the bridge is reliable for the investor’s goals. An investor who needs REIT ownership by a particular date should confirm whether the anticipated DST exit supports that objective. An investor who sees the REIT conversion as essential should understand how assured the exit actually is.

The more complete the investor’s understanding of timing, exit certainty, sponsor discretion, and the destination REIT, the more realistic the decision. The DST bridge is a specific structure, not a promise that every passive real-estate investment eventually becomes a REIT interest.

## Key Takeaways

- A direct 1031 exchange into a REIT is not available because REIT shares and OP units are not like-kind real property.
- A DST is the bridge: it is 1031-eligible real property and can be acquired in a later 721/UPREIT transaction.
- The sequence is a Section 1031 exchange into the DST followed by the DST’s Section 721 exit into OP units. Both steps generally preserve deferral.
- Before entering the DST, verify that it has a potential 721 exit, understand how certain and when that exit may occur, and evaluate the destination REIT.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I do a 1031 exchange directly into a REIT?

No. REIT shares are corporate stock and OP units are partnership interests. Neither is like-kind real property, as Section 1031 requires. A direct exchange of property for either type of REIT ownership would be taxable. To reach a REIT structure tax-deferred, an investor can use a DST as an intermediate replacement interest and later receive OP units through a 721 exchange.

### Can you 1031 then 721?

Yes, effectively, through a DST bridge rather than a direct 1031 into the REIT. The investor first 1031s into a DST that is like-kind real property. Later, the REIT may acquire the DST property in a 721 exchange, converting the DST interest into OP units. The two steps are generally tax-deferred and bridge direct property to REIT ownership.

### Why is a DST used as the bridge?

A DST is both 1031-eligible and capable of participating in a planned 721 roll-up. Its interest is treated as a direct real-property interest for 1031 purposes, while the DST property can be acquired by the REIT operating partnership for OP units. It is the practical intermediary that satisfies the like-kind requirement before moving the investment into a REIT structure.

### How does the 1031-then-721 sequence work?

First, the investor completes a 1031 exchange into a DST within the 45-day identification and 180-day completion requirements, deferring gain under Section 1031. The DST must contemplate a future 721 exit. Later, the REIT acquires the DST property, and the investor receives OP units under Section 721, again generally without recognizing gain. The investor then holds OP units tied to the REIT, and gain has carried from property through DST interest to the units.

### Is the whole 1031-then-721 strategy tax-deferred?

The two steps are generally non-taxable: Section 1031 defers gain into the DST and Section 721 preserves it when the DST property becomes OP units. Deferral continues until the investor converts units to shares or cash, or otherwise makes a taxable disposition. If units are held until death, a step-up in basis may eliminate the deferred gain. A CPA should confirm the treatment for the particular transaction.

### What do I need to confirm before investing in the DST?

Confirm that the DST is structured for a potential 721/UPREIT exit and that an affiliated REIT intends, or has the option, to acquire its property and issue OP units. Also assess the exit’s expected timing and certainty, and evaluate the destination REIT’s portfolio, quality, and traded or non-traded status. This diligence determines whether the DST can actually serve as the bridge rather than remain a standalone holding.

### Is the 721 exit guaranteed?

Not always. A sponsor may have a planned timetable, a contractual or highly likely exit, or simply an option to acquire the property at a later date. Some structures have no fixed date. The investor should understand both whether an exit is expected and how assured it is before investing; it should not be assumed to be guaranteed.

### What if the DST is not structured for a 721 exit?

It will not bridge to the REIT. The investor will own a standalone DST, which can remain a passive, tax-deferred real-estate holding but does not accomplish the intended REIT transition. An investor pursuing the 1031-then-721 strategy must select a DST specifically structured for a potential 721/UPREIT exit.

### Can I 721 my own property directly into a REIT?

It is possible in principle, but usually impractical. The REIT must want the investor’s particular property, and a typical individual property may not meet the REIT’s portfolio criteria. The DST bridge is the practical route for most investors: exchange into a DST that the REIT is structured to acquire instead of attempting to contribute the individual property directly.

### How long until the 721 exit happens?

It depends on the structure. Some have a target number of years; others leave the timing to sponsor discretion without a fixed schedule. The exit may occur after a few years or on a less defined timetable. If reaching the REIT by a specified date matters, review the anticipated timing before investing and recognize that it may not be precise or guaranteed.

### Who should use the 1031-then-721 DST bridge strategy?

It can suit accredited investors who want a tax-deferred transition from direct property to diversified REIT ownership, are ready to leave active management for passive ownership, value diversification and eventual liquidity, and see estate-planning value in a possible step-up on OP units. They must be comfortable with the securities nature of the investment, the loss of property-level control and future 1031 flexibility after a roll-up, and reliance on the exit structure. It is the practical path for an investor seeking this full transition from property to a REIT structure.

## Glossary

**1031-then-721:** Reaching REIT ownership through a 1031 exchange into a DST followed by a 721 exchange into a REIT structure.

**DST Bridge:** Use of a DST as the intermediary between direct property and a REIT.

**Delaware Statutory Trust (DST):** The 1031-eligible real-property interest that can bridge to a REIT.

**Direct 1031 into REIT:** The unavailable route because REIT ownership is not direct real property.

**Like-Kind Real Property:** Property required by a 1031 exchange; a DST can qualify, while REIT shares and OP units do not.

**721/UPREIT Exit:** A REIT’s acquisition of the DST property and conversion of interests to OP units.

**Step One:** The Section 1031 exchange into the DST.

**Step Two:** The Section 721 exit of the DST into the REIT.

**OP Units:** REIT operating-partnership units received in step two.

**Continuous Deferral:** Carrying the deferred gain through both steps from property to OP units.

**Standalone DST:** A DST without a 721 exit that does not lead to a REIT.

**Exit Timing:** The schedule for the 721 exit, which may not be fixed.

**Exit Certainty:** How assured the 721 exit is, from likely or contractual to optional.

**Destination REIT:** The REIT the investor would ultimately own and should evaluate before entering the DST.

**Revenue Ruling 2004-86:** IRS guidance treating qualifying DST interests as 1031-eligible real property.

**Accredited Investor:** Investor status typically required for these securities.
