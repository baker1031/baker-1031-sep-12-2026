Missouri Tax Treatment of a 1031 Exchange

Under legislation enacted in 2025 (HB 594), Missouri allows individuals to subtract 100% of federally reported long-term capital gains from Missouri taxable income, effectively eliminating state income tax on qualifying capital gains beginning with tax year 2025. Ordinary income remains taxed at a top rate of 4.7%. Missouri continues to conform to IRC Section 1031.

Because the state gain may already be exempt for individuals, the primary benefit of a 1031 exchange for many Missouri sellers is deferring the federal capital-gains tax and depreciation recapture. Missouri does not impose nonresident real estate withholding at closing and has no clawback or continued-reporting rule for out-of-state exchanges; investors should confirm eligibility and any entity-level nuances with a tax advisor.

Missouri now exempts individual long-term capital gains from state tax (2025), so for many Missouri sellers a 1031 exchange chiefly defers the federal tax and recapture rather than a state tax.

Missouri Market Snapshot

Missouri's two large metros, St. Louis and Kansas City, offer extensive industrial and logistics corridors, deep multifamily inventory, and growing healthcare and net-lease sectors. Springfield and Columbia add regional multifamily and university-driven demand, and the state's central location supports significant distribution activity.

Central U.S. logistics positioning, relatively low costs, and steady metro job growth draw replacement capital into industrial and apartment assets, and the new capital-gains exemption adds to Missouri's appeal for investors.

Why 1031 & DST Investors Look at Missouri

  • Missouri exempts individual long-term capital gains, and 1031 still defers the federal tax.
  • St. Louis and Kansas City provide deep industrial and multifamily exchange markets.
  • Passive DST ownership lets Missouri investors exit active management while deferring federal gain.

Replacement-Property Options for Missouri Investors

Missouri exchangers can place proceeds into like-kind real estate anywhere in the United States, so a St. Louis or Kansas City property can be exchanged into professionally managed Delaware Statutory Trust interests nationwide. DSTs provide fractional, passive ownership across industrial, multifamily, and net-lease sectors and are commonly used to defer federal capital-gains tax and depreciation recapture; a later 721 UPREIT exchange can convert DST interests into REIT operating-partnership units, and Opportunity Zone funds remain a separate deferral option.

Missouri 1031 Exchange FAQ

Does Missouri tax a 1031 exchange?

No, and as of tax year 2025 Missouri also exempts individual long-term capital gains from state tax entirely. Missouri still conforms to IRC Section 1031, so a like-kind exchange defers the remaining federal gain.

Can I exchange Missouri property for out-of-state DSTs?

Yes. Like-kind real property can be located in any state, so a Missouri property can be exchanged into DSTs holding assets elsewhere while deferring federal gain.

If Missouri exempts capital gains, why still do a 1031 exchange?

The Missouri exemption applies only to state tax on individual long-term gains; a 1031 exchange still defers the federal capital-gains tax and depreciation recapture, which can be substantial.
