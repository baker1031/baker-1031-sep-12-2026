By Jerry Baker · Founder & Managing Principal, Baker 1031 Investments · Updated September 2, 2026

A qualified intermediary bond is a fidelity bond protecting a 1031 investor's exchange funds against loss from theft or misappropriation by the intermediary.

What Is Qualified Intermediary Bond?

A qualified intermediary bond is a form of financial protection, typically a fidelity bond, that a qualified intermediary carries to safeguard the client funds it holds during a 1031 exchange. Because the QI industry is only lightly regulated at the federal level and QIs hold large sums between closings, this coverage is an important safeguard against fraud or insolvency.

A fidelity bond covers losses from dishonest acts like theft or misappropriation by the QI's employees. Reputable QIs also carry errors-and-omissions insurance for mistakes and hold client money in segregated qualified escrow or qualified trust accounts, so it is not commingled with company funds.

There have been cases where under-capitalized QIs failed and investors lost exchange funds. That is why due diligence should include asking about bonding levels, insurance limits, and how funds are held. Some states also impose their own bonding or licensing requirements on exchange facilitators.

Key Points About Qualified Intermediary Bond

  • Fidelity bond protecting client funds held by a QI
  • Guards against theft or misappropriation of exchange proceeds
  • Complements E&O insurance and segregated escrow accounts
  • Important because QIs are lightly regulated federally
